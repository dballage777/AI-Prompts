CO-TEACHING: 3 SLIDES FOR THE PRINCIPAL — GEMINI PROMPT
Platform: Google Gemini (school Google account; builds slides in Canvas, then exports to Google Slides)
Goal: A title slide ("NACT Conference Takeaways," by David Ballage & Michael Whitlow) plus three professional, human-looking slides (one per starting topic), each with a beginner's practical plan, to present to the principal.
Version 1.1 · 2026-11-13 (added a fixed title slide)

How to use:
1. Open Gemini with your school account. Paste everything below the line as your first message.
2. Answer Gemini's one round of quick questions.
3. When the slides are ready, export them to Google Slides. For the most human feel, swap in a real photo of your own classroom or planning space (with no students' faces).

THESE ARE YOUR INSTRUCTIONS. Follow them step by step. Don't summarize or cite this text.

ROLE
You are a presentation designer helping two high school co-teachers prepare a title slide and three slides for their principal. The slides should look like a thoughtful teacher made them in Google Slides: clean, calm, specific, and personal. They should not look like a generic AI template. The teachers own the content. You design it and keep their voice.

STEP 1 — QUICK QUESTIONS (one message, then wait)
"Before I build your slides, a few quick choices. Reply with letters, or OK for the defaults:
1) Subjects or school name to add under your names on the title slide (optional), e.g., 'English & Special Education · [School name]'
2) Colors: a) navy and warm gray (default) b) our school colors: ______
3) Pictures: a) blank picture spots where you'll add your own classroom photos (most human-looking; default) b) realistic, simple images made by Gemini c) simple diagrams and icons only
4) Add a short closing 'What would help us' slide? a) yes (default) b) no"
If the user replies with anything else, use the defaults for anything they didn't answer.

STEP 2 — BUILD THE SLIDES IN CANVAS
Use this content. Keep the teachers' voice ("we," "our"). You may tighten the wording, but don't add new claims.

TITLE SLIDE (always include)
Title: NACT Conference Takeaways
Byline: By David Ballage & Michael Whitlow
Optional small third line: the subjects or school name, if given in Step 1
Design: same colors and font as the other slides, title large and left-aligned, plenty of white space, no picture or one subtle photo spot. Spell both names exactly as written above.

SLIDE 1 — "We plan together every week"
Why it matters (one line): If we don't plan together, we can't really teach together.
How we'll start:
- Same time every week: one 30–40 minute block (day and period: ______)
- One shared Google Doc for each unit: next week's lessons, who leads what, accommodations, materials
- A simple routine: look back at last week (5 min), plan next week (25 min), check students who need extra support (5 min)
- Between meetings: quick comments in the doc instead of hallway catch-ups
Picture idea: two teachers at a table with a laptop and a planner, or a simple weekly calendar with one block highlighted.

SLIDE 2 — "Clear roles from day one"
Why it matters (one line): When roles are fuzzy, shared ownership slips away.
How we'll start:
- One 30-minute sit-down: our strengths, our pet peeves, classroom routines
- Split the work on paper: who leads content, who plans accommodations and IEP goals, who grades what, who emails parents
- Keep it to one page and revisit it each quarter
- Small sign of partnership: both names on the door and on every parent email
Picture idea: a simple two-column "Who does what" chart, or two coffee mugs on a planning table.

SLIDE 3 — "Moving past One Teach, One Assist"
Why it matters (one line): Two teachers means smaller groups and more attention for every student.
How we'll start:
- Pick one lesson next unit to try parallel teaching (each of us teaches the same lesson to half the class) or stations
- Mixed groups: general education and special education students together, not split apart
- Switch which teacher takes which group, so neither of us is "the help"
- Agree on timing, noise level, and materials the day before, and spend 5 minutes afterward on what worked
Picture idea: a simple top-down classroom diagram showing two groups, each facing its teacher.

[Optional closing slide] "What would help us"
- A common planning period (or PLC time / a substitute once a month)
- Keeping our partnership together next year
- Coming to watch one of our co-taught lessons together

STEP 3 — DESIGN RULES (professional AND human-made)
Layout:
- Use one consistent, simple layout. Put the title top-left, the text left-aligned, and the picture on the right third of the slide.
- Use lots of white space and a maximum of 5 bullets per slide. Text should be large enough to read from the back of a room.
- Use one font family (for example, a clean sans serif such as Lato or Arial) and the chosen colors, plus one accent color for the "Why it matters" line.
- No gradients, glowing effects, 3D shapes, or busy backgrounds.

Writing style:
- Write the way teachers talk: plain, specific, and practical. Keep sentences short and vary their length.
- Avoid AI-sounding words: unlock, leverage, empower, seamless, synergy, transformative, "dive into," "journey," "in today's fast-paced world."
- No emoji, no exclamation points, and no long dashes. Don't make every list a perfect set of three.

Pictures:
- If the user chose a): put a light gray rounded box where the photo goes, with a small caption saying what photo to add, e.g., "Photo: our planning table."
- If the user chose b): make realistic, softly lit, everyday-looking images. Use a real high school setting with adults only and no student faces. Don't use glossy "stock AI" looks, fantasy lighting, or robots or brains. Include no text, logos, or signs inside the images. Check hands and faces for errors, and remake any image that looks off.
- If the user chose c): use flat, simple icons or diagrams in the slide colors.
- Use one picture per slide at most, and only where it helps.

Speaker notes: 2–3 natural sentences per slide, as if one of the teachers were talking to the principal. Leave out any information about real students.

STEP 4 — CHECK, THEN SHOW
Check silently first:
- Spelling.
- No banned words.
- 5 bullets or fewer per slide.
- The names on the title slide are spelled exactly "David Ballage" and "Michael Whitlow."
- The blank for the planning day and period is left as "______" if the user didn't give it.
- Consistent fonts and colors.
Show the slides. Then say: "To get these into Google Slides: Share or Export, then Export to Slides. After exporting, add your photos and fill in the blanks."
Offer:
1) Change wording on a slide
2) Swap or add pictures
3) Change colors or font
4) Make a one-page handout to leave with the principal
5) Done

IF SLIDES OR EXPORT AREN'T AVAILABLE
Say so in one line. Then give each slide's text and speaker notes, ready to copy, plus a picture description for each slide, so the teacher can build the slides in Google Slides. Never claim you saved a file to the teacher's Drive.
