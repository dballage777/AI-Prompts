## Learned points
1. [Documented] Core rule: "Episodes should be added exactly as they first aired on the original network (title, date, order, season)." The Seasons page sets the same goal: "reproduce the seasons exactly as they first aired on the original network", except for anime, which has its own guidelines. The default TMDB order is original air order, not production order, DVD order or streaming order.
2. [Documented] Re-organized seasons are not re-arranged. When a network re-organizes seasons (e.g. American Dad, Futurama after a network change), "we do not re-arranged the aired seasons/episodes. The new season is simply added as the next season."
3. [Documented] Alternative orders live in Episode Groups: "The Episode Groups section can be used to create different orders such as the production order, the DVD order, a new order, or country-specific orders." For anime: "Any alternative order including but not limited to the TV cours, the US seasons, the Japanese DVD releases, the different story arcs and the absolute order can be created with episode groups."
4. [Documented] Specials are always Season 0 ("The special season is always Season 0"). The first episode of a season is always "1", never "0". Episodes released as "Episode 0", "0.5" or any other odd-numbered or unnumbered episode go in Specials. So do bloopers, recaps, previews, best-ofs, live specials, unaired pilots and blacklisted episodes. A network-labeled "special" that has a real season/episode number stays in its season. Theatrical films are not allowed in Specials; they are movie entries.
5. [Documented] Season numbers run 1, 2, 3, and years must not be used as season numbers. A season's Original Air Date comes from its first episode and cannot be edited directly. It is "null" when no first episode exists ("We do not yet properly support continuous numbering").
6. [Documented] Continuous shows: "These shows should only have one season, which contains all the episodes" (often soaps and anime). For anime, long-running series with continuous numbers (867, 868, 869...) are a single season.
7. [Documented] Anime original order: episodes must follow "the exact order in which they were originally released in Japan". International releases "often have episode orders, episode counts and season splits that are wildly different from the original Japanese release" and must not be used as templates. The official Japanese website's order can differ from first-air order, e.g. Pokémon episode 38, banned after its first broadcast.
8. [Documented] Rules for anime sequels in seasons. A sequel that directly continues the story goes in the same TMDB entry. If numbering continues, it goes in the same season. If numbering restarts at Episode 1, it becomes a new season. Split-cour runs (1–13, break, 14–26) stay one season (example AKB0048). Multi-part releases with continuous numbering stay one season even after a year-plus hiatus (examples Fairy Tail / Fairy Tail (2014), Dragon Ball Z Kai / The Final Chapters, Naruto / Naruto Shippuden). Reboots, spin-offs, alternative-timeline series and new adaptations of the same source get a new TV entry (example Digimon).
9. [Documented] Inferred consequence, stated by the rules above: Naruto Shippuden sits inside the Naruto TMDB entry, while MAL (S12) keeps separate entries per season/sequel. TMDB seasons and MAL entries are not one-to-one. The bible itself warns "every database has their own, unique guidelines" and says AniDB, MyAnimeList, ANN and Syoboi "should be used with caution".
10. [Documented] Cours: a Japanese TV year has four three-month cours (Jan–Mar, Apr–Jun, Jul–Sep, Oct–Dec), with about 12 episodes per cour. Formats are single cour, double cour, split cour and continuous.
11. [Documented] Specials and OVAs for anime. Single-episode OVAs, recaps (unless numbered as regular episodes), DVD bonus episodes and anything not in the first Japanese TV broadcast go in Specials. Standalone OVA series are TV entries with a blank Network and TV Show Type "Video". Standalone single-episode OVAs are movie entries. OVAs that add to an existing series go in that show's Specials. Movies are movie entries and are not duplicated in Specials.
12. [Documented] Re-edits are generally not supported. Dragon Ball Z Kai and Mobile Suit Gundam Unicorn RE:0096 are the named exceptions.
13. [Documented] Segments. Kids' cartoons broadcast with 2–3 segments per half hour are split into one episode per segment, because segments get re-paired later. This does not apply to streaming-original series. Anime segments are split when the official site gives each its own title (Chio's School Road, Shin-chan), but not otherwise (Aho-Girl). Episode counts for segmented kids' shows and anime can therefore be about double the broadcast slot count.
14. [Documented] Air dates. Only the original air date on the original network is supported. Anime past-midnight times are normalized: "2017-05-12 26:00" is 2:00 AM the next day, so the air date is 2017-05-13 (the same rule as MAL, S12). Guessed dates are not allowed, and unknown dates are left blank. Digital previews are usually ignored, but an on-network preview date can be used.
15. [Documented] Sources. Contributors are told to use official sources, and "Data found on databases like IMDb and Wiki is often wrong." When a series is cancelled early, episodes that only came out digitally move to Specials (examples: Powerless, Don't Trust the B---- in Apartment 23).
16. [Documented] Episode names keep the original title and the first official translated title per language. No "TBA" and no unofficial translations. Multi-part episodes use "Title (1)" / "Title (2)". Crossover episodes use roman numerals (I), (II).
17. [Inferred] For the recommender: (a) when citing "Season N Episode M" from TMDB for anime, warn that it may not match streaming services or MAL entries; (b) look in Season 0 for specials and OVAs, and in Episode Groups (TV episode_groups endpoint) for absolute, DVD or story-arc orders; (c) a new TMDB entry usually means reboot/spin-off/new adaptation, while a new season usually means numbering restarted.

## Limitations or bias
- These are contributor guidelines. Actual entries are crowd-edited and may not follow them, especially older ones.
- No last-updated date is shown. I did not make a live API call to check Episode Groups, because it needs a TMDB key (the endpoint name is my knowledge, not from this page; check it against the TMDB API docs).
