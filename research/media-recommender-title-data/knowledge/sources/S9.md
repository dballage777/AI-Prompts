## Learned points
1. [Documented] Who rates: "Raters are all current parents," not industry experts. A new rater must have a child aged 5–15 and can serve up to seven years or until their youngest child turns 21. Rater identities are confidential except for senior raters.
2. [Documented] Process: the board watches every film in full, each rater votes, and the Senior Rater leads a discussion. The rating and descriptor are set "by a simple majority vote of the raters." Content factors are violence, language, sex, nudity, drugs, smoking and thematic elements.
3. [Documented] Submission is voluntary. MPA members agree to submit all films that get a theatrical release, and the Rules (Art. I §1.F) say member films "may only be exhibited in the United States with a CARA rating." Independents are not required to submit, but many do because "most theaters prefer to exhibit rated films."
4. [Documented] Streaming: "most streamers submit their feature film for a rating." This is a soft statement; it does not say all of them do.
5. [Documented] Unrated or not rated: a non-member "may elect not to accept the rating ... or to surrender the rating ... and thereafter may exhibit or distribute the motion picture in the United States without a rating" (Rules §1.G). The filmmaker FAQ says a non-member releasing theatrically "may withdraw your film from the rating process and release the film without a rating." Home-video releases with bonus material must carry the legend "This product contains the _-rated motion picture and additional unrated material."
6. [Documented] Re-rating and edited versions: a rating "may not be used in connection with the exhibition or distribution of any different version" (§1.H). A replacement re-rating is possible only if the original version has not yet been shown or distributed in the US; the original certificate is then surrendered. Otherwise the new cut is a "differently-rated" version. After theatrical release there is usually a withdrawal period of at least 90 days before the new version can be promoted, and the new version must carry "This _-rated version contains material different from the original _-rated version" and a title showing that it differs.
7. [Documented] A film that doesn't get the expected rating has three options: edit and resubmit (no extra fee), appeal, or, for non-members releasing theatrically, withdraw and release unrated (submit-a-film FAQ).
8. [Documented] Appeals: the Appeals Board is chaired by the MPA CEO and includes MPA member-company, NATO (theater owner), IFTA, independent and non-affiliated representatives. An appeal must be filed no more than 25 business days after certification and no fewer than 25 business days before release. The board may overturn only if the rating was "clearly erroneous," and only by a two-thirds vote of the members voting.
9. [Documented] Enforcement: most US theaters (members of Cinema United) enforce ratings. R "does not prohibit parents from bringing younger children," though many theaters have their own policies. NC-17 means "children are not admitted even if accompanied by an adult."
10. [Documented] The ratings are US-only: "Every country has their own system for rating and classifying movies."
11. [Documented] The MPA does not rate TV. The TV Parental Guidelines Monitoring Board does, and the MPA sits on that board (see S6).
12. [Documented] Publication timing: a film's rating becomes searchable on filmratings.com "the first Tuesday evening (sometimes Wednesday morning)" after certification, and it also appears in the Weekly Bulletin. The rating is emailed within 24 hours of the screening.
13. [Documented] Ratings and descriptors "are not judgments of the quality or the value or the social importance of a film."
14. [Documented] The FAQ's resource list links to third-party sites, including IMDb, Kids in Mind, Rotten Tomatoes, Fandango and the TV Parental Guidelines. These are only links; no data relationship is described.
15. [Inferred] For the recommender: (a) "Not Rated" or "Unrated" on a feature is a legitimate state, common for non-member or limited releases, and is not missing data; (b) unrated extended or director's cuts are different versions with no MPA rating of their own, so the recommender should not carry the theatrical rating over to them; (c) a very new film may lack a searchable rating for up to about a week after certification.

## Limitations or bias
- These are self-descriptions by the rating body. The streamer coverage claim gives no numbers.
- The main FAQ page does not cover unrated status, appeals or edited versions itself. Those points come from the Rules PDF and the submit-a-film FAQ on the same site.
- The appeals section of the Rules PDF was skimmed (organization, deadlines, standard, vote threshold). Hearing procedure details were not read in full.
