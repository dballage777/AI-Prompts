## Learned points
1. [Documented] ESRB runs "a three-part system that includes Rating Categories, Content Descriptors, and Interactive Elements," founded by the video game industry in 1994.
2. [Documented] Rating categories: Everyone ("generally suitable for all ages. May contain minimal cartoon, fantasy or mild violence and/or infrequent use of mild language"); Everyone 10+ (ages 10 and up; more cartoon, fantasy or mild violence, mild language, minimal suggestive themes); Teen (ages 13 and up; violence, suggestive themes, crude humor, minimal blood, simulated gambling, infrequent strong language); Mature 17+ (intense violence, blood and gore, sexual content, strong language); Adults Only 18+ ("prolonged scenes of intense violence, graphic sexual content and/or gambling with real currency").
3. [Documented] Rating Pending and "Rating Pending — Likely Mature 17+" appear only in marketing for physical games awaiting a rating and "should be replaced by a game's rating once it has been assigned." They are not final ratings.
4. [Documented] There are 30 content descriptors, grouped as Substances, Blood/Gore, Humor, Violence, Gambling, Language, Nudity and Sexuality: Alcohol Reference, Animated Blood, Blood, Blood and Gore, Comic Mischief, Crude Humor, Drug Reference, Fantasy Violence, Gambling Themes, Intense Violence, Language, Lyrics, Mature Humor, Nudity, Partial Nudity, Real Gambling, Sexual Content, Sexual Themes, Sexual Violence, Simulated Gambling, Strong Language, Strong Lyrics, Strong Sexual Content, Suggestive Themes, Tobacco Reference, Use of Alcohol, Use of Drugs, Use of Tobacco, Violence ("May contain bloodless dismemberment"), Violent References.
5. [Documented] Descriptors "are applied relative to the Rating Category assigned and are not intended to be a complete listing of content," and the prefix "Mild" conveys "low frequency, intensity or severity."
6. [Documented] Interactive Elements "do not influence the rating assignment": In-Game Purchases; In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items) (e.g., loot boxes); Users Interact (exposure to unfiltered user-generated content); Shares Location; Unrestricted Internet. There is also a separate notice, "Online Music Not Rated by the ESRB."
7. [Documented] Rating Summaries, which describe content in context, are assigned to "many physical games." They show up in ratings search on esrb.org and in the free ESRB Rating Search App.
8. [Documented] Physical games are rated by "consensus of at least three specially trained raters." Raters do not play the games during rating; the FAQ gives game length (up to 50+ hours) and branching play as the reasons. They review the content the publisher discloses and play-test many games after release. Rater identities are confidential.
9. [Documented] IARC: ESRB is a founding authority of the International Age Rating Coalition, which runs a "streamlined process" for digitally delivered games and apps. Games and apps available only online are rated through IARC. The listed storefronts include Microsoft Store/Xbox, Nintendo eShop, PlayStation Store, Google Play, Google Stadia and the Oculus Store, with ESRB ratings shown in North America. The Ratings Guide and esrb.org search cover only games rated through the traditional process, but ESRB also rates non-game apps via IARC.
10. [Documented] DLC usually inherits the core game's rating. If the DLC exceeds that rating it must be submitted and may get a different rating. An update found to be incompatible can trigger a rating change for the DLC or, if the update is required, for the core product.
11. [Documented] The system is voluntary, but "all console manufacturers as well as certain U.S. retailers and mobile or online storefronts require ESRB ratings." Retailers age-verify sales of M and AO games, and the FTC is cited as finding that unaccompanied children are stopped 87% of the time.
12. [Documented] Enforcement: post-release testing and public comment monitoring. Ratings found to rest on incomplete disclosure are corrected. Physical games face sanctions, including fines, through the Advertising Review Council.
13. [Inferred] For the recommender: ESRB applies only if games are in scope. It is not a film or TV system, so do not map E/T/M to MPA or TV ratings one-to-one. A digital-only title's rating comes via IARC and won't appear in the esrb.org search, so "not found on esrb.org" does not mean the title is unrated.

## Key quotes

- "Content Descriptors are applied relative to the Rating Category assigned and are not intended to be a complete listing of content." — https://www.esrb.org/ratings-guide/
- "ESRB raters do not play through games during the rating process" — https://www.esrb.org/faqs/

## Limitations or bias
- This is the rating body describing itself. Ratings for physical games rest on the publisher's disclosure of content, not on full play-throughs.
- The IARC storefront list includes Google Stadia, which suggests the FAQ text is dated. No update date is shown.
- The letter codes (E, E10+, T, M, AO, RP) appear on the guide as icons; the FAQ text uses "M (Mature) and AO (Adults Only)."
- The non-game app criteria page and the IARC site were not opened.
