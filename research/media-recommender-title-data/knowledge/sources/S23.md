## Learned points
1. [Research] The paper sorts OOD (out-of-catalog) mitigation into three grounding paradigms: (a) retrieval-based, which embeds the user and items and retrieves in-domain items; (b) constrained generation, which restricts decoding to catalog item titles through a prefix tree (trie); and (c) item-tokenizer, where each item becomes a short sequence of discrete codes decoded under prefix-tree constraints. Agentic frameworks, where an LLM controller calls a traditional recommender (Chat-Rec, InteRecAgent), are cited as a retrieval variant.
2. [Research] Mechanism: the LLM is fine-tuned to emit special tokens `<SOI>` (start-of-item) and `<EOI>` (end-of-item) around each recommendation. Only the span between them is grounded, so normal conversation is unconstrained.
3. [Research] RecLM-ret: the hidden state at `<SOI>` is projected into an item-embedding index (BGE-M3 over title, description, and category, plus an adapter). The nearest catalog item is retrieved and its title inserted. Training uses an LM loss plus a retrieval loss.
4. [Research] RecLM-cgen: a GRPO-trained Title Rewriter compresses verbose item metadata into short, distinct titles, which are then used to build the prefix tree. Its five reward terms are user-to-item, item-to-item, decoding complexity (perplexity), conciseness, and discriminative power (picking the original out of 4 similar titles). A "scope mask" loss limits the training softmax to tokens the trie allows.
5. [Research] RecLM-token: each item gets 4 codes from an RQ-VAE (4 codebooks × 256 entries) over Llama3-8B embeddings, for example `<a_11><b_2><c_135><d_157>`. Training is SFT plus GRPO with rank-based rewards.
6. [Research] How zero OOD is achieved: every recommendation either comes from an index lookup (ret) or is decoded only along trie paths that end at a real catalog item (cgen, token). Output outside the catalog is therefore structurally impossible. All three variants report OOD@10 = 0.00% on Steam, Amazon Movies & TV, and Amazon Toys.
7. [Research] Baseline OOD@10 for ungrounded models. On Amazon Movies & TV: GPT-4o 61.21%, Llama3-8B 52.52%, and prompt-only constrained Llama3-cgen 11.91%. On Steam: GPT-4o 16.08% and Llama3 15.26%. On Toys: GPT-4o 89.57% and Llama3 90.99%. Fine-tuned baselines without hard constraints still left OOD: CtrlRec 8.13% and PALR 14.85% on Movies.
8. [Research] Ablation on Movies: a fine-tuned model that emits `<SOI>` but decodes without constraints (v0) had OOD@10 17.48% and Repeat@10 39.26%. Adding the trie constraint (v1) brought both to 0.00%.
9. [Research] Data setup: users with at least 17 interactions, sequences truncated to 17, 10K users sampled, leave-one-out evaluation, and a Llama3-8B-Instruct backbone with LoRA rank 16. On Movies, RecLM-token had the best HR@10 (0.1700) and NDCG@10 (0.1622), and SASRec reached HR@10 0.1510.
10. [Research] Conversation is preserved. In a 3-turn dialogue (math, then a recommendation request, then math), cgen and token emitted exactly 10 `<SOI>` in the recommendation turn about 100% of the time and almost never in the math turns. General benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, CSQA, HumanEval) dropped only slightly.
11. [Research] Stated limitations: autoregressive inference latency is a problem for real-time systems, and evaluation covered accuracy only (no diversity, fairness, or long-term satisfaction measures).
12. [Inferred] For a personal Claude Code recommender, fine-tuning is not an option, but the principle transfers. The "Amazon Movies & TV" OOD numbers show that even film catalogs give high OOD when the target catalog is a specific product listing. The practical equivalent is to make the catalog the only source of titles: the model proposes a search query or criteria, the tool returns real items with IDs, and the model may only pick from returned IDs (retrieve-then-rank, the RecLM-ret and agentic pattern). Alternatively, the model can generate freely, and every title is resolved to an ID afterwards and dropped if unresolved.
13. [Inferred] The catalog here defines what counts as in-domain. For a personal recommender, the "catalog" could be TMDB, AniList, or Wikidata IDs, or a user's watchlist or streaming availability. The choice determines what counts as a hallucination.

## Limitations or bias
- Requires fine-tuning an open model (Llama3-8B), so it is not directly usable in a hosted assistant session.
- The datasets are product-review catalogs (Amazon) and Steam. "Movies" here means Amazon product listings (often DVD or edition strings), so the high GPT-4o OOD partly reflects the difficulty of matching product titles, not inventing films.
- OOD@10 = 0 guarantees catalog membership only, not that the item is relevant or that descriptions are correct.
- The authors propose the method and report state-of-the-art results, so there is a potential for favorable baseline tuning.

## Best used for
- A taxonomy of grounding approaches (retrieve, constrain, tokenize) and why hard constraints give zero invented titles.
- Baseline evidence that frontier models with prompting alone produce many catalog misses, which supports a "pick only from tool-returned IDs" design.
