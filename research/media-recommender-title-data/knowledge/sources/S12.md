## Learned points
1. [Documented] MAL's scope for "anime" has four tests. The work must be animated (manga panels and VN-style stills do not count, claymation does). It must be made by professional staff in Japan for the Japanese market. It must be meant as an animated work (game cut-scenes are excluded and VTuber content does not count). It must not be an edit, cut or promo of another work (for example, Robotech is excluded as a foreign edit). Section I.1 also admits Korean "aeni", Chinese/HK/Taiwanese "donghua", and qualifying joint productions. So MAL does contain non-Japanese titles, and type alone does not tell you the country.
2. [Documented] Under I.2.2, a joint production qualifies only if a creator from the I.1 countries holds a key role with creative control (director, script, storyboard or character design). If only the animation is outsourced, the work is not eligible (examples given: Neo Yokio, Pacific Rim). Western shows animated in Japan are therefore often missing from MAL by design.
3. [Documented] Type is set by the first release, as long as most episodes came out that way (II.1.1). Example: ep 1 online and eps 2–12 on TV gives type TV. Limited theatrical pre-screenings do not give type Movie (II.1.2). The guidelines warn that a title "may be added to the database with the Movie type, but then later be changed to TV or OVA". Shows that aired on TV and a subscription platform at the same time (Netflix, Amazon Prime) stay TV (II.1.3).
4. [Documented] Type definitions. OVA = episodes distributed on BD/DVD separately from other anime episodes (mail order, bundled with manga volumes, given at events) (II.5). Special = episodes released alongside a TV series, movie or OVA: episode 0, recap episode 8.5, compiled 1+ hour broadcasts, unaired episode 13 on the last BD, short BD extras, picture dramas, event shorts (II.6). ONA = released online (II.7). Music = professional music videos with new animation and the full song (II.8). CM = commercials of decent length with substantial plot (II.9).
5. [Documented] Season splitting. A TV series with a gap of at least one season (~12 weeks) between its two parts gets two entries, but only for shows still airing. Finished shows with such a gap are not split after the fact (Cardcaptor Sakura and Big O are not split; White Album and Asura Cryin are) (II.3.1). With no gap, a continuation under a new title gets its own entry (II.3.2). Overflow episodes released online are included in the main entry (II.3.3). As a result, MAL "seasons" do not line up one-to-one with TMDB/TVDB seasons.
6. [Documented] Re-edits and remasters. A TV re-edit or remaster does not get a new entry (Psycho-Pass, Gundam SEED), unless it has fewer episodes and a shorter total runtime (II.3.5). A series later compiled for theaters gets a separate Movie entry (II.2.3). An OVA series later broadcast on TV with recompiled episodes gets a separate TV entry (II.2.1). Same-format re-releases are added only if they include significant new story material (II.2.4).
7. [Documented] Movies get separate entries unless all parts screened together, or the premieres of all parts were under 3 months apart (II.4). OVA episodes are combined into one entry unless the franchise is OVA-only and the new episodes are labeled a sequel, or a new TV/ONA season aired in between (II.5).
8. [Documented] Main title = MAL's romanization of the official title (III.1). When two entries share a title but differ in type, the newer one gets the type in parentheses, e.g. "(TV)". When they share a type, the newer one gets the year (III.1.1). 第2期 becomes "2nd Season" (III.1.2). 劇場版/映画 becomes "Movie", moved to the end (III.1.2, Movies). A sequel announced with no format is "(Zoku-hen)", and a new project is "(Shinsaku Anime)". These placeholder titles change later (III.1.4).
9. [Documented] English title field (III.3) holds one official title, in this order of precedence: (i) English physical-release title, (ii) English simulcast title, (iii) English title shown in the official logo or on the Japanese site. When seasons can't be told apart, "2nd Season" is added (III.3.4). Synonyms (max 225 characters) hold official alternates, acronyms and fansub romanizations (III.4). Title matching should therefore check the main, English, Japanese and synonym fields.
10. [Documented] Air dates use JST and convert late-night "24+ hour" times. "25:40 JST on the 5th" is recorded as a start date of the 6th and a 1:40am broadcast slot (IV.5.1). Pre-airings go in a separate Preview field (IV.5.2). Incomplete OVA/ONA series are set to Finished Airing after 18 months with no activity (IV.5.3). Expect off-by-one dates against Western databases.
11. [Documented] Episode counts come from official information, or else from the number of airing slots (IV.12.1). When broadcast and BD numbering conflict, broadcast numbering wins, and for multiple broadcasts the first release wins (IV.12.2, examples Princess Tutu and Saiki Kusuo no Ψ-nan).
12. [Documented] Source (IV.10) is the medium the anime is directly adapted from (for SAO, the light novel, not the web novel). "Original" is only for anime announced as original, even if manga chapters came out first. Franchise movies (Pokemon, Conan) are not "Original". "Mixed Media" is for anime announced at the same time as other media. "Web Manga" means first published online.
13. [Documented] Demographics (IV.13.1). Josei/Seinen/Shoujo/Shounen are given only to (a) adaptations whose manga or light-novel source has that demographic in MAL's manga DB, with changes requested on the manga side first, or (b) projects documented as aimed at that demographic in news or interviews. Kids (IV.13.2) is given to picture-book or juvenile-literature (児童文学) sources, or shows airing in a children's TV slot. Original anime often have no demographic. The live check confirms this: Code Geass (MAL 1575, Source: Original) shows no Demographic field.
14. [Documented] Genres and themes must apply to "at least 70-80% of the series" (IV.13.3). A few jokes do not make a show Comedy, and a few emotional scenes do not make it Drama.
15. [Documented] Relations (IV.7). "Adaptation" is used only between the Anime and Manga databases. Recaps link only to their parent anime. Adaptations link only to the original source, not both. A new project announced with no stated relation is set to "Other" ("Do not make assumptions as to whether or not it's a sequel or a remake/reboot"). The guidelines do not define the other relation types (Sequel, Prequel, Side Story, Alternative Version, Alternative Setting, Spin-Off, Summary, Parent/Full Story, Character). The live 1575 page uses them, e.g. the compilation movies "Hangyaku no Lelouch I/II" are labeled "Alternative Version" and "Boukoku no Akito" is labeled "Alternative Setting".
16. [Documented] Ratings (IV.8). R+ = non-hentai with exposed female nipples, including uncensored BD nudity. Rx = erotica/hentai with exposed or mosaic-censored genitalia. Sex scenes alone don't make R+/Rx. The live page shows "R+ - Mild Nudity" for Code Geass. MAL rating is a nudity-based in-house label, not an age board rating.
17. [Documented] Background (V.2) should state source coverage, e.g. "The adaptation starts at volume __, chapter __ and ends at volume __...". It may also note deviations such as "An original ending was written for the series." This makes MAL Background a possible but optional place to find manga-to-anime coverage. Reviewer opinions and fan polls are excluded (V.4.4).
18. [Documented] Episode types (VI.3): Main, Filler (side-story padding between adapted and source content, not checked if only half the episode is filler) and Recap (more than half recap).
19. [Inferred] For the recommender, treat MAL type, episodes and dates as rule-based but revisable. Type can change after a pre-screening, and the "(TV)" or year suffix in a title marks a same-name collision. Missing demographics usually mean an Original or Mixed Media source, not "for everyone". Use the relation label (Sequel/Prequel vs Alternative Version/Setting) to tell continuations apart from retellings, and never assume an "Other" entry is a sequel.

## Limitations or bias
- These are moderator guidelines. They describe intended practice, not guaranteed data quality. Older entries may predate the rules (the guidelines note that past entries are not split retroactively).
- The text says it is "general guidelines only" and that staff may change rules at will. Section numbering has typos (two III.1.2s, IV.9 listed as IV.8.x, V/VI mixed).
- The relation types other than Adaptation/Other are not defined here. Alternative Version vs Alternative Setting vs Sequel is learned only from a live entry, not from policy.
- A live Jikan API call (api.jikan.moe/v4/anime/1575/relations) failed through the network proxy (tunnel closed), so the API field names were not verified this session.
