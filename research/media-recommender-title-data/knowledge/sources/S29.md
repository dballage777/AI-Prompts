- Access status: OPENED · Date read: 2026-11-14
- Pages read: https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03214 (full HTML: Sections 1–7; appendices referenced)
- Published / last updated: v2 dated 22 Nov 2023 (first posted Oct 2023). All models were evaluated on 26 April 2023.
- Type: paper (dynamic QA benchmark + prompting method); dataset at github.com/freshllms/freshqa

## Learned points
1. [Research] FreshQA: 600 natural questions (500 test, 125 per type; 100 dev; plus 15 demonstrations) in four types. Never-changing answers almost never change. Slow-changing answers change over several years. Fast-changing answers change "within a year or less". False-premise questions have an incorrect premise that must be rebutted. Each type has one-hop and multi-hop questions.
2. [Research] Questions are tied to dates. Annotators recorded the year the answer last changed, a supporting URL, and a next review date. Questions whose answers change more than about weekly were excluded. A question can change type over time; for example, a false-premise question becomes fast-changing if facts change.
3. [Research] Two-mode human evaluation (more than 50K judgments). Relaxed checks only that the main answer is correct. Strict requires every claim to be correct and up to date, with no hallucination. A hedge such as "as of my knowledge cutoff" only counts if the fact has not changed. Inter-rater agreement was 99% (Relaxed) and 96% (Strict). The automatic FreshEval judge agreed with humans 96.5% and 96% of the time.
4. [Research] Models without search did poorly: overall Strict accuracy ranged from 0.8% to 32.0%, and Relaxed from 0.8% to 46.4%. On fast-changing and post-2022 knowledge, GPT-4 "never exceeds 15%". Bigger models show "flat scaling" on fast-changing and false-premise questions.
5. [Research] Refusing vs. outdated answers: GPT-4 refused about 60% of fast-changing questions, and ChatGPT refused about 16%. Many answers were outdated "as of my knowledge cutoff" statements.
6. [Research] False premises: T5 and PaLM scored 0.0–1.6%. GPT-3.5, ChatGPT, and GPT-4 scored 25.8–42.7% (Strict) and 32.3–66.9% (Relaxed). Adding "Please check if the question contains a valid premise before answering" raised false-premise accuracy (+23.4 pts for GPT-3.5 and +6.4 for GPT-4, Strict) but cut GPT-3.5's overall accuracy by about 21 pts. GPT-4 was essentially unaffected.
7. [Research] Chain-of-thought (CoT) and verbose answers increase hallucination. CoT beats few-shot under Relaxed but loses under Strict. Verbose demonstration answers cost 2.6 pts under Strict. Concise, direct answers reduce hallucination.
8. [Research] FreshPrompt method: query Google Search (via SerpApi) with the question verbatim. Collect organic results, related questions, crowdsourced QA, the knowledge graph, and the answer box. Format each piece of evidence with source, date, title, snippet, and highlights. Sort from oldest to newest so the newest evidence sits at the end of the prompt. Add 5 few-shot demonstrations that reason over the evidence to find the most recent answer. This takes one LLM call.
9. [Research] Results (Strict, all questions): GPT-4 alone 28.6%, GPT-4 + Self-Ask 47.8%, Perplexity.AI 52.2%, Google Search answer box / top snippet alone 39.6%, and GPT-4 + FreshPrompt 75.6% (77.6% with 15 pieces of evidence). On fast-changing questions, GPT-4 went from 12.0% to 59.2%. The Strict–Relaxed gap fell from 17.8 to 2.2 pts, meaning far fewer outdated or hallucinated claims.
10. [Research] The number of pieces of evidence matters most: going from 1 to 15 adds +16.2 pts (Strict). Ordering by search rank or date beats random order by up to 2.2 pts. Removing the answer box and related questions costs about 3 pts. More demonstrations (5 to 15) slightly hurt.
11. [Research] Search also helps "old" knowledge: GPT-4 + FreshPrompt gained +30.5 pts on valid-premise questions about pre-2022 facts.
12. [Research] Limitations: answers go stale between maintainer updates. The method was tested only with Google Search features (answer boxes may not exist elsewhere), uses one query per question, and covers English short-form QA only.
13. [Inferred] For the recommender: release status, latest season, whether a show is renewed, cancelled, or ended, episode counts of airing series, streaming availability, and upcoming release dates are all "fast-changing" facts. The model's memory fails on them even when it sounds confident. Always fetch these from a live API (TMDB `status`, `last_air_date`, `next_episode_to_air`; TVmaze; AniList `status`/`nextAiringEpisode`) and show the date checked.
14. [Inferred] User questions can carry false premises (for example "season 3 of X", a sequel that does not exist, or an anime adaptation that was never announced). The session should check the premise against the API before answering, and should say when something does not exist instead of inventing it.
15. [Inferred] Prompt design: keep factual answers short, put the newest retrieved records last, and prefer more evidence records over more examples.

## Limitations or bias
- 2023 models (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, PaLM, Flan-T5). Current models, many with built-in search, would score differently. The pattern (no live data means stale answers) is what transfers.
- General-domain questions, not media-specific. Only an incidental Emmy example relates to TV, so the applicability to new releases and ongoing series is inferred.
- Search-augmented results depend on Google SERP features at one date (26 April 2023).
- The authors are mostly from Google, and the method uses Google Search.
