## Learned points
1. [Documented] Purpose: IMDb carries MPA and international certifications. The Parents Guide adds information "that cannot be fully conveyed by certificates," drawn from "the collective insights of the global IMDb community." It is user-generated, not an official rating.
2. [Documented] Five categories. The data dictionary spells them "Sex & Nudity", "Violence & Gore", "Profanity", "Alcohol, Drugs & Smoking", "Frightening & Intense Scenes"; the help page spells them "Sex and Nudity", "Violence and Gore", "Profanity", "Alcohol/Drugs/Smoking", "Frightening/Intense Scenes".
3. [Documented] Schema per category: `category`; `severity` (one of "None", "Mild", "Moderate", "Severe"); `votes` {`noneVotes`, `mildVotes`, `moderateVotes`, `severeVotes`}; and `parentsGuideItems` [{`text`, `isSpoiler`}].
4. [Documented] Severity is set by vote. The data dictionary says "The severity level for a particular category is determined by the median (middle) of all user's votes," and gives a worked example (5 None, 10 Mild, 5 Moderate, 0 Severe → Mild).
5. [Documented] The two official pages conflict on the method. The help page says "Parents guide severity ratings are an average calculation across all votes."
6. [Inferred] The dictionary's own sample JSON doesn't fit a plain lower median. Violence & Gore with votes 6/6/5/7 (24 votes) is labeled "Moderate", but the 12th vote falls in Mild, and the 13th, or a mean of about 1.54 on a 0–3 scale, would give Moderate. A consumer should therefore recompute or display the raw vote counts rather than trust the label at the boundaries.
7. [Documented] Free vs paid: `parentsGuideItems` (the scene descriptions) "is available in the advanced version of Parents Guide dataset" and requires an upgrade through imdb-licensing-support@imdb.com. The basic dataset has category, severity and votes only.
8. [Documented] Delivery: "Our full range of bulk data products are delivered through Amazon Data Exchange and updated daily." This is a licensed product; free samples are available from imdb-licensing@imdb.com. Nothing read says it is a key-free public API.
9. [Documented] Who writes it: registered contributors submit one item per submission. Guides must be in English. Profanity goes only in the Profanity section, and contributors may not count profanity uses or add manual scores such as "7/10". Spoilers must be flagged. Items may be duplicated across categories. Editors have discretion over what is kept.
10. [Documented] For TV series, contributors should "only provide series or season details. Episode specific items should be added to the episode." Series and episode guides are therefore separate records.
11. [Documented] Unreleased titles may get items based on trailers or earlier films, but only if the source is stated (e.g., "(based on the trailer)").
12. [Documented] Parents Guide data is summarized on the IMDb title page. Those summaries "cannot be directly updated"; inaccurate ones are fixed by reporting the underlying item. Vandalism is handled by fixing it or reporting it through Contact Us.
13. [Inferred] For the recommender: use IMDb Parents Guide severity as a crowd signal and show it with its vote count, since few votes means unreliable. Label it as community-sourced, separate from the official MPA or TV rating. Getting it programmatically requires a license, and the free IMDb non-commercial datasets are not mentioned on these pages.

## Limitations or bias
- The data is crowd-sourced and editor-moderated. Coverage and vote counts vary by title, and the severity method is stated inconsistently.
- No pricing, rate limits or license terms were read; the overview only points to Amazon Data Exchange and a licensing email.
- Whether the Parents Guide website pages are free to view, and the scraping terms, were not checked here.
