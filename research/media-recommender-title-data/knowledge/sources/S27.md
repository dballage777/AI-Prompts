## Learned points
1. [Reported] Method as described: 2,600 popular movie and TV titles across 13 countries (Australia, Brazil, Canada, France, Germany, Japan, Mexico, Netherlands, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, UK, US). A "leading" LLM (not named) was asked to answer from training data alone. Its answers were compared with answers grounded in Gracenote video data via an MCP server.
2. [Reported] Fields checked: title, description, actors, genres, release year, and runtime "where applicable". These were chosen as objective, independently verifiable attributes.
3. [Reported] Headline: the ungrounded LLM "hallucinated all measured metadata" for 506 of 2,600 titles (19.5%, described as "nearly one in five"; the insight post rounds this to 20%).
4. [Reported] Cast: for the top 100 US movies, only 53% of the ungrounded LLM's primary-actor answers matched the grounded data.
5. [Reported] Look-alike titles: the model returned the right title and year for the 2025 thriller "Heel" but took the description, cast, and genre from "Heels" (Starz drama, 2021–2023). It also conflated the 2024 horror-thriller "Trucker" with a 2008 film of the same name. Same-name and near-name collisions are a main failure mode.
6. [Reported] Recency: the model could not give information on new titles. Examples named are "GOAT" (2026 film, about $200M global box office before arriving on Netflix), "Mercy", "Send Help", "Solo Mio", and "It Was Just an Accident". The insight post says ungrounded LLMs struggle with films "premiering over the past two years" regardless of box office or press coverage.
7. [Reported] Quality scoring (insight post): each title's full result got a zero, low, medium, or high quality score, combining attribute matching against grounding data with factual accuracy. Results that were zero, low, or medium quality made up 77%–91% (apparently the range across countries). Less than one-third were high quality, and under 10% in the Netherlands and Mexico.
8. [Reported] Episode study (July 2026 post): 1,300 episodes (100 popular episodes in each of 13 countries) were queried by series plus season and episode number. The ungrounded LLM gave no information (title, description, actors, genre) for 604 episodes (46.5%). Sweden had 73 of 100 empty and the US 31 of 100.
9. [Reported] For the top 100 US episodes, about 27% of program descriptions were accurate on average, compared with about 69% for descriptions of the top 100 TV shows and 100 movies. Answers often described a different episode or merged several. Example: Stranger Things S02E06 combined details from several episodes.
10. [Reported] Gracenote's explanation: episode metadata is more unique than show metadata, and "semantic guessing, predictive probability and a lack of indexing" cause the errors. Viewers rarely search by episode number. They ask contextual questions ("Christmas episodes of The Office", "Friends episodes with Bruce Willis").
11. [Reported] Context claims: TV episodes are 85% of the content distributed by six global SVOD providers tracked in Gracenote Data Hub, and the number of available TV episodes grew more than 21% in about 18 months. The comedy "Rooster" (Steve Carell) is cited as too new for a 2025-released LLM.
12. [Reported] Remedy pitched: grounding through Gracenote data licensing or its "Video MCP Server", which connects to Gracenote's entertainment knowledge graph (50M+ titles, 70+ languages, 80+ countries claimed). The insight post also warns that an ungrounded assistant may recommend a film "your service doesn't even have the rights to distribute".
13. [Inferred] For the recommender: verify description, cast, genre, year, and runtime from an API, not memory, and treat same-name titles as a top risk. Always key on ID plus year plus medium, and compare cast or overview against the API record. For TV, verify episode-level facts (season/episode numbering, guest stars, holiday episodes) from an episode-level source (TMDB or TVmaze episode lists) and never from memory. Anything released within about 2 years of the model's training cutoff must be looked up.

## Limitations or bias
- Vendor study with a direct commercial interest: Gracenote sells the grounding data and the MCP server.
- The model, its version, the prompts, the scoring rubric, and the matching rules are not given on these pages. "Hallucinated all measured metadata" is not defined (for example whether empty answers count).
- Ground truth is Gracenote's own data. Disagreements are scored as model errors even when the data might differ legitimately (regional titles, runtime versions).
- The model was told to answer from training data only, so this reflects a deliberately ungrounded setup, not a model with web search.
- Not peer-reviewed. The full report was not read, so all numbers are as reported in marketing copy.
