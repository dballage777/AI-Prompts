## Learned points
1. [Documented] The five current MPA ratings are G, PG, PG-13, R and NC-17. The site's title search also lists obsolete ratings X, GP, M and M/PG, and you can search by title, rating, year rated and certificate number (filmratings.com search form).
2. [Documented] Official meanings from Rating Rules §3.C: G "General Audiences. All Ages Admitted."; PG "Parental Guidance Suggested. Some Material May Not Be Suitable For Children."; PG-13 "Parents Strongly Cautioned. Some Material May Be Inappropriate For Children Under 13."; R "Restricted. Children Under 17 Require Accompanying Parent or Adult Guardian."; NC-17 "No One 17 and Under Admitted." The motionpictures.org page gives shorter consumer wording (e.g., G "Nothing that would offend parents for viewing by children"; NC-17 "Clearly adult. Children are not admitted.").
3. [Documented] Hard content thresholds in the Rules: under PG "There is no drug use content"; "Any drug use will initially require at least a PG-13"; "More than brief nudity will require at least a PG-13"; one use of a harsher sexually derived word as an expletive requires at least PG-13, and more than one (or one used sexually) requires R, unless the raters vote by a two-thirds majority to keep it PG-13 because of context or inconspicuous use.
4. [Documented] Theme alone cannot push a film above PG-13: "No film can be rated above PG-13 for theme or subject matter alone. It is the depiction of a theme that determines whether a rating of R or NC-17 is warranted." (Ratings Guide, Theme section; also in Rules §3.C(3)).
5. [Documented] The G rating "is not a 'certificate of approval,' nor does it signify a 'children's' motion picture" (Rules). NC-17 "does not mean 'obscene' or 'pornographic'" (Rules).
6. [Documented] Every rated film has three parts: the letter rating, a rating descriptor ("unique to each film," explaining what led to the rating), and the rating definition. Descriptors have been part of every PG, PG-13, R and NC-17 rating since 1990. G carries no descriptor (FAQ, see S9).
7. [Documented] The descriptor categories in the guide are Violence, Action, Language, Sex, Nudity, Drugs, Smoking, Theme and Other. Each has a "by rating" ladder plus named qualifiers, and each qualifier is tagged with the ratings it usually appears at, e.g., "Bloody Images [Generally R and PG-13, occasionally PG]", "Gore ... [R]", "Peril [Usually reserved for PG ...]", "Terror [PG-13, R]".
8. [Documented] Frequency qualifiers have defined meanings. For Language: "Brief" means 1–3 instances; "Some" means infrequent but more than brief; "Throughout" means frequent (usually R); "Pervasive" means constant (R). For PG-13, "Brief strong language" usually means one F-word, and "Some strong language"/"strong language" may mean more than one. "Mild" before an Action descriptor indicates "the lower end of PG, borderline G."
9. [Documented] The theme ladder runs Thematic Elements < Thematic Material < Thematic Content, with "Mature Thematic" for subjects suited to older audiences that still don't reach R. The listed theme examples include bullying, coming-of-age, death/grief, illness, divorce, domestic abuse, historical atrocities, infidelity, mental health, racism, religion, reproductive issues and suicide.
10. [Documented] Suicide has its own rating ladder: PG = brief mention, no visual; PG-13 = attempt or heavily implied suicide with non-graphic aftermath; R = visuals of the act or graphic aftermath.
11. [Documented] Smoking is a ratings factor. "Teen smoking" is tagged [PG-13, R], and very frequent smoking by characters too young to smoke legally is described at the NC-17 tier of the ladder.
12. [Documented] Descriptor sentences follow the pattern "Rated PG-13 for some war violence/bloody images, thematic elements and smoking" (examples from the @FilmRatings feed on the page). The guide says it "does not replace the Ratings Rules for Filmmakers."
13. [Documented] CARA ratings are assigned by a board of current parents, led by a Chair (Kelly McMahon, Chair since May 6, 2019) and senior raters. The Advertising Administration reviews all marketing for rated films (who-we-are page). The system was established in 1968 (motionpictures.org).
14. [Documented] Ratings are US-only and are not judgments of quality (FAQ wording quoted in S9; the Rules say the Board reflects "the opinion of most American parents").
15. [Inferred] For the recommender, store the MPA rating together with its full descriptor string. The qualifiers (brief/some/strong/throughout/pervasive; bloody/graphic/horror/disturbing) carry more filtering signal than the letter alone. Also treat a descriptor as specific to one cut (see S9 on re-rated versions).
16. [Inferred] No page read gives a public API or bulk download. The only lookup shown is the website search form, so verification would mean scraping the search page or getting the data secondhand. Check the site terms before scraping (not read here).

## Limitations or bias
- This is the rating body describing its own system. It presents the system as "the gold standard of voluntary industry self-regulation."
- The descriptor guide uses "generally" and "usually" throughout. Its rating tags show typical placement, not binding rules; only the Rules PDF is binding.
- The motionpictures.org short definitions differ from the Rules wording (e.g., R there reads "Parents are urged to learn more..." and leaves out the under-17 accompaniment wording). Treat the Rules as canonical.
- The "by rating" ladders on the guide page are not labeled with rating letters in the text extract (the labels are presumably icons). My mapping of the 4–5 steps to G→NC-17 is inferred from the order and content.
