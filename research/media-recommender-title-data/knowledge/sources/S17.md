## Learned points
1. [Research] Benchmark: 18,171 template-generated QA pairs (423 templates) across four domains. Movie (from an IMDb non-commercial dataset snapshot of 21 May 2023) has 13 templates and 3,093 questions; Book (Goodreads 2017) 4/3,000; Academics (MAG + DBLP) 13/2,946; Open (DBpedia, Dec 2022) 393/9,132. About 1K questions per head, torso, and tail bucket in each specific domain.
2. [Research] What "head/torso/tail" means: entities are sorted by popularity, and the cumulative popularity is cut into thirds. Head is the entities that make up the top 1/3 of total popularity, torso the middle 1/3, and tail the bottom 1/3. For IMDb, popularity is traffic, measured as `numVotes` (vote count, not rating value). For a person, it is the total votes of the titles they are "known for".
3. [Research] The split follows a power law. Only 767 IMDb titles (0.01%) are head and 4,113 (0.05%) are torso, so 7,536,482 titles (99.94%) are tail. IMDb persons split 34,903 (0.48%) head / 87,645 (1.21%) torso / 7,111,496 (98.31%) tail. "Tail" here therefore covers almost the whole catalog, not only obscure items.
4. [Research] Recency was filtered out on purpose. Only IMDb entities up to 2020 were kept, so the benchmark avoids questions that need knowledge from after the models' training cutoff. The tail results therefore measure obscurity, not newness.
5. [Research] Question design: each question asks one attribute of an entity. The authors removed attributes that are vague, dynamic, source-specific (for example IMDb `averageRating`), or non-text. Disambiguators such as the year were added to names where needed (for example "Tj Singh (known for John Carter (2012))").
6. [Research] Metrics: accuracy A, hallucination H (a wrong or partly wrong answer), and missing M (the model says "unsure" or gives no answer), with A+H+M = 100%. ChatGPT judged correctness, and this agreed with human checks on 98% of 840 sampled answers. Rule-based EM, F1, and ROUGE-L correlated strongly with the LLM judge.
7. [Research] Movie domain, GPT-4: head A 59.3% / H 14.8%, torso A 55.0% / H 16.9%, tail A 10.9% / H 14.7%. Llama 2-70B on Movie: head 39.2 / 28.2, torso 33.9 / 29.8, tail 10.7 / 44.9. Accuracy collapses at the tail, and for the weaker model hallucination rises there.
8. [Research] Overall, GPT-4 reached A 30.9% and H 19.7%. In the Movie domain it reached A 41.7% and H 15.5%, the best domain for every model, while Academics was the worst. LLaMA-33B hallucinated about 80% overall, so non-RLHF models guess instead of saying "unsure".
9. [Research] Even the most popular titles fall short. On the top-10% head questions in the Movie domain, GPT-4 got A 63.5%, H 13.5%, M 22.9%. When told to give a best guess instead of "unsure", it got A 68.8% and H 16.7%. The paper's summary: even for the top 0.5% popular Movie entities, LLMs answer only about 60% correctly.
10. [Research] Prompting matters. Explicitly allowing "unsure" "could significantly reduce hallucination rate". In-domain few-shot examples raised accuracy (+8.9/+7.9/+5.9 pts head/torso/tail) but also raised hallucination (+7.5/+8.2/+9.7 pts). Rewording the questions in cloze style barely changed results in the Movie domain.
11. [Research] Larger models are not reliably more factual: LLaMA-33B was slightly better than LLaMA-65B. Instruction-tuned variants (Vicuna, Falcon-Instruct) said "unsure" more often (Vicuna-13B had 26.9 pts more M than LLaMA-13B) but still hallucinated a lot.
12. [Research] The authors conclude that torso-to-tail and recent knowledge "might be best represented as triples", meaning an external knowledge graph or database that is retrieved when needed, instead of facts recalled from the model.
13. [Inferred] For the recommender: never trust a model's memory for attributes of the 99.9% of IMDb titles outside the head bucket (cast, crew, year, genre). Even for blockbusters, roughly 1 in 3 to 1 in 7 attribute answers are wrong or missing. Verify every attribute against an API (TMDB, Wikidata, IMDb datasets). Prompt the session to say "unsure" instead of guessing, and use the title + year pattern for lookups.
14. [Inferred] Vote count (`numVotes` in IMDb's title.ratings dataset) works as a proxy for how likely the model is to "know" a title. A session could flag low-vote titles as high-risk for memorized facts.

## Limitations or bias
- Models tested are from 2023 (GPT-4-0613, gpt-3.5-turbo-0301, Llama 2, and others). Absolute numbers for current models will differ, though the head-to-tail pattern is the paper's main claim.
- Simple single-attribute questions with an "unsure" escape. Real recommender prompts (free-form lists) give no such escape and may hallucinate more.
- Post-2020 entities were excluded, so the paper says nothing about new releases (see S29 and S27 for that).
- IMDb "titles" include shorts, episodes, and other types. The bucket counts are over all title types, not only feature films.
- Authors are from Meta, and the benchmark is in English only.

## Best used for
- Evidence that model memory of film and TV facts falls sharply with popularity, and a concrete definition of head, torso, and tail based on IMDb `numVotes`.
- Justifying "verify every attribute" and "allow unsure" rules in the recommender prompt.
