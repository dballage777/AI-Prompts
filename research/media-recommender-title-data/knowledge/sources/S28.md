## Learned points
1. [Reported] Shōnen is "an editorial category of Japanese comics targeting an audience of mainly adolescent boys". With shōjo, seinen and josei it is "one of the primary demographic categories of manga and, by extension, of Japanese anime". It is "traditionally published in dedicated manga magazines". (The lead paragraph is uncited. The body says the segmentation "is now openly used as a categorization system by manga publishers[2][3] and extends into works that are adapted from manga, such as anime.[4]")
2. [Reported] The shōnen target age is given as either 10–19 [5] or 12–21 [6]. Shōnen is the most popular of the four categories in Japan [7][8].
3. [Reported] Readership goes well beyond the target. The actual readership of shōnen, "as is the case for all demographic categories of manga,[8][9]", "extends significantly beyond this adolescent male target group to include all ages and genders.[10]". A 2006 survey of female manga readers found Weekly Shōnen Jump was their most popular magazine, ahead of magazines aimed at women [3]. A demographic label describes the magazine's marketing target, not who will enjoy the work.
4. [Reported] Weekly Shōnen Jump (first published 1968) is described as "the best-selling manga magazine across demographic categories, a position it holds to this day". Shōnen Sunday and Weekly Shōnen Magazine (1959) were the first weekly shōnen magazines [21][8].
5. [Reported] Shōnen's editorial focus is mainly action, adventure and fighting clearly defined evil. The lead (uncited) also notes "deep editorial diversity", including comedy, crime, romance, slice of life and sports. So shōnen is not a genre.
6. [Reported] Seinen is "an editorial category of Japanese comics marketed toward young adult men". The term describes "the target audience of magazines aimed at young adult men". The Publishing Science Research Institute (tracking since 1979) separates seinen ("youth") magazines from "mature" (narunen) magazines for explicit material. The label is set by "sociopolitical considerations" rather than reader age: mainstream adult titles from major publishers like Shueisha or Kodansha are called seinen [1]. The target is men aged 18–30 [2][3] "or up to 40" (uncited).
7. [Reported] Shōjo: "Strictly speaking, shōjo manga does not refer to a specific style or a genre, but rather indicates a target demographic.[6]" It is typically defined as manga marketed to adolescent girls and young women [7], "though shōjo manga is also read by men[8] and older women.[9]". The market is segmented by gender (shōjo/shōnen) and age (josei/seinen).
8. [Reported] Josei (also "ladies' comics"/redikomi) is manga marketed to adult women. The article says "In practice, the distinction between shōjo and josei is often tenuous". A third category, "young ladies" (ヤングレディース, Young You 1987, Young Rose 1990, Feel Young 1991 [6]), sits in between. The term "josei manga" was coined by critics in the late 1990s and is "not commonly used among general Japanese audiences [6]" but is the usual Western term [7]. Josei has been "regularly adapted into anime since the 2000s" (lead, uncited).
9. [Reported] Kodomo: the "Kodomo_manga" title redirects to "Children's anime and manga". The definition ("directed towards pre-adolescent children") carries a "[better source needed]" tag. It names boys' children's magazines (CoroCoro Comic, Saikyo Jump, Comic BomBom) and a girls' one (Pucchigumi), and notes that the Shogakukan and Kodansha Manga Awards have children's categories [5][6].
10. [Reported] Iyashikei (癒し系, "healing") is "a sub-genre of slice of life" meant to have a healing effect, with cited sources [1][2][3]. It emerged as a distinct subgenre in 1995, after the Great Hanshin earthquake and the sarin attack (Roquet 2009, Journal of Japanese Studies [5]). The example list, each with citations, includes Aria, Mushishi, Laid-Back Camp, Non Non Biyori, Flying Witch, Natsume's Book of Friends, Yokohama Kaidashi Kikou and My Neighbor Totoro. This matches MAL's choice to define Iyashikei as a theme under Slice of Life (S18).
11. [Reported] Isekai is "a subgenre of Japanese fantasy and science fiction" about people transported to another world, through death and reincarnation or summoning (lead, uncited). In a "reverse isekai" a character comes to Earth. The first modern isekai is credited as Warrior from Another World (1979) [2][4] and the first isekai anime as Aura Battler Dunbine (1983) [2]. Web-novel sites such as Shōsetsuka ni Narō drove the 2000s growth [5][6]. Sword Art Online is "considered" a modern pioneer, though "its creator disputes this" [6][7]. "Isekai" entered the Oxford English Dictionary in March 2024 [11].
12. [Inferred] For the recommender: (a) treat demographic as a fact about the source magazine or label (as MAL does, S12 IV.13.1), never as an age rating or a "who will like it" filter; (b) do not exclude shōjo or josei titles from a male user, or shōnen from anyone, on demographic alone; (c) when demographic is missing (original anime), say "no demographic", not a guess; (d) the shōjo/josei and shōnen/seinen borders are fuzzy, so don't use them as hard filters.
