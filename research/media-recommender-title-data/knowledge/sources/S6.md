## Learned points
1. [Documented] There are six age-based categories: TV-Y, TV-Y7, TV-G, TV-PG, TV-14, TV-MA (aboutUs history, Aug 1, 1997). There is also TV-Y7-FV for children's programs where "fantasy violence may be more intense or more combative than other programs in this category."
2. [Documented] Definitions from the PDF. TV-Y "All Children": designed for a very young audience "including children from ages 2-6," and "not expected to frighten younger children." TV-Y7 "Directed to Older Children": age 7 and above; may include mild fantasy or comedic violence. TV-G "General Audience": "Most parents would find this program suitable for all ages," though it "does not signify a program designed specifically for children"; little or no violence, no strong language, little or no sexual dialogue or situations. TV-PG "Parental Guidance Suggested": some suggestive dialogue (D), infrequent coarse language (L), some sexual situations (S), or moderate violence (V). TV-14 "Parents Strongly Cautioned": unsuitable for under 14; intensely suggestive dialogue, strong coarse language, intense sexual situations, intense violence. TV-MA "Mature Audiences Only": designed for adults, "may be unsuitable for children under 17"; crude indecent language, explicit sexual activity, graphic violence.
3. [Documented] Content descriptors: D = suggestive dialogue ("usually means talk about sex"), L = coarse or crude language, S = sexual situations, V = violence, FV = fantasy violence ("children's programming only"; exclusively TV-Y7 per the 1997 history). The same letter means more at higher tiers (e.g., V = "moderate" at TV-PG, "intense" at TV-14, "graphic" at TV-MA).
4. [Documented] Ratings are applied episode by episode: "This can result in individual shows within a series carrying different ratings." The FAQ confirms that programs "may carry a different rating based on the content" week to week.
5. [Documented] Ratings are version-specific. Shows and movies may be edited differently by channel or time of day (premium cable, basic cable, broadcast), "which can result in one show having different ratings to reflect the specific version being aired."
6. [Documented] Movies on TV: made-for-TV movies get TV ratings. Theatrical movies edited for broadcast or basic cable get a TV rating after editing. Premium cable (HBO, Showtime) runs them uncut with "the original MPAA movie rating" plus network advisories.
7. [Documented] Exemptions: "news and sports are exempt." Religious and home shopping programs "do not typically carry a rating." Commercials are not rated and cannot be blocked. Promos often show the program's rating.
8. [Documented] Who rates: "Programs are voluntarily rated by broadcast and cable television networks, or program producers." There is no central rating board. For syndicated programs, "The distributor of such programs is usually responsible for rating them."
9. [Documented] Display: an icon appears in the upper-left corner for the first 15 seconds, reappears at the start of the second hour for longer programs, and on many channels shows again after each commercial break.
10. [Documented] Monitoring Board: it seeks "uniformity and consistency" by reviewing complaints and public input. It meets "annually or more often" and runs regular calls among standards-and-practices executives. Members: a chairman (currently Hon. Cory Gardner, President and CEO of NCTA), 18 industry representatives appointed by NAB, NCTA and the MPA, and five public-interest members appointed by the chairman.
11. [Documented] History: the system came from the Telecommunications Act of 1996. It was announced Dec 19, 1996, revised with content descriptors July 10, 1997, and found acceptable by the FCC on March 12, 1998, which also adopted the V-Chip technical rules.
12. [Documented] Streaming: a Streaming Task Force was set up in 2020. Its "Ratings Best Practices Guidance for Streaming Services" came out Sept 16, 2021 and was updated Oct 21, 2024 to promote "broader adoption and standardized content labels." The Spot Check Review Program began in January 2020.
13. [Documented] V-Chip blocking is cumulative: blocking TV-14 also blocks TV-MA. It can also block TV-14-V specifically and uncut MPAA-rated movies.
14. [Reported] Board-commissioned surveys report that parents rely on the system: 2024 findings are 83% use it and view it favorably, and 91% are aware of it and understand it. These are self-commissioned numbers.
15. [Inferred] For the recommender: a series-level TV rating (as aggregators such as TMDB or TVmaze may report it) is at best a typical or maximum value, because the official unit is the episode. Treat it as approximate, check the mature episodes, and expect a streaming version's rating to differ from a broadcast edit of the same show.

## Limitations or bias
- This is the industry's own monitoring body, so the survey results are self-reported.
- Networks self-apply ratings, and there is no published per-title database to verify a rating against.
- In the PDF the codes (TV-Y ... TV-MA) are icons. I mapped the text headings to codes using order and age wording (2-6, 7, all ages, younger children, under 14, under 17), which matches the six-category list on the aboutUs page.
- The streaming best-practices document itself was not opened, so its contents are not covered.
