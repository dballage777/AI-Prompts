# S13 — PetMD: Can Puppies Eat Adult Dog Food?
- URL: https://www.petmd.com/dog/nutrition/can-puppies-eat-adult-dog-food | Final URL: same (HTTP 200)
- Access: OPENED (full article text read) | Date read: 2026-11-08 | Published/updated: Published Jan. 19, 2023 (no update date shown)
- Source type & bias: Reported (consumer pet-health site, vet-written). By Jamie Lovejoy, DVM (Tufts graduate, 2012), who is not identified as a board-certified nutritionist. PetMD is operated by Chewy, a pet food retailer, and the page notes it "may contain Chewy links."

## Findings
1. To avoid serious problems "such as fractured bones, heart disease, or bone marrow issues," it is "extremely important that puppies are fed diets formulated specifically for them." [Q4][Q2] [Reported]
2. Puppies need a higher percentage of calories from protein than adults. The protein-to-energy ratio matters even when the total protein matches an adult food. [Q4] [Reported]
3. Puppy foods should contain more fat than adult foods, "but not so high as to cause obesity or accelerated growth rates." [Q4] [Reported]
4. After weaning, puppies need omega-3s such as DHA from the diet, usually from fish or fish oil, for brain, vision and skin development. [Q4] [Reported]
5. Puppies need more calcium than adult dogs. Large-breed puppies (over 50 lb as adults) can be very sensitive to excess calcium or a wrong calcium-to-phosphorus ratio. [Q4][Q2] [Reported]
6. Vitamin amounts are often similar in puppy and adult foods. A healthy puppy on a balanced diet should not need vitamin supplements. [Q4][Q5] [Reported]
7. Puppy food is generally more calorie-dense than adult food, but there is a limit. Monitor body condition and growth rate with the vet to avoid obesity and skeletal deformities. [Q4] [Reported]
8. AAFCO profiles come in two categories: adult maintenance (usually over 1 year) and growth and reproduction. "All life stages" means the food meets both. Claims are supported by lab analysis or by a feeding trial, and feeding-trial diets are "always preferred." [Q1][Q6] [Reported]
9. The AAFCO program is described as voluntary: "Not all diets are formulated to these standards." Check the label's nutritional adequacy statement for the puppy standard. [Q1][Q6] [Reported]
10. The article gives example label statements, such as "for growth, except for growth of large-size dogs (70 pounds or more as an adult)" and "for all life stages, including growth of large-size dogs." [Q6] [Reported]
11. All-life-stages diets "are fine to feed small-breed or medium-breed puppies." They may be too high in calories for many adults and may not suit large-breed puppies. [Q4][Q6] [Reported]

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- For a probably medium-sized mixed-breed puppy, an all-life-stages food is described as acceptable. An adult-maintenance-only food is not, in this article's framing.
- An "all life stages" food bought for both dogs might be too calorie-dense for the 9-year-old who needs to lose weight. That trade-off is relevant if the owner wants a single bag.

## Limitations
- The title asks "can puppies eat adult food," but the article never gives a direct yes or no. It answers implicitly through the AAFCO categories. It is consumer-level with no citations, written by a non-specialist author, and published by a Chewy-operated site.
