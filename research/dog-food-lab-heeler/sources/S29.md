# S29 — FDA: Update on Investigation into Potential Connection Between Certain Diets and Cases of Canine Heart Disease (DCM)

## Findings
1. FDA first alerted the public in July 2018. This February 2019 update covers reports received through November 30, 2018. [Q3] [Expert body]
2. Between January 1, 2014, and November 30, 2018, FDA received 300 DCM reports (294 canine, 6 feline). Of these, 276 came after the July 2018 public notice. [Q3] [Expert body]
3. Reports covered a wide range of breeds, many without a known genetic predisposition. Genetic DCM is typically seen in large and giant breeds, plus Cocker Spaniels. [Q3][Q4] [Expert body]
4. Among dogs that ate a single primary diet, 90 percent ate a grain-free food and about 10 percent ate a grain-containing food, some of them vegan or vegetarian. [Q3] [Expert body]
5. A large share of the diets, grain-free and grain-containing alike, listed peas and/or lentils within the first 10 ingredients. The products included "commercially available kibble, canned and raw foods, as well as home-cooked diets." [Q3][Q5] [Expert body]
6. FDA advice at the time: no specific diet-change recommendation for dogs without DCM symptoms; owners should consult their veterinarians. [Q3][Q8] [Expert body]
7. Investigation methods included statistical case analysis, Vet-LIRN lab testing, cardiology case collaboration, consultation with board-certified veterinary nutritionists, and review of manufacturer ingredient sourcing and formulation. [Q3] [Expert body]
8. Linked page, update of June 27, 2019: 524 DCM reports (515 canine, 9 feline) from January 1, 2014, to April 30, 2019. Dogs: 560 animals affected, 119 deaths. Ingredients of concern were described as peas, lentils, other legume seeds (pulses), and/or potatoes as main ingredients. [Q3] [Expert body]
9. Linked page, notice of December 23, 2022: "FDA does not intend to release further public updates until there is meaningful new scientific information to share." Adverse-event numbers "do not supply sufficient data to establish a causal relationship." [Q3] [Expert body]

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- Home-cooked diets appeared among the DCM case reports, so a home recipe heavy in peas or lentils carries the same concern as a pulse-heavy commercial food. Ingredient choice matters more than the format.
- The Lab is a large-breed-type dog. FDA mentions large breeds only in the context of genetic DCM; a cardiac question for an individual dog belongs with the veterinarian.

## Limitations
- The main page dates from 2019 and is out of date; S30 (Tufts, 2026) is more current. The data are voluntary, unverified case reports with known reporting bias and no denominator, so they cannot establish causation.
