# S26 — Oba, Kelly, Kostiuk, Swanson 2023, J Anim Sci: "Effects of weight loss and feeding specially formulated diets on the body composition, blood metabolite profiles, voluntary physical activity, and fecal metabolites and microbiota of obese dogs"
- URL: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:36879442%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json (PMCID PMC10083730, PMID 36879442) | Final URL: same
- Access: OPENED (abstract only) | Date read: 2026-11-08 | Published/updated: J Anim Sci 2023 (vol. 101); DOI 10.1093/jas/skad073
- Source type & bias: Research (peer-reviewed controlled feeding study). The test diet ("FT") is described as "specially formulated." Sponsor and funding are not stated in the abstract. The diets suggest commercial involvement may exist, but this is unverified.

## Findings
1. 24 obese dogs (BW 15.2 ± 1.7 kg; BCS 8.7 ± 0.4 on a 9-point scale; MCS 3.5 ± 0.3; age 7.2 ± 1.6 yr) were studied for 24 weeks. [Q4] [Research]
2. After a 4-week baseline on a control food to find maintenance intake, dogs were fed the control (OR) or test (FT) diet to lose "1.5% BW/wk." [Q4] [Research]
3. Restricted feeding reduced BW, BCS, fat mass, blood cholesterol, triglycerides, glucose, and leptin. It "increased MCS and lean body mass percentage." [Q3][Q4] [Research]
4. Blood cholesterol reduction was greater on FT than on OR. [Q3] [Research]
5. Weight loss and diet changed fecal metabolites and microbiota. Weight loss increased alpha-diversity (weeks 16, 20, and 24 above weeks 0 and 4). [Q3] [Research]
6. Authors' summary: restricted feeding "promoted safe weight and fat loss" in obese dogs. [Q3][Q4] [Research]
7. Weight loss was done on controlled diets with measured intake and close monitoring (DEXA scans, blood work). It was not an owner reducing a regular adult food. [Q4][Q5] [Inferred]

## Exact quotes (URL above)
- "restricted feeding promoted safe weight and fat loss, reduced blood lipid and leptin concentrations, and altered fecal microbiota of obese dogs."
- "fed to lose 1.5% BW/wk."

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- The study dogs were middle-aged to older (mean 7.2 yr). Controlled weight loss in that age group kept muscle condition score and lean-mass percentage, which is reassuring for a 9-year-old. But these were very obese dogs (BCS ~8.7/9) on purpose-made diets, and the 9-year-old's BCS is unknown.
- The 1.5% BW/wk rate was a study protocol, not a recommendation for this dog. A vet should set any rate and target.

## Limitations
- Abstract only. Diet composition, sponsor, adverse events, and per-group results for many measures were not seen.
- Small sample (n=24), research setting, and the dogs' breed is not stated in the abstract.
