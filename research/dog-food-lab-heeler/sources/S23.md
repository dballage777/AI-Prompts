Written for owners, with no citations in the text; it links to other Petfoodology posts. The post is mainly about dogs that are NOT food-motivated, so weight loss is a side topic.

## Findings
1. Food motivation varies by breed and individual: "most Labrador Retrievers living to eat." Labs are described as "always struggling to avoid obesity." [Q4] [Expert body]
2. Food motivation is influenced by genetics, training, early life exposures, diet, single- vs multi-dog household, and other factors. [Q4][Q7] [Expert body]
3. Ideal body condition score is "between 4 and 5" on a 9-point scale. A healthy weight "is often lower than what most owners think it should be." [Q4] [Expert body]
4. Calorie density of dry over-the-counter dog foods ranges "from about 300 calories per cup to over 700 calories per cup." Calorie information is required on labels but is often in tiny font, and new regulations will make it more consistent. [Q6][Q4] [Expert body]
5. Dry foods are about 10% moisture versus 75–80% (or more) for most wet foods. Refrigerated or frozen cooked foods are "often less than 200 calories per cup." [Q6][Q7] [Expert body]
6. "Don't rely only on the label's feeding direction." Accuracy of feeding directions varies across manufacturers. [Q6][Q4] [Expert body]
7. More over-the-counter foods are labeled "intended for intermittent and supplemental feeding only," meaning they do not contain all required nutrients. [Q1][Q6] [Expert body]
8. "Unless formulated by a board certified veterinary nutritionist, home-prepared diets are very likely to be deficient in many nutrients." [Q1][Q5] [Expert body]
9. Talk to a veterinarian if there is new reduced intake, a recent food or batch change, other signs of illness, unintentional weight loss, BCS below 4/9, or muscle loss. [Q2][Q8] [Expert body]
10. To choose a tasty, high-quality food, owners should talk with a veterinarian or a board-certified veterinary nutritionist and use "objective criteria, not just on marketing." [Q6] [Expert body]
11. Keeping dogs trim throughout life helps keep them healthier and living longer. The post links an earlier Petfoodology obesity post ("Research has shown"); that post was not followed. [Q3][Q4] [Expert body]

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- The 9-year-old is a Lab cross and likely food-motivated. Weight control may take deliberate portioning rather than relying on appetite.
- "~2 cups/day" means little without the food's kcal/cup, given a stated range of roughly 300 to over 700 calories per cup. Check the Nutrish bag's kcal/cup for both recipes, which may differ.
- Any home-prepared meals would need a board-certified veterinary nutritionist to formulate them to avoid deficiencies (relevant to Q5).
