## Findings
1. Comparing price per bag, can or pound is misleading because pets eat different amounts of different foods. The accurate comparison is "cost per kilocalorie." [Q6][Q7] [Expert body]
2. Method: find daily calories (amount fed × calories per cup, can or gram), then the food's price and bag weight, then its kcal/kg (dry) or kcal/can (wet). These are on the label or the maker's or retailer's website. [Q6][Q7] [Expert body]
3. If you don't know your pet's needs (for example, it is free-fed), the Pet Nutrition Alliance calorie calculator is suggested "to get a ball park number." [Q4] [Expert body]
4. Worked dog example: a 10-year-old neutered male Lab needing 1000 kcal per day, on a dry food at $45.60 per 35 lb (15.9 kg) bag with 3665 kcal/kg, eats 0.273 kg (273 g) per day at "$0.78 each day," about "$23 a month or $282 a year." [Q7] [Expert body]
5. Worked cat example: 250 kcal/day of canned food at $1.25 per 160-kcal can costs $1.96/day, about $59/month and $704/year. This is more than the large dog, because canned food costs much more per calorie than dry. [Q7] [Expert body]
6. The calculator asks for "calorie content of your food in kcal/kg," which the page says "will be on the label, near the feeding instructions." [Q6] [Expert body]
7. If two foods are otherwise similar, the post advises choosing the better value. [Q7] [Expert body]

## Exact quotes
- "To provide an accurate estimate of feeding costs, it is critical to figure out the cost per kilocalorie, not the cost per pound or can." (https://sites.tufts.edu/petfoodology/2019/06/10/pet-food-cost-calculator/)
- "That gives us $0.78 each day to feed him. His overall feeding costs are about $23 a month or $282 a year." (same URL)

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- To compare Nutrish with any replacement food or a homemade plan, the owner should use cost per kcal: price, bag weight, and the kcal/kg and kcal/cup on each label. Comparing bag prices is not enough.
- The worked senior-Lab example (1000 kcal/day; about $0.78/day in 2019 prices) is a rough point of comparison only. Dog 1's actual calorie target for weight loss should come from his veterinarian, not from this example.

## Limitations
- Prices are from 2019 and are not current.
- Covers commercial dry and wet food only. It does not cost out home-cooked diets (S8 separately says these are "often more expensive").
- The interactive calculator was not run. Only its input fields were read.
