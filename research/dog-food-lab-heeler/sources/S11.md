# S11 — Tufts Petfoodology: What's the Best Food for your New Puppy?

- Source type & bias: Expert body. Vet school blog (Cummings School of Veterinary Medicine, Tufts) by Lisa M. Freeman, DVM, PhD, DACVIM (Nutrition). The site's disclosure lists funding or services from pet food companies (Hill's, Nestlé Purina, Royal Canin, Mars and others).

## Findings
1. Puppies need a diet that meets their special nutritional needs "until they are at least 1 year old (18 months for giant-breed dogs)." [Q4] [Expert body]
2. "All life stage" dog foods meet the requirements for both puppies and adults. They may or may not be marketed that way, so a food doesn't have to be labeled "puppy" to suit growth. [Q6][Q4] [Expert body]
3. "Complete and balanced" means the food is designed to meet AAFCO minimums for growing puppies and to stay under AAFCO maximums. You confirm this from the nutritional adequacy statement on the label. [Q1][Q6] [Expert body]
4. For puppies expected to weigh more than 50 lb as adults, the author advises a large-breed puppy food, or one whose statement says "growth/all life stages including growth of large-size dogs." Those foods are lower in calcium and usually in calories. AAFCO uses a 70 lb cutoff; the author prefers the more conservative 50 lb. [Q4][Q6] [Expert body]
5. A food can be shown complete and balanced by feeding trials or by formulation, and the label statement says which. Feeding trials are "ideal," but a formulated diet from a manufacturer that passes Steps 1–3 "should be fine." [Q1][Q6] [Expert body]
6. Step 1: choose a manufacturer that meets the WSAVA Guidelines on Selecting Pet Foods. More than 200 manufacturers sell pet food in the US and Canada. [Q6] [Expert body]
7. Calorie density (calories per cup or can) must appear on every pet food label. Among about 60 puppy foods it ranged "from about 300 calories per cup to more than 500 calories per cup." [Q6][Q4] [Expert body]
8. Start with the feeding directions on the label, then adjust. Directions vary widely between manufacturers. Aim for a body condition score (BCS) of 4–5 on a 9-point scale, especially during growth. [Q4][Q6] [Expert body]
9. Spaying or neutering "reduces your puppy's calorie needs up to 30%," so cut the amount of food at the time of surgery but keep feeding puppy food until 12–18 months. [Q4] [Expert body]
10. Most preference filters (grain-free, high protein and so on) are "more about marketing." The author advises against obsessing over protein percentage. Until diet-associated DCM is understood, some owners avoid peas, lentils, chickpeas, potatoes and sweet potatoes. [Q3][Q6] [Expert body]
11. A food's nutritional value and quality are "not necessarily related to its cost." Some excellent foods are sold in grocery stores. [Q7] [Expert body]
12. Puppies with medical issues that need a therapeutic diet should be fed under the guidance of a Board Certified Veterinary Nutritionist. [Q5] [Expert body]

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- The 6-month-old is eating an ADULT formula. The fix the source points to is checking the Nutrish bag's AAFCO statement: does it say "growth" or "all life stages," or only "adult maintenance"? If it says adult maintenance only, the source's guidance points to a puppy or all-life-stages food until at least 12 months.
- Whether the "large-size dogs" wording matters depends on the puppy's expected adult weight against the 50 lb (author) or 70 lb (AAFCO) cutoff. A Heeler × Beagle mix at 25 lb at 6 months may fall below it, but that is for the vet to judge.
- "2 cups/day" means little without the kcal/cup figure, since calorie density varies by more than 200 kcal/cup between foods.

## Limitations
- Published in 2021, and no specific products are named. Written for a dry-food buyer. Industry funding is disclosed. The page doesn't address an adult-only food fed to a puppy beyond the general rule.
