# S31 — ASPCA: People Foods to Avoid Feeding Your Pets

## Findings
1. Alcohol can cause vomiting, incoordination, breathing difficulty, tremors, blood pH changes, coma, and death. Raw yeast dough can bloat the stomach, which may twist, and it produces alcohol in the stomach. [Q2] [Expert body]
2. Chocolate, coffee, and caffeine contain methylxanthines, which can cause vomiting, abnormal heart rhythm, tremors, seizures, and death. Darker chocolate is worse; baking chocolate and cocoa powder have the highest concentrations. [Q2] [Expert body]
3. Grapes and raisins: "exposure to grapes/raisins can lead to kidney damage." Tartaric acid is the suspected toxin. [Q2][Q5] [Expert body]
4. Onion, garlic, and chives (Allium species) can cause GI irritation and red blood cell damage leading to anemia. Cats are more susceptible, but dogs are at risk depending on the amount eaten. [Q2][Q5] [Expert body]
5. Xylitol (gum, candy, baked goods, toothpaste) can cause hypoglycemia and possibly liver damage. Liver damage can occur within 12–24 hours. [Q2] [Expert body]
6. Macadamia nuts cause weakness, tremors, and hyperthermia in dogs; signs appear within 12 hours, with recovery in 24–72 hours. Other nuts and fatty foods can cause vomiting, diarrhea, and possibly pancreatitis. [Q2][Q7] [Expert body]
7. Raw meat and eggs can carry *Salmonella* and *E. coli*. Raw eggs contain an enzyme that interferes with absorption of certain vitamins. Raw bones can cause GI injury or obstruction that may need surgery. [Q2][Q5] [Expert body]
8. Milk and dairy can cause diarrhea because pets lack significant lactase. Excess salt can cause electrolyte problems, seizures, and death. Citrus causes only minor stomach upset in small amounts. Avocado is mainly a problem for birds, rabbits, horses, and ruminants. [Q2][Q7] [Expert body]
9. If a pet eats any of these, note the amount and contact a veterinarian or ASPCA Poison Control at (888) 426-4435. [Q2] [Expert body]

## Exact quotes
- "Allium species can cause gastrointestinal irritation and red blood cell damage, which can lead to anemia (low red blood cells)." (https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/people-foods-avoid-feeding-your-pets)
- "Dogs/cats might sustain injury or obstruction to the gastrointestinal tract when ingesting bones, which could be serious and require surgery." (same URL)

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- Home meal prep should leave out onion, garlic, chives, grapes and raisins, xylitol-containing ingredients, macadamia nuts, raw dough, and bones. Seasoned human leftovers are a common source of Allium, salt, and fat.
- Fatty table scraps and nuts conflict with the senior dog's weight-loss goal and carry a pancreatitis risk.

## Limitations
- The page is undated, gives no doses or thresholds, and is not exhaustive; it says it covers "common" foods. It also covers species other than dogs. It does not address nutritional completeness.
