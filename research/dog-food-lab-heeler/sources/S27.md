# S27 — FDA: Get the Facts! Raw Pet Food Diets can be Dangerous to You and Your Pet

- Access: OPENED (full page text read) | Date read: 2026-11-08 | Published/updated: "Content current as of: 02/22/2018"
- Source type & bias: Expert body (US regulator, FDA Center for Veterinary Medicine). Consumer-education page summarizing an FDA/Vet-LIRN study (Nemser et al., Foodborne Pathog Dis 2014;11:706-709). Public-health framing; no commercial interest evident.

## Findings
1. FDA CVM screened "over 1,000 samples of pet food" for foodborne-illness bacteria in a two-year study, October 2010 through July 2012; raw food was added only in year 2. [Q2] [Expert body]
2. Of 196 commercial raw dog and cat food samples, 15 were positive for *Salmonella* and 32 for *Listeria monocytogenes*. [Q2] [Expert body]
3. Comparison groups in the same study: dry dog food 120 samples, 0 *Salmonella*, 0 *Listeria*; dry cat food 120 samples, 1 *Salmonella*, 0 *Listeria*; jerky-type treats 190, 0 and 0; semi-moist dog food 120, 0 and 0. Canned food was not tested. [Q2] [Expert body]
4. The page concludes raw pet food "was more likely to be contaminated with disease-causing bacteria" than the other types tested, and CVM is "concerned about the public health risk" for pets and for owners who handle it. [Q2] [Expert body]
5. Handling advice: wash hands with soap and water "for at least 20 seconds" after handling raw pet food; clean then disinfect surfaces, bowls, utensils, and cutting boards. [Q2][Q5] [Expert body]
6. Freeze raw meat until use and thaw in the refrigerator or microwave, "not on your countertop or in your sink"; don't rinse raw meat; keep raw food separate; cover and refrigerate or discard uneaten food immediately. [Q2][Q5][Q7] [Expert body]
7. For home-made cooked food: "cook all food to a proper internal temperature as measured by a food thermometer"; thorough cooking kills *Salmonella*, *L. monocytogenes*, and other foodborne bacteria. (The page gives no target temperatures.) [Q5] [Expert body]
8. Both cooked and raw pet foods get recalled, including for *Salmonella* and *Listeria*; the FDA Recalls & Withdrawals page lists them. [Q3][Q6] [Expert body]
9. The safe-handling instructions apply "No matter what type of pet food you feed your pet." [Q2] [Expert body]

## Exact quotes
- "compared to other types of pet food tested, raw pet food was more likely to be contaminated with disease-causing bacteria." (https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/get-facts-raw-pet-food-diets-can-be-dangerous-you-and-your-pet)
- "If you're using raw ingredients to make your own cooked pet food, be sure to cook all food to a proper internal temperature as measured by a food thermometer." (same URL)

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- If the owner home-cooks, raw-meat handling is a kitchen and household hygiene issue, and that matters more with a growing puppy at home. Cooking to temperature removes most of the bacterial risk that raw feeding carries.
- The current dry kibble was in the lowest-contamination group of this one study. That is not a guarantee: recalls of cooked foods do happen.

## Limitations
- The data are from 2010–2012, and the page was last updated in 2018. Only commercial raw products were tested, not home-made raw or home-cooked food. The study measured contamination, not illness in pets. The page is a consumer summary; the full paper was not read.
