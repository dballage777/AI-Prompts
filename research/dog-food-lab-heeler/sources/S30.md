## Findings
1. Genetic DCM affects certain breeds, such as Doberman Pinschers, Great Danes, and other large-breed dogs. Secondary DCM can be caused by drugs, toxins, or nutritional factors, such as deficiencies of taurine or some B vitamins. [Q2][Q3] [Expert body]
2. In 2018 FDA alerted the public to a possible link between a unique, non-breed-specific secondary DCM and diets that are grain-free or contain pulses (peas, lentils, chickpeas, dry beans). [Q3] [Expert body]
3. Diet-associated DCM affects both predisposed and non-predisposed breeds. Cats seem susceptible but less sensitive than dogs. [Q3] [Expert body]
4. Possible signs are coughing, difficulty breathing, weakness, and fainting. Affected dogs can also show no symptoms and may have no heart murmur. [Q3] [Expert body]
5. The disease "can slowly improve if diet is changed (especially if caught early)," but it remains potentially deadly. [Q3][Q8] [Expert body]
6. It appears to be associated with diets high in pulses, whether grain-free or grain-inclusive. It "does not appear to be caused by a lack of grains or a deficiency of taurine or other nutrients." [Q3][Q6] [Expert body]
7. The specific cause has not been identified. The team found a urine biomarker suggesting phospholipidosis, an abnormal fat metabolism, as one possible mechanism. [Q3] [Research, as summarized]
8. More than 30 scientific studies on diet-associated DCM have now been published, and research continues. [Q3] [Expert body]

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- When judging the current Nutrish recipes or any home recipe, check where pulses appear in the ingredient list. Grain-free or grain-inclusive status alone does not answer the DCM question.
- Dogs can have this disease with no symptoms, so a cardiac concern for either dog is a question for the veterinarian, not something to rule out at home.
