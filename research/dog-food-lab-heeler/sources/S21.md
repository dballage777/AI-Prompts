## Findings (5–15; each: text [Q#] [label])
1. "AAFCO does not regulate, test, approve or certify pet food. It establishes model language that states and other governing bodies may adopt into law." Pet food laws are enforced by FDA plus states. [Q1][Q6] [Expert body]
2. AAFCO and its members "are not practicing veterinarians"; it gives no veterinary advice; sick pets should get veterinary care first. [Q2] [Expert body]
3. Pet-food-caused illness/death events are "Uncommon, but well-publicized"; feeding the wrong product "can have minor to devastating effects." [Q2][Q3] [Expert body]
4. (Reading Labels) The nutritional adequacy statement "is perhaps the most important part of a label." "Complete" = all required nutrients; "Balanced" = correct ratios. Recognized life stages: gestation/lactation, growth, maintenance, all life stages. [Q1][Q6][Q4] [Expert body]
5. (Reading Labels) Three standard formats: "formulated to meet the nutritional levels established by the AAFCO Dog... Food Nutrient Profiles for ___"; "Animal feeding tests using AAFCO procedures substantiate that ___ provides complete and balanced nutrition for ___"; and the rarely used "comparable to a product which has been substantiated using AAFCO feeding tests" ("family products"). [Q1][Q6] [Expert body]
6. (Reading Labels) "This product is intended for intermittent or supplementary feeding only" = not complete and balanced; limited to non-continuous/supplemental use. Treats labeled "snack"/"treat" need no adequacy statement. [Q6][Q7] [Expert body]
7. (Reading Labels) Guarantees for substances not in the nutrient profiles must carry the asterisked disclaimer "Not Recognized as an essential nutrient by the AAFCO Dog..." (matches Nutrish taurine/vitamin C notes, S16–S18). [Q6] [Expert body]
8. (Reading Labels / Calories) Calorie statement must be under heading "Calorie Content", in kcal per kg as fed and per familiar unit (e.g., per cup); guarantor must state which method (calculation or AAFCO testing) was used. Calorie labeling requirements "changed significantly in the year 2014." A "lite" dry dog food "cannot contain more than 3,100 kilocalories per kilogram." [Q6][Q4] [Expert body]
9. (Reading Labels) Feeding directions for complete and balanced foods must give at least amount of food per weight of animal per unit time for its life stage; all-life-stage products "should have different feeding directions for gestation/lactation, growth and maintenance"; directions "are guidelines that may need modification." [Q4][Q6] [Expert body]
10. (Reading Labels) If guarantor is not the actual manufacturer, name must be preceded by "manufactured for" or "distributed by." [Q6] [Expert body]
11. (Selecting) "Calorie-rich foods designed for young animals may make a less active adult animal obese"; "If an animal is gaining or losing weight, it is either getting too much or too little food and its intake should be adjusted accordingly." [Q4] [Expert body]
12. (Selecting) Owners should "select a food labeled for the pet's species, life stage and condition" and follow feeding directions. [Q4][Q6] [Expert body]

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred, 1–3 bullets)
- All three Nutrish statements (S16–S18) use the "formulated to meet" format, i.e., formulation, not AAFCO feeding tests; adult foods are "adult maintenance", puppy food is "All Life Stages" incl. large-size growth.
- Dog 2 (growth) should be on a food labeled for growth/all life stages per AAFCO's "life stage" rule; Dog 1 (less active senior, weight loss) — AAFCO notes calorie-rich young-animal foods can promote obesity in less active adults, so sharing a puppy/all-life-stages food is a trade-off to discuss with a vet.
- "AAFCO" on a bag is not an approval; the statement means the company claims to meet AAFCO model profiles.
