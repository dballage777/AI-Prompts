## Findings
1. Design: paired feeding study of 48 Labrador Retrievers; one dog in each pair was fed 25% less food than its pair-mate "from 8 weeks of age until death." [Q3] [Research]
2. Serum biochemistry, body condition score, and body composition were measured annually until 12 years of age. [Q3] [Research]
3. Food-restricted dogs weighed less and had lower body fat content and lower serum triglycerides, triiodothyronine, insulin, and glucose concentrations than controls. [Q3][Q4] [Research]
4. Median life span (age when 50% of dogs had died) was significantly longer in the food-restricted dogs. The abstract gives no actual ages. [Q3][Q4] [Research]
5. Onset of clinical signs of chronic disease "generally was delayed" in the food-restricted dogs. [Q3][Q4] [Research]
6. Authors' conclusion: 25% restriction in food intake increased median life span and delayed signs of chronic disease "in these dogs" (a limited claim). [Q3] [Research]
7. Restriction was lifelong from puppyhood. The study did not test weight loss that starts in middle or old age. [Q4] [Inferred]

## Exact quotes (URL above)
- "Results suggest that 25% restriction in food intake increased median life span and delayed the onset of signs of chronic disease in these dogs."
- "Median life span was significantly longer for dogs in which food was restricted."

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- This supports the general aim of keeping the 9-year-old Lab mix lean. Labradors were the study breed, and he is part Lab.
- The study did not test weight loss that starts at age 9. For his ~5 lb goal, it is supporting background only and gives no method or target.
- Restriction began at 8 weeks of age, so the finding loosely supports keeping the puppy lean during growth. It gives no guidance on how much to feed.

## Limitations
- Only the abstract was read. Absolute life spans, effect sizes, body condition scores, diet details, and funding were not available.
- Single breed, kennel-housed colony (inferred from the design; not stated in the abstract), n=48.
- 2002 study; restriction was relative to the pair-mate's intake, not to a defined calorie target.
