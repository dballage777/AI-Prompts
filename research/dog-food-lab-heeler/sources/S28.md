## Findings
1. Several new studies show a risk of bacterial, viral, and parasitic contamination in raw pet foods, including freeze-dried and frozen products. [Q2] [Expert body]
2. Cats, both exotic and domestic, have died in the past year from H5N1 influenza traced to raw poultry or raw milk in their diets, including several commercial raw cat foods. The post gives the fatality rate in cats as "greater than 50%". [Q2][Q3] [Expert body]
3. A child's *E. coli* O157:H7 infection was traced to a commercial raw frozen food fed to the family dog. The manufacturer refused to recall it, and FDA later issued an advisory for *Listeria* and *Salmonella* in other products from the same company (Darwin's Natural Pet Products, named in the FDA links). [Q2][Q3] [Expert body, citing FDA advisories]
4. The Cornell study bought 85 commercial raw cat foods (28 refrigerated or frozen, 49 freeze-dried, 8 in kibble form) and 27 cooked cat foods (22 canned, 5 kibble). [Q2] [Research, as summarized]
5. Potentially dangerous bacteria (*Salmonella enterica*, *E. coli*, *Klebsiella*) grew from 42% of the raw foods and from none of the cooked foods. One *Salmonella* strain was identical to a reported human-infection strain. [Q2] [Research, as summarized]
6. The raw foods carried more antibiotic-resistance genes than the cooked foods, and parasite genes were found only in raw foods. [Q2] [Research, as summarized]
7. Almost 18% (15/85) of samples contained animal proteins not listed on the label, most often chicken or turkey. Cooked foods had slightly more mislabeling than raw (9 vs 6). [Q1][Q6] [Research, as summarized]
8. Caveat: the study did not say whether any raw foods used high-pressure processing (HPP), which could reduce positive results. [Q2] [Expert body]
9. All freeze-dried products were confirmed raw by their manufacturers, but only a "small minority" of the 49 said so clearly on the label. The author's advice is to assume freeze-dried meat foods and treats are raw "until proven otherwise." [Q2][Q7] [Expert body]
10. Take-home: "Feeding cooked pet foods will reduce infection risk and reduce the risk of spreading antibiotic resistance genes in both pets and people." [Q2] [Expert body]

## Exact quotes
- "it seems prudent to assume that all freeze-dried meat pet food or treats are raw until proven otherwise" (https://sites.tufts.edu/petfoodology/2025/10/27/raw-pet-food-research-update/)
- "Feeding cooked pet foods will reduce infection risk and reduce the risk of spreading antibiotic resistance genes in both pets and people." (same URL)

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- Any meal prep plan should be cooked, not raw. Freeze-dried toppers and treats count toward the raw-food risk unless the label says cooked.
- The finding that 18% of foods contained unlisted proteins is relevant if either dog is ever put on an elimination diet for allergies.

## Limitations
- The Cornell study tested cat foods, not dog foods. Most data are the blog's summary, not the primary paper. Contamination measures DNA or culture results, not disease rates. The author has disclosed industry ties.
