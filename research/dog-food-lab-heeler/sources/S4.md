# S4 — O'Brien JS, Lawson E, Dog Aging Project Consortium, Tolbert MK, Ruple A. "Findings from the Dog Aging Project: home-prepared diets for companion dogs feature diverse ingredients, and few are nutritionally complete." Am J Vet Res 2025

- Access: OPENED (abstract only; full text not open access) | Date read: 2026-11-08 | Published/updated: first publication 2025-08-27; Am J Vet Res 86(11)
- Source type & bias: Peer-reviewed observational study (Research). Authors include a board-certified veterinary nutritionist (Tolbert, per S5). Completeness assessed with an owner-facing website designed by veterinary nutritionists (named "Balance It" in S5) — a tool-based estimate, not lab analysis.

## Findings
1. Observational study of free-text owner diet descriptions submitted January 1, 2023 – December 31, 2023 within the Dog Aging Project cohort; 2 independent coders described ingredients. [Q1] [Research]
2. 1,726 home-prepared diets analyzed; ingredients included meat and organs, vegetables, fruits, grains, nuts and seeds, and added oils. [Q1] [Research]
3. Most common ingredients: meat (90%) and vegetables (65%). [Q1] [Research]
4. Nearly half (45%) of owners added some commercially prepared diet or base/topper to the home-prepared ingredients — i.e., mixed feeding is common. [Q7] [Research]
5. Only 6% of the diets were "potentially nutritionally complete" against AAFCO adult dog maintenance standards. [Q1] [Research]
6. Completeness was judged by 5 independent coders entering ingredients into a website for pet owners designed by veterinary nutritionists. [Q1][Q5] [Research]
7. Composition "varied substantially," and few are likely nutritionally complete. [Q1] [Research]
8. Clinical recommendation: veterinarians should fully examine reported home-prepared diets and use tools including consultation with board-certified veterinary nutritionists (DACVIM-Nutrition) to help owners select a maintenance diet. [Q5] [Research]
9. The standard used was adult maintenance; the abstract reports no growth/puppy assessment and no health-outcome data. [Q3][Q4] [Research]

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- Real-world owner-made diets (not just published recipes, cf. S1) are rarely complete even for adults; risk is plausibly higher for the growing puppy, whose needs were not the standard used.
- Mixing commercial food with home ingredients is common, but the abstract does not show that mixing makes a diet complete; a topper approach for these dogs would still need attention to balance and calories.

## Limitations
- Abstract only. Free-text owner reports without exact amounts (S5 quotes the author: true share may be below 6%).
- Tool-based completeness estimate, adult standard only; no outcome data. Volunteer cohort (Dog Aging Project) may not represent all owners.
