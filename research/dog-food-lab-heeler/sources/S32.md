# S32 — Fresh-cooked vs extruded kibble in dogs: two industry-funded peer-reviewed studies
Two papers in one file:
- (A) Tanprasertsuk J, Perry LM, Tate DE, Honaker RW, Shmalberg J. "Apparent total tract nutrient digestibility and metabolizable energy estimation in commercial fresh and extruded dry kibble dog foods." *Transl Anim Sci* 2021;5(3):txab071. PMID 34278234 · PMC8279163 · doi:10.1093/tas/txab071
- (B) Melby MC, Timlin CL, McCracken FB, Rojas CA, Carchedi C, White B, Cassibry L, Blake A, Coon CN. "Impacts of a fresh-frozen dog food on fecal consistency and microbiome in colony dogs." *J Anim Sci* 2026;104:skag303. PMID 42768438 · PMC13645448 · doi:10.1093/jas/skag303

- Source type & bias: Research (peer-reviewed), with heavy industry ties in both papers.
  - (A) All authors are affiliated with NomNomNow, Inc. (one also with Univ. of Florida). Conflict statement: authors "are employees of and/or hold stock or stock options in NomNomNow, Inc." The fresh test diets were NomNomNow's own recipes (Chicken Chow, Beef Mash, Pork Potluck, Turkey Fare). The acknowledgment reads that NomNomNow "provided support in the form of salaries for all authors, but did have any role in the preparation of the manuscript" [sic; the wording appears to be missing "not"].
  - (B) "This research and the APC were funded by Ollie Pet Foods." Ollie provided and purchased the diets. Three authors are Ollie employees who "played a role in funding, study design, write-up, and final review of the manuscript, and decision to publish." The other authors are from Four Rivers Kennel (a contract kennel, whose president co-authored), Animal Biome, and the Texas A&M GI Lab (fee-for-service). The test fresh diet was Ollie Chicken Dish with Carrots; the kibble was Blue Buffalo Life Protection Chicken and Brown Rice.

## Findings
1. (A) Preliminary study. Separate cohorts of normal-weight dogs ate one of five diets for 10 days: chicken kibble (n = 12) or chicken (n = 12), beef (n = 6), pork (n = 6), or turkey (n = 6) fresh food. [Q3] [Research]
2. (A) Comparing the chicken fresh diet with the chicken kibble at similar intake, the fresh diet gave lower defecation frequency (1.2 ± 0.2 vs. 1.7 ± 0.5 times/d), lower fecal dry matter (24 ± 8 vs. 47 ± 10 g/d), and lower fecal calories (92 ± 31 vs. 189 ± 43 kcal/d); all adjusted P < 0.001. [Q3][Q7] [Research]
3. (A) Apparent total tract digestibility of dry matter, protein, fat, NFE, and calories was significantly lower for the kibble than for every fresh diet. Measured metabolizable energy (ME) per dry matter was higher in all fresh diets except pork. [Q3] [Research]
4. (A) Calorie estimation: the AAFCO modified Atwater calculation underestimated kibble ME, and NRC 2006 was the most accurate for kibble. For fresh diets, the best method varied by recipe. The authors propose standard Atwater factors for commercial and home-prepared fresh diets, "or preferably for commercial products, by direct measurement in conforming feeding trials." [Q4][Q5][Q6] [Research]
5. (A) Full-text limitation: calorie intake differed among diets, and body-weight change during the kibble period was significantly higher than during the non-chicken fresh diets, which "may confound any observed results." All diets met AAFCO adult-maintenance nutrient standards. [Q3][Q4] [Research]
6. (B) Randomized parallel design: 16 adult Labrador Retrievers (mean age 4.2 ± 0.26 yr; mean weight 25.7 ± 3.18 kg), fresh-frozen vs extruded kibble, for 6 months. [Q3] [Research]
7. (B) Fresh-fed dogs had better fecal consistency (P < 0.01) and higher fecal butyrate (P < 0.01). There were "No differences" in fecal moisture, pH, ammonia, or other short-chain and branched-chain fatty acids. [Q3] [Research]
8. (B) Microbiome composition (beta diversity) differed by diet (P < 0.01), with fresh-fed dogs enriched in short-chain fatty acid producers. Neither diet harmed general health, judged by weight maintenance and veterinary wellness exams. [Q3] [Research]
9. (B) The fresh diet was "cooked to achieve pathogen lethality (>161°F)," rapidly cooled, and frozen. This was a cooked commercial product, not raw (from the full text). [Q2][Q5] [Research]
10. Across both papers: the outcomes are digestibility, stool, and microbiome markers. Neither study measured long-term disease, lifespan, or clinical outcomes, and neither tested home-prepared food. [Q3] [Inferred]

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- Fresh food can be more digestible and more calorie-dense per gram of dry matter than kibble. Cup-for-cup or calorie estimates don't carry over between formats, so a switch needs re-planned portions, especially for the dog on a weight-loss plan.
- The evidence for fresh food rests on stool and microbiome markers in a few healthy adult dogs, over 10 days to 6 months. It doesn't show long-term health benefits, and no puppies or senior dogs were studied.

## Limitations
- Both studies were funded or run by fresh-food brands. Samples were small (n = 6–12 per diet in A; n = 8 per group in B). (A) used short 10-day periods and separate cohorts. (B) used colony Labradors and one brand comparison. Each paper tested commercial recipes, not home-cooked food. Only the abstracts plus funding, conflict, and diet sections were read; full methods and results tables were not reviewed.
