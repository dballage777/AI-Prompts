## Findings
1. Growing puppies' nutritional requirements differ from those of adult dogs. Imbalances that may do little harm in an adult "can have disastrous results in a growing puppy," including malformed or fractured bones, anemia, poor growth, skin problems, or heart disease. [Q4][Q2] [Expert body]
2. Puppies are sensitive to too much or too little of a nutrient, and to an improper balance between nutrients. [Q2][Q4] [Expert body]
3. Extra calories make puppies grow too quickly even before they become overweight. This raises the risk of bone and joint problems, especially in large breeds. [Q4] [Expert body]
4. Target a BCS of 4–5 out of 9. It is "better to err on the side of" a 4 or even a little lower than to go above 5. [Q4] [Expert body]
5. BCS alone "is not sensitive enough in growing puppies" to confirm the ideal growth rate. The author recommends WALTHAM Puppy Growth Charts, built from data on 50,000 healthy young dogs. Charts are chosen by sex and expected adult weight (10 options, none above 88 lb expected adult weight). [Q4] [Expert body]
6. Weigh the puppy at least monthly until 6 months, then every 2–3 months. Use a calibrated or digital pet or baby scale, not the method of subtracting your own weight on a human scale. If the puppy jumps between growth curves, talk to the vet. [Q4] [Expert body]
7. Treats should make up "no more than 10% of a dog's total daily calories." Count every treat, including rawhides, bully sticks, dental treats and people food. Going above 10% can unbalance the diet. [Q7][Q4] [Expert body]
8. If a puppy is growing too fast, the options are to stop overfeeding, cut treats, or switch to a puppy food with fewer calories per cup. The author also says "PLEASE don't change to an adult food before 12 months." [Q4][Q8] [Expert body]
9. Wording issue: the page says no adult food before 12 months "in small, medium, or large-sized breeds (18 months of age in large breed puppies)." This appears internally inconsistent. S11 by the same author gives 18 months for giant breeds. [Q4] [Expert body; inconsistency noted, Inferred]

## Relevance to these two dogs (Inferred)
- The 6-month-old is on an adult food about 6 months before the earliest point this author says to switch (12 months).
- Regular weigh-ins plotted on a growth chart would show whether the current 25 lb and 2 cups/day are on track. That is a question for the vet, not something to judge at home by eye.
- The 10% treat rule applies to both dogs, and for the 9-year-old it also bears on his weight-loss goal.
