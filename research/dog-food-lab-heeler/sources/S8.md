# S8 — Think Twice: Reasons to Avoid a Home-cooked Diet (Tufts Petfoodology)

- Source type & bias: Expert body. Author Lisa M. Freeman, DVM, PhD, DACVIM (Nutrition), Tufts Cummings School. Names BalanceIT, a commercial recipe service run by a board-certified nutritionist, as a resource.

## Findings
1. Situations to "definitely avoid" a home-cooked diet include young, growing animals: "less than 12 months of age for cats and most dogs; less than 18 months for a giant-breed dog." [Q4] [Expert body]
2. Pregnant or nursing animals should also avoid home-cooked diets. [Q4] [Expert body]
3. Overweight pets on a weight-loss diet should avoid home-cooked diets: "It's very difficult to meet all of a pet's nutritional needs during the sometimes long process of weight loss." [Q4] [Expert body]
4. Home-cooked diets are "often more expensive and always more time-consuming than commercial diets." [Q7] [Expert body]
5. A medical condition does not by itself call for home cooking. Commercial therapeutic diets exist for most conditions and often have research supporting their benefits. [Q1][Q3] [Expert body]
6. Pets may eat plain home cooking (for example, chicken or beef and rice) eagerly but may be "much less enthusiastic" once the diet is made nutritionally balanced. [Q5][Q7] [Expert body]
7. Home-cooking a healthy dog can make it harder to move the dog onto a beneficial therapeutic commercial diet later (for example, for bladder stones or kidney disease). [Q7][Q8] [Expert body]
8. Avoid all home-prepared and commercial diets with raw or insufficiently cooked meat: "these are unsafe for you and your pet!" [Q2] [Expert body]
9. If cooking at home, work with a Board Certified Veterinary Nutritionist® (BalanceIT is named as one resource for healthy dogs) and follow the recipe exactly. Only 13% of owners were still feeding the original recipe "a median of 1 year later" (Tufts study, 2015). [Q5] [Expert body citing Research]
10. Even carefully designed nutritionist recipes "don't completely account for bioavailability issues or potential interactions between nutrients." Owners should tell their veterinarian about the diet and monitor over time. [Q1][Q5] [Expert body]
