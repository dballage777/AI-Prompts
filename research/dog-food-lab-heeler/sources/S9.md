## Findings
1. AAFCO (Association of American Feed Control Officials) sets pet food labeling guidelines but "doesn't approve, test, or certify pet foods." Most states turn the guidelines into law, and states can add their own rules. [Q1][Q6] [Expert body]
2. AAFCO allows personal or commercial endorsements on labels "provided the endorsement is not false or misleading." [Q6] [Expert body quoting regulator guideline]
3. The AAFCO Pet Food Committee says a "veterinarian recommended" claim needs a "statistically valid survey of veterinarians," but it does not define the number of veterinarians or the survey format. [Q6] [Expert body]
4. State control officials, not AAFCO, decide what evidence supports a label claim. The claim "is probably rarely questioned," so how rigorous the evidence is "remains unknown." [Q6] [Expert body]
5. AAFCO guidelines say veterinarians do not approve labels or products, but "veterinarian approved" wording may still appear on labels or websites. [Q6] [Expert body]
6. The recommendation is not to rely on "veterinarian recommended" claims. Use other criteria, ask your veterinarian, and see the Petfoodology "questions you should be asking about your pet's food" post. [Q6] [Expert body]
