# Weekly Meal Prep Plan: Complete Base Food + Fresh Toppers
For: Dog 1 (9-year-old Lab × Heeler, 55 lb, weight-loss goal) and Dog 2 (6-month-old Heeler × Beagle puppy, 25 lb)
Version 1 · 2026-11-08
**This plan is to review with your vet. It is not veterinary advice.** Show it at your next visit and adjust it to the calorie targets your vet sets.

**How this plan works**
- Each dog eats a nutritionally complete bagged food as the base.
- Fresh toppers are capped at 10% of daily calories [S12], so the diet stays balanced.
- Total daily calories stay the same, because the base is reduced to make room for the toppers.

Sources: S-numbers refer to REPORT.md. Topper calorie numbers are approximate general nutrition values, not from the report sources. Check your package labels.

---

## 1. Each dog's daily numbers
Until your vet gives a target, keep each dog's total calories where they are now: about 700 a day (2 cups of the current food at 340–365 calories per cup [S16, S17]).

| | Dog 1 (senior) | Dog 2 (puppy) |
|---|---|---|
| Base food | His current adult food, **one recipe only.** Chicken & Veggies is 340 calories per cup [S16]; switching recipes changes his calories. Ask your vet about a weight-management food [S24]. | **Switch to a growth or all-life-stages food** [S11, S12]. Example: the Nutrish puppy recipe, 390 calories per cup [S18]. |
| Daily calories (for now) | About 680 (don't increase) | Ask your vet. Puppies need enough calories to grow [S11, S12]. |
| Topper budget (10%, including treats) | Up to 65 calories (aim lower: about 40) | Up to 70 calories |
| Base food per day | (680 − topper calories) ÷ 340 = **about 1¾ cups** | (vet's calorie target − topper calories) ÷ 390. Write it here: ____ cups |
| Meals | 2 a day, base split in half | 2–3 a day, split evenly |

**How to measure:** use a real 8-oz measuring cup leveled off, not a scoop. Better yet, weigh the food on a kitchen scale.

## 2. The toppers (cooked, plain, no seasoning)
**Approved topper list.** Calories are approximate.
| Topper | Approx. calories | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Skinless chicken breast, boiled or baked, chopped | ~45 per oz | Lean protein. Cook to 165°F [S24]. |
| 93% lean ground turkey, browned and drained | ~50 per oz | Cook to 165°F. |
| Green beans, plain (frozen or canned with no salt added) | ~2 per tbsp | Low-calorie filler. Good for the senior. |
| Carrots, steamed and diced | ~3 per tbsp | |
| Pure canned pumpkin (**not** pie filling) | ~5 per tbsp | Pie filling has added sugar and spices. |
| Blueberries | ~5 per tbsp | Raw is fine. |
| Hard-boiled egg (cooked through) | ~75 each | Don't feed raw eggs: they contain avidin, which blocks the B vitamin biotin [S24]. Use only occasionally because of the calories. |

**Never add:** onions, garlic, chives, grapes or raisins, chocolate, macadamia nuts, xylitol (a sweetener found in some peanut butters and sugar-free foods), alcohol, salty or seasoned foods, cooked bones or whole bones, or raw meat [S31, S5, S27]. Avoid dairy, because dogs don't digest lactose well [S31].

**Daily topper portions**
| | Dog 1 (senior): about 40 calories | Dog 2 (puppy): about 60 calories |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | ½ oz chicken (~23) | 1 oz chicken or turkey (~45–50) |
| Vegetable | ¼ cup green beans (~8) | 2 tbsp carrots (~6) |
| Extra | 2 tbsp pumpkin (~10) | 1 tbsp blueberries (~5) |
| **Total** | **~41 calories** | **~56–61 calories** |

Split each dog's toppers across its meals. Treats come out of the same budget, so on days with treats, skip the egg or meat extras.

## 3. Weekly shopping list (both dogs, 7 days)
- [ ] Skinless chicken breast, about **1 lb raw**. It cooks down to about 12 oz, and you need about 10½ oz for the week.
- [ ] (Optional swap) 93% lean ground turkey, 1 lb
- [ ] Plain frozen green beans, 1 bag (12–16 oz)
- [ ] Carrots, 1 small bag (or frozen diced carrots)
- [ ] Pure canned pumpkin, 1 can (15 oz). You'll use about half; freeze the rest in an ice-cube tray.
- [ ] Blueberries, 1 small container (fresh or frozen)
- [ ] Base food: the senior's single chosen recipe, plus the puppy's growth or all-life-stages food
- [ ] Supplies: 14 small containers or zip bags (7 per dog), labels, a kitchen scale, a measuring cup, and a meat thermometer

## 4. Weekly prep session (Sunday, about 45 minutes)
1. **Cook the chicken.** Boil or bake it plain until it reaches **165°F** inside [S24]. Cool it and chop it small. Wash your hands, cutting board and knife after handling the raw meat [S27].
2. **Cook the vegetables.** Steam or microwave the green beans and carrots plain, then cool and dice them.
3. **Portion one container per dog per day,** labeled by dog and day:
   - **Senior's container:** ½ oz chicken, ¼ cup green beans, 2 tbsp pumpkin.
   - **Puppy's container:** 1 oz chicken, 2 tbsp carrots, 1 tbsp blueberries.
4. **Store:** days 1–3 go in the fridge, and days 4–7 go in the freezer. Move the next day's container to the fridge each night. Throw out any cooked topper that's been in the fridge more than 3–4 days. (That's a general food-safety guideline, not from the report sources.)
5. **Pre-measure the base food:** fill 7 labeled bags or a marked bin with each dog's daily base amount. This keeps portions exact even when someone else feeds the dogs.

## 5. Daily routine (about 2 minutes)
- **Morning:** half the base food, plus half the day's topper container, mixed in.
- **Evening:** the other half of each. The puppy can have a third small meal if your vet prefers.
- Feed the dogs apart so neither eats the other's food. The senior is on a calorie budget.
- Keep fresh water available at all times [S24].

## 6. Switching the puppy's food (week 1)
- Change gradually over about a week, mixing more of the new food each day: roughly ¼ new for 2 days, then ½ for 2 days, then ¾ for 2 days, then all new.
- None of the veterinary sources in this research gave a schedule; this one is common advice from pet food brands. Ask your vet to confirm it.
- **Hold off on toppers for the puppy until the switch is done,** so you can tell what causes any stomach upset.

- **Target:** a body condition score of 4–5 out of 9 for both dogs [S12, S23]. At that score you can feel the ribs easily and see a waist from above.
- **Senior:** steady, slow loss. Your vet sets the pace; one study found about 1.5% of body weight per week safe [S26]. If he isn't losing after 3–4 weeks, ask your vet before cutting more. Merck recommends a weight-management food rather than less of a regular one [S24].
- **Puppy:** he should gain steadily without getting chubby. Your vet will check his growth [S12].

## 8. When to stop and call the vet
Call your vet if either dog has:
- vomiting or diarrhea that lasts more than a day;
- refusal to eat;
- sudden low energy;
- any sign it ate something on the "never add" list.

For a possible poisoning, call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at **(888) 426-4435** [S31].
