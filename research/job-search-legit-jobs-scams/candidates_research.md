## Notes

- AP, CNBC, and NYT: no AP- or CNBC-hosted article was found in searches. AP job-scam stories appear only as syndicated copies (WTTW was blocked). Not pursued further, to avoid paywalls and mirrors.
- Conflicting numbers:
  - FTC H1 2024 job-scam losses: "over $220M" in the text, $223M in the footnote.
  - BBB 2025 employment-scam report counts: 23,234 vs about 22,000 in secondary coverage. Use the BBB PDF (item 10).
  - Fortune's "almost $300M in H1 2025" is attributed to the FTC, but I did not confirm it on an FTC page.
- Ghost jobs are not scams. Greenhouse, Clarify Capital, and Resume Builder are vendor self-report surveys with different definitions (stated intent vs. how long a posting stays live), so their figures are not comparable. Greenhouse's 18–22% platform figure comes from secondary coverage (Fortune, Entrepreneur); I did not find it on a Greenhouse primary page.
- Academic gap: the original EMSCAD paper (MDPI) is blocked here. The Kaggle copy is the practical access point, but its license was not visible. No Federal Reserve primary analysis of ghost jobs was found. The arXiv ghost-jobs paper is a non-peer-reviewed preprint.
- Victim research is thin. The BBB 2020 study (7.1% response rate) and ITRC case counts are the main sources. A Resumetemplates.com survey (vendor) appeared in search results but was not opened, so it is not listed.
- APIs and querying:
  - arXiv: public API, no key.
  - Kaggle: API needs a free account token, and the dataset license must be checked before reuse.
  - FTC Explore Data: Tableau dashboards; no API or terms stated on the page.
  - State business registries (via NASS): each state's own search page, with terms that vary; there is no unified API.
  - None of the news or explainer sources offer an API.
- Verification guidance (from search summaries; treat as candidate advice, not yet confirmed per page):
  - Check the employer with the state Secretary of State business search.
  - Confirm the recruiter's email domain matches the company's official site.
  - Find the role on the company's own careers page by typing the URL yourself.
  - Never pay to get a job.
  - Ignore unexpected job texts or WhatsApp messages (FTC advice).
  - NPR's own recruiter-impersonation notice (item 22) is a clear real-employer example of these rules.
