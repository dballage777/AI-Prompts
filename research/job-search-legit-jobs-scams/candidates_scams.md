## Notes
- Access pattern: consumer.ftc.gov and ftc.gov return 403 to plain curl but open with WebFetch. That is how all the FTC pages above were confirmed. No headers were spoofed.
- Statistic conflicts: news coverage (Fortune, Nov 2025) says FTC job-scam reports rose 19% in the first half of 2025, with almost $300M in losses. I did not open the FTC source for this, so it is not listed. AARP and FTC alerts cite job-scam losses of $90M (2020) and $501M (2024); this figure came from search snippets and was not verified on the FTC page. IC3 figures are lower than FTC figures ($362.9M in 2025) because the two agencies use different complaint pools. Do not add them together.
- The BBB task-scam count is "about 680" on the study page. Some news outlets say "600+". Use the BBB page.
- BBB Scam Tracker entries and AARP's Scam Tracking Map are crowd-reported. Treat them as anecdotal evidence of a pattern, not as verified fraud findings about a named company.
- APIs: none of these scam-guidance sources has a public API. BBB Scam Tracker has a queryable lookup URL (bbb.org/scamtracker/lookupscam?q=...). Check bbb.org's terms before any automated querying; I did not review them. FTC Consumer Sentinel publishes aggregate data dashboards, but I did not open them this session.
- Bot-design implications (from the sources above): a consistent rule set across the FTC, FBI, BBB, LinkedIn, and Indeed is (1) never pay to get a job or to get paid, (2) never deposit a check and send money back, (3) treat unsolicited text, WhatsApp, or Telegram job offers as scams, (4) treat reshipping and "optimization" or task jobs as scams, (5) verify the job on the employer's own careers site, and (6) report to ReportFraud.ftc.gov, ic3.gov, and USPIS for reshipping. The 2026 IC3 North Korean IT-worker PSA adds a newer red flag: gigs asking someone to host laptops or interview on someone else's behalf are illegal facilitation, not jobs.
