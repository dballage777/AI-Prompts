## Learned points
1. The Job Finder searches open apprenticeship jobs from employers nationwide; users apply directly with the employer or program sponsor, not on Apprenticeship.gov. — [Documented]
2. Data sources: the Finder "pulls jobs from RAPIDS, NLx, state job banks, and other commercial job boards" (RAPIDS = DOL's Registered Apprenticeship Partners Information Data System). — [Documented]
3. Each result carries a tag: "Registered Partner" (the organization is registered with the Office of Apprenticeship) or "Registered Occupation" (the occupation title/code matches one registered with the Office of Apprenticeship). An occupation tag does not mean the employer itself is a registered sponsor. — [Documented; last sentence Inferred]
4. In-app tooltip text: "Registered" means the occupation "has been vetted by industry and approved by the U.S. Department of Labor for use in a Registered Apprenticeship Program." — [Documented, from site script]
5. How search works (help FAQ): search by keywords and/or location; results can be filtered by location, program type, date posted, and skills; sorted by "most relevant" or "nearest to you." — [Documented]
6. Filter option values seen in the site script: Mile Radius 25/50/100 miles (only when a location is entered); Date Posted Today / Last 7 / 30 / 90 days; Program Type Registered Partner vs Registered Occupation; sort also offers Name A–Z / Z–A. — [Documented, from site script]
7. How apply works: open a listing, click "apply" (upper right); it takes you to the job listing website to complete the application. An exit notice says DOL "does not endorse, takes no responsibility for" the linked site. So the final application page is third-party and should be checked by the user. — [Documented; last sentence Inferred]
8. Query parameters: the front-end script builds URLs such as `?occupation=…&location=…` and `?searchType=…&search=…&location=…`, but no public URL-parameter or API documentation was found. The search runs through a client-side search index, not a published public API. A bot should not rely on these URLs or scrape the backend; it can send users to the Finder page instead. — [Inferred from site script]
9. Employer posting paths: RAPIDS sponsors post via "Recruit Talent," and postings appear within 24 hours. Non-RAPIDS employers with a career website and at least five openings can have NLx index their jobs (allow 10–14 days, no cost). Employers with fewer openings post through their state job bank. — [Documented]
10. NLx-indexed listings must include job title, city, state, description, application method, and URL. A posting missing these basics is a quality warning sign. — [Documented; last sentence Inferred]
11. If no matching job is open, DOL points seekers to the Partner Finder to learn when programs accept applications, and to an American Job Center (CareerOneStop locator) for guidance. — [Documented]
12. Registered Apprenticeship is defined as paid work experience with a mentor, progressive wage increases, classroom instruction, and a portable, nationally recognized credential. It spans more than 1,000 occupations. — [Documented]
13. DOL has no official definition of internship. It lists differences in length, structure, mentorship, pay, credentials, and college credit. — [Documented]
14. Completers of a program registered with DOL, a State Apprenticeship Agency, or USMAP can download a transcript via Verify My Apprenticeship. Employers can verify completion with the apprentice number and date of birth. This is useful for resume verification. — [Documented]
15. Veterans: civilian programs may give advanced standing or credit for military training. The GI Bill Comparison Tool can search employers for on-the-job training and apprenticeships. — [Documented]
16. Employer tip: titles should include "apprentice" or "apprenticeship," and descriptions should name the training and credential, plus words like "wage" and "classroom training." This helps seekers recognize real apprenticeship listings. — [Documented; last sentence Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- Live results were not viewed (JavaScript app), so there are no counts of active listings and no example postings.
- Pulls from commercial job boards, so not every listing is DOL-vetted. The "Registered Occupation" tag describes the occupation, not the employer.
- No terms of use, rate limits, or public API for the Finder were found. URL parameters were inferred from code and may change without notice.
- Promotional tone toward apprenticeship; no scam guidance on these pages.
## Best for
- Pointing users to the official apprenticeship search, and explaining the Registered Partner vs Registered Occupation tags and that apply happens on the employer's site.
