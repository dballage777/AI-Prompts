## Learned points
1. Overall 2025: 1,008,597 complaints; $20.877 billion reported losses; 26% increase in losses from 2024; average loss $20,699; IC3 averages almost 3,000 complaints per day. — Documented
2. Employment fraud complaints: 24,688 (2025) vs 20,044 (2024) vs 15,443 (2023) — up about 60% over two years. — Documented
3. Employment fraud losses: $362,934,762 (2025) vs $264,223,271 (2024) vs $70,234,079 (2023) — roughly 5x in two years. — Documented
4. IC3 definition: "Employment Fraud: An individual believes they are legitimately employed and loses money, or launders money/items during their employment." Related type "Overpayment": sent a payment and told to keep part and forward the rest. — Documented
5. Employment complaints by age: under 20: 835; 20–29: 4,555; 30–39: 5,025; 40–49: 4,524; 50–59: 3,671. Losses: under 20 $1,489,854; 20–29 $33,049,422; 30–39 $42,865,213; 40–49 $57,604,303; 50–59 $100,853,442. — Documented
6. Complainants 60+: 2,853 Employment complaints and $78,712,899 Employment losses in 2025. — Documented
7. Complainants 17 or younger filed 246 Employment complaints (teen job seekers are targeted too). — Documented
8. Employment complaints with a cryptocurrency nexus: 10,338 complaints and $288,199,807 losses — i.e., most employment-scam dollars involved crypto. — Documented (ratio Inferred)
9. AI-related Employment complaints: 691, with $12,550,185 losses; text says victims reported "almost $13 million" to AI-involved employment scams. — Documented
10. AI/deepfake interview warning: voice spoofing or possible voice deepfakes in online interviews; lip movement and actions do not match the audio; coughs/sneezes not aligned with video; goal generally appears to be gaining access to private computer networks rather than direct dollar loss. — Documented
11. DPRK (North Korean) IT worker scheme: thousands of individuals deceiving US businesses into hiring them as remote IT workers to fund weapons programs, then exfiltrating data; IC3 PSAs on this dated 23-Jan-25 and 23-Jul-25. — Documented
12. Reporting advice: if you discover a fraudulent transfer, "time is of the essence" — immediately contact your financial institution and request a recall of funds; regardless of amount, file at www.ic3.gov with full transaction details. — Documented
13. IC3 Recovery Asset Team Financial Fraud Kill Chain 2025: 3,900 incidents, $1,163,919,846 attempted theft, $679,013,183 frozen, 58% success rate — evidence fast reporting can recover money. — Documented
14. Recovery scams: report notes increases in complaints of impersonation of government officials and "recovery firms," and an Aug 13, 2025 PSA on fictitious law firms offering to recover crypto-scam funds; also PSAs warning of scammers impersonating IC3 (18-Apr-25) and spoofing the IC3 website (19-Sep-25). — Documented
15. A 24-Apr-25 PSA covered criminals impersonating employee self-service websites to steal information and funds. — Documented (title only)
16. Data caveats: complaints are self-reported, one crime type per complaint, possible duplicates, age optional, statistics are point-in-time and may change. — Documented
17. Bot implication: warn that "recovery" services promising to get scam money back are themselves a known scam; send victims to their bank first, then ic3.gov and ReportFraud.ftc.gov. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- Self-reported complaints; underreporting likely; figures can change after analysis.
- "Employment" category mixes scams against job seekers with fraud during employment; the deepfake-interview passage mostly describes fake applicants targeting employers, not scams against job seekers.
- 60+ age column in the age table was not cleanly extracted; 60+ figures taken from the separate elder-fraud section.
