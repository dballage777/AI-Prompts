## Learned points
1. LinkedIn defines scams as fraud, "usually committed through email". Scammers create false identities or impersonate real people or companies to steal money or, in some cases, identity. — [Documented]
2. Generic warning signs of a scam message: offers that seem too good to be true, spelling or grammar mistakes, messages not addressed to you personally, and requests for personal or financial information. — [Documented]
3. LinkedIn describes job scams as people pretending to be recruiters or employers who offer high-paying jobs for little work. Named subtypes: mystery shopper, work from home, and personal assistant scams. — [Documented]
4. Job red flag, financial requests: never give payment or account credentials while applying. Legitimate companies should not require transfers, checks, gift cards, or wired funds as a condition of applying. — [Documented]
5. Job red flag, promises of compensation: a posting that prominently features first-year pay, an advance, or a signing bonus. — [Documented]
6. Job red flag, early requests for personal information: asking for an SSN, banking details, or ID photos before formal hiring. — [Documented]
7. Job red flag, off-platform pressure: urgent requests to move the conversation to WhatsApp, Telegram, or external forms. — [Documented]
8. Job red flag, mismatched email domains: email from generic addresses such as Gmail or Yahoo instead of @company.com. Postings with multiple misspellings and grammar errors are also a flag. — [Documented]
9. Company impersonators: postings that mirror a reputable company but have no link to the business, mismatched email or website domains, or no findable address, business phone, or email. Watch for recruiter profiles that are incomplete, have few connections, or are connected to few employees of the company they claim to represent. — [Documented]
10. Equipment-purchase scam: be cautious if a "company" asks you to buy your own computers, iPads, or phones and mail them to its IT department. — [Documented]
11. Follow-up link scam: be cautious of application follow-ups that require clicking a link to answer more questions, especially if the questions ask for an SSN. — [Documented]
12. Verification method: if you can't quickly verify the poster's identity, put the sender's phone number, email, and social media handles into a search engine to fact-check them. Anonymity may be deliberate. — [Documented]
13. Reporting channels: report scam messages received on LinkedIn through its contact form. If you were victimized, report to local law enforcement. LinkedIn also points to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (ic3.gov), to report job scams at the FTC (reportfraud.ftc.gov, with a LinkedIn org code in the link), and to Interpol for cross-border crime. — [Documented]
14. LinkedIn offers no phone support. Sites that sell "LinkedIn phone support" are not affiliated with LinkedIn, and LinkedIn says it never asks for your password or access to your computer. — [Documented]
15. From the linked identity-theft page: email is not a secure channel for sensitive information. Enter data on the website directly, and check for https:// in the address bar. LinkedIn says valid LinkedIn messages include your name and current headline in the footer. — [Documented]
16. The identity-theft page promotes paid identity-protection vendors (LifeLock, Experian, TransUnion) with trial or discount offers. This is a commercial referral, not neutral guidance. — [Documented]
17. For a bot: the red-flag list can serve as a checklist to screen postings and recruiter messages a user pastes in. Flags include payment requests, SSN or bank details before hire, a move to WhatsApp or Telegram, a Gmail or Yahoo recruiter address, equipment purchases, and pay-focused postings. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- This is vendor guidance focused on LinkedIn's own platform. Its advice to stay on-platform also serves LinkedIn's interests.
- The page has no absolute publication date, no statistics, and no detail on how to report a specific job posting.
- It gives no recovery steps (bank, identity theft) beyond pointing to law enforcement, IC3, and the FTC.
- The identity-theft subpage includes commercial vendor promotions.

## Best for
- A concrete, platform-tested checklist of job-scam red flags, plus the reporting channels (LinkedIn form, local police, IC3, FTC) that a job-search bot can cite when warning users.
