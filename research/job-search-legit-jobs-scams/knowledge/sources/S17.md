## Learned points
1. NLx is a public-private partnership between NASWA and DirectEmployers Association (DE), built in 2007 "for purpose, not profit." Both nonprofit member associations administer and run it. — [Documented]
2. Every day NLx collects and distributes "millions of unduplicated and authentic job openings exclusively from original and vetted sources" to every U.S. state and territory and to a network of partners. — [Documented]
3. State participation: all 50 state workforce agencies, DC, Guam, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands take part, along with "hundreds of thousands of employers." — [Documented]
4. NLx powers "hundreds of job boards, career navigation tools," and other workforce tech. So the same NLx listing can appear on many state and partner sites. — [Documented; second sentence Inferred]
5. Job Feed: a daily file of vetted, unduplicated postings contributed by NASWA and DE members and "NLx vetted employers." It goes to all states and territories and a vetted network of syndicated partners (list available on request). Each posting links back to the original source. — [Documented]
6. Job Indexing: NLx automatically extracts postings from specific employers' websites or applicant tracking systems. NASWA says the term is used interchangeably with spidering or scraping. — [Documented]
7. Job Search API: "participating members" get dynamic search with advanced filtering and sorting. It is described as a member offering, not a public API, and no endpoints, keys, or limits are published on these pages. — [Documented; "not public" Inferred from wording]
8. Job View Page: hosted by NLx to show title, description, qualifications, employer details, and application instructions. It may sit behind a login where user verification is needed or where applications go by email. — [Documented]
9. Hosted Labor Exchange System: job feed, job search, and job posting tools that meet "federal workforce development legislation" requirements, offered to participating members. — [Documented]
10. Job Microsites: extra entry points to the same job pool, up to five included, and they stay online even if a state job bank is down. A legitimate-looking NLx microsite is therefore not a different job source. — [Documented; last sentence Inferred]
11. Policy support covers WIOA, Wagner-Peyser, VEVRAA, state pay-transparency laws, skills-based hiring, and OFCCP job-posting compliance. — [Documented]
12. USNLx.com is described as "the official job search platform powered by the NLx," with listings "sourced directly from employers and state workforce agencies." — [Documented]
13. Data access rules: the NLx Research Hub gives real-time and historical job posting data "via API or monthly files" on request (request form on nlxresearchhub.org, not read). Data tools include the ON DEMAND dashboard and NLx Job Opening Estimator (JOE). — [Documented]
14. The full NLx Menu of Services is proprietary and limited to NASWA and DE members; it "must not be shared." Most NLx tools are member services, not open to individual developers. — [Documented; last sentence Inferred]
15. Support requests go through a NASWA ITSC service-desk portal. The contact is nlx@naswa.org. — [Documented]

## Limitations / bias
- Self-description by a partner organization. "Vetted," "authentic," and "verified" are claimed but the vetting criteria are not explained on these pages.
- No API documentation, terms, eligibility rules, or rate limits are given. The Research Hub request form on a separate domain was not read.
- No numeric job or employer counts on naswa.org beyond "millions" and "hundreds of thousands" (see S20 for USNLx figures).
