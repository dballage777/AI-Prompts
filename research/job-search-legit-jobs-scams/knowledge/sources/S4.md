## Learned points
1. Reported losses to job scams more than tripled from 2020 to 2023 and topped $220 million in the first half of 2024. — [Research] (FTC Consumer Sentinel report data)
2. Reports of gamified "task scams" rose to about 20,000 in the first half of 2024, up from about 5,000 in all of 2023. — [Research]
3. Cryptocurrency losses to job scams were about $41 million in the first half of 2024, versus about $21 million in all of 2023. People now report losing more money to job scams through crypto than through any other payment method. — [Research]
4. The FTC notes that most fraud goes unreported, so these figures understate the harm. — [Documented]
5. How it starts: an unexpected text or WhatsApp message offers online work, often with vague details. — [Documented]
6. The "work" is simple, repetitive tasks such as liking videos or rating product images, done inside an app or platform that shows commissions growing with each click. — [Documented]
7. Small early payouts build trust. Later the victim is told to make a deposit to unlock the next set of tasks or withdraw earnings. The earnings are fake and the deposit is lost. — [Documented]
8. Hesitant victims may be added to group chats full of fake "success stories." — [Documented]
9. Advice: ignore generic or unexpected job texts and WhatsApp messages, since real employers don't recruit that way. Never pay anyone to get paid; a deposit demanded to access earnings is a clear sign of a scam. Be wary of offers to pay you to rate or "like" things online. — [Documented]
10. Report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Learn more at ftc.gov/scams. — [Documented]
11. Bot implication: if a user describes unsolicited text or WhatsApp job offers, app-based "tasks," commissions, or needing to deposit money or crypto to withdraw, the bot should name this as a likely task scam, tell the user to stop sending money, and give reporting steps. — [Inferred]
