## Learned points
1. FTC (Apr 2026) warns of a text-message scam where fake recruiters pose as well-known companies and offer remote jobs, often quoting pay but no real job details. — Documented (summary-only)
2. Red flags in the alert: unexpected or generic job texts; vague remote roles (e.g., "online assessor," "remote position"); pay quoted per day or week without explanation. — Documented (summary-only)
3. New twist: instead of a link, scammers ask you to reply "YES" or "INTERESTED" to start engagement. — Documented
4. The alert ties these texts to two end-games: fake check scams (deposit a check, send money back) and task scams (do online "tasks" such as posting ratings/reviews, then pay in your own money). — Documented (summary-only)
5. Core advice: ignore generic, unexpected job messages on text, WhatsApp, or Telegram; never pay to get a job or to get paid; distrust anyone paying for positive ratings or likes. — Documented
6. Reporting channel: ReportFraud.ftc.gov; more info at ftc.gov/scams. — Documented
7. FTC "Job Scams" article lists scam types: work-from-home (pay for kits/training/certifications), reshipping, reselling merchandise, nanny/caregiver/virtual-assistant (overpayment check), mystery shopper, job placement firms charging fees, and fake federal/USPS job ads that charge a fee. — Documented (summary-only)
8. The article states applying for federal and Postal Service jobs is always free; any fee request is a scam signal. — Documented (summary-only)
9. Additional red flags: high pay for little work; pressure to act fast or skip a second opinion; unsolicited offers from strangers; reshipping packages; a company whose phone or website goes dead after you start; placement firm demanding an advance fee. — Documented (summary-only)
10. Verification steps: search the company/person name with "scam," "review," or "complaint"; talk to someone you trust; confirm no payment is required; never rely on a check you must partly forward (it will likely bounce and you owe the bank). — Documented (summary-only)
11. FTC points job seekers to official sources: USAJobs.gov (federal), CareerOneStop (Department of Labor), and USA.gov (state/local government jobs). — Documented (summary-only)
12. Honest placement firms are generally paid by the hiring company, not the candidate. — Documented
13. If you paid a scammer: contact the payment company used (card, payment app, wire, gift card, reload card, crypto) immediately, report the fraud, and ask to reverse the transaction; method-specific steps are on FTC's "What To Do If You Were Scammed" page (not read here). — Documented (summary-only)
14. Report to FTC (ReportFraud.ftc.gov) and to your state attorney general (via NAAG directory link). — Documented (summary-only)
15. Bot implication: a job-search assistant should treat any recruiter contact by text/WhatsApp/Telegram, any pay-to-work request, any check-forwarding, and any "rate/like for pay" task as a stop-and-warn trigger, and route users to ReportFraud.ftc.gov. — Inferred
