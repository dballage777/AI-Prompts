## Learned points
1. The FBI warns of fake work-from-home jobs built on simple tasks, such as rating restaurants or "optimizing" a service by clicking a button. — [Documented]
2. Scammers pose as legitimate businesses, such as staffing or recruiting agencies. They usually reach victims through unsolicited calls or messages. — [Documented]
3. The pay structure is confusing. Victims must send cryptocurrency to the "employer" to earn more or to "unlock" work. — [Documented]
4. A fake interface shows earnings that the victim cannot withdraw. — [Documented]
5. Red flag: being directed to make cryptocurrency payments to your employer as part of a job. — [Documented]
6. Red flag: a job described as "optimization" that involves simple tasks. — [Documented]
7. Red flag: no references are required during hiring. — [Documented]
8. Protection: be wary of unsolicited job offers, and don't click links, download files, or open attachments in them. Never send money to an alleged employer. Don't share financial or personal information with people who make unsolicited job offers. — [Documented]
9. Watch for follow-on recovery scams. Do not pay anyone who claims they can recover lost cryptocurrency. — [Documented]
10. Report to IC3 at www.ic3.gov and include transaction details. The PSA refers to PSA I-082423-PSA for what information to provide. — [Documented]
11. Bot implication: the bot should never suggest paying an employer, in crypto or any other way. It should warn that "fund recovery" offers after a loss are often a second scam, and it should list both IC3 and ReportFraud.ftc.gov as reporting channels. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- The PSA is short and gives no loss statistics.
- It focuses on crypto task scams only. It does not cover fake checks, phishing interviews, or fee-based placement scams.
- I read a condensed rendering.

## Best for
- FBI-backed red flags for crypto task scams, a warning about recovery scams, and the IC3 reporting channel.
