## Learned points
1. Most NASS member offices, usually the Secretary of State, register domestic and foreign corporations (profit and non-profit). Filings include incorporation, partnerships (including limited partnerships), mergers or consolidations, and dissolutions. Fees vary by state. — [Documented]
2. The Corporate Registration page is mainly a state directory. A drop-down links to each state's (and DC's and Puerto Rico's) business registration page and corporate name database. It covers 50 states plus DC and PR. — [Documented]
3. The directory links point to different kinds of state pages, so terms and entry points vary. Some go straight to entity search (New York "bus_entity_search", Wisconsin "CorpSearch", Puerto Rico "CorporationSearch.aspx"). Others go to general hubs (Florida sunbiz.org, Delaware corp.delaware.gov, Texas sos.state.tx.us/corp). Some go to "start/register a business" pages (Kansas, Louisiana, Minnesota, West Virginia). Maryland's link goes to a login page. — [Documented]
4. In some states the registry is not run by the Secretary of State. The links show Alaska's Commerce department, Arizona's Corporation Commission (azcc.gov), Hawaii's Commerce and Consumer Affairs (cca.hawaii.gov/breg), Michigan's LARA, New Jersey's Treasury, Virginia's State Corporation Commission (scc.virginia.gov), Utah corporations.utah.gov, and DC's DCRA. — [Documented]
5. Several directory links use plain http:// or old URL paths, for example Michigan's "0,4601,7-154-..." path. Some may now redirect or be out of date. These links were not tested in this read. — [Inferred]
6. NASS says no user registration is needed to access the state Business Services links. — [Documented]
7. Key verification caveat: state entity formation is "ministerial", meaning offices record filings rather than vet businesses, and the information filed is public. A registry listing shows only that an entity is on file. It does not show the employer is legitimate or that the job is real. — [Documented] (caveat wording [Inferred])
8. Secretaries of State educate businesses about widespread business scams and business identity theft. NASS links infosheets, made with the Cybercrime Support Network, on business email compromise and on what to do if someone impersonates your business. This matters because scammers impersonate real, registered companies. — [Documented]
9. A business name (trade name, assumed name, fictitious name, or "DBA") is different from a trademark. Registering a business name does not create trademark rights. Federal trademarks are with the USPTO, and state trademarks are usually on Secretary of State websites. — [Documented]
10. Beneficial ownership: as of the NASS update, all US-created entities are now exempt from reporting beneficial ownership information to FinCEN. Existing foreign companies had until about April 25, 2025, for most companies. So no federal ownership registry is available to a job seeker. — [Documented] (implication [Inferred])
11. NASS says Secretaries of State help federal law enforcement act against "front companies and shell entities". Shell entities can be registered and still look legitimate in a registry. — [Documented]
12. NASS also links a December 2024 "Summary of Business Entity Information Collected by States" (not opened here), which may describe what each state's records contain. — [Documented]
13. Suggested bot method: (a) find the state named in the posting or where the company claims to operate. (b) Go to that state's registry through the NASS directory. (c) Search the exact legal name and any DBA. (d) Check status (for example active or in good standing), formation date, and registered address against the posting. (e) Treat a match as one weak signal, never proof. Pair it with the employer's own careers site and official contact details. — [Inferred]
14. Red-flag patterns a bot could look for: no entity record at all, a very recent formation date for a "large established" employer, an address that doesn't match, or a name only slightly different from a well-known company. These are not stated on the NASS pages. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- This is a link directory, not a guide. It gives no step-by-step instructions for searching a registry or reading entity status, and says nothing about job scams.
- Some directory URLs look old (http, legacy paths) and may redirect or be broken. They were not tested.
- Registry presence does not show legitimacy (ministerial filing). Scammers can impersonate real registered companies or register shell entities.
- Steps and red flags marked [Inferred] are this reader's synthesis, not NASS guidance.

## Best for
- Sending a bot user to the correct official state business-entity registry to check that an employer exists, with the caveat that registration is ministerial and does not prove legitimacy.
