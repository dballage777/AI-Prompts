## Learned points
1. USAJOBS is the federal government's official employment site, run by OPM. It lists federal openings in the US and abroad. — [Documented]
2. A USAJOBS profile needs only an email address to sign up. Sign-in is through login.gov. — [Documented]
3. The application flow on the Get Started page has eight steps. (1) Create a login.gov account. (2) Create a USAJOBS profile. (3) Build or upload a resume (up to five stored). (4) Search using filters for keyword, occupation, agency, location, salary, or pay grade, and optionally save searches with daily, weekly, or monthly email alerts. (5) Read the announcement's eligibility, qualifications, and How to Apply sections. (6) Prepare the application and attach documents. (7) Submit, which transfers you to the hiring agency's own application system. (8) Track status in your USAJOBS account. — [Documented]
4. Eligibility depends on each announcement. Applicants must read its "This job is open to" and "Who may apply" sections. — [Documented]
5. The home page says more than 16 million job seekers have created accounts. It also promotes a Career Explorer quiz, early-career and attorney roles, and recruiting events. — [Documented]
6. None of the pages I could open mention fees, fraud, or scams. The About page has only the generic .gov security notice (check for HTTPS and the lock icon before sharing sensitive information). — [Documented]
7. A legitimate federal application ends in the agency's own application system after USAJOBS hands it off. A message asking you to apply or pay outside that flow does not match the official process. — [Inferred]
8. The FTC (S1) says applying for federal jobs is always free. USAJOBS's own fee and fraud statements could not be verified because the help center was blocked. — [Inferred]
