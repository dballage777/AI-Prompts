## Notes

- Community evidence is anecdotal. The Reddit, HN, Stack Exchange, and Ask a Manager items show what job seekers experience and which sources they trust or distrust. They are not authoritative on facts. Main anecdotal themes: (a) many people believe ghost jobs are widespread, and HN threads cite an arXiv paper and press stories (check the primary sources before stating any figures); (b) fake recruiters now go after developers with malware "coding challenges"; (c) proxy-interview and identity-renting schemes; (d) public "open to work" posts attract scammers.
- Several sites were blocked by bot protection in this session, and no workaround was used. Reddit (JS challenge), Stack Exchange HTML (403), Ask a Manager (Cloudflare 403), goodwill.org (403), CareerOneStop finders (Cloudflare 403), YouTube watch pages (429 Google bot check), and idtheftcenter.org (Cloudflare 403) all failed. Search results offered unofficial Reddit mirrors (Redlib/Libreddit instances); these were deliberately not used. The Workplace SE and Money SE question titles and URLs came from the official Stack Exchange API, but the pages themselves did not open.
- Discord and Telegram: no public, openable pages from official job-search communities were found, so none were listed. The FTC and BBB name Telegram and WhatsApp recruiting as a red flag.
- API details:
  - Reddit Data API: needs a registered OAuth token. Commercial use needs a separate agreement. It bans using User Content to train ML/AI models without rightsholder permission, and bans masking the user agent. A commercial bot should not query Reddit without a deal.
  - Stack Exchange API: works without a key at a low daily quota and requires attribution. It is a workable way for an assistant to surface scam-check Q&A, labeled anecdotal.
  - HN Firebase and Algolia APIs: no key needed. The monthly "Who is hiring" thread can be found through Algolia (author:whoishiring) and read item by item.
  - Idealist Terms (last updated 2026-06-15): ban bots, spiders, scrapers, and crawlers (§3(d)(j)) and republishing data (§3(d)(k)). Its APIs need a separate agreement (§1(b)). Do not let an assistant scrape Idealist job search; just link to it.
  - YouTube channels can be listed through the YouTube Data API, which needs a key. That was not tested.
- Community-recommended alternatives to big boards: Reddit could not be read, so direct community-recommendation evidence comes only from HN ("Who is hiring" threads, whose rules ban recruiters and job boards). Other alternatives here (Idealist, NAWB Career Center, Goodwill/My Career Advisor, Hire Heroes USA, local workforce boards and American Job Centers) are nonprofit or public-system channels, not community-ranked. Commercial "best Indeed alternatives" blogs from search were vendor content and were excluded.
- Conflict worth flagging: NAWB says it represents more than 570 workforce boards, while one search snippet said boards oversee about 2,300 American Job Centers. These count different things, so they do not truly conflict, but check the AJC count against DOL/CareerOneStop.
