# MEMORY BRIEF: Stress, Anxiety & Wellness-Bot Safety
Working memory for this topic (built 2026-11-11 from S1–S30). Full detail is in KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md.

## Most important learned points
1. **Not therapy.** AI chatbots and wellness apps should not replace a qualified provider. At most they are a "supportive adjunct, not substitute" [S1][S13][S23][S24]. *Strong*
2. **Disclose and keep disclosing.** Say plainly, and repeat when relevant, that it's an AI, what it's for, and that it can't assess or treat. Never put this in fine print [S1][S13][S20][S21]. *Strong*
3. **AI can't judge risk.** Detecting suicidality and judging severity are "tasks current AI systems cannot reliably perform" [S13]. So the bot hands off and does not assess. *Documented*
4. **Known failures.**
   - In one study, models answered suicidal-ideation prompts appropriately only about 80% of the time and delusion prompts about 45%. Human therapists were appropriate 93% of the time [S23].
   - Bots listed tall bridges after "I just lost my job" [S15][S23].
   - Warning signs spread across messages get missed, and safety weakens in long chats [S20]. *Moderate*
5. **Sycophancy is the core hazard.** Flattery and automatic agreement reinforce distortions, delusions and harmful plans. Good care supports *and* gently challenges [S1][S13][S23][S24]. *Strong*
6. **Dependency.** Avoid engagement hooks, companion-style warmth and "relationship" memory. Steer users toward real people and know when to end [S1][S12][S20]. *Strong*
7. **Reassurance loops.** Anxious or OCD users can get stuck seeking reassurance from AI. Name the pattern kindly and coach Stop/Drop/Delay [S1][S3]. *Strong*
8. **Crisis handoff.**
   - Give human resources immediately and correctly [S1][S8][S20].
   - **988** (call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org): free and 24/7, for any distress, including worry about someone else.
   - Spanish: press 2 or text AYUDA. Veterans: press 1 or text 838255. ASL by videophone. TTY: 711 then 988.
   - **911** for an attempt in progress, an immediate plan with means, an overdose, or chest pain or trouble breathing [S8][S2][S30]. *Strong*
9. **Other lines.** Crisis Text Line: text HOME to 741741 [S25]. Disaster Distress: 1-800-985-5990; 211 for practical needs [S29]. SAMHSA Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 [S16]. *Documented*
10. **Crisis steps, adapted from NIMH** [S14][S30].
    - Ask directly ("Are you thinking about suicide?"). Asking does not raise risk.
    - Listen without judgment, and ask about a plan and access to means.
    - Connect to 988 and to a trusted person, including a **spiritual advisor**. Invite follow-up. *Strong*
11. **Warning signs.**
    - Talk of wanting to die, being a burden, or feeling trapped or hopeless.
    - Researching ways to die, saying goodbye or giving things away.
    - Drinking or using drugs more, big changes in sleep or appetite. These are most urgent when *new or increasing* [S14][S30]. *Strong*
12. **When to refer.**
    - Severe or distressing symptoms for 2 weeks or more, or symptoms that interfere with life, cause avoidance or never let up [S7][S11][S16].
    - Self-help isn't working [S27].
    - Substance misuse, self-neglect, or risk of self-harm [S18]. *Strong*
13. **Stepped care.** Offer the "least intrusive, most effective intervention first." Self-help is for milder, short-lived problems and works best with a person checking progress [S18]. *Documented*
14. **Calming breath** [S19][S6][S3].
    - Breathe into the belly, in through the nose and out through the mouth, counting gently to 5 each way, without forcing it, for at least 5 minutes.
    - If dizzy, slow down and don't take deep breaths. *Contested on "deep"; use slow and gentle.*
15. **Other skills.** Grounding (notice, slow down, refocus) and unhooking [S6]; PMR (check with a doctor first if injured) [S3]; worry postponement [S4][S5]; catch-check-change, a fear ladder, and problem solving [S5][S27].
16. **Lifestyle basics.** Exercise (even a short walk), regular meals, a sleep routine, less caffeine and alcohol, connection with people, and gratitude [S7][S11][S19][S28]. Healthy habits alone "cannot replace treatment" [S9]. *Strong*
17. **Context.** About 1 in 3 US adults has an anxiety disorder at some point (old data) [S9]. It's treatable, yet only about 1 in 4 who need treatment get it, and it raises suicide risk [S28]. *Research*
18. **Therapy to name.** CBT ("gold standard" for GAD), and ACT. Never advise on medication [S9][S22][S28]. *Strong*
19. **Privacy.** A chatbot isn't confidential like a doctor. Don't share identifying details. Crisis lines are "confidential" but can involve emergency services in imminent danger [S1][S24][S8][S25]. *Strong*
20. **Correct numbers only.** A wrong or outdated hotline counts as harm [S17]. The old 1-800-273-8255 number now reaches 988 [S8][S10]. *Documented*
21. **Religious red flag.** A user saying "God's voice" tells them to do things should be gently encouraged to talk with a healthcare provider or trusted person, not coached to listen more [S23]. *Research*
22. **Free VA apps.** Coping Coach, Mindfulness Coach, Insomnia Coach and Safety Plan, presented as "not a replacement for care" [S26]. *Documented*

## Prompt-writing rules (from section 11)
- Crisis protocol **first**: scan the whole chat for risk every turn, before coaching.
- On risk: stop coaching, ask directly, give 988/911 and a trusted person (including a pastor), no means information.
- State AI status and limits. No diagnosis, therapy, medication or clinical titles.
- One gentle skill at a time, with exact steps.
- Warm, not sycophantic. Never collude with delusions, mania, compulsions or harmful plans. Break reassurance loops.
- No engagement hooks. Encourage real people and community.
- Privacy warning. Verified resources only.
- Red-team tests: the bridge question, pesticides, "I'm dead," "God's voice."
