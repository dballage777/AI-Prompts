## Learned points
1. Usage stat: an estimated 52% of US adults use AI chatbots such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and usage is rising (linked to an Elon University survey news release, March 12, 2025). — Reported
2. Possible reasons people use chatbots for health: cost or information barriers to care, reduced stigma and fear of judgment, distrust of the health-care system (linked to a New York Times article, Nov 16, 2025). — Reported
3. Risk stat: in October 2025 OpenAI released data indicating more than a million ChatGPT users each week show "explicit indicators of potential suicidal planning or intent". — Reported (secondary)
4. Position: chatbots can give quick health information but "are not a replacement for a therapist or medical professional"; using AI in place of therapy or treatment can harm the person and people around them. — Documented
5. Message 1 — "Conversation isn't the same as therapy": trained therapists diagnose, reduce harm and build a tailored plan. — Documented
6. Message 2 — AI can be dangerous to people in crisis or with serious symptoms; chatbots built to validate or mimic emotions can reinforce harmful behaviour. — Documented
7. Message 3 — chatbots generate responses, not treatment; internet-scraped information may be false, fabricated or out of context; some models use emotions to keep users in conversation, which may create reliance and harm mental health. — Documented
8. Message 4 — "Every brain is one-of-a-kind, chatbots are one-size-fits-all": professionals ask questions, explore options and tailor treatment. — Documented
9. Message 5 — privacy: with a chatbot "doctor-patient confidentiality does not exist" and privacy is not guaranteed. — Documented
10. Message 6 — "Chatbots are not telehealth professionals": if in-person therapy isn't possible, virtual therapy sessions are an alternative. — Documented
11. Message 7 — for immediate support, call or text the 988 Lifeline (linked to 988lifeline.org); for ongoing support, research professional options or ask a doctor for a referral; plus a placeholder for a local community resource. — Documented
12. Implication for a wellness bot: it should not validate harmful plans to keep users engaged, should avoid engagement-maximizing emotional hooks, should be honest that chats aren't confidential, and should route crisis to 988 and ongoing needs to professionals, including telehealth and doctor referral. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- Short public-messaging toolkit aimed at communicators, not clinical guidance; statistics are second-hand (survey, newspaper, company data) and not independently verified here.
- Written to discourage chatbot use for mental health; offers no guidance on what a chatbot should do well, nor self-help content.

## Best for
- Plain-language, US-public-health-endorsed wording the bot can echo about its own limits (not therapy, not private, can be wrong) and the referral ladder: 988 now, doctor/therapist/telehealth for ongoing care.
