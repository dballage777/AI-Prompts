## Learned points
1. How to reach the line: dial 988 then press 1, text 838255, or chat at VeteransCrisisLine.net/Chat. Service is free, confidential, and available 24/7/365. The old number, 1-800-273-8255 then press 1, still works. — [Documented]
2. Who can use it: Veterans, service members, National Guard and Reserve members, and their families and friends. Users do not need to be enrolled in VA benefits or health care. The line serves Veterans "no matter your discharge status." — [Documented]
3. Deaf and hard-of-hearing access: chat or text 838255. TTY users can use their preferred relay service or dial 711 then 988. VA footers also list TTY 1-800-799-4889. — [Documented]
4. What happens on a call: a recording confirms you have reached the VCL, then there is a brief pause while you are connected to a qualified responder. The responder asks a few questions, including whether you or the person you're concerned about is in immediate danger or at risk for suicide. You decide how much to share. — [Documented]
5. Outcomes by risk level. If someone is in danger, the responder helps them through the crisis and connects them to services at the local VA medical center or in the community. If the person is in crisis but not at imminent risk, the responder listens, offers support, and helps make a plan to stay safe. — [Documented]
6. Follow-up: if the person shares contact details, a local VA suicide prevention coordinator (SPC) will contact them by the next business day. Every VA medical center has an SPC. — [Documented]
7. Chat steps: go to VeteransCrisisLine.net/Chat, click to start, enter a first name or nickname, then a responder joins (or a waiting notice appears). — [Documented]
8. Text: send any message to 838255 and keep messages under 160 characters. Text STOP to end the conversation. The automated reply quoted on the site still references 800-273-8255, which is a minor legacy inconsistency. — [Documented]
9. Emergency dispatch: "If you're in imminent danger or at risk of harming others, the responder will coordinate a dispatch of emergency services with local officials." Information is shared only with the person's authorization or as permitted by law (HIPAA, Privacy Act, FOIA). This matters when a bot describes "confidential": it is not unconditional. — [Documented]
10. 988 background: the National Suicide Hotline Designation Act (2020) made 988 the 3-digit Lifeline number, and US carriers had to activate it by July 16, 2022. Pressing 1 routes callers to the same trained VCL responders, who are "trained in crisis intervention and military culture." — [Documented]
11. Overseas: separate numbers apply by DoD region. PACOM +1 844-702-5493; EUCOM +1 844-702-5495; CENTCOM +1 855-422-7719; AFRICOM +1 888-482-6054; SOUTHCOM +1 866-989-9599 (all off base); or DSN 988 on base. Chat works anywhere with internet, and the short code 838255 works only on US carriers with international texting. — [Documented]
12. Scale (data as of 09/2026): more than 10.41M calls, 630,000 texts, 1.18M chats, 2.01M referrals to SPCs, and 478,000 emergency dispatches. The line launched in 2007 with 14 responders and now has 1,100 responders in 3 call centers. — [Reported]
13. Programs: Caring Letters (8 letters over 1 year; more than 2.7M letters to more than 359,000 Veterans since June 2020) and a Peer Support Outreach Call Center staffed by trained Veterans. — [Reported]
14. Crisis signs that need immediate action (call 911 for medical needs, or VCL): thinking about hurting or killing yourself, looking for ways to kill yourself, talking about death, dying, or suicide, and self-destructive behavior such as drug abuse or risky use of weapons. — [Documented]
15. Warning signs that call for contacting VCL: persistent sadness, hopelessness, anxiety, agitation, sleeplessness or mood swings, feeling there is no reason to live, excessive guilt or shame, rage, risky activities, increasing alcohol or drug use, loss of interest, self-neglect, withdrawal, violence, giving away prized possessions, and getting affairs in order or writing a will. — [Documented]
16. Conversation guidance: remind the person you are there; listen more than you speak; let them decide how much to share; make supportive comments and avoid invasive questions; stay calm and avoid judgment. It is okay to ask directly, "Are you thinking about taking your own life?" Asking does not increase risk. A bot's crisis responses can follow the same rules. — [Documented]
17. A supporter can offer a three-way call: introduce the Veteran to the responder and stay on the line until they feel safe, or let them continue privately. — [Documented]
18. Inferred bot rule: if a user says they are a Veteran or service member, or worried about one, give "dial 988 then press 1, text 838255, or chat at VeteransCrisisLine.net/Chat." Mention that VA enrollment is not required. Use 911 for immediate medical danger. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- The site promotes its own service, and the statistics are self-reported with no outcome evaluation.
- The claim that asking about suicide doesn't increase risk is stated without a citation on the page.
- Most pages show no last-updated date.
- The text-reply sample on the site still shows the legacy 800 number.

## Best for
- Exact, current instructions for reaching the Veterans Crisis Line by call, text, chat, TTY, or from overseas; what to expect, including limits on confidentiality; and a crisis vs warning-sign checklist.
