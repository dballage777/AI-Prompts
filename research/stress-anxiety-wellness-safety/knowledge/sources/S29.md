## Learned points
1. The Disaster Distress Helpline (DDH) is described as "the first national hotline dedicated to providing year-round disaster crisis counseling." It is toll-free, multilingual, and available 24/7 to all US residents and residents of US territories. — [Documented]
2. How to reach it: call or text 1-800-985-5990. For Spanish, call or text the same number and press 2. Deaf and hard-of-hearing ASL callers can call or text the same number through their preferred relay provider. — [Documented]
3. Scope: emotional distress related to natural or human-caused disasters. The listed types are tornadoes and severe storms, hurricanes, floods, wildfires, earthquakes, drought, extreme heat, mass violence, anniversaries and trigger events, infectious disease outbreaks such as COVID-19, and community unrest. — [Documented]
4. Who can call: survivors, loved ones of victims, first responders, rescue, recovery and relief workers, clergy, and parents and caregivers. People can call for themselves or on behalf of someone else. Clergy are named explicitly, which is relevant for a faith-based bot. — [Documented]
5. Who answers: trained counselors from a network of crisis call centers across the US. They provide crisis counseling, information on recognizing distress, referrals to local crisis centers for follow-up, and healthy coping tips. — [Documented]
6. Privacy: "There is no need to give any identifying information." End-of-call questions are optional and used for tracking. SAMHSA says it will not sell phone numbers. Standard text and data rates apply. — [Documented]
7. Languages: more than 100 languages through third-party interpreters. Less common languages may need a scheduled callback. — [Documented]
8. For practical disaster needs (evacuations, shelters, food, clothing, volunteering), the page directs people to call 211. A bot should separate emotional distress (DDH) from practical needs (211). — [Documented]
9. SAMHSA's helpline menu also lists the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, the Veterans Crisis Line, the SAMHSA National Helpline, and a Spanish DDH line as separate services. This supports routing: suicide or immediate crisis goes to 988; disaster-related distress goes to DDH. — [Documented; routing Inferred]
10. Normalization: "It is common to feel stress symptoms before or after a crisis." Most symptoms are temporary and resolve fairly quickly, but for some people, especially at-risk populations, they can last weeks or months. — [Documented]
11. Disaster distress symptoms: eating or sleeping too much or too little; anger or lashing out; overwhelming sadness; withdrawal; low energy; feeling you have to keep busy; unexplained aches; helplessness or hopelessness; heavier smoking, drinking, or drug use (including prescription drugs); constant worry or unexplained guilt; trouble readjusting to home or work; and "thinking of hurting or killing yourself or someone else." — [Documented]
12. Inferred bot rule: if a user mentions a disaster, mass-violence event, or anniversary and shows distress, offer DDH (1-800-985-5990). If they mention thoughts of harming themselves or others, escalate to 988 or 911 rather than DDH. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- A service promotion page with no data on call volume or outcomes.
- The page does not explain how DDH relates to 988, such as whether calls are transferred.
- The DDH page was last updated September 2025. Check the number before publishing.

## Best for
- An exact, verified disaster-distress referral (call or text 1-800-985-5990, press 2 for Spanish, relay for ASL) and the 211 referral for practical needs.
