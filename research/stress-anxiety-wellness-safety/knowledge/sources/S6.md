## Learned points
1. Purpose and format: a WHO stress-management self-help guide for coping with adversity, "for anyone who experiences stress"; a few minutes a day of practice is described as enough; usable alone or with accompanying audio exercises. It is a component of WHO's Self-Help Plus (SH+) course, in the WHO Series on Low-Intensity Psychological Interventions; content created by Russ Harris (ACT author, *The Illustrated Happiness Trap*). — Documented
2. Evidence base: described as "informed by available evidence and extensive field testing" (field tests in Syria, Turkey, Uganda, Ukraine). Cites a cluster randomised trial of guided self-help (SH+) for South Sudanese female refugees in Uganda (Tol et al., Lancet Global Health 2020). The guide itself reports no effect sizes. — Documented (citation only; results not stated in guide)
3. Definitions: "Stress" = "feeling troubled or threatened by life." A little stress is not a problem; very high stress often affects the body (headaches, neck/back pain, heavy chest, stomach upset, tight muscles, poor appetite) and behavior/mood (can't focus, anger, sadness/guilt, worry, restlessness, poor sleep, crying, fatigue, appetite change). — Documented
4. Core model ("hooked"): difficult thoughts and feelings are a natural part of stress; problems arise when we get "hooked" by them and pulled away from our values, producing "away moves" (fights/arguments, withdrawing from loved ones, lying in bed a lot). "Towards moves" are actions in line with values. Values = "your deepest desires for the sort of person you want to be." — Documented
5. Tool 1 GROUNDING (for "emotional storms"), three steps: (1) NOTICE how you are feeling and what you are thinking; (2) SLOW DOWN and CONNECT with your body — slow your breathing, empty lungs completely then let them refill as slowly as possible, slowly press feet into floor, slowly stretch arms or press hands together; (3) REFOCUS and ENGAGE with the world — five things you can see, three or four things you can hear, what you can smell, touch your knees/surface/an object, notice where you are, who is with you and what you are doing. Short version takes ~30 seconds, "anytime, anywhere." — Documented
6. Breathing safety instructions (longer grounding exercise): breathe SLOWLY and GENTLY; breathing too hard or too quickly "could make you feel panicky, anxious, dizzy or short of breath." If dizzy, light-headed or tight-chested, slow breathing, breathe in "as SLOWLY and SHALLOWLY as possible" and explicitly do NOT take deep breaths. After exhaling fully, silently count to three before inhaling; refocus on breath each time attention drifts ("if your thoughts and feelings hook you 1000 times … we unhook ourselves 1000 times"). Suggested practice: 1–2 minute and 5–10 minute sessions. — Documented
7. Grounding's stated purpose is NOT to remove feelings: it keeps you safe until the storm passes and helps you engage in life, focus, and act on values. The guide explicitly says unhooking/grounding exercises are not meant to get rid of difficult thoughts and feelings. — Documented
8. Tool 2 UNHOOKING, three steps: (1) NOTICE a difficult thought/feeling has hooked you; (2) NAME it silently ("Here is a painful memory," "Here is anger," "Here is tightness in my chest"); adding "I notice…" ("I notice here is a heaviness in my chest") usually unhooks a bit more; (3) REFOCUS on what you are doing and engage fully with whoever is with you. Practise repeatedly through the day. — Documented
9. Common control strategies the guide flags as backfiring (they give short relief, then thoughts "soon come back" and pull you from values): yelling, trying not to think about it, staying in bed, isolating, avoiding people/places/situations, alcohol, tobacco, illicit drugs, giving up, starting arguments, self-blame/criticism. Alternative: stop struggling with thoughts and unhook. — Documented
10. Tool 3 ACTING ON YOUR VALUES: values ≠ goals ("Goals describe what you are trying to get. Values describe the sort of person you want to be"); goals may be impossible now, but values can always be acted on via small ACTIONS (what you do with hands/feet, what you say). Example values list: kind, caring, generous, supportive, helpful, patient, responsible, protective, disciplined, hardworking, committed, brave, persistent, forgiving, grateful, loyal, respectful, trustworthy, fair/just. Values apply to self and others ("If you do not take care of yourself… it will be hard … to care for others"). — Documented
11. Values action-plan method: pick an important relationship (someone you see regularly) → choose values for it → choose one or more concrete actions for the next week (what you'll say/do) → check if it seems realistic; if not, pick something smaller and easier. Expect difficult thoughts to show up when planning action ("It is too hard!", "There is no point!") and unhook from them. Start each day thinking of 2–3 values. Role-model exercise: identify values of someone you admire. — Documented
12. Three approaches to any difficult situation: 1. LEAVE; 2. CHANGE WHAT CAN BE CHANGED, ACCEPT THE PAIN THAT CANNOT BE CHANGED, AND LIVE BY YOUR VALUES; 3. GIVE UP AND MOVE AWAY FROM YOUR VALUES. Leaving is not always possible (e.g., prison, or leaving is worse than staying). — Documented
13. Tool 4 BEING KIND: unkind self-judgements ("I am weak," "It is my fault," "I can never forgive myself") are natural but hooking; notice and name them ("I notice here is a harsh self-judgement"), then ground. Speak to yourself kindly ("This is hard. This hurts."). Kind-hand exercise: imagine filling a hand with kindness, place it on heart/belly/forehead or where pain is, feel warmth, be kind to yourself through it. Small acts of kindness to others ("This is really painful. I am here for you.") matter. — Documented
14. Tool 5 MAKING ROOM: thoughts/feelings are like weather and you can be like the sky that has room for it. Steps: (1) NOTICE the thought/feeling with curiosity, imagine a painful feeling as an object with size, shape, colour, temperature; (2) NAME it; (3) allow it to come and go — breathe out slowly, pause for a count of three, breathe in slowly imagining breath flowing into and around the pain; imagine a difficult thought as words on a book page resting on your lap; then re-engage with the world. Aim is to "stop fighting it and give it space," not to make pain go away. — Documented
15. Sleep tip: when lying awake, practise slowing down/connecting with the body (and Making Room / kind hand); the guide says it "probably will not make you sleep (although sometimes it does), but it will help you feel rested." — Documented
16. Practice guidance: read one section every few days; take "at least a day or two" practising each section before moving on; make a plan for when, where, how often to practise; even 1–2 minutes counts; expect barriers (too tired, too sad, "This is useless!") and unhook from them; "Do not simply read about it; actually do it!" — Documented
17. Escalation/referral: if the exercises are not enough to cope with intolerable stress, seek help from relevant health or social services or trusted people in the community. The guide contains no crisis-line numbers or suicide-specific guidance. — Documented
18. Implication for a bot: the guide's step scripts (Ground: Notice → Slow down/Connect → Refocus; Unhook: Notice → Name → Refocus; Make Room: Notice → Name → Allow/breathe around) are short, non-clinical, plain-language and can be offered as self-help skills, framed as aids to engagement rather than symptom elimination, and paired with a referral line. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- Written for a global, often humanitarian/low-resource audience (refugees, conflict); examples (war, displacement, prison) may need adapting for US adults in career contexts.
- Self-labelled "field test version"; evidence is cited (one RCT reference) but no outcome data are given in the guide.
- Grounded in one therapeutic school (ACT, via Russ Harris); does not teach cognitive restructuring, which differs from NIMH's "identify and challenge" advice (S7).
- No crisis or suicide protocol, no US resources; must be paired with S8/S10 for safety handling.
- Text extracted from an illustrated PDF; some speech-bubble wording is fragmentary, so quotes were limited to clean running text.
