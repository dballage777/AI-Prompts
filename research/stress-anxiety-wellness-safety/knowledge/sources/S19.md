## Learned points
1. NHS describes a calming breathing technique "for stress, anxiety and panic" that takes a few minutes, can be done anywhere, and gives most benefit when done regularly as part of a daily routine. — Documented
2. Position: standing, sitting in a chair that supports your back, or lying on a bed or yoga mat; make yourself as comfortable as possible and loosen clothes that restrict breathing if you can. — Documented
3. Lying down: arms a little away from your sides, palms up; legs straight or knees bent with feet flat on the floor. Sitting: arms on the chair arms. Sitting or standing: both feet flat on the ground. Any position: feet roughly hip-width apart. — Documented
4. Exact steps: (1) Let your breath flow as deep down into your belly as is comfortable, without forcing it. (2) Try breathing in through your nose and out through your mouth. (3) Breathe in gently and regularly; some people find it helpful to count steadily from 1 to 5 (you may not reach 5 at first). (4) Let it flow out gently, counting from 1 to 5 again if helpful. (5) Repeat for at least 5 minutes. — Documented
5. Notable safety/tone features: "without forcing it" and "You may not be able to reach 5 at first" — the method is gentle and non-perfectionistic; no breath-holding is included. A bot should reproduce it as written rather than adding breath holds or fast breathing. — Documented (implication Inferred)
6. 10 stress busters, first step: identify the cause of stress; the most unhelpful thing is turning to unhealthy coping such as smoking or drinking; feeling no control makes stress worse. — Documented
7. Keys to stress management per NHS: building emotional strength, taking control of what you can, a good social network, and a positive outlook. — Documented
8. The 10 stress busters: be active; take control; connect with people; have some "me time"; challenge yourself (new goals or skills); avoid unhealthy habits (alcohol, smoking, caffeine as crutches); help other people; work smarter, not harder; try to be positive; accept the things you cannot change. — Documented
9. Work-relevant tips: "Working smarter" means prioritising tasks that make a real difference, leaving least important tasks to last, and accepting you won't have time for everything; if a company is making redundancies, focus on what you can control, such as looking for a new job. Directly usable by a career-focused bot. — Documented
10. Gratitude practice: write down 3 things that went well, or for which you're grateful, at the end of every day. — Documented
11. Exercise "will not make your stress disappear" but can reduce emotional intensity and help you deal with problems more calmly — a calibrated, non-overclaiming framing. — Documented
12. 5 steps to mental wellbeing (NHS says "evidence suggests"): connect with other people; be physically active; learn new skills; help other people (giving/kindness); pay attention to the present moment (mindfulness). — Documented
13. Connection caution: do not rely on technology or social media alone to build relationships. Implication for a bot: encourage real human connection rather than reliance on the bot. — Documented (implication Inferred)
14. Learning-new-skills examples include taking on new responsibility at work (mentoring, presentation skills) — useful bridge between career coaching and wellbeing. — Documented
