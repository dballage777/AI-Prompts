## Learned points
1. What 988 is: a national hotline offering one-on-one support for mental health, suicide and substance use-related problems, for anyone, 24/7. Reach it by calling or texting 988 or chatting at 988lifeline.org (chat.988lifeline.org; Spanish chat at chat.988lifeline.org/?lang=es). Free: no payment or insurance information required (standard carrier text/data rates may apply). — Documented
2. Not only for suicide: reasons to contact include drug/alcohol problems, emotional distress, anxiety, depression, mental illness, loneliness, trauma, bullying, stress, relationship troubles, "and just needing someone to talk with." "There is no reason too big or too small." People can also contact 988 about a loved one they are worried about. — Documented
3. Languages and access: call, chat and text in English and Spanish; phone interpretation in more than 240 additional languages at no cost (Language Line Solutions). Deaf, DeafBlind, DeafDisabled, Hard of Hearing and Late-Deafened callers can dial 988 on a videophone to reach counselors who communicate in ASL. Available in all states and five territories and on tribal lands where phone/cell/internet service exists. — Documented
4. What happens when you CALL: greeting message, then a menu to choose the Veterans Crisis Line (Veterans, service members and families; press 1) or Spanish; otherwise routed to a local 988 crisis center by approximate location or area code; dial 0 to bypass menu. Hold music until a counselor answers. Counselor introduces themselves, asks about your safety, listens, works to understand how the problem affects you, gives judgment-free support and shares resources. Unanswered local calls roll over to a national backup center. — Documented
5. What happens when you TEXT: prompts first offer Veterans Crisis Line (text 838255 directly) or Spanish (enter AYUDA); reply NEXT to continue; you answer questions to assess needs while waiting; the counselor then assesses safety, listens, supports and shares resources. — Documented
6. What happens when you CHAT: a brief pre-chat survey (under five minutes), click "Start a chat," then a counselor introduces themselves, asks about safety, listens, supports and shares resources. — Documented
7. Wait time: at FAQ publication, average answer time after the greeting menu was "typically under a minute"; may be longer — "please stay on the line." Wait varies by center and time of day. — Documented
8. Effectiveness: SAMHSA states "Numerous studies have shown" most callers are significantly more likely to feel less depressed, less suicidal, less overwhelmed and more hopeful after talking with a counselor; most contacts are resolved by the counselor, resources or community connections without involving 911. Specific studies are not cited on the page. — Reported
9. 988 vs. 911: 988 provides emotional support, de-escalation and links to local resources without law enforcement or EMS "unless necessary." 911 is for medical emergencies, fire, crimes in progress or situations needing immediate physical intervention. A "small percentage" of 988 contacts require 911, many with the caller's consent, when there is imminent risk to life that cannot be reduced during the contact. — Documented
10. Situations where SAMHSA says calling 911 is appropriate: a suicide attempt in progress; a person has a specific suicide or self-harm plan they are or intend to immediately carry out and has the means; suspected overdose in progress; physical symptoms such as chest pain or shortness of breath that could be a panic attack or an acute medical emergency. SAMHSA encourages reaching out to 988 early, before reaching that point. — Documented
11. Hospitalization: all centers follow the 988 Lifeline Suicide Safety Policy — support in "the least restrictive setting possible"; most needs are resolved in the contact; mobile crisis teams can often help without hospitalization. Involuntary hospitalization "may be needed in circumstances of imminent risk" where other efforts fail. — Documented
12. Privacy: counselors see your phone number (call) or IP address (chat) but not who or where you are; no personal information is required. Calls "may be monitored or recorded for quality assurance"; centers may use recordings for training. Data is not sold (administrator Vibrant Emotional Health is non-profit). In rare imminent-risk cases, 911 may be asked to do a precise geolocation lookup via the carrier/ISP; 988 itself does not use geolocation. — Documented
13. Georouting: calls are routed to a nearby center by general location (not pinpoint) where carriers have implemented georouting; otherwise by area code + prefix. FCC rules: nationwide carriers have implemented call georouting; non-nationwide carriers' deadline for calls is Dec. 14, 2026; text georouting deadlines are April 16, 2027 (nationwide) and Oct. 16, 2028 (non-nationwide). — Documented
14. Legacy number: the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline (1-800-273-8255) was relaunched as 988 in July 2022 and the 10-digit number still works. Congress designated 988 in 2020. — Documented
15. 988 vs. 211: 211 generally provides health and social-service information and referrals; 988 handles suicidal crisis and mental health distress; many local 988 centers also answer 211. — Documented
16. Network facts: over 200 locally operated crisis centers; funded by SAMHSA, administered by Vibrant Emotional Health since it began as the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline in 2005; national sub-networks for veterans, Spanish speakers, and Deaf/Hard-of-Hearing people. HHS has invested nearly $1.5 billion in 988 capacity. Counselors follow network standards for Suicide Risk Assessment and Imminent Risk interventions and take 988 Lifeline Core Clinical Training. — Documented
17. Mobile crisis teams (where available; availability varies by location): triage including explicit suicidality screening, assessment, de-escalation, peer support, coordination with follow-up care, creating a safety plan, and connecting to community support. — Documented
18. Data cited by SAMHSA: in 2021 and 2022 the U.S. had one death by suicide about every 11 minutes; suicide was the second-leading cause of death for ages 10–14 and 25–34; more than 950,000 youth (12–17) and 1.6 million adults attempted suicide; about 107,000 people died from drug overdoses. (Hub page separately states one suicide death every 11 minutes in 2020.) — Documented
19. Implication for a bot: describe 988 accurately as free, 24/7, call/text/chat, for any distress (not only suicide), usable for a loved one, with Spanish, interpretation, ASL videophone and veteran options; set honest expectations (counselor asks about safety, may be recorded, most contacts don't involve 911); reserve "call 911" for the specific imminent-danger situations listed. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- Promotional/advocacy framing by the agency that funds 988; effectiveness claims cite "numerous studies" without references.
- Performance-metrics figures (answer rates, wait times) were not captured because the dashboard is script-rendered.
- FAQ wait-time figure is "at the time of publishing"; menu options and routing details can change — re-verify before quoting in a prompt.
- Page is aimed partly at states/partners; some content (funding, grants) is not user-facing.

## Best for
