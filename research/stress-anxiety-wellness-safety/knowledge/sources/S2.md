## Learned points
1. Three ways to reach the line: call 988, text 988, or chat at chat.988lifeline.org. The service is available 24/7/365, and conversations are "free and confidential" (homepage). — Documented (summary-only)
2. Who can use it: anyone "depressed, going through a hard time, needs to talk, or is thinking about suicide" (chat FAQ). A person can also contact 988 to get help for a friend or loved one. The user does not need to be suicidal. — Documented (summary-only)
3. Call flow: (1) a greeting with menu options: Press 1 for the Veterans Crisis Line, Press 2 for Spanish, Press 3 for LGBTQ+ youth, or stay on for the local 988 contact center; (2) hold music while connecting to a live counselor; (3) the counselor introduces themselves and asks if you are safe; (4) the counselor listens, works to understand how the problem is affecting you, offers support, and may share resources. — Documented (summary-only)
4. Text flow: send any message to 988 to start. Text AYUDA for Spanish or PRIDE for LGBTQ+ youth. Veterans and service members can text 838255 for the Veterans Crisis Line. Replying "Next" accepts the Terms of Service. A brief survey follows, then a "Wait" message, then a counselor who asks if you are safe and offers support and resources. — Documented (summary-only)
5. Chat flow: click Chat and answer a few short pre-chat questions. Clicking "Start a Chat" accepts the Terms of Service. You are then connected to a live counselor who asks about safety. The stated goal is to help you "reduce stress and feel empowered to make healthy decisions." If chat cannot connect, the site suggests calling 988. — Documented (summary-only)
6. Spanish: text and chat services are available in Spanish, and callers can press 2 for a trained Spanish-speaking crisis counselor. A Spanish homepage exists at /es/inicio/. The site also lists an Interpretation Services page, which I did not read. — Documented (summary-only)
7. LGBTQ+ youth: the homepage footer says the LGBTQ+ youth specialized service ("Press 3 option") "has been restored." That implies it was suspended for a period. The page gives no dates or reasons. — Documented / Inferred
8. Deaf and hard of hearing: ASL by calling 988 from a videophone (988 Videophone). TTY users can use their preferred relay service or dial 711, then 988. Text and chat also work. Users do not have to disclose that they are deaf. Veterans with hearing loss can text 838255 or use the Veterans Crisis Line chat. — Documented (summary-only)
9. Routing: the network "covers all areas of the country, even if there is no local crisis center." Calls route to a local contact center using "your general location," and each center's area depends on its reported capacity. Calls to the old 1-800-SUICIDE number have gone through the Lifeline network since March 2007 and now route to the Veterans Crisis Line (FAQ). I could not retrieve the exact georouting mechanism or wait-time figures; those answers were truncated. — Documented (summary-only)
10. Confidentiality: data sharing is restricted, but information may be shared when "there is imminent risk of harm to you or to someone else," when required by law, or with informed consent. Counselor notes may document "connections to emergency services." States and centers may use identifiable information to coordinate follow-up care. Users can ask their counselor not to share personal information. Records are kept at least 3 years. Confidential is not the same as anonymous. — Documented (summary-only)
11. Data collected: general location for routing, IP address, and device characteristics. Some network centers may independently record calls (calling FAQ). — Documented (summary-only)
12. Cost: using 988 is free (What to Expect). Whether 988 shows on a phone bill "depends on your phone carrier." — Documented (summary-only)
13. Helping someone else: 5 "evidence-based action steps": Ask; Be there; Help keep them safe; Help them connect; Follow up. Warning signs matter most when behavior is "new, has increased, or seems related to a painful event, loss, or change." "Never keep it a secret if a friend tells you about a plan to hurt themselves." — Documented (summary-only)
14. 911: the pages I retrieved did not state when to call 911 or when 988 sends emergency services. That boundary is UNVERIFIED from this source. — Documented gap
15. Bot implications (my inference): when the bot gives 988, it should list call, text, and chat; mention Spanish (press 2 or text AYUDA), Veterans (press 1 or text 838255), LGBTQ+ youth (press 3 or text PRIDE), and TTY or videophone access; explain that a trained counselor will ask about safety and listen, and that the service is free and confidential with limits; and say that 988 also helps people worried about someone else. — Inferred
