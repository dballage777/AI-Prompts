## Learned points
1. Definition: CBT is "a time-sensitive, structured, present-oriented psychotherapy" that the institute says has been found effective in more than 2,000 studies for many health and mental health conditions. — Documented
2. CBT rests on Aaron T. Beck's Cognitive Model: how a person perceives a situation is more closely connected to their reaction than the situation itself. — Documented
3. The cognitive model describes how thoughts and perceptions influence feelings and behaviour; it is the core of CBT and of how therapists conceptualize clients' difficulties. — Documented
4. Core method: trained therapists help clients identify distressing thoughts and evaluate how realistic they are; as clients become aware of and evaluate their thoughts, "they feel better". — Documented
5. CBT also includes problem-solving, learning new skills, and setting and achieving meaningful goals; clients are taught to evaluate thoughts and practise skills on their own outside therapy. — Documented
6. The Beck Institute's contemporary approach emphasizes clients' strengths and values, prioritizes a strong therapeutic relationship, and adapts treatment to each client's background, culture, experiences and preferences. — Documented
7. Treatment is guided by an individual formulation (case conceptualization), used for the relationship, goals, treatment plan and choice of interventions. — Documented
8. Fourteen tenets of good CBT: evolving cognitive conceptualization; sound therapeutic relationship; continual progress monitoring; cultural adaptation and individual tailoring; emphasizes the positive; collaboration and active participation; aspirational, values-based and goal-oriented; initially emphasizes the present; educative; time-sensitive; structured sessions; guided discovery to respond to dysfunctional cognitions; action plans (therapy homework); variety of techniques to change thinking, mood and behaviour. — Documented
9. Evidence claim: "More than 2,000 studies have demonstrated the efficacy of CBT for psychiatric disorders, psychological problems and medical problems with a psychiatric component." No individual studies or effect sizes are given on the page. — Reported (self-reported by the institute)
10. Assessment tools named: Beck Depression Scale, Beck Hopelessness Scale, Personality Belief Questionnaire (for practitioners). — Documented
11. Resources-for-individuals page frames CBT as evaluating and shifting unhelpful patterns of thoughts and behaviours, noting "Small changes in thinking can lead to meaningful improvements in wellbeing"; it offers pamphlets, a non-professional newsletter ("Thinking Well"), a certified-clinician directory, and "Cognitive Behavioral Wellness Coaching". — Documented
12. Implication for a self-help bot: CBT as described is delivered by trained therapists within a relationship, with formulation and monitoring; a bot can explain the cognitive model and invite thought-evaluation but is not delivering CBT treatment. — Inferred
13. The page does not list specific conditions, step-by-step thought-record instructions, or self-help exercises. — Documented (absence)

## Limitations / bias
- Promotional institutional page from an organization that sells CBT training, certification, therapy and coaching; the "2,000 studies" claim is uncited on the page.
- Overview only: no step-by-step techniques, no condition-specific guidance, no safety/crisis guidance.
- Describes therapist-delivered CBT, not self-help or AI delivery.
