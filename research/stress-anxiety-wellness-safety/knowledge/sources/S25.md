## Learned points
1. How to reach (US): text HOME to 741741, "24/7, free, confidential". Spanish: text HOLA to 741741. The FAQ says the first message "can say anything"; keywords like HOME, START or HELLO just tell them how you found them. — Documented
2. Other channels: web chat (connect.crisistextline.org/chat, linked as "Chat With Us") and WhatsApp — 443-SUP-PORT (443-787-7678) for English or 442-AYUDAME for Spanish. Not available on Google Voice or TextNow; short codes are blocked on many T-Mobile prepaid plans, so use WhatsApp or web chat. — Documented
3. Who answers: live, trained volunteer Crisis Counselors, supervised by full-time staff with Master's degrees in a relevant field or equivalent crisis-intervention experience. Volunteers complete a 15-hour training (active listening, reflecting, anxiety, stress, self-harm, suicide, depression, bullying, LGBTQ+ issues) and commit 4 hours/week to 200 hours. — Documented
4. Counselors are "not a medical professional and can't provide medical advice". — Documented
5. What happens step by step: you text in, get a couple of automated messages (one asks what's going on), then a counselor usually replies within five minutes (longer at busy times such as late night); they listen, ask questions, offer non-judgmental support, and help you create a plan to feel safer; the conversation ends when you feel safe and calmer, usually 15–45 minutes. — Documented
6. Stated goal: help the texter "move from a hot moment to a cool calm" and use their own strengths and coping strategies. Topics include stress, anxiety, loneliness, bullying, substance use, self-harm and suicidal thoughts. — Documented
7. Practical: free on AT&T, T-Mobile, Sprint and Verizon (nothing appears on the bill); other carriers charge standard text rates and 741741 may appear on the bill. Messages are limited to 160 characters. Text STOP to end a conversation. — Documented
8. Not a substitute for ongoing care: you can text again, but "you should not feel dependent on us"; it is not a replacement for long-term counseling or in-person therapy. — Documented
9. Imminent risk: if a texter is in immediate danger of suicide or homicide (per counselor plus supervisor risk assessment), the first step is to build a safety plan together; if the texter cannot plan for their own safety, a supervisor may start an Emergency Service Intervention (ESI), which may dispatch a wellness check. — Documented
10. Privacy policy says saying "I'm feeling suicidal" alone does not necessarily trigger emergency services; sharing with emergency services may happen if messages show intent, desire and ability, a plan to act soon, and inability or unwillingness to de-escalate. They must also report child (under 18) or dependent/elderly adult abuse or neglect as required by law. — Documented
11. Data: conversations are recorded; they may receive phone number, carrier, IP address; PII kept up to seven years, then anonymized or deleted; anonymized data may be shared with researchers and others "for any reason"; automated processes may triage the queue by urgency. Text DELETE to request deletion of PII and transcripts. WhatsApp use also falls under WhatsApp's policies. — Documented
12. Privacy policy notes: "If you are in danger or need emergency help, call 911"; CTL itself does not counsel outside the US — partner services in Canada (text 686868), UK (85258) and Ireland (50808) are listed. — Documented
13. Accreditation: received International Council for Helplines (ICH) accreditation in 2024. — Reported (self-reported)
14. Past controversy disclosed in the FAQ: a 2018–2022 relationship with for-profit Loris.ai (customer-service empathy training) that CTL exited, requiring data return/deletion; CTL states it never shared personally identifiable data with Loris.ai and has never monetized PII. — Documented (self-reported)
15. Homepage impact claims mention supporting "over 11 million" US conversations and "over 350 million Messages"; the exact percentages and counselor totals were in dynamic counters that did not load. — summary-only / PARTIAL for this item
16. For a wellness bot: CTL is a good text-based option for users who prefer not to call; tell users plainly what to expect (automated messages first, a volunteer within minutes, 160-character limit, not medical advice, emergency services possible only in imminent danger). — Inferred
