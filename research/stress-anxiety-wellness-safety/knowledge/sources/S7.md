## Learned points
1. Definition of stress: "the physical or mental response to an external cause," e.g., lots of homework or an illness; a stressor can be one-time/short-term or repeated over a long time. Everyone feels stress from time to time. — Documented
2. Definition of anxiety: "your body's reaction to stress and can occur even if there is no current threat." — Documented
3. Stress vs. anxiety comparison: stress is generally a response to an external cause (big test, argument), goes away once the situation is resolved, and can be positive (meet a deadline) or negative (lose sleep). Anxiety is generally internal (your reaction to stress), usually a persistent apprehension or dread that doesn't go away and interferes with life, and is constant even without an immediate threat. — Documented
4. Shared symptoms of stress and anxiety (mind and body): excessive worry, uneasiness, tension, headaches or body pain, high blood pressure, loss of sleep. — Documented
5. Health consequences: if anxiety doesn't go away and interferes with life, it could affect sleep and the immune, digestive, cardiovascular and reproductive systems, and raise risk of a mental illness such as an anxiety disorder or depression. — Documented
6. Warning signs of possible anxiety disorder: you may be at risk if it feels like you can't manage the stress and symptoms (a) interfere with everyday life, (b) cause you to avoid doing things, (c) seem to be always present. — Documented
7. Self-help coping list (it "may take trial and error" to find what works): keep a journal; use an app with relaxation exercises (deep breathing, visualization) or mindfulness tips; exercise and eat healthy, regular meals; keep a sleep routine and get enough sleep; avoid excess caffeine (soft drinks, coffee); identify and challenge negative and unhelpful thoughts; reach out to friends/family who help you cope positively. Learning your triggers is also recommended. — Documented
8. Mindfulness is defined as "a psychological process of actively paying attention to the present moment." — Documented
9. When to escalate to a professional: if you are struggling to cope or stress/anxiety symptoms won't go away. Psychotherapy (talk therapy) and medication are named as the two main treatments for anxiety; many benefit from a combination. — Documented
10. Crisis instruction: if in immediate distress or thinking about hurting yourself, call or text 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline at 988 or chat at 988lifeline.org. — Documented
11. Audience tilt: examples (homework, school performance, big test) and related resources (child and adolescent mental health) suggest a youth/student audience, though content applies generally. — Inferred
12. Note a tension with S6: NIMH lists "deep breathing" apps, whereas WHO's guide warns against deep breaths when dizzy and stresses slow, gentle breathing. A bot should favor slow, gentle breathing and stop if dizzy. — Inferred
13. Implication for a bot: the three "at risk" criteria (interferes, causes avoidance, always present) plus "won't go away" form a simple, non-diagnostic trigger for suggesting a professional, without labeling the user. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- Short consumer fact sheet: no evidence citations, no numbers, no step-by-step methods (e.g., how to "challenge" thoughts).
- Examples skew toward students/young people; adapt for working adults.
- No review date shown on the web page (only publication number).

## Best for
- Plain-language stress vs. anxiety distinction, a simple self-care checklist, and clear "time to see a professional / call 988" triggers.
