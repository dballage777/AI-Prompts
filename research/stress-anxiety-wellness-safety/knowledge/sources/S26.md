## Learned points
1. VA sorts its apps into three groups: Self-Help (PTSD Coach, PTSD Family Coach, Beyond MST, Coping Coach, Mindfulness Coach, VetChange, AIMS, Veterans Wellness Path); Treatment Companions, for use with a provider (CPT Coach, PE Coach, CBT-i Coach, Strength at Home, ACT Coach, STAIR Coach); and Related (Safety Plan, Pain Coach, WellWithin Coach, Insomnia Coach, Concussion Coach, Couples Coach, StayQuit Coach). — [Documented]
2. Every app page includes a disclaimer that the app does not replace professional care (for example, "These apps are not intended to replace needed professional care"). A bot that recommends these apps should repeat that framing. — [Documented]
3. The apps are free and publicly available on iOS and Android. They are not limited to Veterans: Coping Coach "was created for everyone." PTSD Coach is for "anyone who wants to learn about and manage symptoms after a trauma." — [Documented]
4. PTSD Coach offers psychoeducation on PTSD and treatments that work, symptom screening and tracking with the PTSD Checklist (PCL), stress-management tools, and direct links to support. The PCL is called reliable and valid "but it is not intended to replace professional evaluation." — [Documented]
5. PTSD Coach has been downloaded more than 1 million times in 115 countries. PTSD Coach Online is a desktop version with 17 stress-management tools covering sleep, trauma reminders, and anger, with video coaches. — [Reported]
6. Mindfulness Coach defines mindfulness as "grounding yourself in the present moment." It says mindfulness "has been shown to be helpful for reducing stress and coping with unpleasant thoughts and emotions." It offers a gradual, self-guided training program, guided exercises, strategies for barriers, a practice log, and reminders. It can be used alone or alongside face-to-face care. — [Documented]
7. CBT-i Coach supports Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I), which the page calls an evidence-based psychotherapy. The app is meant for use with a provider: "It is not meant to be used for self-care." For self-managed sleep problems, the page points to a separate app, Insomnia Coach. A bot should recommend Insomnia Coach for self-help, not CBT-i Coach. — [Documented]
8. CBT-i Coach features include sleep-routine and sleep-environment tips, relaxation tools, guidance on what to do when you can't sleep, a sleep diary, personalized feedback, and bedtime and wake reminders. The page also states that insomnia "often requires professional evaluation and treatment." — [Documented]
9. The Safety Plan app is for "anyone who has experienced thoughts about suicide or self-harm." It lists six steps: (1) Warning Signs; (2) Ways I Can Cope; (3) My Distractions (places and people); (4) Friends and Family I Can Call; (5) Professionals I Can Call, including crisis resources; (6) Keeping Myself Safe (make your space safer, remove risk, share the plan). — [Documented]
10. The Safety Plan page advises making the plan while you are not in distress, "so you can think clearly." It says building the plan with a mental health provider "can be helpful." Other features include reasons-to-live lists with photo and video, coping tools, mood tracking, goals, and links to the Veterans Crisis Line. — [Documented]
11. Coping Coach covers stress education, self-care and stress tools, mood and goal trackers, progress graphs, and resources for basic needs and for finding professional help. It can be used on its own or alongside professional care, and it helps users build a personal support network. — [Documented]
12. Privacy policy for VA mental health apps: no identifying data is sent to VA or third parties, and anything you enter cannot be accessed by VA. Anonymous usage data is collected for research, and users can turn it off in Settings ("Anonymous Usage Data"). VA does not currently support data deletion requests. — [Documented]
13. Self-monitoring data belongs to the user. HIPAA does not apply while the data stays on the device or is shared by the user, but it does apply once a provider receives it. Users are advised to set a device passcode. — [Documented]
14. Every page footer shows the Veterans Crisis Line: call 988 (press 1), text 838255, chat, and TTY 1-800-799-4889. — [Documented]
15. Inferred for a wellness bot: these apps are credible, free, government-made tools to suggest for stress (Coping Coach), present-moment skills (Mindfulness Coach), self-managed sleep problems (Insomnia Coach), and crisis preparation (Safety Plan). Always pair the suggestion with the "not a replacement for care" message. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- These are product pages written by the developer, the VA. The efficacy claims are brief and cite no studies.
- The focus is on PTSD and Veterans. The general-population stress content is thin.
- The Insomnia Coach page itself was not opened. It is mentioned here only as described on the CBT-i Coach page.
- Download counts are self-reported.

## Best for
- Recommending free, credible government self-help apps, and copying the six-step safety-plan structure plus the "not a substitute for care" framing.
