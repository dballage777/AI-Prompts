## Learned points
1. Legal basis: EO 13932 (June 26, 2020), EO 14170 (January 30, 2025) and the Merit Hiring Plan (May 29, 2025), which "prohibits the current longform Federal resume structure" and sets a two-page limit. — [Documented]
2. Scope: applies to all competitive service and excepted service announcements under Title 5, internal and external, posted on USAJOBS or another platform. — [Documented]
3. Effective date: starting September 27, 2025, USAJOBS restricts all resumes to two pages — stored profile resumes, new uploads, resume-builder resumes, and searchable resumes in the Agency Talent Portal (ATP). Transition period ended September 27, 2025. — [Documented]
4. Consequence of a longer resume: if the only resume received is longer than two pages, the applicant is ineligible for further consideration and gets the status message "Your application is not being considered due to not meeting the resume page number requirement in the job announcement." If both a two-page and a longer resume are submitted, only the two-page one is reviewed. — [Documented]
5. What counts as a page: the document does not define a "page" beyond the two-page total; it says resumes "must be legible" and that applicants should receive recommended font size, font type and margins. No minimum font size is mandated. — [Documented]
6. USAJOBS Help Center formatting advice (quoted in the guidance): file must be 5MB or less; PDF recommended "to maintain formatting and number of pages"; also accepts GIF, JPG, JPEG, PNG, RTF, TXT, PDF, ODT, DOC, DOCX; PDF portfolio files not accepted. — [Documented]
7. Recommended fonts/layout: sans-serif such as Lato; alternatives Calibri, Helvetica, Arial, Verdana, Source Sans Pro, Roboto, Noto Sans (the PDF prints typos "Open San Source Sans Pro" and "Noro Sans"); 0.5-inch margins; consider 14-point for titles and 10-point for main text. — [Documented]
8. USAJOBS resume builder applies these recommendations and pre-fills from the USAJOBS profile. — [Documented]
9. Exceptions: non-Title 5, judicial and legislative branch agencies may accept longer resumes only if the announcement explains how to submit them via "other documents"; positions requiring a CV (typically medical and research) may request the CV as an "other document". — [Documented]
10. Required content per relevant job: job title and employer (with series and grade for federal positions); start and end dates in month/year (e.g., 05/2019 – 08/2022); hours worked per week. Descriptions should align with the announcement and address all required qualifications. — [Documented]
11. Contact info: name, email, phone. Education (when relevant/required): school, completion date, degree type, cumulative GPA; plus certification/licensure documentation such as transcripts. — [Documented]
12. Optional details when relevant: current security clearance, job-related training, language skills, organizations/affiliations, professional publications, eligibility for special programs (e.g., military spouses, individuals with disabilities). — [Documented]
13. "Helpful Hints": prioritize most relevant and recent experience; concise, results-focused language; align language from the job announcement; focus on demonstrating skills and competencies; remove outdated or unrelated experience. — [Documented]
14. Agencies must state the two-page rule in "How to Apply" and "Required Documents"; resumes directly uploaded into an agency Talent Acquisition System (TAS) need a TAS-provider technical solution. — [Documented]
15. For a resume bot: federal (USAJOBS, Title 5) resumes are now a distinct mode — hard 2-page cap, month/year dates, hours per week, series/grade, PDF output recommended — and the old "federal resumes are 3–5+ pages" advice is obsolete for Title 5 jobs. Always check the specific announcement for exceptions. — [Inferred]
