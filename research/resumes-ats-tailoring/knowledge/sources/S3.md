## Learned points
1. Framing: the authors encourage AI use in the job search (resumes, interview practice, LinkedIn, cover letters) but list eight common traps when students or educators over-rely on it. — [Documented]
2. Trap 1, lack of personalization: generic prompts yield generic output; AI may not capture the full scope of a person's skills and cannot judge which skill would impress a hiring manager more. Fix: give detailed, specific input (values, skills, experiences, pain points, goals); treat AI output as a first draft and refine critically. — [Documented]
3. Trap 2, loss of personal voice: AI-only materials are generic and lack the personal touch; AI struggles to tell a person's story authentically. Fix: develop your own narrative first, then use AI to refine; review and tweak outputs to reflect your voice; get feedback from mentors/peers. — [Documented]
4. Trap 3, bias: AI can inherit biases from training data, and output may be untrue or inaccurate; always critically evaluate and seek diverse human perspectives. — [Documented]
5. Trap 4, missed opportunities: AI relies on online data, so fields poorly represented online, niche/emerging roles, and jobs filled internally or by referral can be missed; human connections are what actually lead to jobs. — [Documented]
6. Trap 5, outdated industry knowledge: AI may not reflect current industry trends or job-market demands; stay current through industry news, events and people in the field. — [Documented]
7. Trap 6, privacy and data security: users may paste personal information without knowing how it will be used; the authors warn input data can be reused with no accountability and, for career educators, could raise FERPA issues. Fix: limit sensitive personal data, read the platform's data policy, prefer tools with strong privacy/security. — [Documented]
8. Trap 7, lost personal development: over-reliance on AI for resume writing or interview prep deprives job seekers of learning and growth; AI content "often seems complete" but must be critically analyzed and refined. — [Documented]
9. Trap 8, AI making career decisions: do not depend on AI for choices of role or path without checking fit with personal interests, values and long-term goals; process AI suggestions with a career educator or ally. — [Documented]
10. Overall stance: career education is "deeply human-centered"; AI is a support tool, not a replacement for judgment, relationships and self-assessment. — [Documented]
11. Implication for a resume bot: ask for rich, specific user input before drafting; keep the user's voice; flag accuracy and currency limits; minimize collection of sensitive personal data; route big career decisions back to the user and human advisers. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- Opinion/best-practice piece with no data; one endnote (a Dice blog). Written for college students and career educators.
- Its claim that data put into AI platforms becomes "public" and accessible to anyone is broader than platform policies generally state; treat as a caution, not a technical fact.
- 2024 vantage point; does not address ATS mechanics, keyword stuffing, or auto-apply tools specifically.
## Best for
- The bot's "how to use AI well" guardrails: gather specific input first, preserve the user's voice, privacy-minimizing data handling, and leaving decisions to the user.
