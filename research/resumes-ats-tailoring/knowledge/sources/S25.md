## Learned points
1. Methodology (as stated): responses from 2,200 active job seekers across the US, UK and Ireland; no fieldwork dates, sampling method or margin of error on the page. — [Documented]
2. Market sentiment: just 7% of candidates believe the market favors them; 66% feel intense pressure; 45% of Gen Z say it is harder than ever to stand out. — [Research]
3. 72% of candidates report the job they applied for turned out different from what was offered (bait-and-switch listings). — [Research]
4. 80% of US workers feel insecure in current roles; 28% of workers face some form of employment uncertainty. — [Research]
5. AI use: 45% use AI to prepare for interviews; 22% use bots to apply to roles automatically (31% of Gen Z); 31% say AI tools have helped their job search. — [Research]
6. AI can backfire: 26% say AI has made it harder to stand out (45% among Gen Z) — mass/auto-applying and generic AI text do not guarantee visibility. — [Research] / [Inferred]
7. Fabrication is common: 28% admit using AI to generate fake work samples; 32% have claimed AI skills they don't actually have. — [Research]
8. Norms unclear: 27% have never seen an employer policy on AI use in hiring; 19% of US candidates say using AI in a live interview crosses the line; 21% of Gen Z say AI is fair game in any circumstance vs 11% of Boomers. — [Research]
9. Bias: 53% of US job seekers faced illegal or discriminatory questions; 61% of Boomers report age discrimination vs 11% of Gen Z. — [Research]
10. Resume responses to bias: 57% have removed older experience from resumes; 30% of underrepresented candidates changed their names to avoid bias. Relevant to age-proofing advice (trimming old roles is common and legitimate) — a bot may discuss it but must not alter facts or identity without the user's informed choice. — [Research] / [Inferred]
11. Greenhouse's framing: hiring is in an "AI doom loop"; "intent will be the new differentiator" — targeted, genuine applications over volume. — [Reported]
12. For a resume bot: do not offer auto-apply/spray-and-pray, never generate fake work samples or claim skills the user lacks, and treat removal of older experience as an optional, honest editing choice. — [Inferred]
