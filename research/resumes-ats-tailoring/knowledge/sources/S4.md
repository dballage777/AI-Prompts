## Learned points
1. General format checklist: use Microsoft Word; "Do not use a template; applicant tracking systems have trouble reading it." Margins consistent, 0.5–1.0 in; font 10–12 pt, easy to read (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman named). — [Documented]
2. Length: one page; two pages allowed with an advanced degree or extensive experience (10+ years). — [Documented]
3. Styling: enough white space; bold and italics used appropriately (headers, positions); avoid underlining; dates, format and punctuation consistent; sections in order of importance to the employer; descriptive headings (e.g., "Research Experience", "Leadership & Service"). — [Documented]
4. Contact info: legal name clear and bold at top (and on page 2); phone (with professional voicemail); professional-sounding email. If a U.S. citizen or permanent resident and readers might assume otherwise, consider stating it. — [Documented]
5. Education: spell out institution names (not acronyms); official degree name; month and year earned or expected; GPA optional if strong, with scale; coursework aligned to the search. — [Documented]
6. Experience: organization and title; city and state (or country); dates for each role; project, activity and results for each; start each phrase with an action verb, past tense for past roles and present tense for current; quantify (size, scale, budget, staff); use industry and job-listing keywords; no first-person pronouns. — [Documented]
7. Skills: include all relevant skill types (programming languages, foreign languages, lab skills) and the relevant skills within each. Activities/honors/leadership: list the relevant ones. — [Documented]
8. Framing: recruiters spend "just a few seconds on average" on a resume; "even minor errors can take you out of the running." — [Reported]
9. AI page, tailoring: give AI the resume plus job description and ask which experiences match the core competencies, which competencies are missing, and where relevant experience is undersold; then ask what keywords the posting emphasizes and how to mirror that language "where it genuinely applies" without changing meaning. Gaps found can inform interview prep or a decision that the role is not a fit. — [Documented]
10. AI page, bullets: ask AI to analyze rather than just rewrite; check which bullets follow PAR (project/problem, action, result) while noting not every bullet needs PAR; check redundancy, but repetition of a skill core to the role is fine. — [Documented]
11. AI page, what not to do: do not let AI invent experiences; it may hallucinate or exaggerate to match a job description, especially when asked to find gaps; review every suggestion skeptically. Do not lose your voice (resume should sound like you; ask whether you could talk about it in an interview). Beware skill atrophy if new to resume writing. — [Documented]
12. AI page, data: be careful what personal data you upload; use a secure institutional platform or the AI tool's "temporary chat" feature so data is not saved. — [Documented]
13. Implication for a resume bot: when gap analysis finds a missing requirement, report it as a gap (or interview-prep topic), never fill it with invented content; preserve meaning when inserting posting language. — [Inferred]
