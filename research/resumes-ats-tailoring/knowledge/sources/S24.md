## Learned points
1. Two data sources: Job Outlook 2026 Spring Update (employers; Feb 12 – Mar 17, 2026; 185 respondents: 142 members = 19.9% of eligible, 43 nonmembers; sponsored by Jobscan) and the 2026 Student Survey (Mar 12 – May 15, 2026; 17,000+ students from 258 colleges, incl. 1,860+ graduating seniors). — [Documented]
2. Employer demand: more than one-third of entry-level jobs require AI skills, nearly triple six months earlier. — [Research]
3. AI skills in job descriptions rose from 10.5% (fall) to 16.5% (spring); 28% of employers seek early-career talent who can use AI in their work. — [Research]
4. Nearly 60% of employers assign interns projects that use AI tools and skills — intern AI work is a legitimate, citable resume item when real. — [Research] / [Inferred]
5. NACE gives examples of AI skills: "asking effective questions/prompts or checking AI outputs" — useful vocabulary for describing genuine AI competence on a resume. — [Documented]
6. Student attitudes (graduating seniors): 31% say AI skills of little/no importance, 29.1% somewhat, 40% very/extremely important. — [Research]
7. Building AI skills: 50.5% not building, 33.0% building, 16.4% planning to. — [Research]
8. AI in job search: 46% of graduating seniors used AI in their job search, 54% did not; non-users "would rather do it themselves", didn't need it, never considered it, or cited environmental impact or doubts about AI accuracy/helpfulness. — [Research]
9. NACE CEO frames a "striking disconnect" between employer expectations and student skepticism; says long-term impact of AI on early-career talent is too early to tell. — [Reported]
10. For a resume bot: some users will be AI-skeptical — the bot should respect a preference to write their own content, explain what it does, and keep the user's voice; it should prompt users to surface real AI-tool experience (e.g., prompting, verifying outputs) when relevant, never invent it. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- Press release summary; full figures are images described in alt text only.
- Employer sample small (185) and member-skewed; sponsor Jobscan is a resume/ATS vendor.
- College-student focus; not representative of experienced or non-degree US job seekers.

## Best for
- Current (2026) evidence on rising entry-level AI-skill expectations and student AI-skepticism, to shape how the bot handles AI skills and AI-averse users.
