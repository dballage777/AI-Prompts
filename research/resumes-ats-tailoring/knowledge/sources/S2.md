## Learned points
1. Purpose: a resume is a focused summary of the most relevant experience, typically one or two pages, used for industry roles; a CV (academic/faculty) is comprehensive with no strict page limit. References are usually not included on a resume. — [Documented]
2. Reader context: recruiters typically spend six to ten seconds on a resume and may receive hundreds or thousands of applications, so the most relevant information must be immediately visible. (Number given without a cited study.) — [Reported]
3. What employers seek: cites NACE's annual employer survey — problem solving, analytical and interpersonal skills, hard work, flexibility, initiative, relevant technical skills; show a mix of interpersonal, conceptual and technical skills. — [Reported]
4. Format do's/don'ts: one page, two maximum if substantial relevant content; readable font, do not shrink the font to fit; margins at least 0.5 in, ideally 0.75 in; leave white space. Slides: 10–12 pt font, margins no smaller than 0.5 in. — [Documented]
5. Content don'ts: no hours worked; usually skip one-time events; no first person ("Managed a team of three", not "I managed..."); skip "responsibilities included" and start with the action verb. — [Documented]
6. Order: reverse chronological within each section (most recent by end date first; current roles end "Present"). Typical section order: contact info at top, then Education, then Experience (can be labeled "Relevant Experience" / themed, with "Additional Experience" for the rest), then optional Awards, Projects, Activities/Leadership, Skills. — [Documented]
7. Education entry: institution, city/state (city/country abroad), degree and major, completion or expected date (end date only); optional GPA with scale; optional relevant coursework, 5–6 courses within about two lines. — [Documented]
8. Experience entry: organization, location, title, month/year date range; bullets on accomplishments and skills with strong action verbs and quantified results. Unpaid work, volunteering, internships, freelance and personal projects all count if relevant. — [Documented]
9. Bullet formulas: PAR (Project, Action, Result) or Action verb + subject (what you did) + end result. Worked progression: "Led a team design a new product" → "...of five...focusing on reducing production costs" → "...achieving a 20% reduction in production costs." If exact numbers are not available, describe role and positive outcome instead. — [Documented]
10. Experienced candidates: keep a comprehensive master resume; build targeted 1–2 page versions by removing less relevant/duplicative details. Career changers and early-career: emphasize transferable skills and relevant unpaid/project work. — [Documented]
11. Tailoring steps: analyze the job description; note repeated action verbs and key terms (e.g., communicate, collaborate, optimize); match job-specific and industry terminology; treat posting bullets near the top as most important. Include those terms only "if they align with your experience" / "if you have the relevant experience". — [Documented]
12. ATS: many companies (including many Fortune 500) use ATS to filter high volumes by keywords and criteria before a human reviews. ATS "may struggle" with tables, columns, graphics and images; use standard round bullets, not unusual symbols; choose a basic, text-focused ATS-compatible template; incorporate keywords naturally; re-review after tailoring. Slides add "Some templates" to the avoid list. — [Documented]
13. Generative AI uses endorsed: strengthen bullet points, get industry-specific tailoring advice, ask about key skills/trends for a role. Cautions: AI knowledge can be outdated, AI makes mistakes, check suggestions reflect actual experience, avoid sharing address, NDA-covered or security-clearance details; "you are your own expert." — [Documented]
14. Research experience: simplify language for non-specialist/HR readers, break research into transferable-skill components, state intended outcomes for ongoing work. — [Documented]

## Limitations / bias
- Aimed at MIT students, postdocs and alumni (research-heavy); some advice (GPA on a 5.0 scale, research bullets) is institution-specific.
- ATS claims are general ("may struggle"), with no vendor names or testing data; the six-to-ten-second figure is uncited.
- Recommends an ATS-friendly template, while MIT's own checklist (S4) says not to use a template; an internal inconsistency to resolve.
- Transcript is spoken-style; a few typos ("Led a team design").
