## Learned points
1. Defines an ATS as software that nearly all organizations use to screen applications, often by matching keywords from the job description. — Reported (summary-only; "nearly all" has no data in the summary)
2. Myth correction: recruiters usually still see the resume. What can filter applicants out is the application's qualifying or knockout questions (for example, years of experience), so the article advises applying to roles where you meet the stated requirements. — Reported (summary-only)
3. Format: a simple, clean layout; standard fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman); standard headings ("Education," "Skills," "Experience"). Avoid columns, tables, text boxes, graphics, and header or footer features. — Reported (summary-only)
4. Keywords: tailor keywords to each job description, using both the acronym and the spelled-out phrase. Use reverse chronological order. — Reported (summary-only)
5. Readability: bullet points, consistent formatting, 1.0–1.15 line spacing, 1-inch margins. — Reported (summary-only)
6. Content: emphasize relevant internships, work, volunteer experience, and coursework. Include measurable results (the article's example is "Increased social media engagement by 30%"). Leave out personal details such as age and marital status. Aim for one page, two if needed. — Reported (summary-only)
7. Contact information: name, phone, email, and a customized LinkedIn URL. Keep LinkedIn and Handshake consistent with the resume. — Reported (summary-only)
8. AI use: use AI only as an editing aid, not as the full writer, because AI-generated resumes tend to look alike. This matches S11 (CMU). — Reported (summary-only)
9. File: save as a text-based PDF, not an image, named with the person's first and last name (example: "John Doe Resume 2024"). Use a naming system for multiple versions, and keep a master file of past bullet points. — Reported (summary-only)
10. The article reportedly cites a TopResume article as a source for its ATS information. TopResume is a commercial resume service. — Reported (summary-only; the citation was not verified)
11. Implication for a bot: keep a master resume or bullet bank and generate a tailored version for each job. Pair acronyms with their full terms. Warn users about knockout questions rather than promising to "beat the ATS." — Inferred
