## Learned points
1. Core claim: an ATS is primarily a database/workflow tool; "no major ATS" autonomously rejects a resume without a human; there is no universal "ATS score" visible to employers. Based on Huntr's own data plus four recruiter interviews (Feb–Mar 2026). — [Reported]
2. Knockout (pre-qualification) questions are described as the main automated filter: answers on work authorization, required certifications, minimum education or years of experience can auto-disqualify before a recruiter sees the resume. — [Reported]
3. Typical workflow stages: Applied > Recruiter Screen > Hiring Manager Review > Assessment > Interview > Offer/Decline; a human moves candidates between stages. Recruiters can see a candidate's full application history at that company. — [Reported]
4. Market share from 1,665,447 job postings saved by Huntr users in 2025 (lower bound; ATS on company domains undercounted): Greenhouse 30.6%, Workday 19.6%, Ashby 10.7%, iCIMS 7.4%, Workable 6.5%, Lever 6.2%, SmartRecruiters 5.2%, Other 13.8%. — [Reported]
5. Most major ATS now offer AI ranking (Greenhouse match tiers; Workday HiredScore A–D grades; iCIMS Role Fit; Workable Screening Assistant with semantic matching; Lever Talent Fit, LLM-based; SmartRecruiters Winston Match 4-star scale). Ashby is described as refusing to rank. Article concedes low-graded candidates on Workday "may never be reviewed" if a recruiter has enough A-graded ones. — [Reported]
6. Parsing: Workday described as the worst parser (OCR to searchable PDF, then field mapping; loses header info, struggles with non-standard bullets); cites an unnamed study that .docx had 23% fewer parsing errors than PDF on Workday. Recruiters can still open the original file. — [Reported] (study not identified)
7. Formatting advice given: clean readable format; PDF or Word; avoid Canva/LaTeX non-standard formats unless saved as PDF; no contact info in headers/footers; font no smaller than 10 pt; if using columns, fill them. Article claims two-column layouts/graphics will not cause ATS "rejection" but a recruiter relying on a bad preview might miss information. — [Reported]
8. Huntr data claims: tailored resumes convert to interviews at about 6% (1 in 17) vs ~3% (1 in 33) for generic; two-page resumes perform as well or better than one-page at all experience levels; fuller education sections correlate with higher conversion; average search about 83 days. Correlational, self-reported user tracking. — [Reported]
9. Recruiter scan described as 6–10 seconds checking qualifications, skills, scope of experience, location; recruiters sort into yes / no / maybe. Put strongest qualifications in the top third of page one. — [Reported]
10. Content advice from recruiters: read the "role and responsibilities" section, not just requirements; write a "technical manual of accomplishment" with numbers, percentages, dollar amounts; use numerals ("4") not words ("four"); match the scope of the role; add one line of context about a small employer or one's division at a large employer. One recruiter said she does not read the summary. — [Reported]
11. Anti-fabrication: recruiters flagged AI-written resumes that mirror the job description too closely as a red flag; article says use AI to present real experience, not to fabricate new experience. Answer knockout questions honestly. — [Reported]
12. Against spray-and-pray / auto-apply: applying to ~15 jobs at one company is a red flag; AI-bot applications are "easily spotted" (return email, formatting, application velocity). Notable because Huntr itself sells an application autofill tool. — [Reported]
13. "ATS score" tools (Jobscan, Resume Worded named) score against their own criteria, not any real ATS; useful only as a keyword-alignment self-check. Most keyword advice is really about recruiter sourcing (Boolean database search), which matters more for LinkedIn profiles. — [Reported]
14. Workable's semantic matching recognizes related terms (e.g., "project management" vs "program management"), making keyword stuffing "less effective (and less necessary)". — [Reported]
15. Article tested chatbots on ATS questions and says they mostly overstated auto-rejection and AI screening (4 of 5 claims judged wrong against Huntr's findings). Implication for an AI resume assistant: do not assert that ATS auto-reject resumes for keywords/formatting as fact. — [Reported] / [Inferred]
16. Legal context cited: Mobley v. Workday (age/race/disability discrimination claims; allowed to proceed as nationwide collective action in May 2025); Kistler v. Eightfold AI (filed Jan 2026, FCRA claims about scoring candidates 0–5). — [Reported]
17. Apply early: one recruiter said pipelines often close once about five candidates are interviewing. Cover letters: reportedly unread at large companies, can matter at small companies/startups. — [Reported]
18. Tension with S27: Huntr says formatting rules are largely outdated; S27 gives strict formatting rules. Both agree on no contact info in headers/footers and standard fonts. A cautious assistant can follow simple formatting (low cost) without claiming it determines rejection. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- Vendor content promoting Huntr's tailoring product; "2x" tailoring figure is internal, correlational (users self-report moving jobs to "Interview"), not peer reviewed or independently verifiable.
- Only four recruiter interviews; generalizations ("none autonomously reject") rest on vendor documentation and these interviews; ATS configurations vary by employer.
- Market share derived from what Huntr users save, a non-representative sample (likely skewed to tech/white-collar).
- Unnamed "study" for the Workday .docx statistic; some claims rely on vendor marketing (e.g., SmartRecruiters "85% accuracy").
- Contradicts other career-center guidance; treat as one side of the ATS-myth debate.

## Best for
- Counterweight to ATS fear-mongering: knockout questions, human review, no universal ATS score, honesty and anti-spray guidance, plus a current (2025–2026) map of major ATS platforms and their AI ranking features.
