## Learned points
1. Definition and pipeline: an ATS extracts raw text from the file, parses it into structured fields (job titles, skills, education), scans for keywords/phrases aligned with the job description, then scores and ranks the resume by relevance. — Documented
2. Statistics given without a primary citation: "Nearly 90% of employers rely on ATS" and "Approximately 70-80% of resumés never make it through to a hiring manager." The only reference listed is vendor webinars (Quinncia ATS 1.0/2.0/3.0), so treat these numbers as unverified. — Reported
3. Layout rules: simple layout with traditional section headings (Education, Experience, Skills); no creative headings; avoid fancy templates, two-column layouts, photos, charts, tables, graphics, headers, footers and text boxes. — Documented
4. Keywords: tailor to each posting using exact phrases for skills, qualifications and job titles; use keywords naturally; overstuffing "can make your resumé look artificial and may get it flagged"; a perfect match is not necessary. — Documented
5. Hidden text: explicitly warns against pasting the job description into the background, stating ATS can detect it. (Bot rule: never insert hidden or white-font keywords.) — Documented
6. Fonts: Arial, Times New Roman or Calibri; 11–12 pt body; name and headings may be larger; limit to 3 font sizes and 2 font styles. — Documented
7. Write out a term and include its abbreviation, e.g., "Certified Public Accountant (CPA)". Vertical divider lines only in the contact line (Phone | Email | LinkedIn), not in the body. — Documented
8. Education formatting: university and degree on separate lines; comma between degree and major ("Bachelor of Science, Accounting"); hyphen for emphasis ("English - Creative Writing"); minor on the next line ("Minor, Biology"); GPA allowed ("GPA: 3.9"); repeat the university name for each degree from the same school; no bullets in Education other than GPA (they "get flagged"); put coursework under a "Relevant Coursework" subheading and achievements in a separate "Achievements" section after Education. — Documented
9. Experience section: start bullets with strong action verbs; past tense even for current jobs (to highlight achievements, not duties); skills shown inside experience are valued more by ATS; use "Experience" in headings (Work/Leadership/Volunteer/Project Experience) to get credit for time using skills; always include dates; prefer chronological over functional/skills resumes. — Documented
10. Skills that can't be shown in Experience go in a separate "Skills" section, using bulleted columns so each skill is read individually (note: this is in mild tension with its own warning against two-column layouts). — Documented
11. Employer entries: use the company's legal name (include LLC/Inc for smaller firms) and exact job title; list a promotion/internal move as if a separate employer; for university employers add the department ("Utah Valley University - Career & Internship Center"). — Documented
12. File creation: build in Word; avoid hopping between editors (Google Docs → Word → PDF) because the PDF can become corrupted; from Google Docs save directly to PDF; avoid Canva and other online builders (including vendors' AI resume tools) because the back-end text is read as "one big text box". — Documented
13. File type: submit .docx or a text-based PDF, PDF preferred to keep formatting for the human reader; never image-based/scanned PDFs. On a PC, "Save as Adobe PDF" or File > Export > Create PDF/XPS is recommended over "Save As → PDF", which "can sometimes cause formatting and encoding issues". Re-save a fresh PDF every 15 days or monthly. — Documented (the claim that PDFs degrade with "repeated use" is unusual and unsupported by evidence in the handout)
14. Self-test: copy all text from the PDF and paste into a blank Word document; selectable, correctly ordered text = compatible. Warning signs: boxes or question marks instead of bullets, extra spaces, jumbled or missing text, letters changing (T's to D's), words run together; scanned/graphic resumes paste as an image. — Documented
15. Two-column resumes: ATS may skip or misread the second column and mix content under wrong headings; upload an ATS-friendly version in the resume field and, if desired, a designed version under "Additional Document"; two-column is fine when emailing a recruiter directly. — Documented
16. Signs an employer uses ATS: online application portal with standardized questions, postings on major job boards, keyword-heavy job descriptions, pre-screening questions, automated confirmation emails. — Documented
17. Proofread: spelling/grammar errors can stop the resume being read properly; spell check misses wrong-but-real words (hear/here); do a manual review or ask a friend. — Documented

## Limitations / bias
- Statistics (90%, 70–80%) have no primary source; the only cited reference is a resume-tech vendor's webinars (Quinncia).
- Some advice is idiosyncratic or not widely evidenced (PDFs "corrupting" with repeated use, re-saving every 15 days, bullets in Education being "flagged", past tense for current jobs, ATS valuing skills more in Experience).
- Student/university-focused examples; no named ATS products are tested.
- Slight internal tension: avoid columns overall, yet use "bullet points in columns" for skills.

## Best for
- Concrete, checkable ATS formatting rules and a simple copy-paste parse test the bot can tell users to run before submitting a .docx/.pdf.
