## Learned points
1. Core problem ("authenticity gap"): the gap between what a candidate submits and what they can justify in a live professional conversation; AI has widened it. The author describes three students in one week submitting near-identical AI-generated resumes, one of whom could not explain a volunteer entry because she had not written it. — [Reported]
2. Copy-pasted AI resumes are risky because AI "can imagine having a credential and hallucinate information", the voice is not the candidate's, and the candidate cannot defend the content in a face-to-face interview. — [Reported]
3. Homogenized AI output creates "professional camouflage": applicants look and sound like everyone else, which is a liability in a hiring environment already wary of AI content. — [Reported]
4. Data cited (secondary, from Greenhouse 2025 AI in Hiring report, >4,100 applicants/recruiters/hiring managers in four countries): 49% of U.S. job seekers submitted more applications than the prior year; 34% of recruiters spend up to half their workweek filtering spam/junk applications. — [Research, secondary]
5. Fraud data cited (same Greenhouse report): 91% of U.S. recruiters have spotted candidate deception; 65% of hiring managers have seen applicants appear to cheat with AI, including reading scripts, "hiding prompt injections in resumes to bias applicant tracking system scoring", and (less often) deepfakes in video interviews. — [Research, secondary]
6. Further cited figures: Checkr survey of 3,000 U.S. managers — 59% personally suspected a candidate of using AI to misrepresent themselves; nearly two-thirds think job seekers fake better than recruiters detect. Gartner estimate (via People Management): by 2028, one in four candidate profiles globally will be fabricated. Greenhouse CEO: trust at an all-time low (via Fortune, Nov 2025). — [Research/Reported, secondary]
7. AI auto-apply platforms submit hundreds of applications in one sitting with AI-tailored resumes/cover letters per posting; the author argues the result is a flood of homogeneous, ghostwritten applications in which genuinely qualified candidates "can barely surface." — [Reported]
8. Employer-side AI context: 78% of businesses use AI in at least one function (McKinsey 2025, up from 72% early 2024 and 55% the year before); "Some applicant tracking systems rank candidates before a human reads a single word" (citing a Jobscan 2025 ATS usage report). — [Research, secondary]
9. Position: banning AI is the wrong response; the issue is AI use without responsibility, critical assessment, and the candidate's authentic voice. 2026 NACE survey: 55% of career centers offer AI-assisted job-search workshops, another 20% plan to. — [Reported / Research]
10. "GPS principle": AI is the co-pilot, the student is the driver and ultimate owner of every output. Five ethical principles taught: transparency, fact-checking, finding bias, holding oneself responsible, privacy protection. — [Documented (course design)]
11. Accountability test for any AI-assisted document: could you be asked about it in an interview and defend it? Suggested advising question: how did you apply AI here and where was your own voice used? — [Documented]
12. Tailoring method used in the course: paste the job posting into an AI tool, which suggests the most commonly used keywords; the student then updates the resume with best-matching keywords "in their own words and based on their own experience." AI flags the gap; the human fills it truthfully. — [Documented]
13. Fact-checking example the course poses: what to do when AI lists qualifications not in the posting (i.e., verify every AI-added qualification). Bias example: AI may undervalue a student's experience. — [Documented]
14. Division of labor: AI for analysis, evaluation and identification; meaning-making stays with the person. Structured 200–300-word self-reflections are used so "the human step" cannot be skipped. — [Documented]
15. Evidence quality is explicitly limited: two semesters (15 students fall 2025, 9 spring 2026), practitioner observation, not an RCT; observed better alignment between written materials and verbal self-presentation among engaged students. — [Reported]
16. Equity observation (unplanned): first-generation and neurodivergent students reported relief at a clear, structured AI process; without structure they tend to fall back on sophisticated-sounding but generic AI output. Cited: only 16% of first-gen students use career centers in their first year. — [Reported]

## Limitations / bias
- Opinion/practitioner piece; most statistics are secondary (vendor surveys from Greenhouse, Checkr, Jobscan; Gartner estimate via press). Vendor surveys may have commercial interest; methodology not shown in the article.
- Course evidence is small-n (24 students), unblinded, single institution; author states this explicitly.
- Student/new-graduate focus; little on experienced workers. Describes auto-apply and prompt injection as problems but gives no technical detail on how ATS score resumes.
## Best for
- Grounding the bot's "never fabricate, never hide prompt injections/keywords, no mass auto-apply, user must be able to defend every line" rules with a credible professional-body source and current (2025–2026) fraud/trust statistics.
