## Learned points
1. Methodology: multi-market survey of 4,136 respondents (2,900 job seekers; 1,236 recruiters and hiring managers) in the U.S., U.K., Ireland and Germany; U.S. subsample = 1,200 job seekers and 665 recruiters/hiring managers. No fieldwork dates, sampling frame, panel provider or margin of error given. — [Documented]
2. Prompt injection ("hidden text designed to bypass AI filters") is widespread: 41% of 1,200 U.S. job seekers admit using it, and 52% of non-users say they are considering it (press release). The PDF gives 40% U.S. / 42% Europe and 51% considering — the two documents disagree by 1 point. — [Research]
3. Hiring managers catch it: 65% of U.S. hiring managers have caught applicants using AI deceptively, including "hiding prompt injections in resumes (22%)", reading AI-generated scripts (32%) and deepfakes (18%). Implication for a resume bot: hidden text/prompt injection is a known, detected tactic and must never be produced. — [Research] / [Inferred]
4. 91% of U.S. recruiters (release) / "91% of U.S. hiring managers and 89% in Europe" (PDF) have spotted or suspected candidate deception/AI-driven misrepresentation — wording differs between the two documents. — [Research]
5. 74% of U.S. hiring managers are more concerned about fake credentials, deepfakes or misrepresented experience than a year ago (69% in Europe); 61% of U.S. hiring managers use software to detect AI use in hiring (PDF). Fabricated or inflated claims carry high detection risk. — [Research]
6. Volume pressure: 49% of U.S. job seekers submit more applications than a year ago (PDF: "just to get past automated filters"; Europe 21%); 34% of U.S. recruiters spend up to half their week filtering spam/junk applications. — [Research]
7. ATS/AI screening reliance: 53% of U.S. recruiters "have handed over most screening to AI/ATS systems"; 25% are not confident in their AI systems; 8% don't know what their AI prioritizes; only 21% are very confident their systems aren't rejecting qualified candidates. — [Research]
8. Trust: 46% of U.S. job seekers say trust in hiring decreased in the past year, 42% of those blame AI; 62% among U.S. Gen-Z entry-level workers; only 8% think AI makes hiring more fair; 35% think AI shifted bias from humans to algorithms, 18% say it amplified bias. — [Research]
9. Employer view: 70% of U.S. hiring managers say AI helps them make faster and better hiring decisions; 50% of recruiters say AI improved hiring overall, mainly time saved on screening and scheduling. — [Research]
10. 74% of U.S. job seekers personally use AI in the job hunt (78% in U.K./Ireland/Germany); 87% say employer transparency about AI use is important; 55% suspect AI evaluated their application without being told (PDF). — [Research]
11. Other market signals: 69% of U.S. candidates encountered fake job postings; 54% have encountered an AI-led interview; 36% used AI to alter appearance, voice or background in video interviews. — [Research]
12. Employer countermeasures: 39% of U.S. hiring managers run more in-person interviews to verify authenticity; 68% are more involved in hiring than last year; 50% of U.S. managers cite authenticity as top challenge (U.K.: 52% cite application volume). — [Research]
13. Greenhouse's stated position: fix comes from transparency, "good friction" like identity verification, and better hiring signals, not better AI; CEO calls the dynamic an "AI doom loop" (job seekers mass-apply with AI, employers filter with AI). — [Reported]
14. For a resume assistant: the evidence argues for authentic, verifiable, targeted applications over volume/auto-apply, and against any hidden-text or keyword tricks, since employers increasingly screen for authenticity and misrepresentation. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- Vendor survey by an ATS company that sells AI hiring and "authenticity"/identity products (Real Talent, Dream Job); framing of a "trust crisis" and "good friction" supports its products.
- No fieldwork dates, sampling method, weighting or margin of error published; self-reported admissions.
- Internal inconsistencies between release and PDF (41% vs 40% prompt injection; 52% vs 51% considering; "recruiters" vs "hiring managers" for the 91% figure). Cite the exact document used.
- PDF charts are images; only takeaway text was extracted.

## Best for
- Evidence that hidden-text/prompt-injection and misrepresentation are common, detected and penalized, justifying hard "never do" rules in the resume bot's prompt.
