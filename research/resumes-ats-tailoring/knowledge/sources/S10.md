## Learned points
1. Definition: ATS is employer software that filters applications, scanning resumes for keywords and qualifications and ranking candidates by alignment with job requirements. — Documented
2. Three-step model: (1) resume screening — scan and rank to narrow the pool; (2) ranking and selection — top matches prioritized for recruiter review; (3) keyword matching. Flow shown as Resume Submission → ATS Screening → Manual Review by Recruiter. — Documented
3. Exact wording matters: "Even slight variations (e.g., "marketer" vs. "marketing") may cause a mismatch." — Documented (not evidenced; behavior varies by system)
4. Keyword method: use exact language from the job description for job titles, skills and action verbs; include both full term and acronym ("Search Engine Optimization" and "SEO"); spell out degrees followed by a comma and major ("Bachelor of Arts, Social Ecology"). — Documented
5. Focus on hard skills and "avoid keyword stuffing"; write naturally in a professional business tone. — Documented
6. Formatting: avoid complex layouts; ATS struggles with images, tables and unusual fonts; do not build the resume in Canva or LaTeX; standard section titles ("Experience," "Education," "Skills"); "Limit columns: Use one or two columns" (more permissive than S6, which says avoid two-column). — Documented
7. File type: Word document or PDF; avoid complex file formats. — Documented
8. Tailor for every application using job-specific keywords. Match-rate targets: "Aim for at least a 75% match"; a 40–60% keyword match is still worth applying with. — Documented (thresholds are not sourced; appear to derive from match-score tools)
9. Tools listed: Big Interview's "Big Resume" (instant feedback, free for UCI students); ChatGPT with the right prompts to analyze job descriptions and suggest keywords; Jobscan for match percentage (free for two uses/month). — Documented
10. Include job-description keywords in cover letters too. — Documented
11. Statistic: "97.4% of Fortune 500 companies use ATS" — no source cited. — Reported
12. Different ATS (named examples: iCIMS, Taleo) may parse resumes differently — so a bot should not promise a resume will pass any given system. — Documented
13. Bot implications: report a keyword-coverage view (which posting terms the resume already covers vs. missing) but add only terms the user truly has; treat match percentages as rough guidance, not a goal to game; never pad to hit 75%. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- Two-page marketing-style handout; statistics (97.4%) and match thresholds (75%, 40–60%) are unsourced and likely vendor-derived.
- Promotes licensed/vendor tools (Big Interview, Jobscan) and ChatGPT.
- Student audience; no detail on how specific ATS parse files.

## Best for
- A simple, student-friendly keyword-tailoring routine (exact terms, full term + acronym, natural writing, per-job tailoring) and a caution that match-score targets are heuristics.
