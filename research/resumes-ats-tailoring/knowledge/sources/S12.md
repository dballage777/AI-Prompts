## Learned points
1. Tailoring is described as "the final step" before attaching a resume to an application. Its purpose is to show clear connections to the specific opportunity. — Documented
2. The three-step method: (1) read the job description, (2) highlight its key words and phrases, (3) insert those key words and phrases into the resume "where appropriate." — Documented
3. Keep standard sections (Education, Skills, Work Experience, Volunteer and Leadership Experience). Rename headings to fit the industry. For example, "Work Experience" can become "Research Experience" when the posting involves research, with another section titled "Additional Employment Experience." — Documented
4. Edit accomplishment statements and action verbs so they reflect skills and key words in the posting. Make sure any summary statement matches the posting. Put the most relevant descriptors first within each bullet. — Documented
5. Must-do: save a copy of the job posting as a PDF rather than just bookmarking it, so it stays available later (for example, after the posting is taken down and before interviews). — Documented
6. Show transferable skills (leadership is the example) in bullets even when the posting does not list them. Worked example: "Led the Business Oriented Women (BOW) recruitment process by marketing to potential members; resulting in an increase in applications from 70 to 114." — Documented
7. Avoid: sending a generic resume ("Ideally, each application you submit has a tailored resume"), repeating action verbs, and deleting sections that seem irrelevant without first considering the transferable skills in them. — Documented
8. Resume Tips, structure: active voice. Start each bullet with a strong verb. Present tense for current roles, past tense for past roles. Avoid "responsible for" and "duties include." Order sections by relevance, and order each section in reverse chronological order. — Documented
9. Resume Tips, formatting: fonts such as Cambria, Calibri, Times New Roman, Garamond, or Arial. Body text 10–12 pt. Name 16 pt or larger, bold, not in all caps. Margins 0.5–1 inch. Black ink. Numerals rather than spelled-out numbers ("5" not "five"). No tables. No personal pronouns. No full sentences or paragraphs. Keep styling (bold, caps, italics) consistent. — Documented
10. Resume Tips, general (student audience): one page. List only relevant coursework. Rate language skills honestly (beginner, intermediate, advanced, or native, optionally split into written and conversational). Show technical skill levels with phrases such as "proficient in" and "familiar with." No photo. References go on a separate page that repeats the contact information. — Documented
11. Verb upgrade ladder (Good → Better → Best): Worked → Contributed → Improved; Responsible for → Managed → Spearheaded; Learned → Gained Experience → Implemented. The Verbs page lists verbs grouped by skill area (administrative, analytical, creative, project, and others). — Documented
12. The Transferable Skills page tells users to inventory their skills, choose the 10 they most enjoy using, and write a short real example of each from a job, class, internship, or activity. This is a "skill + evidence" bank that tailoring can draw on. — Documented
13. The sample posting (Strategy Consulting Analyst) is the material for the tailoring video. It shows the kinds of keyword sources in a posting: project management, demonstrated leadership, critical thinking, qualitative and quantitative analysis, and Excel modeling. — Documented
14. "Honest" and "where appropriate" language (honest language-skill levels, inserting keywords "where appropriate") implies tailoring means reframing real experience, not adding unearned skills. Duke does not explicitly discuss fabrication. — Inferred
15. Duke's "no tables" rule is presented as general readability advice. ATS parsing is not named as the reason anywhere on the pages read. — Documented (absence noted)

## Limitations / bias
- Written for students and early-career readers (for example, the one-page rule and the coursework advice), so some rules do not fit experienced US job seekers.
- No dates and no evidence or data. The advice is practitioner convention.
- The Resume Guide page's substantive content was not retrievable as text (it is mostly an image and links). The tailoring video itself was not watched.
## Best for
- A concrete, step-by-step tailoring workflow (read, highlight, map keywords onto real accomplishments, rename headings, save the posting) plus practical formatting and verb rules.
