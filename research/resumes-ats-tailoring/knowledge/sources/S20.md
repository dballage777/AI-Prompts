## Learned points
1. Appropriate AI uses: formatting and getting suggestions for improving your resume and cover letter, tailoring how you frame your experience, practicing interview questions, and brainstorming career plans. — [Documented]
2. Inappropriate AI uses: submitting AI output without proof-editing and personalizing it, sharing personal information (likely on your resume) with AI, over-relying on AI tools and agents for your job search, and cheating or using AI scripts in interviews. — [Documented]
3. For the bot: the user must review and personalize all output; the bot should minimize collecting sensitive personal data (for example, it doesn't need a full address or ID numbers); and it should not act as an autonomous job-search agent (supporting a no-auto-apply rule). — [Inferred]
4. Accuracy warning: AI may return outdated or false information. Fact-check against official sources and carefully review generated text before adding it to your resume. — [Documented]
5. Bias warning: generative AI reflects and amplifies bias in its training data (gender, race, socioeconomic background). — [Documented]
6. "Trust people first": consult career counselors or mentors for nuanced situations, and don't rely on AI alone for major career decisions. — [Documented] (summary-only)
7. Sample resume prompts it offers: "Write concise resume bullets for my role as [Job Role], where I [List job responsibilities]" and "Turn my [Personal Project] into a resume bullet point and accomplishment." — [Documented]
8. Employers use AI for applicant tracking and resume screening, sourcing on LinkedIn, AI-led first interviews, and video-interview analysis. — [Documented] (summary-only)
9. Tools it names: the NCDA AI Toolkit, LinkedIn Career Explorer, Quinncia (resume and interview feedback, free to some students), Google Career Dreamer, and ChatGPT Edu for the CSU system. — [Documented] (summary-only)
10. Resume page: tailor to each role; one page, or two with substantial relevant experience; bullet formula of action verb + how + result; order bullets by the employer's stated priorities; include coursework, labs and projects. — [Documented] (summary-only)
11. Formatting (stated for CVs): a consistent font (Arial, Calibri, Tahoma or Times New Roman) at 10–11 pt, 0.5–1 inch margins, reverse chronological order; avoid borders, colors, images and graphics, which automated systems read poorly. You can combine your resume and cover letter into one PDF when only one upload is allowed. — [Documented] (summary-only)
12. Use a professional email address you check often, and remove the hyperlink. Use 3–5 references, with their permission. — [Documented] (summary-only)
13. ATS repost: ATS behavior varies widely, even within one company. Some (not all) let users scan for keywords and score resumes against the posting. Heavily formatted resumes or ones with images may not parse properly. Mirror the job description's skills, tools and responsibilities. — [Reported]
14. The ATS repost cites no data. Despite its title, it doesn't present evidence on bias against diverse candidates. — [Documented]

## Best for
- Setting the bot's AI-ethics guardrails: the user reviews and personalizes everything, the bot shares or collects minimal personal data, it warns about false output and bias, and it does not over-automate the job search.
