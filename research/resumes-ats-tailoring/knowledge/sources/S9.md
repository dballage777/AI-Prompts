## Learned points
1. Official stance: AI tools are valuable for generating ideas, "but the documents you create and submit should be your own"; do not copy-paste content you did not create into application documents; use AI like a human collaborator — "to brainstorm, edit, and refine your ideas". — Documented
2. Named risks: AI "cannot replicate a human perspective"; it tends to regurgitate verbatim wording from sources it is given; it often creates statements "not based on fact or representative of your experiences" (i.e., fabrication); it "does not eliminate bias". — Documented
3. Privacy: the guide says information given to an AI model "will become part of its data set" and advises removing personal identifying information and sensitive data before sharing resumes. (Bot implication: tell users what is stored, minimize data, and never require unnecessary PII.) — Documented (claim about training is a general statement, not true of every service)
4. Ties AI use to the Georgia Tech Academic Honor Code for students. — Documented
5. Endorsed AI uses relevant to resumes: review a resume and suggest improvements, including against a specific job description; "Assess a keyword fit between a resume and job description to measure ATS match"; summarize a job description's themes and top skills; generate interview questions from a job description; help career changers map skills to new fields. — Documented
6. Prompting guidance: be specific and give context (role, target job, source documents); assign a persona (e.g., "seasoned recruiter at a tech company"); iterate when output is too general; break requests into steps and specify output format (e.g., "5 bulleted recommendations"). — Documented
7. Sample resume prompts: "How can I tailor my resume for a software engineering job?", "Where can I add more metrics to quantify achievements?", "What are key action verbs to use in a resume for a project manager role?" — Documented
8. Cover-letter example asks AI to use the resume and job description and to add "one or two relevant examples as a narrative style" instead of restating the resume. — Documented
9. Further reading list includes UCI's "AI and the Job Search", MIT Sloan on AI hallucinations and bias, IBM on AI bias; also lists general AI tools (ChatGPT, etc.) — no resume-specific vendor endorsements were evaluated. — Documented
10. Bot implications: act as collaborator, not ghostwriter; draft only from facts the user supplies; flag any suggested wording the user must verify; ask for metrics rather than invent them; keep the user's voice. — Inferred
11. Nothing on auto-applying, hidden text or ATS formatting; guidance is about authorship and accuracy. — Documented (absence)

## Limitations / bias
- Student/academic-integrity framing; written for users of general chatbots, not for a purpose-built resume assistant.
- Some tool references are dated (mentions "Bard"); privacy claim ("will become part of its data set") is generalized and not true of all services.
- No evidence or data; guidance is normative.

## Best for
- Ethical guardrails for an AI resume bot: user owns the document, no invented statements, privacy/PII minimization, and AI as editor and keyword-fit checker.
