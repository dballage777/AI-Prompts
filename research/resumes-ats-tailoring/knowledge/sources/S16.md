## Learned points
1. Method: 25 structured interviews with US recruiters (healthcare, tech, construction, consumer goods, education, retail, finance and others), employers of about 120 to over 50,000 staff, done September–October 2025, transcribed and coded. The author says the sample is small and not nationally representative. — [Research] (summary-only)
2. ATS platforms the recruiters named include Workday, iCIMS, Greenhouse, Bullhorn, BambooHR, SuccessFactors, Teamtailor, Phenom and Lever. — [Reported] (summary-only)
3. 23 of 25 (92%) said their systems do not auto-reject resumes for formatting, content or design. — [Research]
4. 8% (2 of 25) had content-based auto-rejection set up, using rules such as match-score thresholds or a required number of skills. — [Research] (summary-only)
5. Knockout questions are the main automatic filter. They are yes/no eligibility checks (work authorization, required license, location), and every recruiter who used them relied on them. So the bot should tell users to answer knockout questions truthfully and check that they meet hard requirements before applying. — [Research]; advice part [Inferred]
6. 44% said their ATS has an AI or "fit" score. About 36% use it only as a guide, 8% treat it as decisive, and 56% ignore it or don't have one. — [Research] (summary-only)
7. The article explains rejection by volume, not software: recruiters run out of time to read every resume. Recruiter estimates were 400–600 applicants per entry-level or administrative opening, 1,000+ for customer service, and 2,000+ for some tech roles. These are estimates, not official statistics. — [Reported] (summary-only)
8. Timing: 52% said applying early helps because they review in order of arrival, and 36% said timing makes little difference. The article suggests applying within 48–72 hours of posting. — [Research] (summary-only)
9. Recruiters said the auto-reject myth comes mainly from job seekers on social media (68%), career coaches and resume blogs (20%), and media headlines (12%). — [Research] (summary-only)
10. Formatting advice: a clear, skimmable structure, short bullets, simple and consistent formatting, one or two pages, reverse-chronological jobs, and no heavily designed or graphic-heavy templates. — [Reported] (summary-only)
11. Keywords: recruiters prioritize natural use of keywords, not keyword stuffing, and mirror the job's core skills where they truthfully apply. — [Reported]
12. Tailor every application. Recruiters notice mass-applied resumes, including some that still name the wrong position. This argues against auto-apply and generic resumes. — [Reported] (summary-only); the auto-apply point is [Inferred]
13. LinkedIn outreach (named by 32%) and a strong cover letter (16%) can help borderline candidates. — [Research] (summary-only)
14. The article does not discuss hidden or white text, tables, columns, PDF versus DOCX, or parsing errors. Its only parsing claims are promotional statements about Enhancv's own templates. — [Documented]

## Limitations / bias
- Conflict of interest: Enhancv sells a resume builder, templates and a "Job Tailoring" feature, and the article calls its templates "ATS-readable." Debunking ATS fear also suits its marketing.
- The sample is small (n=25), not random, US only, self-reported, and recruiters may underreport automated filtering. Percentages of 25 are unstable.
- Volume figures are recruiter estimates.
- I read the page through a summarizer. The quotes above are as the tool returned them and were not checked against the raw HTML.
## Best for
- Countering the "the ATS auto-rejects most resumes" myth, while warning users that knockout questions and hard requirements are the real automatic filters.
