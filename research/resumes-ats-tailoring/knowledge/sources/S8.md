## Learned points
1. Core message: new graduates should provide evidence of skills on resumes, not just list them; NACE's CEO says employers "want to see examples." — Documented
2. Survey: Job Outlook 2026 Spring Update, sponsored by Jobscan (a resume-scanning vendor), fielded Feb 12–Mar 17, 2026; 185 respondents (142 NACE employer members = 19.9% of eligible members, plus 43 nonmembers). — Research
3. Figure 1 "Skills employers look for on resumes" (skills cited by ≥50% of respondents; N = 163): ability to work in a team 85%; problem-solving 82%; verbal communication 78%; technical skills 73%; written communication 72%; strong work ethic 71%; analytical/quantitative 70%; initiative 69%; leadership potential 68%; detail-oriented 65%; interpersonal 61%; flexibility/adaptability 58%; computer skills 50%. — Research
4. More than half of respondents cited 10+ skills — the employer "wish list" is extensive. — Research
5. Recruiting is described as "increasingly based on skills" rather than GPA and major; students should reflect on academic, experiential, extracurricular and work experiences to identify skills and connect them to the target job. — Documented
6. Candidates must also be ready to articulate these skills in interviews — so resume claims must be ones the user can back up verbally. — Documented
7. How to know which skills to highlight: previous NACE research found many organizations state the needed skills in job descriptions (supports reading the posting as the primary keyword source). — Reported
8. Internships: NACE research "consistently finds" that between two equally qualified candidates, employers overwhelmingly choose the one with internship experience. — Reported (no figure given in release)
9. NACE's eight career readiness competencies: Career & Self-Development, Communication, Critical Thinking, Equity & Inclusion (marked "currently under review" because of federal Executive Orders/legal risk), Leadership, Professionalism, Teamwork, Technology; each has sample behaviors (e.g., Communication: "Communicate in a clear and organized manner so that others can effectively understand."). — Documented
10. Competencies were validated with SkillSurvey for associated behaviors and revised in 2017 and 2020; NACE says the eight "can be utilized in any combination". — Documented
11. Bot implication: for students/early-career users, prompt for concrete examples (team projects, class work, clubs, jobs, internships) that demonstrate teamwork, problem-solving and communication, and write them as evidence-based bullets rather than adjective lists ("strong team player"). — Inferred
12. Bot implication: given the E&I competency's "under review" status, a bot should not assume DEI wording is risky or safe; follow the user's target posting and the user's real experience. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- Survey sponsored by Jobscan, an ATS/resume-optimization vendor; small sample (185; figure N = 163) with a 19.9% member response rate; employer self-report.
- Focus on new college graduates/entry level; not representative of mid-career or non-degree job seekers.
- Press release; full report is member-gated (not read). Release text contains a typo/garbled sentence ("These closely with").
- Says nothing about ATS mechanics or formatting.

## Best for
- Evidence (with 2026 percentages) for coaching students to prove soft and technical skills with concrete examples instead of listing adjectives.
