## Learned points
1. Purpose: present your qualifications clearly and strategically, showing your skills, experience and what you can do for the employer. — [Documented] (summary-only)
2. Be concise. The page says employers take an average of 30 seconds to skim a resume, with no source given. — [Documented]; statistic unsourced
3. Keep it to two pages at most. Emphasize recent experience, and cut or minimize experience more than 15 years old. — [Documented]
4. Tailor it to each job, using the job description or the employer's website, and put related work experience and achievements first. — [Documented]
5. Honesty: lying on your resume is never a good idea, so don't overstate skills or results. This is official support for a no-fabrication rule. — [Documented]
6. Quantify results (for example team size or percentage sales growth). Use plain language and action verbs (handled, managed, led, increased). Highlight achievements rather than listing duties, and avoid vague general statements. — [Documented] (summary-only)
7. Include relevant unpaid work under "Work experience" or "Volunteer work." — [Documented] (summary-only)
8. Put contact information at the top of page one: name, address, email and phone, all accurate. Use a professional, name-based email address without nicknames, numbers or special characters. — [Documented] (summary-only)
9. Leave out personal details (age, marital status, religion, political views) and never include your Social Insurance Number. (The US equivalent would be a Social Security number; the bot should never put one on a resume.) — [Documented]; US mapping [Inferred]
10. No photo: "not the norm in Canada." US practice is also generally no photo, but this source speaks only for Canada. — [Documented]; US note [Inferred]
11. Use no more than 5–7 bullets per section or subsection. — [Documented] (summary-only)
12. Avoid personal pronouns. The page says to write in the third person "as if it's being written by someone else." US guidance more often describes this as implied first person (no "I," starting bullets with a verb). The result looks the same, but the bot should not tell US users to write "he/she managed." — [Documented]; US contrast [Inferred]
13. Don't explain why you left past jobs. Don't list references; provide them on a separate sheet only when asked. Leave out hobbies unless they're relevant. Proofread repeatedly and have someone else review it. — [Documented] (summary-only)
14. The page does not address file formats, ATS or keywords. It links to Job Bank's free Resume Builder (https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resume-builder), which requires sign-up. — [Documented]

## Limitations / bias
- This is Canadian guidance and is not authoritative for the US. Differences to flag: SIN versus SSN, "third person" wording, and including a full street address, which many US sources now treat as optional (city and state).
- It does not cover ATS, keywords or file formats.
- The 30-second statistic is unsourced.
- I read it through a summarizer, so quotes were not checked against the raw HTML.
## Best for
- Government-backed basics on honesty, tailoring, concision and what personal details to leave out, adapted carefully for US users.
