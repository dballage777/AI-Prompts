## Learned points
1. Methodology: survey Feb 12 – Mar 17, 2026; 185 respondents (142 NACE employer members = 19.9% of eligible members, plus 43 nonmember companies); sponsored by Jobscan. Regional split led by Great Lakes 30.8%. Hiring projections originally collected Aug 7 – Sep 22, 2025. — [Documented]
2. Resume content employers seek (entry-level, Class of 2026): evidence of teamwork, problem-solving and communication skills; "more than half of respondents citing 10+ skills" on their wish list (full list in a figure not captured). — [Research]
3. Listing skills is not enough — employers want examples showing the skill in use; students should connect academic, experiential, extracurricular and work experiences to the target job. Supports evidence-based bullets over bare skills lists. — [Research] / [Inferred]
4. GPA screening: the landing page gives no GPA-screening figure; the resume-skills article says recruiting is "increasingly based on skills" and "less so on GPA and major". — [Reported]
5. Skills-based hiring: nearly 70% of respondents now use it. Top way to demonstrate skills: prepare examples/situations where skills solved problems, and tie coursework and extracurriculars to professional skills. — [Research]
6. Experiential learning: nearly all respondents value U.S.-based internships; slightly more than three-quarters value co-ops; more than 40% seek on-campus student work and apprenticeships — worth surfacing on entry-level resumes. — [Research]
7. Many employers highlight key skills needed for the position in their job descriptions (citing earlier NACE research) — supports mining the job posting for skills to address. — [Reported]
8. Hiring projection: +5.6% for Class of 2026 (median, not mean; fall 2025 median was -2.4%); employers with >5,000 employees +8.7%; growth uneven. Up: Information, Engineering Services, Wholesale Trade, Construction, Misc. Professional Services; down: Utilities, Computer/Electronics Mfg, Misc. Mfg, Food/Beverage Mfg, Chemical (Pharmaceutical) Mfg. — [Research]
9. Market rating: plurality (45%) rate the Class of 2026 market "fair" (last time "fair" led was 2021, at 52%). Spring recruiting share rising: 37% of full-time and 27% of intern recruiting; pre-pandemic nearly three-quarters of full-time recruiting was in fall. — [Research]
10. AI skills: more than one-third of entry-level jobs require AI skills (nearly triple fall 2025); 28% of employers seek early-career talent who can use AI; nearly 60% assign interns AI-tool projects; landing page stat tiles show 13.3% and 10.5% under AI-skills labels (exact label-to-number mapping unclear in extracted text; see S24 for 10.5%→16.5% job-description figure). — [Research]
11. AI is framed as reshaping rather than replacing entry-level work: >2/3 discuss AI for tasks within jobs, 11% discuss replacing positions; >half say AI is not reducing entry-level tasks. Skills sought let workers use AI to complement human work. — [Research]
12. For a resume bot: for students/new grads, prioritize internships, co-ops, campus jobs, projects and concrete examples of teamwork, problem-solving, communication and (where true) AI tool use; do not invent AI skills to meet rising demand. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- Small sample (185) skewed toward NACE member employers that recruit on campus; entry-level/college-graduate focus only.
- Sponsored by Jobscan, a commercial ATS/resume-scan vendor.
- Full report gated; skill "wish list" percentages and some charts not retrieved; no GPA cutoff data obtained.
- Hiring projection switched from mean to median between fall and spring reports.
