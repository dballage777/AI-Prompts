## Learned points
1. The article cites a Gartner survey of 3,290 job candidates in which 39% used AI during the application process. — [Reported]
2. Of the candidates who used AI, half used it to write cover letters, 36% for writing samples and 29% to draft assessment answers. Per Gartner, more than half used it to generate resumes or CVs. — [Reported] (summary-only)
3. Fraud methods it lists: deepfaked face, voice and expressions in video interviews; fake degrees; altered certifications; and fabricated identities. — [Reported] (summary-only)
4. An unnamed survey of 3,000 candidates found 6% admitted some interview fraud (posing as someone else, or having someone pose as them). No source is given. — [Reported]
5. The article projects that one in four candidates worldwide could be fake by 2028, and names no source (this resembles a Gartner forecast, but the article doesn't attribute it). — [Reported]; unsourced
6. AI is used to submit applications at scale. As an example of volume, it cites nearly 175 applications arriving in a single inbox. — [Reported] (summary-only)
7. ATS were built to analyze skills, experience and keywords. They were not designed to detect deepfakes or AI-generated resumes written to get past them, so such resumes can pass early filters. — [Reported]
8. Under high volume, recruiters often accept applicant information at face value. — [Reported] (summary-only)
9. Employer responses it recommends: background checks, cross-checking with past employers and schools, noting gaps between the resume and interview answers, more in-person interviews, and stronger screening skills. — [Reported] (summary-only)
10. Implication for the bot: employers increasingly verify claims, so any fabricated degree, certification, title or metric risks being found in checks or interviews. The bot must never invent credentials, must not help with identity or interview fraud, and should make sure users can defend every bullet in an interview. — [Inferred]
11. Implication for auto-apply: mass AI-generated applications are framed as part of the fraud and volume problem, which supports a bot policy of tailoring individual applications instead of auto-applying in bulk. — [Inferred]
12. The article gives no advice to candidates. It is written for recruiters. — [Documented]

## Limitations / bias
- This is a SHRM India blog post, not a SHRM research report or US-specific guidance.
- It relies on secondary statistics. Two key figures (the 3,000-candidate survey and the 2028 projection) are unsourced, and the Gartner figures are cited without links.
- Its alarm-raising framing is aimed at employers.
- I read it through a summarizer, so quotes were not checked against the raw HTML.
## Best for
- Explaining to users why honesty and verifiability matter (employers now check for fabricated credentials and AI-generated applications), and supporting no-fabrication and no-mass-auto-apply rules.
