## Learned points
1. Template section order: Name; one contact line (address • city, state zip • email • phone); Education (university, city/state, degree, concentration, optional GPA, graduation date, optional thesis, optional relevant coursework/awards, study abroad, high school with optional GPA/SAT/ACT); Experience; Leadership & Activities; Skills & Interests (optional). — [Documented]
2. Experience entry: Organization, City/State (accessible version adds "or remote"), Position Title, Month Year – Month Year; most recent first. — [Documented]
3. Bullet rules in template: begin each line with an action verb; include details that show accomplishments, skills, knowledge, abilities; quantify where possible; no personal pronouns; each line a phrase, not a full sentence. — [Documented]
4. Section flexibility: if Leadership & Activities is more relevant to the target role, move it above Experience; activities may omit descriptions. — [Documented]
5. Skills block format: Technical (software/programming languages with fluency level), Language (with fluency level), Laboratory (if applicable), Interests (activities that may spark interview conversation). — [Documented]
6. File inspection: the standard Word template uses no tables (tab-aligned dates/locations, Calibri/Times New Roman, roughly 0.4–0.5 in side margins); the "Accessible" Word version uses tables for layout with 0.5 in margins. Given S2's warning that ATS may struggle with tables, the tab-based layout is the safer pattern for ATS. — [Inferred]
7. Guide: a resume is a concise summary that should highlight strongest assets and differentiate the candidate; tailor it to the type of position — not every experience must relate directly, but it should reflect the skills the employer values. — [Documented]
8. Resume language should be: specific not general; active not passive; written to express not impress; articulate, not "flowery"; fact-based (quantify and qualify); written for people and systems that scan quickly. — [Documented]
9. Top five mistakes: spelling/grammar errors; missing email/phone; passive language instead of action words; poorly organized, not concise or skimmable; not demonstrating results. — [Documented]
10. DON'T list (US norms): personal pronouns; abbreviations; narrative style; slang; a picture; age or gender; references; starting each line with a date. — [Documented]
11. DO list: consistent format; balanced white space; consistent emphasis (bold, italics, capitalization); headings in order of importance; reverse chronological within headings; avoid information gaps (e.g., a missing summer); make sure formatting translates if converted to PDF. International resume norms differ by country. — [Documented]
12. Action-verb bank grouped by skill area: Leadership, Communication, Research, Technical, Teaching, Quantitative, Creative, Helping, Organizational (e.g., Spearheaded, Liaised, Synthesized, Optimized, Streamlined, Forecasted, Implemented, Validated). — [Documented]
13. AI stance: AI is useful in editing — brainstorming bullet revisions, incorporating keywords from a job description, improving existing content — but must not be the primary author because output is likely generic; the resume should authentically represent the person. — [Documented]
14. Cover letter rules relevant to resume packaging: one page max, tailored, factual examples tied to the job description; check formatting after PDF conversion; use the same font type and size as the resume. — [Documented]

## Limitations / bias
- Template landing page itself has no advice text; substance comes from the .docx files and the linked guide page (same site). Google Docs version and sample PDFs not opened. The "AI for Professional Development" page linked from the guide was not read.
- Undergraduate audience (high school, SAT/ACT, study abroad); "avoid information gaps such as a missing summer" is student-oriented and could pressure users to pad — the bot should not invent content to fill gaps.
- No ATS-specific guidance beyond "systems that scan quickly"; no stated evidence base.
## Best for
- A concrete, US-standard section layout and bullet style for the bot's .docx output, plus a categorized action-verb bank and a clear "AI edits, the person authors" rule.
