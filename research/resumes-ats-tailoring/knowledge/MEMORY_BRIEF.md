# MEMORY BRIEF: Resumes, ATS & Tailoring
Working memory for this topic (built 2026-11-11 from S1–S30). Full detail is in KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md.

## Most important learned points
1. **Never fabricate.**
   - "Lying on your resume is never a good idea" [S19].
   - AI "will sometimes invent details or exaggerate experience to match a job description" [S4].
   - Use AI to present real experience, "Don't use it to fabricate a new one" [S28].
   - *Strong*
2. **The interview-defense test.** Every line must be something the user can explain in an interview [S1][S4]. Employers now verify with background checks, assessments, in-person interviews and fraud detection [S17][S21][S29]. *Strong*
3. **The person authors and AI edits.**
   - AI "should not be the primary author" [S5].
   - "The documents you create and submit should be your own" [S9].
   - Use feedback prompts, not "build me a resume" [S11]. Keep the user's voice [S3].
   - *Strong*
4. **AI finds the gap; the human fills it truthfully** [S1].
   - A missing requirement becomes an interview-prep topic or a "not a fit" signal, never invented content [S4].
   - Mirror the posting's language "where it genuinely applies, without changing the meaning" [S4][S2][S12].
   - *Strong*
5. **Trust crisis, in numbers.**
   - 41% of US job seekers admit hidden-text prompt injections, and 22% of US hiring managers have caught them [S21].
   - 32% claimed AI skills they lack, and 28% made fake work samples [S25].
   - Hidden text and stuffing are detectable and penalized [S6]. *Research (vendor surveys)*
6. **No auto-apply or spray-and-pray.**
   - Employers "flag or filter AI-autonomous submissions" [S11].
   - Recruiters spot bot applications, and about 15 applications at one company is a red flag [S28].
   - AI mass-applying fuels an "AI doom loop" [S21][S29]. *Strong*
7. **Tailor every application.** Read the posting, highlight its terms, and insert them "where appropriate" [S12]. Vendor data says tailored resumes get about twice the interviews (correlational) [S28]. *Strong*
8. **Evidence over adjectives.** "Employers want to see examples" [S8][S23]. Bullets follow action verb + scope + result, or PAR, with numbers where known [S2][S4]. Never invent a metric. *Strong*
9. **What a real ATS does.**
   - It stores applications, parses resumes into fields, and supports search and ranking.
   - Workday: results "vary based on resume format and order of words," use image-free resumes, and Skills and Languages are not auto-filled [S15].
   - Most ATS now offer AI fit scores [S28]. *Moderate*
10. **Rejection myth vs. reality** *(Contested)*.
    - Knockout questions (work authorization, license, years of experience) are the main automatic filter [S16][S28].
    - 92% of 25 recruiters said they don't auto-reject for format [S16].
    - But 53% of recruiters hand most screening to AI/ATS [S21], 71% of employers let AI reject without human oversight [S30], and low-ranked candidates may never be seen [S28].
    - Never promise anything.
11. **ATS-safe format.**
    - Single column. Standard headings. Calibri, Arial or Times New Roman, 10–12 pt. Margins 0.5–1 in.
    - Contact details in the body, not a header or footer.
    - No tables, text boxes, images, icons or skill bars. Plain bullets and consistent "Mon YYYY" dates.
    - Sources: [S2][S6][S13][S27]. *Strong*
12. **Full term and acronym:** "Certified Public Accountant (CPA)" [S6][S10][S27]. *Strong*
13. **File type** *(Contested)*.
    - Follow the posting [S27].
    - Otherwise use a text-based PDF [S6][S13][S22], never a scanned one, and keep a .docx copy; one vendor says Word files parse better in Workday [S28].
    - Run the copy-paste parse test [S6].
14. **Length.** One page early in a career; up to two with substantial experience [S2][S4][S13].
15. **Federal resumes (USAJOBS, Title 5), since Sept 27, 2025.**
    - **Two pages maximum.** A longer resume makes the applicant ineligible.
    - Include month/year dates, hours per week, and series/grade.
    - PDF, 0.5-inch margins, 10 pt body text [S22]. *Documented*
16. **Keyword targets are self-checks, not goals.** UC Irvine's "75% match" is unsourced [S10]. "A perfect keyword match isn't necessary" [S6]. Scanner scores aren't a real ATS score [S28].
17. **Leave off:** photo, age, marital status, religion, SSN, references, and hours worked (except on federal resumes) [S2][S5][S19]. Use city and state, not a full address (Inferred). *Strong*
18. **Privacy with AI.** Strip personal identifying details, NDA material and clearance details before using AI [S2][S9][S4]. *Strong*
19. **AI self-preference.** LLM screeners favored their own writing 67–82% of the time, and polished text was still favored [S26]. Don't promise that AI polish wins; keep facts fixed. *Research (preprint)*
20. **O\*NET is a recall aid.** Use its tasks, tools and "Hot Technologies" to jog the user's memory, and add only what they confirm [S7]. *Inferred from Documented*
21. **Entry-level employers** want teamwork, problem-solving and communication shown through internships and projects. AI skills are rising (16.5% of job descriptions) [S8][S23][S24]. *Research (small sample, sponsored by a vendor)*

## Prompt-writing rules (from section 11)
- Put the honesty contract **first**: only user-confirmed facts; ask for metrics; flag gaps; interview-defense test.
- Never use hidden text, keyword stuffing, auto-apply, or help with fraud.
- Workflow: posting first; facts in the user's words; master resume; gap table; one tailored version per job.
- Show what changed and why. Keep the user's voice. Offer a "you write, I give feedback" mode.
- Output: the ATS-safe spec; a federal mode with a hard 2-page limit; follow the posting's file type, otherwise PDF plus .docx.
- Be calibrated about ATS. Ask only for the personal data you need.
- Test cases: "add a skill I'll learn later," white-text keywords, a 4-page federal resume, "auto-apply to 50 jobs," no numbers.
