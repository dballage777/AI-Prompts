## Learned points
1. [Documented] The AUP (also called the "Acceptable Use Policy") applies to everyone who submits inputs, explicitly including developers using the API and "the end users of products or services integrating Claude". It has three parts: Universal Usage Standards, High-risk Use Case Requirements, Additional Use Case Guidelines.
2. [Documented] Enforcement: Anthropic's Safeguards Team runs detection and monitoring; on suspected violation Anthropic "may warn you or throttle, limit, suspend, or terminate your access". Real-time safeguards may block outputs, but a block "does not by itself indicate a violation".
3. [Documented] Additional Use Case Guideline, consumer chatbots: "All consumer-facing chatbots or AI agents that communicate directly with external users must disclose to users that they are interacting with AI rather than a human", at minimum at the beginning of each chat session or in the product interface; the disclosure need not name Anthropic, Claude, or the model.
4. [Documented] Additional Use Case Guideline, minors: products serving minors, including those letting minors directly interact with products incorporating the API, must comply with the Support article's guidelines and all applicable laws. Agentic use: developers are responsible for agents' actions through tools/browsers/connected systems complying with the AUP.
5. [Documented] The AUP defines a minor as "any individual under the age of 18 years old, regardless of jurisdiction" (stated in the child-safety section). Neither the AUP nor the minors article sets a minimum end-user age for API-built apps; instead the minors article requires safeguards when minors can use the product.
6. [Documented] Minors article (Mar 16, 2026) safeguards: additional technical measures such as age verification systems, content moderation and filtering, monitoring and reporting mechanisms, and educational resources for minors; implement any Anthropic-provided child-safety system prompt (described as "not infallible"); comply with child-safety and privacy law such as COPPA and state that compliance on the website or public docs; disclose AI rather than human. Anthropic "will periodically audit" and a high violation rate without these measures may lead to suspension or termination.
7. [Documented] High-risk Use Case Requirements (qualified human-in-the-loop review + disclosure that AI was used) apply to "High-risk AI Recommendations" in listed High-risk Areas: legal, medical (incl. mental health), finance (personalized investment/tax), credit, insurance, housing, employment, education and credentials, healthcare access, public benefits, legal status and adjudication. Entertainment/media recommendations are not in the list.
8. [Documented] Exclusions from high-risk requirements include general information/education, wellness content, and "business operations that do not advise or decide about a specific individual".
9. [Documented] Universal standards relevant to a recommender: do not "Infringe, misappropriate, or violate the intellectual property rights of a third party"; do not compile/aggregate/enrich personal data from multiple sources into profiles for sale, licensing, or disclosure to third parties without awareness and agreement (in furtherance of prohibited use); do not deploy products using "subliminal, manipulative, or deceptive techniques to distort behavior by impairing decision-making"; do not exploit individuals based on age; no sexually explicit content or erotic chats; no promoting or glorifying graphic violence/gore.
10. [Documented] Platform-abuse rules: jailbreaking or prompt injection to bypass guardrails without authorization is prohibited (applies to end users too); do not "Resell, proxy, or otherwise provide access to Claude through unauthorized means, including services that route requests through consumer subscriptions or misrepresent the product or client being used"; do not use outputs to train an AI model without authorization; do not circumvent bans via new accounts; do not provide end users access in violation of the Supported Regions Policy.
11. [Documented] Impersonation rule: do not "convince people they are interacting with a human when they are not" (clearly disclosed parody, satire, or fiction excepted).
12. [Documented] Output problems (inaccurate, biased, harmful) can be reported to usersafety@anthropic.com; additional resources include Child Safety Guidance for Developers in the Safeguards Support Center (not read).
13. [Inferred] For the app: a media recommender is not a listed High-risk Area, so human review is not required, but the app must show an "AI-generated" disclosure in the UI or at each session start. If the app is open to under-18s (app-store age ratings for anime/TV content matter here), the minors guidelines apply: age gating, content filtering, monitoring, COPPA statement. Taste-profile data should not be sold or shared to third parties, the explanation feature must avoid manipulative persuasion, and the backend proxy should be a legitimate API integration (own API key), not routed through consumer subscriptions. Because end users are bound by the AUP, the app's terms should pass relevant rules on and the backend should act on repeat jailbreak attempts (see S7).

## Limitations or bias
- Policy text; it does not interpret edge cases (e.g. whether a general-audience app with teen users counts as "serving minors").
- Minimum ages for account holders/end users may appear in Anthropic's Commercial or Consumer Terms, which were not read here.
- Version history and the Child Safety Guidance for Developers page were not read.

## Summary-only items
- Universal Usage Standards sections not listed above (weapons, critical infrastructure, elections, surveillance, etc.) were skimmed only.

## Best used for
- The AI-disclosure requirement, the minors safeguards checklist, confirming a media recommender is outside High-risk Areas, and AUP rules on proxies, profiling, manipulation and IP.
