## Learned points
1. **NASB gratis limit**: the text may be quoted "in any form (written, visual, electronic, or audio)" up to and including **1,000 verses** without written permission. Three conditions apply: the verses must not amount to a **complete book**, they must not exceed **50% of the total text** of the work, and no more than **1,000 verses may be stored in an electronic retrieval system**. — [Documented]
2. **Amplified (AMP 2015 / AMPC 1987)** has the same terms: 1,000 verses, no complete book, no more than 50% of the work, and a 1,000-verse cap on electronic storage. — [Documented]
3. NASB full notice (title page, copyright page, or "the section where the quoted material appears"): "Scripture quotations taken from the (NASB®) New American Standard Bible®, Copyright © 1960, 1971, 1977, 1995, 2020 by The Lockman Foundation. Used by permission. All rights reserved. www.Lockman.org". Use only the last year that matches the edition quoted. For example, do not include 2020 if quoting NASB 1995. — [Documented]
4. AMP notice: "Scripture quotations taken from the Amplified® Bible (AMP), Copyright © 2015 by The Lockman Foundation. Used by permission. www.Lockman.org". AMPC notice: "…Amplified® Bible Classic (AMPC), Copyright © 1954, 1958, 1962, 1964, 1965, 1987…". — [Documented]
5. Abbreviation rules by medium (NASB and AMP alike): in not-for-sale print (bulletins, posters) and not-for-sale digital media (slides, digital orders of service), "(NASB)" or "(AMP)" after the quote is enough. On social media, blogs, and email the abbreviation is allowed with a click-enabled link to www.Lockman.org "when possible." **Web pages or apps must use the full copyright notice** with a clickable www.Lockman.org link. — [Documented]
6. Video, audio, and streaming must carry a spoken or included notice. If downloadable, the notice must also appear, with a link, on the web page and in the app. For merchandise the notice can go on a tag or packaging. — [Documented]
7. **AI Usage & Attribution (AI-specific)**: AI systems and AI-powered applications ("including but not limited to Microsoft Copilot, OpenAI ChatGPT, Anthropic Claude, and Google Gemini") may quote **up to 1,000 verses in a single response**, which "may constitute up to 100% of that response". This covers NASB 1995, NASB 2020, AMP, NBLA, and LBLA. — [Documented]
8. AI attribution requirement: each quotation must name the translation with its abbreviation: (NASB 1995), (NASB 2020), (AMP), (NBLA), or (LBLA). For responses over 10 verses, or where hyperlinks are possible, Lockman "request[s]" a link to lockman.org or the translation page. — [Documented]
9. AI scope: the grant covers "dynamic, user-generated queries, including retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), grounding, search, citation, and quotation". It does **not** authorize bulk downloading, scraping, standalone datasets, or using the translations for "training, fine-tuning, improving, evaluating, or developing" AI or ML models without prior written permission. — [Documented]
10. Not listed in the AI grant: **AMPC (Amplified Classic), NASB 1977, and the Legacy Standard Bible (LSB)**, although LSB is one of Lockman's translations. Only the five editions named are covered. — [Documented (by omission) / Inferred]
11. Spanish translations NBLA and LBLA have stricter non-AI gratis limits: **500 verses**, no complete book, and no more than **25%** of the work. Commercial print requests over the limit go to HarperCollins Christian Publishing, and non-commercial requests go to Lockman. — [Documented]
12. Website (non-Bible) content: AI systems may quote "insubstantial portions" of articles with the attribution "Used with permission from The Lockman Foundation. www.Lockman.org"; this is not bulk reproduction. — [Documented]
13. Trademarks ("NASB," "Amplified," "AMP," "AMPC," "LBLA," "NBLA," the lighthouse logo, and others) need Lockman's permission to use. Lockman describes itself as a "nondenominational Christian ministry." — [Documented]
14. For a Claude-based bot: NASB 1995/2020 and AMP are the only translations among these sources with **explicit AI-response permission**. A bot can quote them in chat if it labels each quote with the exact edition abbreviation, adds a lockman.org link when over 10 verses or when links render, and never stores more than 1,000 verses in its own retrieval index. Note that the AI clause permits 1,000 verses per response, while the general app rule requires the full notice. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- Publisher's own terms. The page is undated, so the AI clause could change, and it should be rechecked before launch.
- The AI clause and the general "apps must use full notice" rule could overlap or conflict for an AI app. The page does not reconcile them.
- No public-domain comparison is offered, since this is a commercial translation rights page.

## Best for
- The only explicit AI-chatbot Bible-quotation permission among the assigned sources: exact verse caps, abbreviation labels, RAG scope, and the no-training limit.
