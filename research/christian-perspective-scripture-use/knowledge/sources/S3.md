## Learned points
1. The hub offers free RCBT manuals and therapist and participant workbooks in Christian, Jewish, Muslim, Hindu, and Buddhist versions, plus conventional CBT manuals ("Research Only") and an Urdu translation of the Muslim manual. It was developed under a John Templeton Foundation grant (2011–2014). A newer series includes *Human Flourishing* titles, among them a Christian title "available in fall 2026" and one "for Non-Believers". — [Documented]
2. The Christian manual is a "10-Session Treatment Manual for Depression in Clients with Chronic Physical Illness" (Ciarrocchi, Schechter, Pearce, Koenig, 2014). It is described as a variant of A. T. Beck's protocol. Sessions are 50 minutes and can be delivered by phone or Skype. — [Documented]
3. Session sequence: (1) Assessment and intro to RCBT; (2) Behavioral Activation "Walking by Faith"; (3) Identifying unhelpful thoughts "Battlefield of the Mind"; (4) Challenging thoughts "Bringing All Thoughts Captive"; (5) Loss; (6) Spiritual struggles and negative emotions; (7) Gratitude; (8) Altruism and generosity; (9) Stress-related and spiritual growth; (10) Hope and relapse prevention. — [Documented]
4. Core integration model: conventional CBT is "the square that fits into" religiously integrative CBT (a square inside a cube). Counselors frame material in standard CBT terms and also listen with a "third ear" for faith framing. — [Documented]
5. Scripture-pairing method: each session has a weekly **memory verse** tied to the CBT skill. For example, Philippians 4:8 goes with thought monitoring, 2 Corinthians 5:7 ("walk by faith") with behavioral activation, 2 Corinthians 10:5 ("lead every thought… captive") with disputing, Isaiah 26:3, and 1 Thessalonians 5:18 with gratitude. Clients post the verse where they will see it and recite it at the next session. Romans 12:2 ("renewing of your minds") and *metanoia* ("change your mind," Matthew 4:17) are used to argue that the CBT model fits Christian teaching. — [Documented]
6. ABCDE disputing with faith: after standard disputing (evidence for or against, evidence you can cope), a "Religious Beliefs and Resources" step asks how "your view of God, your Christian worldview, the Bible and religious writings, spiritual wisdom" give counter-evidence. Religious *practices* (prayer, meditation, talking with someone who shares the client's values) are also used as coping resources. — [Documented]
7. **Contemplative prayer** is the faith-adapted mindfulness practice. Instead of focusing on the breath, the person focuses on a verse, notices mind-wandering without self-criticism ("boats sailing along a deep river"), and brings attention back. The steps: choose a scripture, sit upright, read slowly three times (savor, apply, listen), journal. Home practice is 20 minutes a day. — [Documented]
8. Client-centered rule: therapists should "begin where clients are in terms of their spirituality". They should avoid arguing about religious matters and introduce explicitly religious material gradually "as they are able to receive it". The manual says "Never argue with the client." — [Documented]
9. Spiritual assessment questions in session 1 cover the role of faith, attendance, prayer, Bible reading, and how the presenting issue affects spirituality. The goal is to learn the client's tradition, language, and symbols so integration is client-centered. If asked about the therapist's own beliefs, the therapist returns the focus to the client's faith. — [Documented]
10. Spiritual struggles session: it normalizes questioning God, feeling abandoned by God, and anger at God ("God created us to experience a range of emotions and He can accept all of them"). It assesses whether the client feels "God is punishing" or "abandoning" them (RCOPE items) and uses reflective listening. — [Documented]
11. Behavioral elements kept from CBT: an activity and mood monitor (every 2 hours), a 0–10 mood rating, a daily thought log, and "home practice activities" (renamed from "homework" to be more inviting). When homework is missed, the therapist praises partial effort and takes responsibility rather than letting the client feel they failed. — [Documented]
12. Gratitude exercise: a letter, call, or visit to a living person. Naming God as the recipient is "not a necessary or even a desirable part of the exercise." This is an example of keeping the evidence-based mechanism intact while adding faith. — [Documented]
13. Crisis handling in the manual is brief: give emergency contact numbers before treatment, and stabilize and ensure safety if an emergency arises. — [Documented]
14. Verses in the manual and workbook come from several translations and carry no labels. Some read like NIV (Phil 4:8) or Amplified-style (2 Cor 10:5, James 1:2), and there is no copyright notice. — [Inferred from text comparison; no translation stated]
15. Evidence on site: the Research Publications page lists the RCT papers. Examples are Koenig et al. 2015, *J Nerv Ment Dis* 203(4):243–251, "Religious vs. conventional cognitive-behavioral therapy for major depression in persons with chronic medical illness", and Pearce et al. 2015, *Psychotherapy* 52(1):56–66. Secondary-outcome papers cover optimism, gratitude, generosity, purpose in life, spiritual struggles, therapeutic alliance, and inflammatory markers. **No effect sizes, sample sizes, or results summaries appear on the pages read**, so outcomes cannot be stated from this source. — [Documented (citations only)]
16. Implication for an AI bot: the bot can pair one short verse with one CBT step (thought record, behavioral activation, gratitude), invite rather than impose, and offer contemplative-prayer-style verse meditation as an alternative to breath focus. It must not present this as therapy, since RCBT is a clinician-delivered 10-session treatment. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- The manual targets clinically depressed adults with chronic medical illness in a research trial, delivered by trained therapists. It is not a self-help or AI protocol.
- Some language is strongly evangelical ("We are at war!", replacing thoughts with "the Truth of God"), which may not suit every Christian or a non-denominational tone.
- Templeton-funded and authored by RCBT developers, so there is possible allegiance bias. Outcome data were not available on the pages read, and the publications list is dated (2012–2016, several "in press").

## Best for
- A concrete, clinically grounded template for pairing one Scripture verse with each CBT skill, and client-led, non-argumentative faith integration.
