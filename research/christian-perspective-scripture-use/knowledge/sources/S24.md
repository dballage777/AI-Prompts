## Learned points
1. CAPS calls itself "the nation's largest nonprofit association of Christians in the counseling and behavioral sciences"; founded 1956, "more than 1200 members" in the US, Canada and more than 25 other countries. — [Documented]
2. Members include psychologists, counselors, MFTs, social workers, psychiatrists, pastoral and lay counselors, researchers and students, from "a variety of denominations ... yet are united in their commitment to Christ and to professional excellence" — a working example of a non-denominational Christian identity. — [Documented]
3. Integration purpose (Bylaws Art. III): promote "study of the integration of Christianity and the behavioral sciences at both theoretical and applied levels," plus fellowship, member wellbeing, and treating mental-health disciplines "as avenues of ministry." — [Documented]
4. Statement of Faith (Bylaws Art. II): belief in God the Father, Jesus Christ the Son, the Holy Spirit, and the Bible as "our infallible guide of faith and conduct"; agreement is required for membership. Trinitarian, Bible-centered, with no denominational markers. — [Documented]
5. Ethics Statement grounds practice in biblical principles: humans made in God's image; a fallen world where "Difficulties, trials, tribulations, and dilemmas are ... normal and to be expected"; growth through trials; support, encourage, and confront "in love." Each principle cites verses (e.g., John 16:33, James 1:2-4, Galatians 6:1). — [Documented]
6. Key consent rule (4.1b): explicitly Christian interventions are grouped with treatments lacking established techniques; clients must be told of their novel nature, risks and alternatives; participation must be voluntary, and members must respect client autonomy, aware "not all clients will be receptive." Directly supports opt-in Scripture/prayer in a bot. — [Documented]
7. Competence (2.1): no services without proper education, training, supervised experience; seek extra training in new areas and "minimize the risk of harm" in emerging areas of practice. Supports a bot staying within scope and referring. — [Documented]
8. Non-discrimination (4.12): no discrimination by age, color, culture, disability, ethnic group, gender, race, marital or socioeconomic status; when client goals/identity/values conflict with the counselor's, "a referral to an appropriate colleague must be arranged." (Religion and sexual orientation are not in the listed categories.) — [Documented]
9. Advertising (8): no false or deceptive public statements; do not misrepresent credentials, nature of services, or research. Relevant to how a Christian wellness bot describes itself (not a counselor or therapist). — [Documented]
10. Other standards: confidentiality with limits explained up front (3); termination when clients are not benefiting or are harmed, with alternative referrals (4.10); avoid harmful multiple relationships (4.11); assessments only by trained people and with caution about "the limits of automated interpretations" ("Interpretation of Data" section). — [Documented]
11. The Standards are "aspirational"; CAPS "does not serve as an investigational or enforcement agency" and has no power to sanction members (membership may be lost if a license is revoked elsewhere). — [Documented]
12. Ethics Statement says the Statement of Faith is in "Article 11" of the Constitution and By-Laws, but the current Bylaws page puts it in Article II — a minor internal inconsistency. — [Documented]
13. Resources: peer-reviewed Journal of Psychology and Christianity (quarterly; "over 250 college and university libraries" subscribe), IVP Academic book series of 38 integration books, CAPS Conversations video podcasts, annual conference. — [Documented]
14. Implication for a bot: model a "Christ-centered, professionally excellent, consent-first" stance — Scripture offered as an option, evidence-based methods kept primary, referral when values conflict or needs exceed scope. — [Inferred]

## Limitations / bias
- Advocacy for Christian integration by a faith-based membership body; not neutral on religion.
- Ethics Statement dates from 2005, is aspirational and unenforced; written for licensed humans, not AI.
- No research findings or numbers on effectiveness of Christian interventions on the pages read; JPC articles and member-only resources not accessed.
- Home page has no explicit "statement of integration"; integration is described through the bylaws purpose and About page.
## Best for
- A Christian professional body's model for consent-first, non-denominational faith integration and when to refer.
