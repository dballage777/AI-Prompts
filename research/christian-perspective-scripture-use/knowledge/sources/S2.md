## Learned points
1. **Thomas Nelson translations** (NKJV, NCV, ICB, NET [commercial use only], The Expanded Bible, The Voice): you may quote "in any form (written, visual, electronic, or audio)" up to **500 verses or less** without written permission. Scripture must not make up **more than 25% of the total text** of the work, must not account for **more than 50% of an entire book of the Bible**, and must not appear in a commentary or other Biblical reference work. Scripture must be properly cited. — [Documented]
2. Thomas Nelson: "If more than 50% of the Bible is being reproduced, regardless of verse count, written permission is required." The gratis guidelines apply only if *all* conditions are met. Otherwise you must submit the HarperCollins Christian Permission Request Form. — [Documented]
3. **Zondervan translations** (NIV for commercial use in North America; NIrV in North America, UK & Commonwealth, EEC, and EFTA): you may quote in any form up to and including **500 verses**. The verses must not amount to **more than 50% of a complete book**, nor account for **25% or more of the total text** of the work, and must not be in a commentary or reference work. "This permission is contingent upon an appropriate copyright acknowledgment." — [Documented]
4. NIV edition rule: permission covers "the latest edition of the NIV text only (currently 2011)". Earlier NIV editions (e.g., 1984) are not granted. — [Documented]
5. Not covered by gratis use (both groups): standalone-verse products (artwork, note cards, crafts, novelty products, jewelry); study-Bible charts, maps, notes, and reference material; Scripture in lyrics or music; and use of more than 50% of a book or the complete translation. — [Documented]
6. Required notice wording, NKJV: "Scripture taken from the New King James Version®. Copyright © 1982 by Thomas Nelson. Used by permission. All rights reserved." — [Documented]
7. Required notice wording, NIV: "Scripture quotations taken from The Holy Bible, New International Version®, NIV®. Copyright © 1973, 1978, 1984, 2011 by Biblica, Inc. Used with permission of Zondervan. All rights reserved worldwide. www.zondervan.com". The page also gives notices for NIrV (© 1995, 1996, 1998, 2014 Biblica), NCV (© 2005 Thomas Nelson), ICB (© 1986, 1988, 1999, 2015), NET (© 1996, 2019 Biblical Studies Press, http://netbible.com), The Voice (© 2012 Ecclesia Bible Society), and The Expanded Bible (© 2011). — [Documented]
8. NET non-commercial use (not selling and not receiving donations) is handled by the NET Bible itself (https://netbible.com/faq/), not by HarperCollins. — [Documented]
9. Using one of their translations in a **smartphone application or other digital product**, or using a translation in its entirety, requires contacting the **Licensing department**. The gratis rules do not cover app embedding. For NIV/NIrV use outside the U.S. and Canada, and for "electronic rights," requests go to Biblica (https://www.biblica.com/permissions/). — [Documented]
10. The page contains **no AI-specific terms** (nothing on chatbots, AI-generated responses, RAG, or training). For an AI wellness bot quoting NIV or NKJV, the default gratis limits plus the "digital product → Licensing / Biblica electronic rights" route are the only stated guidance. — [Documented (absence) / Inferred (implication)]
11. The page does not describe an abbreviation-only shortcut (such as just "(NIV)") for digital use. Its church-use section allows stand-alone quotations in sermons, bulletins, newsletters, or on screens "provided the source is accurately cited (see Copyright Notice section)". — [Documented]
12. Church photocopying of book content: one chapter or less than 10% of the work, up to 20 copies, with a copyright notice on each copy. Book (non-Bible) excerpts have no general gratis policy; requests over 250 words from one source may carry a fee. Turnaround for permission requests averages 6–8 weeks. — [Documented]
13. Licensing page (summary-only): it mentions licensed products "from audiobooks to applications for smart devices" and "electronic reproduction" rights, but it says nothing specific about Bible text, websites, or AI. — [Documented, summary-only]
14. For a bot: quoting NIV or NKJV verse-by-verse in chat likely stays far under 500 verses per response. Whether a commercial chatbot counts as a "digital product" needing a license is unresolved on this page. The safest reading is to keep quotes short, label the translation, and give the full notice in the bot's about or terms text, or else prefer public-domain text. — [Inferred]
