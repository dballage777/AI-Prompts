## Learned points
1. The NLT terms ARE on this page (the page is run under Tyndale). It describes the NLT as a translation from Hebrew and Greek by 90 scholars "into natural, everyday English". — Documented
2. NLT print/eBook limit: up to and including 500 verses may be quoted without written permission, if the quoted verses are not more than 25 percent of the work and no complete book of the Bible is quoted. — Documented
3. Anything beyond 500 verses or 25%, or any other request, needs written approval from Tyndale House Publishers (permission@tyndale.com or the Permissions Questionnaire). — Documented
4. Required credit line (copyright or title page) when using NLT only: "Scripture quotations are taken from the Holy Bible, New Living Translation, copyright © 1996, 2004, 2015 by Tyndale House Ministries. Used by permission. All rights reserved." — Documented
5. With multiple translations: "Scripture quotations marked (NLT) are taken from…" plus the same notice. With multiple translations where the NLT is the majority: "Unless otherwise indicated, all Scripture quotations are taken from…" plus the same notice. — Documented
6. Non-salable media (church bulletins, orders of service, newsletters, screen projections, "or similar media"): no complete notice is needed, but the initials "(NLT)" must appear at the end of each quotation. — Documented
7. Commercial commentaries or Bible reference works using the NLT always need written permission. — Documented
8. Online sermons and Bible teaching: up to 50 verses per recording, Scripture up to 25% of spoken content, not commercial or monetized, with the full attribution in each post's description. — Documented
9. Online formats (podcasts, YouTube, social media) where Bible content is over 25%: up to 250 verses per recording, no more than 30 NLT-containing posts up at a time, no post may contain an entire book, not commercial or monetized, with attribution in each description. Commercial or monetized, mainly-NLT content may need a fee and a permission letter or license. — Documented
10. Accuracy rule: NLT quotes must be exact, including punctuation, capitalization, and italics. Any intended style deviation must be disclosed to Tyndale in writing before publication. — Documented
11. Permission is specific to the requested use and cannot be passed on to another person. — Documented
12. Music must quote verses word for word, and merchandise or artwork needs the questionnaire. — Documented
13. Apps: "If you are interested in including any of Tyndale's Bible translations in your app, please visit api.Bible". No AI- or chatbot-specific terms appear on the page. An AI bot is closest to the "app" or "online" categories, so its status is unclear. — Documented (absence) / Inferred (fit)
14. KJV: the text is public domain and "can be quoted without permission from the publishers". Notes, commentary, typesetting, and other extra-biblical material in a given edition stay under that publisher's copyright. — Documented
15. Non-Bible book excerpts (Hendrickson, Tyndale, NavPress, and Focus on the Family titles): noncommercial up to 500 words or 2 pages per title and under 10% of the work. Classroom or small group (under 50 participants) up to 5 pages, not sold. Commercial up to 250 words, not a stand-alone product, not a whole chapter, and under 5% of the work. Each requires a "Some content taken from (Title)…" notice. These are publisher-granted terms, distinct from statutory fair use (see S18). — Documented
16. Implication for a bot: short, accurate NLT quotes marked "(NLT)" are the closest match to the page's non-salable or online rules. A per-chat app use is not clearly covered, so the safest default is KJV (public domain) or confirming via api.Bible or Tyndale. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- These are the publisher's own terms (the rights holder's view). They may change, and Tyndale's own NLT site may differ.
- The page has a typo: the Hendrickson credit line repeats "represented by Tyndale House Publishers" twice.
- There are no AI-specific terms, and app use is redirected to api.Bible, whose terms were not read.
- KJV note: the "public domain" statement is this publisher's statement for its own context. Status outside the US was not addressed on the page.

## Best for
- Exact NLT verse limits, the 25% rule, required notice wording, the "(NLT)" per-quote rule, and confirmation that the KJV is public domain. These are the core facts for the bot's translation-quoting rules.
