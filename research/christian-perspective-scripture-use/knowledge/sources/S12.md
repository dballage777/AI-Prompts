## Learned points
1. Access: call 988, text 988, or chat at chat.988lifeline.org. Available 24/7/365 and free. Conversations are confidential, with limits (old S2; old S8). — Documented (summary-only)
2. It is not only for suicide. Per SAMHSA: emotional distress, anxiety, depression, loneliness, stress, relationship troubles, substance use, "and just needing someone to talk with"; "no reason too big or too small." People can also contact 988 about a loved one (old S8; old S2). — Documented
3. Special access lines: press 1 or text 838255 for the Veterans Crisis Line; press 2 or text AYUDA for Spanish; press 3 or text PRIDE for LGBTQ+ youth (the footer says this option "has been restored"); ASL by videophone; TTY via relay or 711 then 988; phone interpretation in 240+ languages, per SAMHSA (old S2; old S8). — Documented (summary-only)
4. What happens: a counselor introduces themselves, asks about your safety, listens, offers support, and shares resources. SAMHSA says most contacts are resolved without 911 (old S2; old S8). — Documented
5. 911 boundary, from SAMHSA rather than 988lifeline.org: call 911 for an attempt in progress, a specific plan with means and intent to act now, a suspected overdose, or medical symptoms such as chest pain (old S8). — Documented
6. Confidentiality limits: information may be shared when there is "imminent risk of harm to you or to someone else." Confidential is not the same as anonymous (old S2). — Documented (summary-only)
7. Helping someone else, five steps: Ask; Be there; Help keep them safe; Help them connect; Follow up. Re-read today: the page calls them "evidence-based action steps" and links elsewhere for details (old S2; re-read 2026-11-12). — Documented (summary-only)
8. Faith-community resources: NONE FOUND on 988lifeline.org. The home page link inventory, Best Practices, Professional Initiatives (#BeThe1To, Follow Up Matters, Zero Suicide, Lifeline for Attempt Survivors, Strength After), Get Involved, and Help Someone Else contain no page, link, or mention for faith communities, faith leaders, clergy, places of worship, or religious organizations. Site searches for "faith" and "religious" returned no faith-community page. The extraction may not show every search result. — Documented (absence, summary-only)
9. The only religion mention found: the Risk Factors page lists, as a suicide risk factor, "Cultural and religious beliefs, such as the belief that suicide is a noble resolution of a personal dilemma." No protective-factors list was on that page. — Documented (summary-only)
10. Other risk factors on that page relevant to a career and self-help bot: "Job or financial loss," "Loss of relationship(s)," "Hopelessness," "Lack of social support and sense of isolation," "Stigma associated with asking for help," and "Easy access to lethal means." — Documented (summary-only)
11. Network policy on the Best Practices page (Suicide Safety Policy): "Utilize least invasive interventions," and engage emergency services involuntarily "only as a last resort." Policy PDFs (2024) and follow-up resources are listed but were not read. — Documented (summary-only)
12. Faith-community suicide-prevention material exists elsewhere, not on 988lifeline.org. The APA Foundation's Faith Resources list (S11) points to the National Action Alliance for Suicide Prevention's "Faith.Hope.Life." initiative and to SAMHSA's faith-based engagement pages. — Documented (via S11)
13. Bot implications: give 988 with all three channels and the special lines; say it is for any distress, not only suicide, and for worried loved ones; do not claim 988 has a faith-specific or Christian line, because none was found. If a user's religious belief frames suicide as noble or deserved, treat that as a risk signal and do not debate it theologically. Job loss and relationship loss are named risk factors, so career distress plus hopelessness should prompt a safety check. — Inferred
