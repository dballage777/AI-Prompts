## Learned points
1. Method: a multi-part study by Lifeway Research, co-sponsored by Focus on the Family. It surveyed:
   - 1,000 senior Protestant pastors
   - 355 Protestant Americans diagnosed with moderate or severe depression, bipolar disorder or schizophrenia (including 200 churchgoers)
   - 207 Protestant family members of people with acute mental illness
   - and ran 15 in-depth expert interviews.
   The page as summarized gives no dates, mode or margin of error. — Research (summary-only)
2. Framing statistic: about 1 in 4 Americans has a mental illness in a given year (citing NAMI). — Reported (summary-only)
3. 66% of senior pastors seldom speak to their congregation about mental illness: 39% rarely, 10% never, 16% once a year. — Research (summary-only)
4. 22% of pastors say they are reluctant to help people with acute mental illness because it takes too much time. — Research (summary-only)
5. Church preparedness, as reported by pastors:
   - 27% have a plan to assist affected families, but only 21% of family members know of one.
   - 14% have a counselor skilled in mental illness on staff.
   - 13% train leaders to recognize mental illness.
   - 68% keep a list of local mental health resources, but only 28% of families know it exists.
   — Research (summary-only)
6. 65% of family members and 59% of people with mental illness want their church to talk openly about mental illness. — Research (summary-only)
7. The page links to an earlier Lifeway finding that about half of evangelicals believe mental illness can be cured by prayer and Bible reading alone. The figure and method were NOT captured from this page and need the linked source before citing. — Reported (summary-only; secondary)
8. Implication for a bot: many Christian users may come from churches that rarely discuss mental illness, and some may believe prayer alone should cure serious illness. The bot should affirm prayer and faith as support while saying clearly that professional treatment is compatible with faith, and should be the one to offer referral resources (988, clinicians), since church referral lists often go unknown. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- The data is from 2014, before 988, the COVID-19 pandemic, and newer Lifeway mental-health studies. Treat it as historical baseline.
- Protestant-only sample, and the research arm belongs to a Christian publisher/denominational entity. Co-sponsored by Focus on the Family.
- Only summary-level access, with no verbatim text. The headline "1 in 4 pastors" figure was not captured in the summary.
## Best for
- Evidence that churches often under-address mental illness and that referral lists go unknown, which justifies a bot that normalizes professional care for Christian users.
