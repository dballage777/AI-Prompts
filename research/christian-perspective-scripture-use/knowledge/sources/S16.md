## Learned points
1. The partnership is a collaboration between psychiatrists and clergy meant to foster dialogue between the two fields, reduce stigma, and account for both medical and spiritual dimensions of care-seeking. Convened by the APA, the APA Foundation, and the Interfaith Disability Advocacy Coalition (a program of the American Association of People with Disabilities). — Documented (summary-only)
2. Stated two-way learning: psychiatrists learn from spiritual leaders, whom people often turn to in mental distress, and faith leaders and their communities learn about evidence-based treatment for psychiatric illness. — Documented (summary-only)
3. Main resource is "Mental Health: A Guide for Faith Leaders" (second edition) with a companion Quick Reference Guide, plus essays (Aist, Peteet, Rennebohm, Gregg-Schroeder), a faith and mental health bibliography, the WPA Section on Religion, Spirituality and Psychiatry, and the Action Alliance for Suicide Prevention Faith Communities Task Force. — Documented (summary-only)
4. Hub page itself gives no formal referral protocol or role definitions. The summary reported only a contact person. — Documented (summary-only)
5. Prevalence figures in the Quick Reference (US, past year): any mental illness affects more than 1 in 5 people, serious mental illness 1 in 20, and substance use disorder 1 in 6. Suicide is listed as the 11th leading cause of death in the US. Cited references include SAMHSA. — Documented
6. Observable signs fall into five categories: cognition, affect/mood, speech, thought patterns/logic, and appearance. The guide warns these "are not definitive signs of mental illness" and further clinical assessment may be needed. The lesson for a bot is to describe concern, not diagnose. — Documented
7. Communication basics: speak slowly and clearly, show empathy and compassion, treat the person with respect, remember that feelings and thoughts are real even if not based in reality, and praise progress no matter how small. — Documented
8. Responses matched to presentation. Loss of hope: instill hope for a positive end result and build personal connection. Anxiety or panic: stay calm, reassure, and seek to understand. Anger: listen, defuse, and avoid threats and confrontation. Trouble concentrating: be brief, repeat, and clarify. — Documented
9. Crisis rule: seek immediate help if a person poses a danger to self or others ("call 988"). An actively suicidal person is called a psychiatric emergency. — Documented
10. Warning signs of suicide: talk or writing about death; hopelessness, helplessness, or worthlessness; more alcohol or drug use; withdrawal; reckless behavior; dramatic mood changes. Risk factors: losses (death, financial or legal trouble, breakup, bullying), prior attempts, trauma or abuse, firearms in the home, chronic illness or pain, exposure to others' suicidal behavior, and family history. — Documented
11. Referral triage looks at three things: level of distress, level of functioning (self-care, problem solving), and possibility of danger to self or others. — Documented
12. Referral tips: have a list of professionals, communicate clearly about the need, make the referral a collaborative process, reassure the person you will journey with them, follow up and stay connected, and offer community resources and support groups. — Documented
13. Faith leaders are told to "Be clear about the difference between spiritual support and professional clinical care". This directly supports a bot rule that spiritual encouragement is not treatment. — Documented
14. Resistance to help can come from stigma, denial, past experience, hopelessness, cultural issues, "or religious concepts". Responses: learn about treatment to dispel misunderstandings, share recovery stories, reassure that people can recover, and, for a person of faith, ask how faith can give them strength to take steps toward healing. — Documented
15. Implication for an AI bot: faith can be framed as a source of strength that moves a person toward care, not as a substitute for it. The bot should not touch users physically (not applicable) and should not escalate confrontation. — Inferred
