## Learned points
1. Status: the WEB is "a Public Domain (no copyright) Modern English translation"; you "may freely copy it in any form, including electronic and print formats." — Documented
2. Permitted uses listed on the copyright page include copy, publish, distribute, sell, quote, broadcast, transmit, post on the Internet, preach and teach "as much as you want"; no royalty, no verse limit, no required notice. — Documented
3. Trademark condition: "World English Bible" is a trademark; the name and logo may be used "only to identify faithful copies" of the public-domain text published at eBible.org, WorldEnglish.Bible and WorldEnglishBible.org. If you change the text or punctuation, you may not call it the World English Bible. — Documented
4. FAQ on use in a book/tract/web site: "Yes, you may use any portion or all of the World English Bible"; the only ask is not to call altered text the WEB. The FAQ also asks users to get a recent copy (eBible.org, WorldEnglish.Bible or the Digital Bible Library) so typo corrections are included. — Documented
5. The FAQ states the WEB has been published electronically since 1997 with public-domain statements, and refers to a Creative Commons CC0 declaration among its dedications. — Documented
6. Editions: Classic (USA spelling, "Yahweh"), Updated WEBU (USA, "LORD"), British Edition WEBBE ("LORD"); otherwise the same text. Subsets: Protestant 66-book (WEBP, WEBPB) and Catholic (WEBC); the standard ecumenical edition includes Deuterocanon/Apocrypha. Abbreviations listed: WEBP, WEBC, WEBPB, WEBBE, WEB, WEBU, WMB, WMBB. — Documented
7. Non-denominational stance: the homepage says the WEB "is suitable for use by all true Christians in all denominations" and offers book-set choices rather than an authoritative answer on disputed books. — Documented
8. Basis and philosophy: based on the 1901 American Standard Version, Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia OT and Greek Majority Text NT; the FAQ says it is "closer to formal equivalence than dynamic equivalence," uses contractions and a spoken register. — Documented
9. The FAQ explains why copyright matters: most modern translations cannot be posted whole online without permission; one publisher reportedly quoted "$10,000 + $10/copy distributed." — Reported (editor's anecdote)
10. eBible.org legal page: modern translations are copyrighted "unless they have been explicitly dedicated to the Public Domain"; other translations hosted on eBible.org carry their own licences, so users must check each work's "about" page. — Documented
11. The legal page notes that original-language Bible texts are public domain due to age, but new translations are presumed copyrighted. — Documented
12. Implication for a bot: the WEB (especially WEBU or WEBP, which use "LORD") can be quoted freely with a "(WEB)" label; if the bot alters wording, it must not label it WEB. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- Publisher's own statements, written from an advocacy position for free Bible distribution; the FAQ is partly opinion (for example on publishers' motives).
- The homepage and legal page list slightly different domain sets for the trademark (the homepage adds WorldEnglishBible.org); the substance is the same.
- The WEB is largely a single-editor-led project (the FAQ addresses this); accuracy reviews from outside scholars were not checked here.
- Classic WEB uses "Yahweh," which may feel unfamiliar to many US readers.

## Best for
- Confirming that the WEB is freely quotable, plus the one trademark condition: do not call altered text the World English Bible.
