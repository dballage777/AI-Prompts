## Learned points
1. The Berean Bible and Majority Bible texts (Berean Standard Bible, BSB; plus Berean Literal, Interlinear and Majority Standard Bible sites) were "officially dedicated to the public domain as of April 30, 2023." — Documented
2. "All uses are freely permitted." There is no verse-count limit, no percentage-of-work limit and no requirement for a per-quote copyright notice. — Documented
3. An attribution notice is offered but labeled "appreciated but not required": "The Holy Bible, Berean Standard Bible, BSB is produced in cooperation with Bible Hub, Discovery Bible, OpenBible.com, and the Berean Bible Translation Committee. This text of God's Word has been dedicated to the public domain." — Documented
4. Linking to berean.bible, interlinearbible.com, literalbible.com, bereanbible.com and majoritybible.com is likewise "appreciated but not required." — Documented
5. Derivatives: public-domain material "may be freely reproduced, integrated, and adapted for both free and commercial resources." — Documented
6. Naming rule (a request, not a legal condition on the page): applications that keep the verbatim text are "invited to bear the Berean name" (apps, audio, video, print, commentaries with cited verses); for derivative works that vary from the official text, the publisher "respectfully request[s] that the Berean name is not used." — Documented
7. Print: developers and publishers may produce and sell the full Berean Bible in any print format. — Documented
8. Licensing page: "Licensing is not required for any use"; an optional licensing form exists only to receive communications about new resources. — Documented
9. The page contains no AI-, chatbot- or app-specific restrictions; digital and app use falls under "All uses are freely permitted." — Documented (absence noted)
10. The page gives no formal trademark statement; the footer says only "Berean Standard Bible." — Documented (absence noted)
11. Implication for a bot: BSB can be quoted in any amount without a notice; best practice is to label quotes "(BSB)" and to quote verbatim, and if the bot paraphrases or alters wording it should not present the result as the BSB. — Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- Publisher's own statement; it does not name the legal instrument (for example CC0) on the page itself, so the exact mechanism of dedication was not verified here.
- Very short page; says nothing about translation philosophy, accuracy or denominational stance (not needed for permissions, but relevant to choosing a default translation).
- The naming request is phrased as a polite request, not a binding term.

## Best for
- Confirming that the BSB is a modern-English translation a bot can quote freely, with no verse limits or required notice.
