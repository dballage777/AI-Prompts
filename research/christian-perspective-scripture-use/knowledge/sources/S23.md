## Learned points
1. Landing page copyright field: "Public domain in the USA." The KJV (eBook #10) can be quoted in any amount in the US with no verse cap, percentage cap, or required notice. — [Documented]
2. eBook header: free for anyone "in the United States and most other parts of the world"; if not in the US, "you will have to check the laws of the country where you are located before using this eBook." — [Documented]
3. License 1.D: the Foundation "makes no representations concerning the copyright status of any work in any country other than the United States." Gutenberg does not mention UK Crown/Letters Patent rights in the KJV on the pages read, so non-US status is simply flagged as the user's responsibility. — [Documented]
4. Permission How-to "US only": not everything public domain in the US is public domain elsewhere; operators outside the US should get professional guidance. Fine for a US-adult bot, a caveat if it serves other countries. — [Documented]
5. Quotes need no permission: "No permission is needed to use quotes from Project Gutenberg items," including commercial use; citing Gutenberg is optional ("your choice"). — [Documented]
6. Whole-text use: you may do "practically ANYTHING in the United States with eBooks not protected by U.S. copyright law" (derivative works, reports, research), but redistribution under the "Project Gutenberg" name is subject to the trademark license. — [Documented]
7. Trademark rule: if you strip all references to Project Gutenberg (license 1.C), the license does not restrict copying the public-domain text in the US; if you keep the name and charge money, a royalty of 20% of gross profits applies (1.E.8). Practical takeaway: when embedding KJV text in a product, remove the Gutenberg header/footer and branding. — [Documented]
8. "No sweat of the brow": Gutenberg claims no copyright for markup, formatting or spelling modernization, and says no one else can claim it in the US either. — [Documented]
9. This eBook is a Gutenberg edition, not a guaranteed match to a specific print source; Gutenberg may modernize spelling or reformat, so verse wording should be checked if exactness matters. — [Documented]
10. Site access rules: the website is "intended for human users only"; automated access risks an IP block. Bulk downloads must use mirrors or the robot harvest endpoint (wget with 2-second wait example). Deep-linking to files is blocked; link to the landing page. Relevant if a bot pipeline would fetch Scripture live — download once instead. — [Documented]
11. Auto-generated summary on the landing page says this edition "contains 80 books" (i.e., includes the Apocrypha), so not every book is in the 66-book Protestant canon; a non-denominational bot should be aware which books it quotes. — [Documented] (summary labeled auto-generated)
12. Terms of Use target audience: "United States persons over 13 years of age"; Gutenberg gives no warranty about copyright status or use outside the US. — [Documented]
13. Text format: verses are numbered "chapter:verse" in plain text (e.g., "30:4 And two golden rings..."), making it practical to build a local verse lookup. — [Documented]

## Limitations / bias
- Gutenberg speaks only to US law; it does not explain the KJV's separate UK status, so non-US rights are unresolved by this source.
- The archaic 1611-style language may be harder for some users; the source says nothing about readability for wellness use (only an automated "7th grade" reading-ease score).
- Gutenberg's text is not certified against a named print edition.
