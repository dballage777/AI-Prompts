## Learned points
1. Faith content: a full-text search of the extracted guide for spirituality, religion, prayer, faith, God and church found no matches. The guide is secular and faith-neutral; any Christian framing would be an addition, not something WHO says. — Documented (absence)
2. Purpose: a stress-management self-help guide "for anyone who experiences stress, wherever they live and whatever their circumstances"; a few minutes a day of practice; usable alone or with audio. Part of WHO's Self-Help Plus, based on acceptance and commitment therapy ideas (content by Russ Harris). — Documented
3. Values definition: values are your deepest desires for the sort of person you want to be; "Goals describe what you are trying to get. Values describe the sort of person you want to be." Goals may be impossible now, but values can always be acted on in small ways. — Documented
4. The guide's sample values list, offered as "not the 'right' values or the 'best' ones – they are simply some common ones": kind, caring, generous, supportive, helpful, patient, responsible, protective, disciplined, hardworking, committed, brave, persistent, forgiving, grateful, loyal, respectable/honourable, respectful, trustworthy, fair/just, plus blank lines for your own. Many overlap with Christian virtues (kindness, patience, forgiveness, gratitude, faithfulness/loyalty), which a bot could note as a bridge. — Documented / Inferred for the bridge
5. User choice is explicit: "You get to decide which values are most important to you!" A faith-based bot should therefore invite, not impose, faith-based values. — Documented / Inferred
6. Meaning and purpose: acting on values is linked to "a sense of meaning or purpose, of being more like the sort of person you want to be"; the back cover says "Even in the face of tremendous adversity, you can find meaning, purpose and vitality in your life." — Documented
7. Role-model exercise: think of "a role model in your community; someone you look up to"; ask what they say or do that you admire, which values they show, and which you would like to model. A Christian user might pick Jesus, a biblical figure or a mentor in their church; this is the user's choice, not WHO's suggestion. — Documented / Inferred
8. Values go both ways (self and others): "If you do not take care of yourself… it will be hard … to care for others"; values can be lived even when separated from family, by "little ways" with the people around you. — Documented
9. Tool 4 BEING KIND: notice and name unkind self-judgements ("I am weak," "It is my fault," "I can never forgive myself," "I do not deserve kindness," "My needs do not matter"), then speak to yourself kindly; small acts of kindness to others ("This is really painful. I am here for you.") matter; "like all values, kindness goes both ways." — Documented
10. Kind-hand exercise: imagine filling a hand with kindness and place it on the heart, belly, forehead or where pain is, and be kind to yourself through it. A bot could offer this alongside, not in place of, a user-requested short prayer or verse on God's care. — Documented / Inferred
11. Community: the guide opens by telling readers they are not alone ("there are many other people in your community and all around the world who are also struggling") and directs people to "trusted people in your community" when skills are not enough. For a Christian user, a pastor or church community can be one such trusted source, alongside health services. — Documented / Inferred
12. Skills a faith perspective could pair with (unchanged from the earlier note, re-checked): Grounding (Notice → Slow down and connect with body → Refocus and engage); Unhooking (Notice → Name → Refocus); Making Room (Notice → Name → Allow, breathing around the feeling); "Change what can be changed, accept the pain that cannot be changed, and live by your values." That last phrase is structurally similar to the widely known Serenity Prayer, though the guide does not cite or mention any prayer. — Documented / Inferred for the comparison
13. Breathing safety: breathe slowly and gently; if dizzy, breathe "as SLOWLY and SHALLOWLY as possible" and do not take deep breaths. Relevant if a bot pairs breathing with a breath prayer. — Documented
14. Escalation: if exercises are not enough to cope with intolerable stress, seek help from health or social services or trusted community members. No crisis lines or suicide guidance. — Documented
15. Licence: CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO: non-commercial use, citation required, adaptations under the same licence, and "there should be no suggestion that WHO endorses any specific organization, products or services." A commercial bot should teach the skills in its own words rather than reproduce or adapt guide text. — Documented / Inferred

## Limitations / bias
- Contains nothing on religion, spirituality or prayer; all faith pairings in this note are marked Inferred and come from this project, not WHO.
- Global humanitarian audience (refugees, conflict); examples need adapting for US adults and career stress.
- "Field test version"; no outcome data in the guide itself.
- One therapeutic school (ACT).
- Text extracted from an illustrated PDF; some quotes are speech-bubble fragments, so only clean running text is quoted.
- Licence limits reuse of the actual text in a commercial product.

## Best for
- A faith-neutral values and self-kindness framework (user-chosen values, role models, kindness both ways, trusted community) that a Christian bot can pair with Scripture without claiming WHO endorses faith practices.
