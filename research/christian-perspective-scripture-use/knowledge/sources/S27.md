## Learned points
1. Treat Bible quotes like any other source material. Verify them and record the source and page so they can be rechecked. — Documented (summary-only)
2. Name the translation you quote, and don't switch translations partway through without telling readers. The example given is a manuscript that silently moved from the NIV to the Good News Translation because the author had only that Bible with him. — Documented (summary-only)
3. Modern Bible translations are copyrighted. Per the article, the King James Version is the exception. — Documented (summary-only)
4. The KJV is public domain in the US and most of the world, but under Crown copyright in the UK, with limits on print use. This independently matches S26. — Documented (summary-only)
5. Verse limits differ by translation. The ESV allows up to 1,000 verses without permission if they are not a complete Bible book and not the whole text of the work; the NIV allows 500. Note that S28 lists the ESV at 500 verses, so check Crossway's current permissions page. — Documented (summary-only); conflict flagged
6. Books carry the translation's copyright notice on the copyright page. The ESV example starts "Unless otherwise noted Scripture quotations are from The Holy Bible, English Standard Version." The full required wording was not captured. — Documented (summary-only)
7. Publishers and Bible societies copyright translations to protect the text's integrity, and modern translations often cost over a million dollars to produce. This is the publisher-side rationale, the counterpoint to S28. — Reported (summary-only)
8. Proofread verse references. The article's example is a book citing Isaiah 28:8 when it meant Isaiah 25:8. — Documented (summary-only)
9. The article recommends Robert Hudson's *The Christian Writer's Manual of Style*, 4th ed. (Zondervan, 2016) as a reference. — Documented (summary-only)
10. A reader comment says some publishers (LifeWay, WestBow) require the translation to be named with each quoted passage. A per-quote version label is safe practice. — Reported (comment, summary-only)
11. Implication for a bot: label every verse with its translation (e.g., "John 3:16, KJV"), quote exact text, verify the reference, and default to public-domain translations to avoid needing permission. — Inferred
