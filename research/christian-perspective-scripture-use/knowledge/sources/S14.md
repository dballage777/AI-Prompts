## Learned points
1. Standard limit (the same for print, digital, and audio): the ESV may be quoted "up to and inclusive of five hundred (500) verses" without a formal license or written permission. — Documented
2. Three conditions also apply: the verses must not amount to "more than one-half of any one book of the Bible or its equivalent measured in bytes"; must not be "twenty-five percent (25%) or more of the total text of the work"; and must not be quoted "in a commentary or other biblical reference work." — Documented
3. Artwork and music apply the same limits per "single artwork product" or "single composition." Audio quotes must be verbatim ("completely conforming to and unchanged from the current ESV text"), and verbal credit must be given to the "ESV." — Documented
4. Digital "common uses" listed: Website, Blog, Social Media, Writing a book (Ebook). Print common uses: church bulletin, Bible studies, classroom use, print book. — Documented
5. Required notice for digital works (the same text as print and audio): "Scripture quotations are from the ESV® Bible (The Holy Bible, English Standard Version®), © 2001 by Crossway, a publishing ministry of Good News Publishers. ESV Text Edition: 2025. The ESV text may not be quoted in any publication made available to the public by a Creative Commons license. The ESV may not be translated in whole or in part into any other language. Used by permission. All rights reserved." — Documented
6. Placement: on print works, the title page or copyright page, "or in a corresponding location when the ESV is quoted in other media." For digital works, the notice "must appear as follows on digital works quoting from the ESV." It is a single work-level notice, not one per quote. — Documented; per-quote interpretation Inferred
7. Multiple translations: the notice should begin "Unless otherwise indicated, all Scripture quotations are from… [etc.]" or "Scripture quotations marked (ESV) are from… [etc.]." That implies each ESV quote is marked (ESV) when translations are mixed. — Documented / Inferred
8. Abbreviation rule: in non-saleable media ("church bulletins, orders of service, posters, transparencies, or similar media"), a full notice is not required, "but the initials (ESV) must appear at the end of the quotation." — Documented
9. Trademarks: "ESV" and "English Standard Version" are registered trademarks of Crossway, and "Use of either trademark requires the permission of Crossway." Naming the source of a quote is not addressed separately. — Documented
10. Restrictions: no quoting of ESV text in any publication released under a Creative Commons license; no translating the ESV into other languages. — Documented
11. ESV API: Crossway allows access to ESV text "from our server" to include "on your website or app, free of charge for non-commercial use," via api.esv.org. API terms were not read. — Documented
12. Beyond the guidelines: written permission or a formal license is required, requested through an online form. The digital form asks for the product/platform, the percentage of the product's text that will be ESV, and the individual verse count. — Documented
13. ESV Study Bible notes and other Crossway content: up to 400 words of study content may be quoted without permission "as long as the source is properly cited." More than 400 words, copies, or images need permission. Books, blogs, and tracts: up to 400 words, noncommercial, with credit. — Documented
14. AI, chatbots, and software: the page and forms contain no mention of artificial intelligence, machine learning, chatbots, or AI-generated output (searched; zero hits). Whether chatbot output counts as a "digital work" is not stated. — Documented (absence)
15. Bot implications: a bot that quotes the ESV should keep quotes short (single verses or short passages, far under 500 verses and the 25% limit). It should label each quote "(ESV)." The deploying product should carry the full Crossway notice in a fixed location, such as an About or credits page. The bot must not alter ESV wording, translate it, or present it inside Creative Commons-licensed content. Because AI use is unaddressed, the deployer should ask Crossway or choose a public-domain translation as the default. — Inferred
