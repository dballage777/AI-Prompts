## Learned points
1. [Documented] NoveList frames a patron's game preferences as a gateway to book recommendations: match game type to book characteristics using NoveList story elements (genres, themes, and appeals).
2. [Documented] First-person shooters (Fortnite, Overwatch): players like being in the middle of the action and using survival skills; try adventure, survival, spy, or thriller and suspense genres.
3. [Documented] RPGs (Legend of Zelda, Dragon Quest): driven by rich fantasy worlds and complicated plots and a journey with a chosen hero; try fantasy fiction with appeals world-building, descriptive, or lush. Adult example: Black Leopard, Red Wolf by Marlon James (described as lush and stylistically complex).
4. [Documented] Collection games (Pokémon, Animal Crossing): may gravitate to quirky and sweeping stories with a large cast of characters.
5. [Documented] World-building/management games (Minecraft, Frostpunk): players are architects of their own world; try NoveList's plot-your-own-stories choices (interactive fiction).
6. [Documented] Story/puzzle games (Undertale, Professor Layton): players like following clues; try mysteries with themes like "solve the puzzle" or locked room mysteries.
7. [Documented] Retro gamers enjoy nostalgia and pixel styles; try the theme "press play to start" (stories about players lost in a game world).
8. [Documented] NoveList offers "For fans of" Recommended Reads lists based on specific video games (examples: Animal Crossing, Five Nights at Freddy's, Hades, Among Us), found with the search "SU Video games AND ND Recommended Reads."
9. [Inferred] The method translates by experiential appeal (immersion in action, world richness, collecting, building/agency, puzzle-solving, nostalgia) rather than by subject, which parallels the appeal lists in S2.
