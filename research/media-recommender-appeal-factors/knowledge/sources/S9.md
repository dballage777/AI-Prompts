## Learned points
1. [Documented] NoveList's appeal vocabulary was "developed almost 15 years ago" (around 2009–2010, consistent with S14's spring 2009 start) and was overhauled after a three-year project, drawing on library-user feedback, data, and "a lot of passionate debate."
2. [Documented] The three stated goals were Distillation (focus on "the essence of the reading experience"), Information retrieval (consistent vocabulary for robust, relevant results), and Discoverability (connecting similar books).
3. [Documented] Category renames: Mood replaces Tone; Style replaces Writing Style; Characterization replaces Character.
4. [Documented] Pace is no longer its own category. Pace terms now sit inside Style, which treats pacing as a property of how the story is told.
5. [Documented] Merges: Accessible and Easy-to-understand (formerly split by audience level) become Accessible. Sympathetic and Relatable merge into Sympathetic "which creates more meaningful matches."
6. [Documented] Deleted as problematic, unclear or redundant: Courageous, Quirky, Awkward, Compelling, Engaging, Mystical, Jargon-filled. Several of these (Compelling, Engaging) are vague praise words rather than descriptive appeals.
7. [Documented] Renamed for clarity: Dramatic → Cinematic, meaning exciting, larger-than-life stories, kept distinct from High drama (back-stabbing social scenes, spiteful personalities). Users had confused the two.
8. [Documented] New terms: Fun read (beach reads, escapist, "popcorn fodder," lighter tone, effortless reading) and Unputdownable (one-sitting reads, often heavier or more sinister, higher stakes, more drama). Both are experience-outcome terms rather than content terms.
9. [Documented] A new Identities grouping spotlights 41+ character identity terms (e.g., Neurodivergent, Indigenous, Muslim).
10. [Inferred] Experience terms borrowed from film ("cinematic," "popcorn") suggest that appeal language is already cross-media.
11. [Inferred] For a recommender: avoid vague praise words, merge near-synonyms for better matching, and separate commonly confused concepts (cinematic scale vs interpersonal melodrama).
