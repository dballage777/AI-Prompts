## Learned points
1. [Research] Sample: 300 random threads (from 4,957 crawled 1 Jun to 3 Aug 2018) with 1,620 comments. 290 were requests (97.0%) and only 9 were offers (3.0%). The analysis focuses on the 290 requests and their 1,561 comments.
2. [Research] Scale of the community: on 30 Apr 2023 it had 944,629 subscribers, plus 20,797 posts and 76,554 comments in the past 365 days (about 57 posts and 210 comments a day), ranking it 813th of 3.4 million subreddits. Activity peaked during the pandemic and has since returned to pre-pandemic levels.
3. [Research] The guidelines encourage at least one example item "and a description of why they like it", with at most 9 examples.
4. [Research] Request shape: the mean is 41.3 words (median 30), with a long tail. 89.8% of requests give more than one seed item (mean 3.0, median 2).
5. [Research] Success rates: 75.5% of requests got at least one reply. The mean is 12.1 recommendations per request (median 6), and the maximum was 143.
6. [Research] What predicts more recommendations: more seed items correlated positively with more recommendations (r = 0.385, p < .001). Request length correlated with neither thread length nor recommendation count. Writing more words is "not necessarily a recipe" for more help.
7. [Research] Links to the examples help a lot (music subset). The number of links correlated with comments (r = 0.501), recommendations (r = 0.488), and OP feedback (r = 0.391), all p < .001. 28.3% of music requests included links, and YouTube made up 88.1% of all links.
8. [Research] Feedback is sparse but positive. 55.5% of threads never got an OP comment. OPs gave feedback on a recommendation in 23.7% of requests, and 129 of 145 feedback instances (89.0%) were positive.
9. [Research] Media mix: music 80.3%, with movies and TV a distant second. Per-request stats:
   - Books (n=9): longest requests (50.6 words), fewest responses (3.8 comments, 4.7 recommendations).
   - Movies (n=18): 43.0 words, 9.3 comments, 19.2 recommendations.
   - TV (n=18): shortest requests (24.3 words), 14.2 recommendations.
   - Games (n=9): 9.6 recommendations.
10. [Research] A possible reason music dominates: streaming music services may make it hard, or less visible, to get recommendations from a single artist or song compared with movie or TV services. The authors offer this as speculation.
11. [Research] Seeds and genre: only 3.4% of music requesters named a genre explicitly. Requests mostly name songs (70.0%) and artists (35.2%). The 196 artist seeds spanned 383 Spotify genres, dominated by rock subgenres.
12. [Research] How people describe appeal: positive attributes name what they like ("scifi vibe", "weirdly chipper stoner rock", "upbeat music"). Negative ones describe what's missing ("lacking positive atmosphere", "chorus not powerful enough"). Frequent positive words were genres, properties (atmospheric, catchy, melodic), and moods (dark, upbeat, free-feeling).
13. [Research] Off-the-shelf sentiment tools may fail on these descriptors, so domain-specific sentiment analysis is needed. Only 55 posts had positive attribute descriptions and 13 had negative ones, which is a small sample.
14. [Research] Human vs algorithm: Spotify API recommendations (10 per seed song) barely overlapped with human ones. The mean Jaccard similarity was 0.000281.
15. [Research] Popularity: seed songs were the most popular, then human recommendations, then Spotify's, a statistically significant difference (Mann-Whitney U, z = 7.63). This contradicts the expectation that humans give more off-the-beaten-path picks. Most seeds were mainstream.
16. [Research] Possible reasons people use the forum: they trust humans more (citing Yeomans et al., Kunkel et al., Park & Kenshiro), current systems can't handle narrative-driven requests algorithmically or in the interface, or they have niche needs. The niche explanation is only partial, since the seeds were mostly mainstream.
17. [Research] Related work summarized: in joke recommendation, Yeomans et al. found algorithms outperformed close friends and partners, but people are averse to relying on them. Kunkel et al. found human explanations were seen as more trustworthy and higher quality.
18. [Research] The paper restates the definition of narrative-driven recommendation (Bogers & Koolen): historical preferences plus a narrative description of desired item aspects plus an optional context of use. It lists the 7 aspects from the LibraryThing analysis: content, metadata, familiarity, engagement, accessibility, novelty, and socio-cultural relevance.
19. [Research] Design implications:
    - Compare seeds with recommendations to discover the latent similarity dimensions.
    - Build interfaces that let users prioritize some attributes over others.
    - Use the threads as conversational-recommender training data, since they mimic turn-taking: an initial need, clarification questions, and critique.
    - Related subreddits: r/moviesuggestions, r/gamesuggestions, r/tipofmytongue, and others.
20. [Inferred] For a recommender: asking the user for 2–5 seed titles, and letting them point to clips or examples, likely beats asking for long prose. Treat "lacking X" phrasing as a negative appeal signal.

## Limitations or bias
- The data are dominated by music (233 of 290), and the authors say non-music conclusions are not generalizable. The per-media n is 9–18 for books, games, movies, and TV.
- The 2018 crawl is older than S6's data. It is a single random sample of 300 threads, coded by the two authors with no reliability statistics reported.
- Recommendation quality is inferred only from explicit OP comments. The study cannot judge Spotify's quality.
