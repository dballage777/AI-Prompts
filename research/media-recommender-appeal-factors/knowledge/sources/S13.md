## Learned points
1. [Research] Task: narrative-driven movie recommendation from free-form requests, such as "I want to watch a thriller with a mind-bending story, like Shutter Island." The study compares 38 open- and closed-source LLMs, from tiny (about 0.5B parameters) to large (up to Llama 3.1 405B), with three GPT models through the paid API. Open models ran under Ollama with q4_0 quantization.
2. [Research] Data: a crowdworker-annotated r/MovieSuggestions dataset (Eberhard et al. 2019). It contains positively mentioned movies, negatively mentioned movies, positive and negative keywords, and genres. Only the test split (Nov 2016 to Jul 2017) was used, and ground truth is the community's recommendations in the comments.
3. [Research] Prompting strategies:
   - Zero-shot: the Task section only.
   - Identity: a Persona plus the Task; personas were a reddit user, a movie critic, and a movie recommender.
   - Few-shot: the Task plus 1, 5, or 10 random input-output examples.
   - Output was JSON with exactly 10 "Title (Year)" strings, with movies restricted to those released before the request date.
4. [Research] Headline result: the best model, GPT-4o with the movie-critic persona, reached F1@10 0.2157, more than 70% above the best prior baseline (doc2vec, F1 0.1258). Collaborative filtering, matrix factorization, and TF-IDF baselines were also beaten. In a sensitivity test mapping outputs to the baseline's IMDb pool, GPT-3.5 Turbo with the reddit-user persona reached F1 0.2348.
5. [Research] Ceiling context: Recall@10 and F1@10 have upper limits of 0.43 and 0.57, because the number of community suggestions varies per post. Even the best LLM matched only a fraction of what the crowd suggested.
6. [Research] Prompting barely matters for capable models. No significant differences were found across strategies for most models, so simple zero-shot prompting works. Smaller models gain more: Mistral 7B went from F1 0.1053 with zero-shot to 0.1342 with few-shot.
7. [Research] Size trade-off: medium models (10–50B) nearly match large ones. GPT-3.5 Turbo averaged F1 0.1932 vs GPT-4o's 0.2137. Gemma 2 27B is roughly on par with GPT-3.5 Turbo. The best open model, Mistral Large 2 123B, reached about 0.20. Some small models (e.g., GPT-4o mini, Gemma 2 9B) also beat doc2vec.
8. [Research] Failure mode, format: valid JSON exceeded 97% for every size and strategy group. Weaker models fell to about 91–95% (Mistral 7B 91.75%, Qwen2 0.5B 92.76%, Llama 3.2 1B 93.39%). Phi-3 14B declined with long few-shot prompts.
9. [Research] Failure mode, list count: large models returned exactly 10 items in over 98% of responses, and medium models in about 96%. For tiny models, under 40% of valid responses had exactly 10. Qwen2 and Qwen2.5 0.5B returned no movies in over 92% of zero-shot and identity responses. Few-shot prompting helped these models.
10. [Research] Failure mode, duplicates: over 99% of recommended movies were unique overall. Gemma 2B and 7B and Qwen2.5 7B had 2–4% duplicates.
11. [Research] Constraint following: over 95% of recommendations respected the release-date cutoff in every size class, and over 99% for larger models.
12. [Research] Stability and diversity: across 30 repetitions of zero-shot prompts, one-way ANOVA found no significant differences. Larger models produce less diverse lists across repeats, and tiny models more varied ones. Few-shot prompting with random examples raised diversity without hurting accuracy, so small prompt variation is one way to add variety.
13. [Research] Bias: there is a recency bias, with recommended movies skewing strongly to post-2000 releases, possibly reflecting training-data availability or popularity. The authors did not study popularity or topic biases.
14. [Research] Data leakage check: GPT-3.5 Turbo was tested on 236 posts made after its knowledge cutoff (Oct 2021 to Jul 2024), with movie mentions extracted by GPT-4o. There was no significant decline, only a minor drop, possibly because relevant newer films were unknown to the model.
15. [Research] Why LLMs win, per the authors: LLMs interpret "complex natural language expressions and nuances" that matrix factorization or doc2vec miss, and extract context directly from narratives without feature engineering.
16. [Research] Evaluation method: exact title and year matching against IMDb titles in all languages, after removing movies already named in the request. Soft matching (Ratcliff/Obershelp at 0.9) changed nothing significant.
17. [Research] Stated limitations: a single domain and dataset; no chain-of-thought or tree-of-thought prompting; no tuning of temperature or sampling; default settings only. Future work includes other domains (books, music, r/ifyoulikeblank) and multimodal inputs.
18. [Inferred] For a personal recommender: a mid-size or frontier model with a plain task prompt is a reasonable base. Spend effort on parsing the request (likes, dislikes, constraints), on verifying titles exist and are unique, on checking date and availability constraints, and on counter-balancing recency bias (e.g., ask for a decade spread), rather than on elaborate personas.

## Limitations or bias
- A preprint under review. Movies only, from one subreddit's 2016–2017 test set. Ground truth is whatever commenters happened to suggest, so a good pick the crowd missed counts as wrong. This understates the quality of novel picks.
- The model list is frozen at June–September 2024 versions. The absolute numbers will date quickly.
- The HTML conversion dropped some inline numbers (e.g., test set sizes, context window size, the robustness F1 values), so those are not recorded here.
- The study does not test whether LLMs reason about appeal factors explicitly. It measures only overlap with crowd picks.

## Summary-only items
- None. The abstract claims were confirmed in the full HTML text.

## Best used for
- Evidence that off-the-shelf LLMs handle "like X but Y" movie requests better than classic recommenders, with known failure modes: format slips, recency bias, low diversity in large models, and a low ceiling against crowd picks.
