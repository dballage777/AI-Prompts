- Pages read: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06201 (full text, as extracted to S6.txt)

- Type: peer-reviewed article (qualitative content analysis, plus some quantitative analysis)

## Learned points
1. [Research] Dataset: all r/ifyoulikeblank posts from 1 Jan to 20 Sep 2022, which came to 19,715 posts and 119,610 comments. Only 4,183 posts (about 21%) followed the formatting rules and had at least one comment. There were 3,834 commenter–OP reply pairs. The authors open-coded 1,511 <post, comment, OP reply> triplets: 1,000 music, plus all 511 non-music items (Film 185, TV 167, Games 64, Image 61, Books 34).
2. [Research] Subreddit rules: posts start with "If I like…" [IIL] or "If you like…" [IYL], include at least one example in the title, and list at most 9 examples. Flairs cover Music, Film, TV, Books, Games, Comics, Art, Podcast, and YouTube/Streaming. The subreddit had about 907,000 members when the paper was written.
3. [Research] Why people come (RQ1), three reasons: (a) other methods fell short, including Google, Spotify, spotalike.com, and rateyourmusic.com, and sometimes months of searching; (b) they don't know how to search, or whether the style they want even has a genre name, and some ask for keywords to search with; (c) they believe the crowd knows more than search systems do.
4. [Research] A key failure of search engines: they match on plot when the seeker cares about some other latent dimension. Example: "all the suggestions are based on the plot, but idgaf about the plot". The authors say that someone seeking movies that share a location may get only plot-similar results from Google.
5. [Research] Final scheme: 9 high-level codes and 37 sub-codes. The query codes are Artifact, Production/Distribution, Artist, OP's Context, and Additional Information. Response and Interaction codes cover the replies.
6. [Research] Artifact sub-codes:
   - Message/Theme: story, characters, settings, and messages. In games, this also covers musical score, dialogue, and storyline.
   - Adjective: surreal, mind-bending, cathartic, steamy.
   - Popularity: wanting less-known items.
   - Date or period: covers both the release window and the period depicted ("futuristic dystopia", "middle Ages").
   - Genre: in film/TV this includes storytelling style such as non-linearity, costume or dress style, and high-level theme. In books it includes protagonist attributes and meta-traits such as "stories about stories". In games it includes first- vs third-person perspective.
   - Language: subtitles and dubbing are acceptable to some seekers.
   - Location: the felt sense of place.
   - Tools: instruments, cinematography tools.
7. [Research] Location as an appeal: one seeker said they were searching "not genre-based" but for shows that make them "feel like I'm living in those places" (Californication/California, Justified/Kentucky, The Americans/DC). IMDB's location filtering is "cruder" than this.
8. [Research] Production/Distribution sub-codes:
   - Completeness: wanting a finished series, or sometimes an ongoing one to follow as it releases.
   - Revision: remixes and covers, "new content in the style of older content". Outside music this could cover adaptations.
   - Non-primary entity: label, producer, publisher.
   - Platform: the device or service (Netflix, YouTube, Xbox) and regional availability.
9. [Research] OP's Context sub-codes, which map directly to the viewing situation:
   - Activity: who it's with, e.g., "4 player split screen game" or a movie to watch with a partner.
   - Scene: 3,025 of 19,715 posts referenced a scene or setting of use.
   - Expertise: novices use elaborate descriptions.
   - Feeling: current mood or the emotional response wanted, e.g., "I feel so safe whenever I watch these. They're so comforting."
   - Idiosyncrasy.
   - Time Commitment: e.g., a 100-hour game or a 100-episode series.
   - Willingness to Repeat: rewatch or replay.
10. [Research] Additional Information sub-codes:
    - Abbreviation (HIMYM, DnD).
    - Transcendence: cross-media requests, e.g., "lo-fi hip hop. Are there movies/TV shows that capture that vibe?"
    - Image: collages for art styles that are hard to describe in words.
    - Justification.
    - Specificity: a timestamp in a song, a particular book's ending, "Relaxing games that I can play with no stress".
    - Desired Recommender (the recommender's expertise).
11. [Research] Cross-media transcendence also shows up in responses. Recommenders sometimes offer other media types, such as a game (Horizon Zero Dawn) in reply to a book request. None of the four platforms examined (IMDB, Spotify, Steam, Amazon Books) supports cross-category queries.
12. [Research] Platform gap analysis: most sub-codes are not filters on IMDB, Spotify, Steam, or Amazon Books. Unsupported everywhere: Tools, Completeness, Revision, Activity Level, Idiosyncrasy, Time Commitment, Willingness to Repeat, Transcendence, Image, Specificity, and all Response and Interaction codes. Feeling or mood is supported only on Spotify and Steam, and the authors judge this "likely limited to popular genres".
13. [Research] Response sub-codes:
    - Justification: descriptive, or a rating, e.g., "Hollow Knight (Darkest game on this list, but easily the cutest and most beautiful. Give it time…)".
    - Preview: a clip or scene link to entice the seeker.
    - Transcendence: answering with a different medium.
    OPs also explain mismatches, e.g., "symphonic power metal is not my cup of tea."
14. [Research] Interaction sub-codes:
    - Affirming Taste: "We're like twins".
    - Endorsement: seconding another commenter's pick.
    - Refinement: exclusion criteria stated upfront, criteria added by editing the post, criteria clarified after a mismatch, and complaints that a pick isn't on the OP's platform.
    - Repetition: asking the same thing again later.
    - Reciprocity.
    - Discussion.
    Of 44 long chains (3 or more back-and-forths), 12 were incremental refinement and 10 were debates about whether an item matched.
15. [Research] Refinement example of a "vibe" spec with calibrated intensity: "something not over corny like a notebook but not zero love". Simulating refinement requires a system to "hypothesize the gap first" and reason about it.
16. [Research] Expertise signal: OPs were more receptive to a list when they recognized a few items in it. A recommender who didn't know the seed items could still offer useful picks, and the OP stayed receptive.
17. [Research] Sentiment (TweetNLP, all 19,715 posts): Joy 7,903, Optimism 5,359, Anticipation 3,667, Love 2,894, Sadness 2,136. About 15% of posts showed negative emotions. Half the posts had no dominant valence. Of the rest, 95% were positive or neutral.
18. [Research] Participation follows a power law: 75% of posters post once, 57% of commenters comment once, and 77% of commented posts get at most 5 comments. Many posts get no response.
19. [Research] Music title analysis: 4,679 identified entities, with artists (2,065) and tracks (2,242) about even and albums only about 8%.
20. [Research] Design dimensions proposed: (1) a human-vs-algorithm axis; (2) query by multiple examples sharing a latent similarity, which may help "evade the popularity bias"; (3) interactivity, meaning dialogue that aligns misunderstandings. LLM-style systems "may lack" a human's rationalization capacity but could simulate taste affirmation and justification.
21. [Research] Operationalizing the codes is hard. Some codes are finite (completeness, demography), while scene descriptions are arbitrary. Specificity may point to content the system must derive first. Users perceive the same code differently (what "active" means). Some codes fall outside a single-domain system's scope (IMDB can't return a game). Justification is still under-researched.
22. [Inferred] For a cross-media recommender: capture context fields (time budget, completeness, rewatch openness, company, platform or region, mood) alongside content appeal. Accept multi-example seeds and ask what latent trait links them. Support cross-media "vibe" transfer. Always justify picks.

## Limitations or bias
- The sample is skewed to music (1,000 of 1,511 coded items). Books (34) and games (64) are thin, and the Flair column shows only where each code was observed.
- Survivorship bias: only posts that got replies were coded, as the authors note. The data cover a single period in 2022.
- The authors call the qualitative coding "not perfect" and a "naïve representation". There are no inter-rater reliability figures. The initial coding was split between authors.
- The platform feature check covers advanced search filters only, not recommendation algorithms.

## Best used for
- A field-tested taxonomy of what people put into "more like this" requests across media (content, production, context, cross-media "vibe"), and which of these mainstream platforms fail to support.
