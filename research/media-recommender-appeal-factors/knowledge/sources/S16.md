## Learned points
1. [Research] Data: 3,581 threads crawled in June 2014 from two IMDB message boards, "I need to know" (3,355 threads) and "Lists & recommendations" (226). The first post of each thread was coded through emergent coding, using 120 threads to develop the scheme and 400 threads in total.
2. [Research] Taxonomy: 30 lower-level relevance aspects grouped by card-sorting into 8 top-level aspects:
   - Comparisons: edition, lists, novelty, sequence and series.
   - Content: dialogue; plot, topic or event; style, setting and tone.
   - Known-item.
   - Metadata: audience, genre, language and country, release year, technical properties, TV shows.
   - People: actors, directors, writers.
   - Recommendation: more like this, not like this, pure recommendation.
   - Secondary properties: awards, book version, credits, DVD, popularity, promotion, soundtrack.
   - Miscellaneous.
3. [Research] Example definitions: Style, setting & tone means "Movies in a specific tone, such as film-noir". Dialogue means "Does the dialogue in the movie have a specific style?". "Not like this" means "Looking for movies that are not like the examples".
4. [Research] 275 of 400 threads (68.8%) were requests for movies to watch. Many requests are complex, combining plot elements, setting, tone, and positive and negative examples.
5. [Research] Frequencies among the 275 requests:
   - Plot, topic or event: 49.5%
   - Known-item (can't recall the title): 46.0%
   - Recommendation: 34.6%
   - Genre: 22.5%
   - Positive or negative example movies: 19.6%
   - Release year: 15.5%
   - Style, setting & tone: 15.0%
   - People: 5.5%
6. [Research] People matter little for discovery. Only 5.5% of requests centered on actors, directors, or writers, against 18.3% of non-request threads. The author suggests that looking up an actor's films is easy enough for IMDB's own search.
7. [Research] Example complex request: disaster movies where strangers band together, preferably based on true events. It combines a genre, a plot element, and examples ("World Trade Center", "Unstoppable") with an exclusion ("Not end of the world type movies"), plus a preference for TV movies and lesser-known films.
8. [Research] Examples move a request away from topical relevance toward recommendation. Many requests go beyond topical relevance to subjective requirements such as tone or style, popularity, or intended audience.
9. [Research] Implication: different information access paradigms are needed. IR suits topical or known-item needs, and recommenders suit preference- or example-based needs. The author asks whether a "grey area" exists between search and recommendation that neither serves well.
10. [Research] Prior work cited: Austin (1979) found plot was the single most important reason high schoolers chose a movie, followed by stars and friends' recommendations. Ross (1999), studying 194 pleasure readers, found book selection draws on mood, subject, characters, author, physical appearance, and recommendations. Reuter (2007) identified 46 factors in children's book selection across 7 dimensions (accessibility, content, engagement, familiarity, metadata and physical entity, novelty, socio-cultural). These dimensions partly shaped this paper's coding scheme.
11. [Research] Movie search is framed as casual leisure search, where motivations and behavior differ significantly from web search.
12. [Inferred] For a recommender: tone, style, and setting is a named but minority aspect in film requests (15%), while plot or topic dominates explicit asks. A tool should therefore elicit tone and mood directly rather than wait for users to volunteer it. Exclusions ("not like this") need first-class handling.

## Limitations or bias
- An exploratory short paper with a single coder (no reliability reported). 400 annotated threads, from 2014 IMDB boards that IMDB later discontinued (the board URLs are dated 2014).
- The "I need to know" board dominates (3,355 of 3,581 threads), which inflates known-item needs relative to pure recommendation.
- In the Figure 1 example column, the People rows appear to repeat the Content examples (e.g., Actor(s) is shown with "Does the dialogue…"). This looks like a layout error in the source, so those examples were not used.
- Movies only. The responses to requests were not analyzed.
