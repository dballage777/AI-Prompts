## Learned points
1. [Reported] Netflix's tagging method, as described at the time: about seven years before 2014, executive Todd Yellin set out to "break down every movie into data." He hired aspiring screenwriters, paid them to watch films, and had them rate levels of romance, gore, quirkiness, and even plot resolution.
2. [Reported] The stated goal was to "reverse-engineer all the Hollywood formulas" so Netflix could mathematically show viewers movies it knew they would like.
3. [Reported] Count: Madrigal scraped Netflix's sequentially numbered category URLs with a script that ran for more than 20 hours and found 76,897 separate microgenre categories.
4. [Reported] How microgenres are built: a "Mad Libs"-style algorithm combines tags according to set criteria, especially how many movies fit the category. A traditional genre (for example, Drama) sits at the center, and descriptors are added around it: actors, directors, time period, location, age level, and adjectives.
5. [Reported] The adjectives are what make the categories feel "uncannily precise." The most frequent adjective is "romantic," in 5,272 categories. Next come foreign, classic, dark, British, critically acclaimed, suspenseful, gritty, independent, visually striking, family, violent, and feel-good.
6. [Reported] The least-used adjectives include experimental, screwball, satanic, stoner, visionary, and Depression-era.
7. [Reported] Example microgenres: African-American Crime Documentaries, Scary Cult Movies From The 1980s, Tearjerkers From The 1970s, Japanese Horror Movies From The 1960s, Violent Action Thrillers Starring Bruce Willis, Understated Romantic Road Trip Movies, Dark Road Trip Thrillers, and Canadian Independent Road Trip Movies.
8. [Reported] Old video-store genres were broad, communal aisles (Action/Adventure, Comedy, Drama). The viewer then searched within one for the right thing, for example "A dark road trip movie with a strong female lead," which yields Thelma & Louise. That example is in effect an appeal-based request: tone plus story pattern plus character.
9. [Reported] Data quirks: Raymond Burr (Perry Mason) has the most dedicated categories, and Bruce Willis is second with 17. Even Yellin was baffled by the number of Burr categories. Madrigal notes that only some of the logic "feels human."
10. [Reported] Madrigal's interpretation: the system takes "our taste, break[s] it down into its constituent parts" and returns it in new and revealing ways.
11. [Inferred] Many of the high-frequency Netflix adjectives (romantic, dark, suspenseful, gritty, feel-good, violent, visually striking) work as tone/mood and style appeal descriptors. A genre core plus an appeal adjective plus a frame (era or place) is a practical template for cross-media recommendation labels.

## Limitations or bias
- The data dates from 2014. Netflix's current tagging and categories are likely different, and this article does not describe them.
- This is a journalist's outside scrape and interpretation, not Netflix's own documentation. It does not give the full tag list or the rating scales.
- The figures are counts of category names, not evidence of recommendation accuracy or user satisfaction.
