## Learned points
1. [Reported] Format: Clark emailed three questions (the view of RA today, its future, key skills) to librarians, partly via LibraryReads. Ten participants are listed by initials; one answer is signed "ML," which is not in the participant list.
2. [Reported] RA has micro and macro meanings: booklists and displays, patron interviews and matching, continuing education, "understanding appeal factors," and curated databases such as NoveList.
3. [Reported] Competing sources: readers get suggestions from social media, online "influencers," friends, and Amazon's algorithm. BookTok and Bookstagram send patrons in with lists of new, in-demand titles that are often unavailable, so read-alikes are needed quickly.
4. [Reported] Cross-media is now part of RA (MY): RA "considers a range of formats, including audiobooks (which adds the element of narration preferences). Plus, there are connections to make to movies, TV shows, and even games!"
5. [Reported] Format is a preference dimension (MJ): a patron may read only e-books or want Spotify audiobooks. Advisors must first find out what kind of book the patron wants, then start from there.
6. [Reported] Vocabulary shift (LD): what NoveList calls appeal factors are "also referred to as catnip, vibes, etc—those tropes, tone, types of characters that people seek out." More readers use these terms themselves. This is useful for mapping user language to facets.
7. [Reported] Practice (MJ): matching books to movies and TV shows "can help boost circulation, especially for backlist titles"; backlist promotion matters because new hits are never available when wanted.
8. [Reported] Practice (MJ): a "no-book book club," where participants talk about what they read recently, succeeded because it was two-way and personal, unlike one-way social-media RA.
9. [Reported] Practitioner view on algorithms (LUD): "People are not always very good at describing what they like or don't like," and a search box will not ask deeper follow-up questions the way a trained staffer can.
10. [Reported] View on purchase data (KT): "I don't think purchasing will ever be able to reflect the actual truth of what materials people enjoy." KT also expects some advisors to help build AI RA tools.
11. [Reported] Caution (MY): AI may be "a useful tool among other tools" but should not replace building RA skill. JT notes the "overwhelming volume" of major, indie, self-published, and AI-generated material, and an influencer culture that feels "way too homogenous."
12. [Reported] Skills for the interview: listen to what the reader is saying, "rather than on what we wish they were saying"; ask clarifying questions; offer several suggestions and let them try (LD); ask "for more information on what appealed to the reader"; do not take rejection personally (SH).
13. [Reported] Skills: be curious about "why they liked or didn't like the item" and probe for "searchable terms" (LUD). Ask the patron to report back whether they liked a suggestion, as relationship-building (MJ).
14. [Reported] Attitudes: be non-judgmental and "Give them what they want," including guilty pleasures and current mood reads (KT). Respect all genres (MJ). Set aside your own predilections (ML, BT). Have humility about not knowing all books (MJ) and be willing to say "I don't know but I'll find out" (ML).
15. [Reported] Tools must be used well (JT): "even with our favorite tools we must know how to use them... It isn't just an automatic." Tools named: NoveList and LibraryReads.
16. [Reported] Theme: RA should be relational, not transactional (BT), and should link theory and practice more closely between LIS researchers and practitioners.
17. [Reported] Saricks (3rd ed., 2005) is quoted as saying librarians are "in the midst of a readers' advisory renaissance." RUSA/CODES set up an RA committee about thirty years before the column.

## Limitations or bias
- Opinion survey of a small, hand-picked group of about ten US librarians (some recruited via LibraryReads). Not a representative or systematic study.
- Respondents are advocates for library RA, so claims about algorithms' limits are unevaluated opinion.
- Publication year is not printed in the text I read (volume 60, issue 3 only).

## Best used for
- Current practitioner signals: cross-media RA (movies, TV, games, audiobook narration), user vocabulary ("vibes," "tropes," "catnip"), and interview skills that an AI recommender should copy (probing likes and dislikes, follow-up questions, asking for feedback).
