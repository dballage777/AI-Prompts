## Anecdotal community findings (opinion, not proof)

- **Most repeated: the database leaks because of configuration, whichever vendor you pick.** In Firebase (Tea, the 900-site study, Arc) and in Supabase (Moltbook, Lovable, "Your Supabase is public"), the failure is the same: client-reachable data with missing or wrong rules or RLS. HN commenters repeatedly say Supabase does warn about RLS. Leaks come from developers or AI tools making public tables without policies, inverted RPC auth checks, or service_role keys in clients or agents.
- **Firebase has no hard spending cap.** This comes up in almost every bill thread ($70k in a day, €54k in 13h, $300 a month). The fix people suggest: restrict API keys, set budget alerts, and paginate Firestore reads. Some HN users avoid pay-per-use backends entirely or move to a VPS.
- **Supabase free-tier friction.** Users complain about pausing of inactive free projects, "nag emails," and no guaranteed free-plan support response. Paid plans are not paused, and keep-alive tools exist as workarounds.
- **Keep a non-Apple fallback login and store the identifiers you need.** The ASO.dev outage is the cited cautionary tale: relay emails bounced and userIdentifiers changed. Apple's 2026 move of relay aliases to @private.icloud.com adds the risk that third parties block relay emails.
- **Preferred alternatives come up often:** PocketBase (single-file SQLite backend), InstantDB, Better Auth (self-hosted auth), and plain Postgres or a VPS. For offline sync the names that keep coming up are PowerSync, Legend-State, WatermelonDB and ElectricSQL. Independent posts warn that each "solved a larger problem" than a small app has, and that simple last-write-wins sync on SQLite with soft deletes is often enough.
- **Lock-in sentiment:** Firebase is seen as easy but Google-locked and "poorly bolted on to GCP." Supabase is seen as more portable because it is Postgres, but harder to self-host than advertised (HN comments 43515450, 49890286; opinion).
