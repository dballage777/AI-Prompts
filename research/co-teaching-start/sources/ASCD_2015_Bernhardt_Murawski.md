# ASCD (2015): "An Administrator's Guide to Co-Teaching." P. Bernhardt and W. Murawski
URL: https://www.ascd.org/el/articles/an-administrators-guide-to-co-teaching | Access: OPENED (full text) | Published: Dec 1, 2015 (Vol. 73, No. 4)
## Key points (Reported, expert)
Five steps for administrators: PD; scheduling; partnering the right teachers; supervising and evaluating strategically; improving and institutionalizing.
- Co-teaching = "co-plan, co-instruct, and co-assess."
- It "shouldn't be seen as a 'special education thing'"
- Scheduling:
  - Put students receiving special education into the master schedule first.
  - Keep students with special needs at no more than about 30% of a general education class.
  - Give a general educator only one special educator to co-teach with.
  - Build in common planning periods. Other options: PLC time, a monthly substitute, banked time, lunch meetings.
- Partnering:
  - "don't throw teachers together and hope for the best!"
  - Start with volunteers and a small pilot.
  - Use the SHARE worksheet (Dieker and Murawski, 2003) on hopes, expectations, responsibilities, and pet peeves.
  - Look for complementary personalities.
- Evaluating: evaluate both teachers at once. Look for evidence of co-planning and co-assessing, regrouping, and not being able to tell the general educator from the special educator.
- Sustaining: keep effective partnerships together; "It takes time and commitment to learn each other's content and ways."
- Evidence caution: "Research continues to find confusing and contradictory results related to co-teaching."
## Quotes
- "Above all, don't throw teachers together and hope for the best!"
- "It takes time and commitment to learn each other's content and ways"
