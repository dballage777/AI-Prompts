## Key points (Documented)
- Resources: "Simply stated, if you don't co-plan, you won’t co-teach." Co-planning must be valued by all. "Administrators must assist co-teachers in finding the time to co-plan to co-teach."
- Training: "You cannot simply train your participants and be done – it is an ongoing initiative."
- Strategies ("Refined and adapted from the foundational co-teaching models established by Cook and Friend"):
  - One Teach, One Observe: the observer collects data, not judgments.
  - One Teach, One Assist: questions get answered faster, and behavior is handled without stopping instruction.
  - Station Teaching: stations can't be hierarchical; discuss pacing, voice, and noise first.
  - Parallel Teaching: same content and strategies to half the class each. "The greatest benefit to this method is the reduction of the student to teacher ratio." Seat students facing their own teacher.
  - Alternative (Differentiated) Teaching: the same outcome reached by different avenues.
  - Team Teaching: no prescribed division of authority. It takes intense planning, which gets easier with time together.
  - Supplemental Teaching: grouping by assessed need. "make sure that one teacher doesn’t always work with the students who are struggling." "Group make-up should be flexible."
  - Combinations and variations.
- Council of Classroom Educators: "built on an equal partnership between licensed professionals, each bringing unique expertise." Includes "co-teaching models that integrate IEP goals into daily classroom routines."
- Council of Administrators: supports principals "in implementing systemic co-teaching practices that foster inclusive and equitable classroom environments."
- Home: NACT's mission statement says co-teaching ensures "every student benefits from shared expertise." This is a mission statement, not an outcome study.
