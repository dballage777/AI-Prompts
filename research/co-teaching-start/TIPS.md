# Where to Start with Co-Teaching
*From our co-teaching conference takeaways, backed by research and guidance. Prepared for our principal (high school).*

## The big idea
Co-teaching means two licensed teachers who **co-plan, co-instruct, and co-assess** one class together. It is "built on an equal partnership between licensed professionals, each bringing unique expertise" [NACT]. It "requires far more than putting two adults in a classroom with students" [Source: ASCD 2020].

## Our 7 takeaways, and what the sources say
1. **Move beyond One Teach, One Assist.**
   - NACT's strategy list includes One Teach/One Observe, One Teach/One Assist, Station, **Parallel**, Alternative, Team, and Supplemental teaching, plus combinations [NACT].
   - In parallel teaching, each teacher teaches the same content to half the class. "The greatest benefit to this method is the reduction of the student to teacher ratio" [NACT].
   - Strong teams use a variety of approaches [Source: ASCD 2015].
2. **Combine content expertise with specialist expertise.** The general educator leads on content. The special educator brings access strategies and the IEP plan. The two are equals with different roles [Source: ASCD 2020; EdWeek 2018].
3. **Clear roles protect shared ownership.**
   - "Establish roles and responsibilities," including how you'll share the workload [Source: ASCD 2020].
   - In our conference notes, this takeaway reads "When roles are ambi[…] shared ownership disappears." Please check the wording; it may have been "ambiguous."
4. **Group students together, not separately.**
   - Make sure "one teacher doesn't always work with the students who are struggling," and keep groups flexible [NACT].
   - Build IEP goals into daily classroom routines [NACT].
5. **All students can benefit from two teachers.**
   - Two teachers allow smaller groups and more support for everyone [NACT].
   - *Honest note:* research on student outcomes is still **mixed**. Results depend on what the two teachers actually do together [Source: EdWeek 2018; ASCD 2015].
6. **Build a good team.**
   - Get to know each other's strengths, preferences, and pet peeves [Source: ASCD 2020].
   - Schools do best when they "don't throw teachers together and hope for the best": start with volunteers and complementary personalities [Source: ASCD 2015].
7. **Time and trust.**
   - "If you don't co-plan, you won't co-teach" [NACT].
   - "Underneath everything, you need to trust each other" [Source: EdWeek 2018].
   - "It takes time and commitment to learn each other's content and ways" [Source: ASCD 2015].

## Our first 2 weeks
1. **Hold a get-to-know-you planning conversation:** strengths, teaching style, pet peeves, classroom routines [Source: ASCD 2020].
2. **Write down roles:** who leads content, who plans accommodations, and who grades what [Source: ASCD 2020].
3. **Set a weekly co-planning time** and protect it [NACT; Source: EdWeek 2018].
4. **Plan at three levels:** the year (mega), each unit (macro), and each day (micro) [Source: ASCD 2020].
5. **Pick one new approach to try,** such as parallel or station teaching. Agree on pacing, voice, and noise levels first [NACT].
6. **Show we're equals:** both names on the door and on parent emails, and both of us leading instruction [Source: EdWeek 2018].
7. **Debrief weekly:** what worked, and what to adjust [Suggested].

## What we're asking from our principal
- **Common planning time** in the master schedule. If that isn't possible, use PLC time, a monthly substitute, or banked time [NACT; Source: ASCD 2015].
- **Balanced class makeup.** About 30% or fewer students with special needs, with those students scheduled into the master schedule first [Source: ASCD 2015].
- **Keep our partnership together** across years. Strong teams take time to build [Source: ASCD 2015].
- **Ongoing PD,** not a one-time training [NACT].
- **Observe us together, as a team,** looking for co-planning, regrouping, and shared instruction [Source: ASCD 2015].
- **Start as a pilot** and share what we learn with other teams [Source: ASCD 2015].

## Common early mistakes and fixes
- **Staying stuck in One Teach, One Assist** → try one new approach each unit [NACT].
- **The special educator acting as an aide** → plan roles so both teachers lead [Source: ASCD 2015].
- **Blaming the schedule** → co-plan by shared Google Doc, quick messages, or video chat [Source: ASCD 2020].
- **The same teacher always taking the struggling group** → rotate groups and keep them flexible [NACT].

## Questions to ask each other
1. What are your strengths, and what do you want me to lead?
2. What are your pet peeves in a classroom?
3. How will we share grading and parent communication?
4. When and how will we plan each week?
5. Which co-teaching approach do we want to try first?
6. How will we handle disagreements, privately and positively? [Source: ASCD 2020]
