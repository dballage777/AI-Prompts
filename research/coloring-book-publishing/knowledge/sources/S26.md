## Learned points
1. NYPL states that "more than 180,000 of the items" in its Digital Collections are public domain. This figure appears on both the landing page and the 2016 post, so it may be dated. Documented
2. Reuse terms on the landing page: everyone may "reuse these materials in almost limitless ways"; "No permission required. No restrictions on use." Documented
3. Public domain items can be downloaded "in the highest resolution available" directly from the Digital Collections website. Documented
4. Since January 5, 2016, out-of-copyright materials have been available as high-resolution downloads, with no administration fees or permission processes for public domain content. Publishers are named among the intended users. Documented
5. Newly digitized public domain collections are to be released the same way ("All subsequently digitized public domain collections will be made available in the same way"). Documented
6. The Digital Collections website has filters that highlight "restriction-free content". The API and GitHub data exports allow bulk use, with "over one million records" of machine-readable data. Documented
7. Holdings relevant to coloring pages named in the 2016 post: botanical illustrations, "Over 20,000 maps and atlases", "More than 40,000 stereoscopic views", medieval and Renaissance illuminated manuscripts, Tale of Genji handscrolls (1554), Anna Atkins' cyanotypes of British algae (1843), WPA-era lithographs and etchings, and New York City collections. Documented
8. Neither page I read says anything about attribution. Neither requires credit; the 2016 post only invites sharing with #nyplremix or by email to digitalcollections@nypl.org. Documented (absence noted)
9. Neither page addresses jurisdiction (US versus worldwide PD), privacy or publicity rights in photographs, or trademarks in depicted items. The "No restrictions on use" statement applies to items NYPL has identified as public domain. Other items in Digital Collections are not covered. Inferred
10. For a coloring book: use the "public domain" filter in Digital Collections and check that the individual item is marked public domain before tracing or redrawing it. Keep the item URL as your rights record, because I could not verify the per-item rights display. Inferred
