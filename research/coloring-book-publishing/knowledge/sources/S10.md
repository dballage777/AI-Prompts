## Learned points
1. The Office's AI initiative began in early 2023; its August 2023 notice of inquiry drew "over 10,000 comments by December 2023." Documented
2. The AI report is issued in parts: Part 1 (digital replicas) July 31, 2024; Part 2 (copyrightability) January 29, 2025; Part 3 (generative AI training) pre-publication version May 9, 2025, with a final version promised "without any substantive changes expected." Documented
3. The hub lists the key registration decisions on AI material: Zarya of the Dawn (Feb. 21, 2023), Théâtre D'opéra Spatial (Review Board, Sept. 5, 2023), SURYAST (Review Board, Dec. 11, 2023), A Recent Entrance to Paradise (Review Board, Feb. 14, 2022), plus the Thaler v. Perlmutter and Allen v. Perlmutter court filings. Documented
4. Zarya of the Dawn (an 18-page comic, one page a cover) is the closest precedent to an AI-illustrated coloring book: the Office held the author owns the text and the "selection, coordination, and arrangement" of text and visual elements, but NOT the individual Midjourney-generated images. Documented
5. The original Zarya registration (filed Sept. 15, 2022) did not disclose AI use and was registered the same day; the Office learned of Midjourney use from the author's social-media posts and reopened it. Lesson: non-disclosure can be discovered after registration. Documented
6. Remedy in Zarya: the Office cancelled the certificate under 37 CFR 201.7(c)(4) "for failure to exclude non-human authorship" and reissued a narrower one excluding "artwork generated by artificial intelligence"; the effective date stayed Sept. 15, 2022. Documented
7. Because the remaining human contribution was mainly text, the new registration was classed as an unpublished literary work (Class TXu), not a visual-arts work. For a coloring book with little or no human text, this implies little may remain protectable if images are pure AI output. Documented (decision) / Inferred (coloring-book implication)
8. Selection/arrangement counted because the author "selected, refined, cropped, positioned, framed, and arranged" images on each page to tell a story; the Office relied on her representation that she did this herself. Documented
9. Minor human edits failed: changes to the character's lips and mouth were "too minor and imperceptible" to be protected. Substantive Photoshop edits to an intermediate AI image "could provide human authorship," but the applicant must clearly explain what was edited. Documented
10. Time, effort, or expense ("sweat of the brow") is irrelevant to copyrightability; the Office "will not consider the amount of time, effort, or expense." Documented
11. The decision is fact-specific to Midjourney as described; the Office noted other AI tools "operate differently." Documented
12. The Office held a June 28, 2023 webinar walking through the March 2023 registration guidance, with a transcript PDF available (not read). Documented
13. Practical rule for a coloring-book prompt or workflow: keep records of what the human drew, traced, redrew, or arranged, so the human contribution can be described on an application. Inferred
