- Access: OPENED | Date read: 2026-11-09 | Published/updated: published 2022-12-14; updated Dec 23, 2025 (page byline and metadata)
- Pages read: full article "Low Content Books: Are They Still Worth It?" by Jason Hamilton
- Source type & possible bias: Secondary blog. Kindlepreneur sells Publisher Rocket (promoted in the article) and has affiliate links; the author is a former Kindlepreneur content manager.
## Learned points
1. Says Amazon defines low-content books as books with repetitive pages and little or no words (notebooks, planners, journals, sketchbooks). Reported (consistent with S3)
2. Coloring books, workbooks, puzzle/game books, activity books and quote books are not low-content in Amazon's eyes because pages are not repetitive; the article calls them "medium content books." Reported (consistent with S3)
3. Practical consequence stated: medium-content books (incl. coloring books) still need an ISBN and should NOT tick the low-content box. Reported (consistent with S3/S13)
4. Market view: low-content used to be a get-rich-quick scheme; mass-producing now fails because of heavy competition; niche research and quality are needed. Reported (opinion)
5. Suggests tie-in coloring books for existing fiction readers as a niche strategy. Reported
6. Creation tips: know your audience/niche, quality over quantity, outsource design when needed, expand into similar books if one sells. Reported
7. Tools named: Canva (cheap design tool; Photoshop/InDesign as more powerful options) and Publisher Rocket (keyword/category research; vendor's own product). Reported
8. Other platforms named: Barnes & Noble Press, Lulu, IngramSpark; Lulu/Redbubble merchandise journals have preset interiors. Reported
9. Royalty claim: 60% for paperback/hardcover, dropping to 40% with expanded distribution, after print cost. Note: S3 says Expanded Distribution is not supported for low-content books, so the 40% statement does not apply to true low-content titles. Verify royalty rates on KDP's own royalty pages. Reported (partly conflicts with Documented S3)
10. Advises print-on-demand before bulk printing to avoid unsold inventory. Reported
11. KDP steps given (create paperback/hardcover, enter details, tick low-content box, upload, set rights/pricing, publish) match S3. Reported
