## Learned points
1. Main failure points are before upload: picking a niche without demand, paying for art before testing the idea, a non-competitive cover, or an interior that "looks fine on screen but falls apart once it's printed". — Reported
2. Two broad types: kids (simple bold illustrations, large open spaces, occasional educational text, fewer pages) and adults (intricate: mandalas/geometric, florals/nature, animals, architecture, fashion, comics/manga, quotes, zendoodles, mixed). — Reported
3. Five steps: (1) research niche, (2) assemble art + cover, (3) format, (4) publish on KDP (and IngramSpark), (5) market. — Reported
4. Manual niche research: use Amazon search autosuggest (append letters, e.g., "adult coloring book a"), check competition, check Best Sellers Rank of top 10 results in Kindlepreneur's KDP Rankings Tool to estimate daily sales. Example category found with Publisher Rocket: Books > Arts & Photography > Drawing > Coloring Books for Grown-Ups > Animals; example keyword "Owl Coloring Book". — Reported
5. Art options: make it yourself, hire an illustrator (pay fairly; quality drives reviews and repeat sales), or AI. — Reported
6. AI art: author advises using it "thoughtfully, not as a shortcut"; says it is rarely on par with a skilled artist; acceptable uses: non-profit/personal projects, launch-then-upgrade, or bonus freebies. No mention of KDP AI-disclosure rules or copyright. — Reported
7. Cover tips: don't reuse interior art; one main image, large readable title, possible subtitle (e.g., "A Relaxing Floral Coloring Book for Adults"); bold saturated colors; high resolution; hire a professional if possible. — Reported
8. Image formatting: uniform style and size for the trim; author likes a black border around each image (Photoshop or Canva). Atticus has a pixel-size helper for full-bleed images; add "Full Page Image" with "Full Bleed", set trim in theme Print Settings, export PDF for KDP or IngramSpark. — Reported
9. Sizes: most common 8.5 x 11 in; 8.5 x 8.5 in occasionally for square books. — Reported
10. Page count: "You will at least need 24 pages to be able to use print-on-demand software"; blank backs count as pages, so 20 illustrations = 40 pages; most books have 16–50 illustrations. — Reported (24-page figure not verified against KDP here; Lulu S20 says paperback minimum is 32)
11. KDP steps: create account → "+ Create" → "Create Paperback" → title, subtitle, author, description, keywords → upload interior and cover → preview every page → territories, pricing, royalty plan → "Publish Your Paperback Book". — Reported
12. Recommends publishing on both KDP and IngramSpark: "no upsides to going exclusive with Amazon for print titles." — Reported
13. ISBN: needed for bookstores/libraries; on KDP an ASIN identifies the book on Amazon only. — Reported (note: the article's claim that KDP "provide[s] you with a free" ASIN as substitute is simplified; KDP's ISBN rules not covered)
14. Marketing: social media (Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest, TikTok), influencers (parent bloggers, mental-health influencers), email list with a free coloring page teaser, Amazon Ads, local library/school events. — Reported
