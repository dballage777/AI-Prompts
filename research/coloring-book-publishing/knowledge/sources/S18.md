## Learned points
1. PDIA is a curated archive of out-of-copyright historical images: "11,373 out-of-copyright works" (homepage counter on 2026-11-09; the About page says "more than 10,000"). New images are added every week. It can be browsed by artist, century, style, theme, and tag, and has catalog, infinite, and shuffle views. Documented
2. The images come from "more than 200 source institutions" (galleries, libraries, archives, museums) "who have made digitised public domain works … free to reuse, even commercially." Each image links back to its source institution. Documented
3. Selection rule: PDIA includes only images "we are confident may be reused without question for any purpose." Not every image published on the PDR qualifies for PDIA. Documented
4. Commercial use: "In most cases, yes." The exceptions are jurisdiction (for example, public domain in the US only) and attribution requirements. Each image page states the rights status of both the underlying work and the digital copy. Documented
5. Two-layer rights model: the underlying work (the original painting, drawing, or book) and the digital copy (the scan or photo) are labelled separately, because institutions sometimes claim rights in digital copies of public domain works. The PDR labels the copy with whatever the institution claims, whether or not that claim would hold up in court. Documented
6. Underlying-work labels: PD Worldwide (described as mostly true of works created or published before 1870); PD 70 Years (author's death plus 70 years: UK, most EU, Brazil, Israel, Nigeria, Russia, Turkey); PD 50 Years (New Zealand, Australia for pre-1955 deaths, and many countries in Africa, the Caribbean, and Asia); PD US (published more than 95 years ago "generally" public domain in the US); PD GOV (US federal works; UK Crown Copyright on published works lasts 50 years from publication); No Known Restrictions; Effectively PD. Documented (the label definitions are PDR's own; the legal summaries are Reported)
7. Caution for US artworks: even pre-1870 artworks may be complicated because of US "publication" rules. The site advises taking care with commercial use. Reported
8. "No Known Restrictions" usually rests on a lack of information, not proof. Photographs labelled this way may still carry privacy or publicity rights. Documented (PDR's guidance)
9. Digital-copy labels: No Additional Rights (often CC0 or the Public Domain Mark; commercial use and modification allowed); Attribution (CC BY; credit the copy as the institution specifies); Share-Alike (CC BY-SA; an altered derivative must be released under the same or a similar licence); PD Wikimedia (the institution may assert restrictions, but Wikimedia treats faithful 2D reproductions as PD; "more risk"); Non-commercial (CC BY-NC or terms-of-use limits, for example the Bibliothèque nationale de France); Unclear (often Internet Archive material, possibly under its default CC BY-NC-SA). Documented
10. Coloring-book implication: a traced or redrawn coloring page made from an image whose digital copy is labelled Share-Alike might have to be released under CC BY-SA. Non-commercial or Unclear copies are unsuitable for a book you sell unless you get the image from a different, unrestricted copy or source. Inferred
11. Edited versions: PDIA retouches images (rotation, exposure) and crops illustrations out of book scans, but "we assert no additional rights to these edited versions." Documented
12. Credit to PDIA is optional ("under no obligation to mention us"), though appreciated. Attribution may still be required by the source institution for some images (see the per-image label). Documented
13. Metadata is different: the site's metadata (titles, themes, tags) is published under CC BY-NC "to ensure against the copying of our site for commercial endeavours." The images themselves are excluded from this. Do not copy their descriptions or tags into commercial listings. Documented; the listing advice is Inferred
14. Resolution: images below 1,500 pixels on the longest edge are mostly old uploads. For a higher-resolution file, follow the link to the source institution. Do not email PDIA for files. About one-fifth of images are large enough to sell as prints. Documented
15. Liability disclaimer: the rights information is "strictly meant as a guideline." PDIA "admit[s] no responsibility" and does not grant permissions. For certainty, contact the source institution. Documented
16. Workflow for a coloring-book creator: (a) check both labels on the image page; (b) prefer PD Worldwide + No Additional Rights; (c) if you sell outside the US, avoid images labelled PD US only; (d) record the source institution, the item URL, and both labels for your rights file; (e) for attribution labels, follow the institution's specified credit format. Inferred
