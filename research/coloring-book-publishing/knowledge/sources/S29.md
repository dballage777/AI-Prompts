## Learned points
1. ALLi's stance is "neither blanket endorsement nor blanket rejection" of AI. Reported
2. Three author archetypes: AI-Minimal (basic embedded tools only), AI-Assisted (research, brainstorming, editing, business tasks, but not generating the creative content), AI-Integrated (AI across the process, including generating text). All three are called legitimate if the author is accountable. Reported
3. Six principles: for AI developers, Clarity, Consent, Compensation; for authors, Curiosity, Creativity; for all, Accountability (the publisher is responsible for accuracy and appropriateness whatever tools were used). Reported
4. ALLi encourages each author to publish an AI policy on their website as a "trust signal." Reported
5. Ethical Author code clause: declare AI use "where appropriate"; edit and curate generative output so it is not discriminatory, libelous, infringing, or illegal; the author, not the tool, is responsible for legal compliance. Reported
6. Copyright summary: humans can claim copyright in their own expression, creative modifications of AI output, and creative selection/arrangement; wholly AI-generated text is not copyrightable; "Prompting alone is not enough." Consistent with USCO S15/S16. Reported
7. Covers and images: prompt-based imagery may be useful for mood boards or initial concepts for designers; "Wholly AI-generated cover art" is not copyrightable. Reported
8. Some prizes, awards, and reader communities may exclude books with AI in the text "or even on the cover." Reported
9. Disclosure: authors are "not legally required to declare the use of AI," but ALLi encourages transparency; not every AI use needs a copyright-page label. Reported
10. ALLi states Amazon KDP and other platforms ask at upload whether any part (text and images) was AI-generated or AI-assisted. Reported (secondhand; verify against KDP's own policy page)
11. Unethical examples: copying AI output without review; claiming a book is fully human-made when AI played a substantial role; not checking facts; relying on outputs that "closely mimic" a living author's style or a specific copyrighted work. Reported
12. Recommends running AI-generated text through a plagiarism checker (ProWritingAid, Grammarly named). Reported
13. Training-data legality is unsettled: ALLi reports one federal court found training on legally acquired books to be fair use, while pirated-book use was treated differently; "early rulings, not the final word." Reported
14. Market saturation: ALLi says the market is already saturated; differentiation comes from a unique voice and real reader connection. Reported
15. For a coloring-book prompt: build in a disclosure step (platform AI questions), an originality/style check that avoids imitating living artists' styles or specific copyrighted works, and a human review of every page. Inferred
