## Learned points
1. Market claim: children's activity books (category incl. coloring books) "sold 16.7 million print copies in 2025—an 18% increase"; sales up "another 18% through May 2026", attributed to Publishers Weekly. — Reported (secondary citation; PW not checked)
2. Coloring books are consumable ("coloring books get used up"), which the author frames as repeatable demand. — Reported
3. Plan by audience: young children need "simple subjects, thick outlines, and plenty of open space"; older children more detail; adults "more intricate patterns". Pick one clear theme rather than unrelated pages. — Reported
4. Paper: "Uncoated paper is a necessity for coloring books" so pages can be drawn on. — Reported (note: conflicts with Lulu's own S30 FAQ, which also recommends 80# coated)
5. Ink: choose premium black & white ink; "standard or economy ink may not create thick enough lines". — Documented (Lulu product advice)
6. Binding: paperback preferred; hardcovers "don't lay flat as nicely"; lay-flat is a key feature. — Reported
7. Single- vs double-sided: single-sided "reduce the risk of markers bleeding onto another illustration"; double-sided uses less paper and costs less. — Reported
8. Artist workflow: pencil sketch → ink → scan at high resolution → clean shadows/smudges in editing software; aim for consistent line weights and open space; review art at actual page size because thin lines "may disappear" in print. — Reported
9. Tools named: Adobe Illustrator, Photoshop (subscription), Procreate (Apple/iPad, one-time purchase), Affinity (described as "free design software from Canva"). Software matters less than exporting a high-resolution file. — Reported
10. Photo-to-line-art: Canva can convert an uploaded photo to line art (free "doodle" conversion; AI cleanup needs paid Canva); automatic conversions "will always need cleanup" (hair, shadows, patterned clothing, busy backgrounds). Choose photos with clear subject, strong contrast, good lighting, simple background. — Reported
11. AI generation: author used ChatGPT with the prompt "make a line art drawing of a bird over a mountain"; for a business, preset instructions for line thickness, page size and bleed. No discussion of AI disclosure or copyright. — Reported
12. Personalized-book business model: generate pages from customer inputs, assemble a print-ready PDF to Lulu specs, fulfil via the Lulu Print API. — Documented (Lulu service)
13. Interior file: a single multi-page print-ready PDF; each illustration on its own page, not exported as two-page spreads. Key specs: 300 PPI images, embedded fonts, flattened transparency, uniform page dimensions, adequate gutter and safety margins. — Documented
14. Cover: single-page PDF with front, back, and spine, or use Lulu's Cover Creator. — Documented
15. Proof: "Do not skip the proof"; color some pages with pencils, crayons, or markers to test. — Reported (vendor advice)
16. Selling on Lulu: Lulu Bookstore (listing + checkout), Lulu Direct (Shopify, Wix, WooCommerce integrations, bulk order upload), Direct Buy Buttons (Stripe checkout; links, QR codes). — Documented
17. A "rundown of our top three design specifications" appears to be an image; its contents were not in the page text. — Inferred (gap)
