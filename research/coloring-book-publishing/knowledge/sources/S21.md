## Learned points
1. The hub links to the Trademark Search system, a search-features updates page, a federal trademark searching webinar series, and handouts (getting started, field tag searching, field tag searching with regex, transitioning from TESS). Logging in with a USPTO.gov account is recommended to avoid errors under heavy traffic. — Documented
2. Likelihood of confusion is "the most common reason for refusing registration." It means consumers would likely think the goods or services come from the same source. — Documented
3. Marks do not have to be identical to conflict. Similarity in sound, appearance, or meaning, or a similar commercial impression, can be enough. A standard-character registration protects the wording regardless of font. — Documented
4. Relatedness of goods matters: identical marks can coexist for unrelated goods (Dove soap / Dove ice cream bars; Delta faucets / Delta air transportation). Goods are related if identical, similar or competitive, used together, bought by the same purchasers, advertised together, or sold by the same maker or dealer. They "don't have to be in the same international class to be related." — Documented
5. A comprehensive clearance search covers: the USPTO search system (registered and pending marks); the Trademark Official Gazette (TMOG, weekly); state trademark and business registries (50 states, DC, Puerto Rico); domain registries (ICANN); Madrid Monitor; WIPO Global Brand Database; EUIPO TMview; and multiple internet search engines. At minimum: the USPTO database, the TMOG, and an internet search for common-law use. — Documented
6. Common-law rights come from use in US commerce alone, without registration. An earlier common-law user can affect a later registrant and in some cases sue, even if they have no federal registration. — Documented
7. Word search method. (a) Knock-out search on the combined mark field: `CM:uspto`, or a phrase in quotes, `CM:"united states patent and trademark office"`. (b) Expand with regex wildcards: `CM:/.*uspto.*/`, and for several words `CM:(/.*united.*/ AND /.*states.*/ ...)`. (c) Search alternative spellings and pronunciations, for example `CM:/.*youesspeeteeoh.*/`. (d) Combine strong words in "Expert" mode by search-history number, for example `1 AND 2 AND 3`. — Documented
8. Design search method: find codes in the Design Search Code Manual, then search, for example `DC:050101` (conical trees), with wildcards `DC:0501??`, and combine, for example `DC:(050101 AND 031507)` (evergreen + owl). Relevant for character or logo-like cover art. — Documented
9. Tips: start narrow, then broaden. Exclude results already reviewed with `AND NOT 1`. Narrow by goods or services only if absolutely necessary; prefer coordinated class over international class. Focus on live marks (uncheck "dead"), but do not ignore dead marks, because they may still be in use under common law. — Documented
10. Click any result to open TSDR (Trademark Status and Document Retrieval) for its full record and documents. — Documented
11. A search cannot guarantee registration. Filing fees are not refunded if the application fails. The examiner runs their own federal search and may reach a different conclusion. — Documented
12. If a registered mark conflicts, the application is refused. If an earlier pending application conflicts, the application is suspended until that one is decided. — Documented
13. Help options: private trademark attorney; law school clinic certification program; Patent and Trademark Resource Centers (can demonstrate searching but give no legal advice); Trademark Assistance Center for general questions. Private search firms supply results only, not analysis. — Documented
14. For coloring books: before using a series title, brand name, or character name, search CM: for the exact and similar wording. Check for live marks covering related goods such as printed books, coloring books, printables, or downloadable images. Also run an internet search for common-law use. Characters from franchises may also be protected by copyright, which this source does not cover. — Inferred
15. The pages do not name the classes that apply to coloring books (for example, printed matter or downloadable files). That would need the ID Manual, which was not read. — Documented absence
