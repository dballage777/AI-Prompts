## Learned points
1. Product features they prioritized from studying competitors: illustrations printed single-sided; option to cut out pages for framing; paper thick enough for main coloring tools; affordability; fair royalty. — Reported
2. Content volume: decided on at least 50 designs, finding 50 "a good average number compared to other coloring books". — Reported (cf. S17: 16–50 illustrations typical)
3. Process: nightly pencil sketches on graph paper (1–2 per night for ~2 months) → scan → clean up in Krita (free, open-source digital painting program). — Reported
4. Line work: chose a consistent line thickness as best for the style and for coloring; reworked designs that were "too detailed" or lacked "breathing room". — Reported
5. QA loop: print out, mark up in red pen, fix, reprint, repeat; two-person review caught mistakes. — Reported
6. Test-coloring: digitally colored all 50 illustrations in 4 color schemes each; this revealed flaws because "coloring brings out the overall shape". Colored versions also sold as art prints (extra product line). — Reported
7. Paper: ordered print tests of various paper types; chose "70 lb standard color paper" as balance of quality and budget. The article does not state which printer/platform or paper product that was. — Reported
8. Timeline: art finished 3 months before a planned launch date (July 6, 2019); soft launch with pre-orders; whole project took "nearly 3 years". — Reported
9. Copyright: they registered artwork with the Copyright Office; noted copyright is automatic but registration "provides extra legal protection". — Reported (general legal statement by non-lawyers)
10. Marketing: keywords drawn from their own perception of the art (abstract, surreal, strange, unusual, peculiar) used in social posts and listing descriptions; Instagram ads outperformed Google keyword ads for exposure; did not gather pre-order reviews (would reconsider). — Reported
