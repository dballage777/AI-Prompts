# S30 — The Engagement-Diversity Connection: Evidence from a Field Experiment on Spotify (Holtz, Carterette, Chandar, Nazari, Cramer & Aral)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08203 (full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.08203)
- Access status: OPENED · Date read: 2026-11-14
- Pages read: full PDF (sections 1–7, Tables 1–4, Appendices D–H read; other appendices skimmed)
- Published / last updated: arXiv v1 17 Mar 2020 (only version). No venue stated in the PDF. Authors are from MIT Sloan and Spotify.
- Type: randomized field experiment (industry–academic working paper)

## Learned points
1. [Research] Design: a randomized experiment on 852,937 premium Spotify users in 17 countries, 18 April – 2 May 2019. Eligible users had never streamed or followed a podcast. All saw a "Podcasts to Try" shelf fixed in the second slot of Home.
2. [Research] Treatment: 10 podcasts chosen by a neural network that predicted follows from music listening history plus demographics. Control: the 10 most popular podcasts among users of the same gender, age bucket and country (a popularity baseline). Both lists were fixed before the experiment, and the only aim was to increase podcast consumption.
3. [Research] Engagement: personalization increased podcast streams per user by 28.90% (±3.81%) and the number of users streaming any podcast by 36.33%. The gain came from new listeners ("compliers", +1.505 streams), not from heavier listening by existing ones ("always takers", −0.082).
4. [Research] Individual diversity was measured as the Shannon entropy of podcast-category shares across 13 Spotify categories. Among streamers, entropy was 11.51% (±1.08%) lower in treatment (non-causal, because it conditions on streaming). The causal estimate for always-takers was −0.070 (95% CI 0.062–0.076).
5. [Research] Aggregate (intragroup) diversity, 1 minus the mean squared cosine similarity to the average user vector (Aral & Dhillon 2016), rose 5.96%, from 0.710 to 0.753. Each user's listening was narrower, but users differed more from one another. The authors call this "Balkanization".
6. [Research] On Home (where the recommendations appeared), streams rose 55.75%, entropy fell 17.70%, and intragroup diversity rose 14.04%. Off Home (organic search and library), streams rose 10.47% and entropy fell 3.31%, but intragroup diversity did not change significantly. So recommendations partly shaped what users went looking for themselves.
7. [Research] Sales diversity (Lorenz/Gini over the top 1,000 podcasts) improved: Gini fell from 0.692 to 0.642. Personalization sent more streams to less popular podcasts than the popularity baseline did. "Diversity" can therefore move in opposite directions depending on the measure.
8. [Research] Long-term (3 May – 17 July 2019): the large effects "quickly shrink in magnitude, and in some cases disappear entirely" after the experiment. This is confounded by the shelf being removed, relaunched to everyone and no longer anchored in slot 2.
9. [Research] The authors' framing is the "engagement-diversity trade-off" when recommendations are optimized only for consumption. They cite Anderson et al. (2020, Spotify observational data): more diverse listeners churn less and are more likely to become paid subscribers. If causal, short-term engagement gains could be neutral or negative for long-run value.
10. [Research] This contrasts with Lee & Hosanagar (2019), who found recommenders neutral-to-positive for individual diversity but negative for aggregate diversity. The authors attribute the difference to different algorithms, settings and diversity measures, and call for many measures across many settings. This is an open debate.
11. [Research] Context cited from the intro: collaborative filtering drives 35% of Amazon product choices (Lamere & Green 2008) and 60% of Netflix consumption choices (Thompson 2008), both older second-hand figures.
12. [Research] Caveat in the reported tables: Table 2's treatment coefficient for entropy among streamers is printed as +0.071/+0.070, while the text reports an 11.51% decrease. Table 4 shows negative coefficients (−0.116 Home, −0.018 non-home). The text is internally consistent about a decrease, so the sign in Table 2 is likely a typo.
13. [Inferred] For a prompt-based personal show/movie/anime recommender: an LLM told only to maximize "what you'll most likely watch" will probably narrow your diet to one niche. Build diversity in explicitly, for example by reserving 1–2 of every 10 slots for a different genre, format, country or era that still connects to a known like.
14. [Inferred] Personalized picks can still be less mainstream than "top charts" (Gini improved). So "personal" and "popular" are not the same axis. Track both individual variety (genre spread in what the user actually watches) and popularity (how mainstream the titles are), and review both periodically.

## Limitations or bias
- A two-week, single-surface experiment in podcasts (a new vertical for users who had never streamed podcasts), not TV, film or anime. Diversity is measured with coarse category tags (13 categories, and 68.23% of podcasts have one tag).
- The individual-diversity effect among streamers is non-causal (it conditions on a post-treatment variable). The causal estimate is only for "always takers", via principal stratification assumptions.
- Spotify co-authors and data, so an industry interest is possible. Long-term results are confounded by product changes. One table appears to have a sign typo.
- The comparison is personalized vs demographic popularity, not personalized vs diversity-aware personalization.

## Summary-only items
- Figures with the long-term time series (Appendix D, Figures I.12–I.20) were described only in the text and not inspected visually. The Anderson et al. (2020) churn finding is second-hand via this paper.

## Best used for
- Causal field evidence that engagement-only personalization raises consumption but narrows individual diversity while increasing between-user divergence. The distinction between individual, aggregate and sales diversity metrics. The case for building diversity into the recommendation objective.
