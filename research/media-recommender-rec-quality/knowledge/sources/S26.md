## Learned points
1. [Research] Core idea: the Language-based Factorization Model (LFM). An encoder LLM turns a user's rating history into a compact natural-language profile. A separate decoder LLM uses only that profile to make predictions. It is framed as an analogue of matrix factorization in which the "user vector" is human-readable text.
2. [Research] Claimed benefits of text profiles: they are intelligible without post-hoc explanation, and editing the profile causally changes later recommendations. Users could steer the system by writing or editing their own profile. Both the encoder and the decoder can also use natural-language item descriptions, which helps cold start.
3. [Research] Setup: MovieLens Tag Genome 2021 dataset. 300 randomly sampled "typical" users with exactly 150 ratings each. History size c varied (examples at 10, 20 and 30 items). 3 held-out movies or pairs per task, repeated 3 times, for 2,700 test points. Models: Llama 2 7B, Llama 2 13B and Sakura-SOLAR 10.7B instruct, all zero-shot with hand-tuned prompts. Baseline: unbiased NMF (15 factors, 10 epochs), plus a "Default" model that predicts the mean rating (0.5 accuracy on pairwise tasks).
4. [Research] Three tasks: rating prediction (cardinal score), pairwise preference (which of two movies the user rated higher), and pairwise choice (which of two movies the user chose to watch and rate).
5. [Research] Main result: on the pairwise tasks, putting everything through a short profile caused "no systematic degradation" compared with feeding the full history to the LLM directly. For rating prediction, the direct approach does better with large histories.
6. [Research] Cold start: LFM is most competitive with NMF when history is small. NMF improves greatly when given background rating data from other users. The zero-shot LLM methods cannot use that data, which the authors call "one of the biggest shortcomings" of zero-shot LFM.
7. [Research] Profile length: going from 50 to 200 words (history size 30) had no consistent effect on error. Rating prediction did not improve, and preference and choice prediction improved only modestly. The authors conclude that "even a short profile can capture much of the relevant information".
8. [Research] Choice prediction (what the user picks to watch) had the best error rate and kept improving with more history. The authors suggest that short profiles capture what kinds of movies a user watches (genre, plot) better than what drives high versus low ratings. Longer profiles help pairwise preference.
9. [Research] Reliability problems: LLMs sometimes give unparseable answers, for example "I would give it a rating of 4 or 4.5 out of 5" or "I am unable to give this a score". LFM is usually as reliable as LLM-direct or more so. SOLAR was the least reliable, possibly because it was trained to refuse uncertain tasks. Llama 2 7B direct became much less reliable at large history sizes because it exceeded its context limit.
10. [Research] Bias: much of the rating-prediction error is systematic bias (positive bias relative to NMF), partly because the LLMs predict whole-number ratings. Zero-shot outputs are "not always ... readable" and "definitely not brief".
11. [Research] Profiles don't follow instructions well: although the prompt said "Do not quote movie titles", Llama 2 summaries often named specific titles and sometimes the user's exact scores. One example profile at input size 30 contains "seemingly nothing else" beyond liked and disliked titles.
12. [Research] Prompt design used (Fig. 7): the history is listed as "I gave <Title (Year)> a rating of X out of 5", followed by "summarize the reasons why I like or dislike certain movies in under 100 words. Do not quote movie titles." Decoder prompts then ask for a score out of 5 or an A/B choice. Inference settings: temperature 0.6, top_p 0.9, top_k 50, repetition penalty 1.2.
13. [Research] Output parsing required regexes that check for consistent scores. For pairwise preference, a second LLM call was needed to extract the answer. The authors admit that this parsing may perform differently across methods.
14. [Research] Efficiency: profiles can be computed offline, and the decoder prompt is much shorter than the full history. On one RTX A6000 GPU (unoptimized, Llama 2 13B, history size 10), summarizing 300 users took about 25–35 min, against seconds for NMF.
15. [Research] Future work named by the authors: fine-tuning both encoder and decoder; adding item and user metadata (comments, tags, transcripts); and user studies on whether people can read and usefully edit their profiles.
16. [Inferred] For a prompt-based personal show/movie/anime recommender: keep a short (about 50–150 word), editable "taste profile" file that the user can read and correct, and have the recommender reason from it plus recent history. The evidence suggests this costs little accuracy and gains steerability.
17. [Inferred] Profiles should describe the reasons behind likes and dislikes (themes, tone, pacing), not lists of titles. Check generated profiles for title-dumping and for invented "reasons". Don't trust LLM-predicted star ratings; ranking or choice-style judgments ("which of these would you rather watch") are more reliable.

## Limitations or bias
- Small, older open models (Llama 2 7B/13B, SOLAR 10.7B). Results may not carry over to current frontier models.
- Only MovieLens, only "typical" users with exactly 150 ratings, 300 users. Results are mostly shown in figures, without a full table of numbers in the main text.
- An unreviewed preprint with template placeholders left in. Code was "not currently set to public" at submission.
- There was no user study of whether people actually understand or edit profiles. Steerability is argued, not tested.
- Regex output parsing may favor one method over another (the authors acknowledge this).

## Best used for
- Evidence for using a short, human-readable, editable natural-language taste profile as the core user model in an LLM recommender. Cold-start behavior of zero-shot LLM recommenders. Practical failure modes (unparseable outputs, integer-rating bias, profiles ignoring instructions).
