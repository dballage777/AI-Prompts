# S6 — Large Language Models as Zero-Shot Conversational Recommenders (He, Xie, Jha, Steck, Liang, Feng, Majumder, Kallus & McAuley)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10053 (full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10053)
- Access status: OPENED · Date read: 2026-11-14
- Pages read: full paper PDF (v1, 11 pages: abstract, sections 1–7)
- Published / last updated: arXiv v1 submitted 19 Aug 2023 (only version). Venue: CIKM '23, Birmingham, UK (October 21–25, 2023). Authors from UC San Diego and Netflix.
- Type: peer-reviewed conference paper (empirical study and new dataset)

## Learned points
1. [Research] The paper prompts LLMs as zero-shot conversational recommenders with no fine-tuning. The prompt has three parts: a task description T ("Pretend you are a movie recommender system"), a format requirement F ("reply me with 20 recommendations without extra sentences"), and the conversation context S. A post-processor (for example, fuzzy matching) maps the generated titles to a ranked list of catalogue items. Temperature was 0 for all models.
2. [Research] Models tested: GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4, BAIZE-v2-13B, and Vicuna-13B, compared with the fine-tuned CRS models ReDIAL, KBRD, KGSF, and UniCRS. Datasets: INSPIRED (999 conversations), ReDIAL (11,348), and the new Reddit-Movie dataset (634,392 conversations, 51,203 movies, 36,247 users, from five movie subreddits, 2012–2022). Reddit-Movie is about 50 times larger than ReDIAL and is described as the largest public real-world CRS dataset.
3. [Research] Reddit requests are richer than crowd-sourced ones. Real users write requests like "Something that I can focus on but nothing too harsh... dreamy visuals." Crowd workers sometimes wrote "Whatever I'm open to any suggestion." Measured by n-gram entropy, Reddit-Movie carries more content/context information than ReDIAL or INSPIRED, close to QA and conversational-search datasets.
4. [Research] There is an evaluation shortcut in CRS benchmarks. "Repeated items" (items already mentioned earlier in the dialogue) count as ground truth, so a trivial baseline that just copies items from the conversation beats most prior models. More than 15% of INSPIRED ground-truth items are repeated items. HIT@1 drops by more than 60% on average when only new items are scored.
5. [Research] Finding 1: on new-item recommendation, the zero-shot LLMs beat all the fine-tuned CRS models on all three datasets, and GPT-4 was generally best. Absolute numbers are low: Recall@1 on Reddit is about 0.022–0.023 for GPT-3.5-t and GPT-4, and about 0.062–0.066 on INSPIRED for GPT-4. The open 13B models trail their teacher, GPT-3.5-t.
6. [Research] Hallucination: string-matching titles against the IMDB title database gives a lower bound on real titles. About 95.5% (GPT-3.5-t) and 94.9% (GPT-4) of top-20 recommendations matched, against 81.6% for BAIZE and 87.0% for Vicuna. The authors conclude there are "only a few" hallucinated titles in the movie domain. Removing out-of-catalogue titles slightly improves measured accuracy.
7. [Research] Probing which knowledge LLMs use: the context was perturbed as ItemOnly (titles kept, text removed), ItemRemoved (titles removed, text kept), or ItemRandom (titles replaced with random ones). ItemOnly cut Recall@5 by more than 60% on average. ItemRemoved and ItemRandom cut it by less than 10% for the GPT models. So LLMs rely mainly on content/context knowledge (genre, mood, actors, descriptions), not on collaborative "people who liked A liked B" knowledge.
8. [Research] Using only item mentions, the LLMs underperform trained CRS and item-CF models (FISM) by about 30% on INSPIRED and ReDIAL. So their collaborative knowledge is weaker. The exception is Reddit, where many items are rare: 12,982 items have no more than 3 mentions as responses. LLMs did better there, which supports earlier findings that LLMs help with cold-start items.
9. [Research] Collaborative signals are platform-dependent. CF encoders (FISM, Transformer) pre-trained on MovieLens-25M beat random on Reddit but were clearly worse than training on Reddit itself, and pre-training then fine-tuning added nothing. Only 63.32% of Reddit items could be linked to ML-25M.
10. [Research] Popularity bias (Finding 10): GPT-4's recommendations are more concentrated on popular titles than the ground truth. The Shawshank Redemption appears about 5% of the time on ReDIAL and about 1.5% on Reddit. By contrast, the most popular ground-truth items are about 2% on ReDIAL (Avengers: Infinity War) and under 0.3% on Reddit (Everything Everywhere All at Once). The popular items are similar across datasets, which suggests they reflect pre-training popularity. The authors warn of a bias amplification loop.
11. [Research] Regional bias (Finding 11): GPT-4's Recall@1 is higher for movies produced in English-speaking regions (USA, UK, AUS, CAN) than for, say, Japanese, French, German, or Italian films. The test linked 7,476 movies to production regions, reporting only regions with more than 300 data points. Reddit's ground truth is itself dominated by English-language films.
12. [Research] Future directions the authors name: ensembling with collaborative filtering, efficient tuning, revisiting debiasing and trustworthiness for LLM recommenders, and cross-regional evaluation. Chain-of-thought prompting was left to future work.
13. [Inferred] For a prompt-based personal recommender, rich free-text taste descriptions (moods, what the user liked about a title) matter more than bare lists of watched titles, because that is the knowledge LLMs actually use. A "taste notes" field per liked title is likely worth more than a longer watch list.
14. [Inferred] Expect a pull toward canonical crowd-pleasers (Shawshank-style picks) and toward English-language titles. For anime or non-English shows, the prompt should explicitly ask for less-popular and non-US options, and the output should be checked against a real catalogue (for example, by title lookup) to catch the roughly 5% of unmatched titles. Exclude titles already mentioned or watched, so "repeats" are not mistaken for recommendations.

- "LLMs suffer from limitations such as popularity bias and sensitivity to geographical regions." — https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10053

## Limitations or bias
- Movies only. The models tested are 2023 models (GPT-3.5/4, 13B open models), so the numbers may not transfer to newer models or to TV or anime.
- The hallucination check is string matching against IMDB, which is a lower bound on real titles. It does not check whether a real title is relevant, or whether descriptions of it are accurate.
- Ground truth comes from what Reddit users suggested, which is a noisy proxy for satisfaction. There is no user study.
- Possible test-set contamination is handled only by using December 2022 conversations. Netflix co-authors may have an interest in LLM CRS research.

## Best used for
- Evidence that LLM recommenders lean on content/context rather than collaborative knowledge. Numbers on popularity bias, regional bias, and title hallucination in LLM movie recommendations. The repeated-item evaluation pitfall. A simple zero-shot prompt template (task + format + context, then map titles to a catalogue).
