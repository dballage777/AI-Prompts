- Access status: OPENED · Date read: 2026-11-14
- Pages read: full PDF v2 (abstract, sections 1–6, tables 2–6). Appendices (fairness, diversity, profile example) were not read in detail.
- Published / last updated: arXiv v1 1 Nov 2024; v2 7 Sep 2026. Venue: The ACM Web Conference 2025 (WWW '25), Sydney. Authors are from Tsinghua, Waterloo, and Huawei Noah's Ark Lab. Code: https://github.com/JiangDeccc/EvaLLMasRecommender
- Type: peer-reviewed conference paper (evaluation framework and benchmark)

## Learned points
1. [Research] The paper proposes a multidimensional evaluation of LLMs used directly as recommenders. It has two conventional dimensions (utility: HR@5 and NDCG@5; novelty: APLT long-tail share and serendipity relative to MostPop) and four LLM-specific ones: history length sensitivity, candidate position bias, generation-involved performance (LLM-written user profiles), and hallucination.
2. [Research] Setup: 7 off-the-shelf LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-3.5, Claude 3 Haiku, Llama-3-70B, Qwen2-7B, Mistral-7B) and 3 prompting strategies (OpenP5 templates, LLMRank recency-focused, LLMSRec in-context demonstrations), against 6 traditional models (BM25, MostPop, BPRMF, GRU4Rec, SASRec, LightGCN). Datasets: Amazon Beauty, Amazon Sports, MovieLens-1M, LastFM. 1,000 sampled users per dataset (checked for representativeness with a K-S test), with candidate lists of 20 (1 positive + 19 negatives for ranking). Random HR@5 is 0.25.
3. [Research] Observation 1: overall, LLMs are less accurate than traditional models but can be more accurate where they have rich domain knowledge. On ML-1M, HR@5 was 0.875 for SASRec vs 0.734 for GPT-4o. On LastFM, LLMSRec with Llama-3-70B reached 0.906 and GPT-4o 0.886, vs 0.860 for LightGCN. 7B models failed to beat MostPop on Beauty, Sports, and ML-1M.
4. [Research] Observation 2: off-the-shelf LLMs were "far less affected by popularity bias than traditional models." Apart from BM25, the top five methods on the long-tail (APLT) metric were all LLMs, and the best LLMs led on serendipity even where their accuracy was lower. (This contrasts with S6's open-ended generation finding. Here the LLMs choose from a fixed candidate list.)
5. [Research] Observation 3, history length: LLMs gain most from the latest one or two history items and usually peak with fewer than 10. Traditional models keep improving with longer histories. With only 1–2 history items, the best LLMs beat traditional models, which helps with cold start. With zero history, LLMs are about at random on Beauty and Sports but above random on ML-1M and LastFM, because of prior knowledge of movies and artists. Qwen2 and Mistral got worse on ML-1M when given history.
6. [Research] Observation 4: "severe candidate position bias." Accuracy drops as the true item is placed further down the candidate list, and most LLMs favour early positions (Mistral on LastFM favoured late ones). This contradicts earlier work that found middle placement best. In ranking on Beauty, GPT-3.5's CandPosBias was 0.5172 (HR@5) and 0.4713 (NDCG@5), where 0 is ideal.
7. [Research] Observation 5: LLM-generated text profiles capture most of the useful patterns. On Beauty and Sports, a profile alone (no history) gives similar accuracy and better long-tail coverage, and profile plus history improves all four dimensions. On ML-1M and LastFM, profile-only loses some accuracy (GPT-4o-mini HR@5: 0.651 vs 0.674 on ML-1M; 0.765 vs 0.873 on LastFM), likely because item-level knowledge matters more there.
8. [Research] Observation 6, hallucination: the share of output items that cannot be matched to the item set (after case, space, and symbol normalization) is mostly about 1% and under 5%. Mistral is the outlier, at 22.88% on Beauty and 20.20% on ML-1M. GPT-4o: 0.46% on Beauty and 0.36% on ML-1M. Claude 3 Haiku: 6.58% on ML-1M. Typical errors: dropping part of a title, merging two titles, and misstating attributes.
9. [Research] Observation 7: LLMs do better as re-rankers of candidates retrieved by traditional models. GPT-3.5 beats the four recall models in both accuracy and niche exposure, and position bias is reduced. The authors suggest ordering the candidates by the recall model's rank to turn position bias into an advantage.
10. [Research] Grounding (mapping hallucinated outputs back to real items, tested on ML-1M): when the hallucination rate is below 0.005, no strategy helps. When it is higher, BM25 retrieval maps well and often beats Llama embeddings, and text-embedding-3-small is generally best. Recommended practice: measure the hallucination rate first. If it is low, simple grounding is enough. If it is high, use the best embedding model, or BM25 when cost matters.
11. [Research] Fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct roughly halves hallucination (ML-1M: 0.425 → 0.001; Beauty: 0.139 → 0.064) and only partly reduces position bias (it worsened slightly on ML-1M).
12. [Inferred] For a prompt-based show/movie/anime recommender, a two-stage design fits this evidence. First build a candidate list from a real source (a watchlist, catalogue search, or LLM brainstorm verified against a database). Then have the LLM re-rank and explain. Shuffle the candidate order, or run the ranking twice in different orders, to counter position bias.
13. [Inferred] Feed the most recent and most representative 5–10 liked titles plus a compact written taste profile, rather than the full watch history. Validate every output title against a lookup step, because about 1% hallucination per item still shows up often over many lists.

## Key quotes
- "LLMs suffer from a severe candidate position bias, favoring items at the beginning most of the time." — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00331
- "LLMs require only brief histories to perform well, while longer histories do not always benefit LLMs." — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00331
- "most LLMs tend to generate below 5% of non-existent items, while some LLMs significantly hallucinate more items." — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00331

## Limitations or bias
- The candidate-list setting (20 items) differs from open-ended "recommend me something" generation. Hallucination and popularity results may not carry over to free generation (compare S6, which found strong popularity bias in open generation).
- The only movie dataset is MovieLens-1M (older films). There is no TV or anime data. The models are 2024 versions, and Claude 3 was tested only as Haiku.
- Leave-one-out next-item prediction measures "predict what they interacted with next," not satisfaction.
- Fairness and diversity results are in appendices not read here.

## Summary-only items
- Appendix results on diversity and fairness, and the profile example (Section A), were not read; they are mentioned only in the main text.

## Best used for
- LLM-specific failure modes for recommenders (position bias, hallucination rates by model, how history length helps or hurts), the evidence that LLMs work better as re-rankers than as rankers, and practical grounding strategies for mapping generated titles to real items.
