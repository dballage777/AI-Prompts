# S29 — Contextualizing Recommendation Explanations with LLMs: A User Study (Feng, Feuerriegel & Shrestha)

- Access status: OPENED · Date read: 2026-11-14
- Pages read: full PDF (sections 1–7, Tables 1–4, Appendix A algorithm). Appendices B–C (interview questions; Claude 3.5 Sonnet and genre regression tables) were only partly read.
- Published / last updated: arXiv v1 21 Jan 2025; latest v2 19 Aug 2025. Accepted to ICWSM 2026 (International AAAI Conference on Web and Social Media), per the arXiv comments.
- Type: peer-reviewed (accepted) pre-registered online experiment plus interviews

## Learned points
1. [Research] Design: a pre-registered between-subjects online experiment with N=759 US adults (stratified-representative sample via Cint) and N=30 follow-up interviews. Three conditions: No Explanation (NE), Generic Explanation (GE), and Contextualized Explanation (CE, explicitly linked to the user's viewing history). The main model was GPT-4 Turbo (gpt-4-1106-preview). A replication used Claude 3.5 Sonnet with 140 more users and gave aligned results.
2. [Research] Sample and data: 1,401 completed the survey, and 759 remained after strict attention and speed exclusions (power analysis required 750). 387 women, 370 men, 2 non-binary. Mean age 52.85 (range 18–88). 2,716 ratings in total (904 NE, 911 GE, 901 CE).
3. [Research] Framework: Uses & Gratifications. Cognitive needs = Scrutability + Transparency + Trust. Affective = Persuasiveness + Satisfaction. Utilitarian = Effectiveness + Efficiency + Helpfulness. Plus UserIntent (willingness to watch). All measured on 7-point Likert scales, with items adapted from Balog & Radlinski (2020).
4. [Research] Cold-start-style procedure: users tick watched movies from a shortlist that starts from 10 popular IMDb Top 250 films. The LLM recommends a movie. If the user has already seen it, it joins the history and the next recommendation combines the history, up to 4 recommendations. Implicit "watched" signals were used, not likes or ratings.
5. [Research] Prompt details: the system prompt asks for "Title - Release Year - Director", a plot summary of up to 40 words, and an explanation of up to 100 words. Explanations focus on concrete aspects (character arcs, plot developments, scenes) and about three key features. The CE user prompt adds: "Highlight how it directly appeals to the provided viewing history." The GE prompt is "For {Movie}, write a recommendation explanation."
6. [Research] Hallucination control: all generated movies were cross-checked against the IMDb dataset "to ensure no hallucinations", and two experts validated the pre-compiled corpus. Every user with the same history and condition saw identical text.
7. [Research] Cognitive results: CE 5.38 vs GE 4.97 vs NE 4.94. CE was significantly higher than both (Holm-adjusted p<0.05). GE was no better than no explanation. In the regression, CE β=0.418 (p<0.001) on Cognitive.
8. [Research] Affective: CE 5.19 was significantly higher than NE 5.05 and GE 4.99. Utilitarian: CE 5.44 was significantly higher than NE 5.32 only (GE 5.34 n.s.). In the mixed-effects regression, the CE effect on Affective and Utilitarian was not significant.
9. [Research] Intent to watch: CE 4.85 > NE 4.61 > GE 4.36. Generic explanations were significantly worse than no explanation at all. CE β=0.277 (p<0.05) on intent.
10. [Research] More history references in an explanation (HistoryNum, up to 4) raised all four outcomes (β 0.048 Cognitive, 0.069 Affective, 0.044 Utilitarian, 0.101 Intent). The effects were small, and some users found long explanations redundant ("Sometimes too much is overwhelming.").
11. [Research] Moderators: users who already find recommender systems useful (RecSysUtility β about 0.27–0.30) and frequent movie watchers (β 0.125–0.175) rated every condition higher. Older users had lower intent (β=−0.147). Women gave higher utilitarian ratings. Comedies were rated lower than adventure films regardless of explanation type.
12. [Research] Interviews: trust came from explanations that named titles the user had seen and stated "true things about the characters". Explanations still helped users rule out unwanted films, which gave them a sense of control. But intent depends mostly on taste fit ("if the movie is not their type, they will not watch it"). Some users disliked spoilers or graphic descriptions.
13. [Research] Risk: LLMs treat viewing history as an implicit preference signal and may over-interpret it. Explanations that credit the user with preferences they do not hold can erode trust. The authors also note the generated corpus "tended to favor Western-centric narratives disproportionately".
14. [Research] Cost estimate: GPT-4o mini at about $0.00018 per interaction (200 input and 100 output tokens). At Netflix scale (301.6M subscribers × 50 recommendations per day), that is about $2.7M per day.
15. [Inferred] For a prompt-based personal show/movie/anime recommender: every "because you liked X" explanation should name 1–3 specific titles the user has actually watched and state a true, concrete shared feature (character arc, tone, premise). Avoid generic hype blurbs, which did worse than saying nothing.
16. [Inferred] Keep explanations short (under about 100 words, about 3 features, spoiler-light), and don't overstate what history implies. Where the link is uncertain, phrase it as a hypothesis ("you watched X, so you might like...") and let the user correct the taste profile. Verify that every recommended title exists before showing it.

## Limitations or bias
- One-shot, short-term online study with a constrained shortlist. Participants could not state niche preferences, and there was no longitudinal follow-up of actual viewing.
- US, English-speaking sample with an older mean age (about 53). Movies only, starting from IMDb Top 250 titles, which is a popularity-biased starting point.
- Participants often rated the movie and the explanation together, so explanation quality is confounded with recommendation accuracy.
- The paper is internally inconsistent in places: the discussion mentions the "insufficiency" of explanations for utilitarian and affective needs, while the abstract stresses affective gains. In the regression, only the Cognitive and Intent effects of CE are significant.

## Summary-only items
- The Claude 3.5 Sonnet replication results (Appendix C.1) and the genre regression table (Table 7) were taken only from the main text's summary, not read in detail.

## Best used for
- Evidence-based design of "because you watched X" explanations from LLMs: contextualized explanations beat generic ones, generic ones can backfire, and more references help a little while too much detail overwhelms. Prompt templates for explanations. The risk of over-interpreting implicit history.
