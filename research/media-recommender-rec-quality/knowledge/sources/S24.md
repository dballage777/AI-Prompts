# S24 — Enhancing Recommendation Diversity by Re-ranking with Large Language Models (Carraro & Bridge)

- Access status: OPENED · Date read: 2026-11-14
- Pages read: full PDF v2 (32 pp.): abstract, intro, related work, method, experiments, results, cost analysis, trade-offs (Sec. 6), conclusion. Appendix tables were skimmed, not analyzed.
- Published / last updated: arXiv v1 21 January 2024; v2 17 June 2024. The PDF says "Manuscript submitted to ACM". Authors: Insight Centre, University College Cork.
- Type: research paper (empirical study, zero-shot LLM re-ranking)

## Learned points
1. [Research] Beyond-accuracy goals, as defined here: diverse (items differ from one another), novel ("new or previously unexplored"), serendipitous ("surprising, unexpected, and going beyond the user's typical preferences"), and fair. The paper calls these under-investigated for LLM recommenders and says it is the first work on LLM re-ranking for a beyond-relevance goal.
2. [Research] Classic greedy diversification re-ranking picks items one at a time from a candidate list CL (size m) into a final list RL (size n), maximizing f = λ·rel(i) + (1−λ)·div(i, RL). Examples are MMR (div = negative maximum similarity to items already chosen), xQuAD and RxQuAD (intent-aware coverage of user aspects).
3. [Research] Diversity metrics used: ILD (average pairwise distance, as 1 − Jaccard on genres), SRecall (share of genres covered), EILD and rSRecall (the same, computed only over relevant items), and α-NDCG (α=0.5), which penalizes redundant aspects. Relevance is measured by NDCG@10. The paper notes that some researchers argue traditional diversity metrics correlate poorly with users' perceptions.
4. [Research] In an informal pilot, ChatGPT re-ranked 20 anime titles alphabetically with only one error, but popularity and release-date orderings only partly matched the ground truth (rank-biased overlap 0.5–0.8 and 0.35–0.7). Asked which of two lists was "more diverse" with no definition given, it chose a genre-coverage definition on its own, but on harder cases its answer contradicted its own genre counts or the dataset's genres.
5. [Research] Method: a zero-shot prompt with instructions, a strict numbered output format ("1-> <item name>"), and the candidate list in triple backticks, with each item's rank shown. Eight templates: T1–T4 are goal-only ("balance relevance and diversity", "maximize the items' genre-based diversity"), T5–T6 add each item's genres, and T7–T8 add a one-sentence plot summary. "Balance" corresponds to λ=0.5 and "maximize" to λ=0.
6. [Research] Data: Anime Recommendation Database (after filtering, 118k users, 2.6k items, 17M ratings, 40 genres; titles in the Roman alphabet only, released before 2021) and Goodreads (166k users, 8k books, 16 genres). Candidates come from matrix factorization, with m=40 (anime) and m=50 (books), n=10, and 500 test users. Models: ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo-0613), InstructGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo-instruct), Llama2-7B-Chat and 13B-Chat.
7. [Research] Anime results (average over the eight templates, % change vs. MF): NDCG fell 10.1% (ChatGPT) to 26.0% (Llama2-13B), compared with 4.6–6.5% for the traditional re-rankers and 58.8% for random. ILD rose about 4.4–4.7% for the LLMs (MMR +12.6%) and SRecall rose 4.7–8.0% (MMR +15.0%). ChatGPT's best template lost only 2.8% NDCG, better than every other re-ranker.
8. [Research] Books were much worse. ChatGPT lost 33.4% NDCG and the Llamas about 60%, close to random (−66.3%). The authors suggest the cause is more invalid generations (13.1% vs. 2.3% for anime) and possibly weaker domain knowledge, longer prompts and similar titles.
9. [Research] Overall: the LLM re-rankers beat random by a wide margin (so they do understand the task) but trail the traditional greedy re-rankers, especially on relevance-aware metrics. ChatGPT was best and Llama2-13B worst, unexpectedly below 7B.
10. [Research] Three reasons for the gap: the LLMs draw lower-ranked candidates (average lowest rank 22.6–28.9 for anime vs. 16–21 for traditional re-rankers), random fill-ins replace invalid outputs, and the LLMs have no access to the user profile or genre distributions, which xQuAD and RxQuAD use.
11. [Research] Hallucination and invalid output: LLMs added extra details, repeated the instructions, renamed titles (for example "Naruto" for "Naruto:Shippuden"), or returned items not in CL. The authors stripped extras, fuzzy-matched titles, and filled gaps with random CL items. Invalid rates: ChatGPT 3.0%, InstructGPT 1.2%, Llama2-7B 18.9%. By template: T1 4.0% up to T8 22.4%, so the richer feature-aware prompts produced more invalid items.
12. [Research] Prompt design matters, and no template was best overall: results varied by model, dataset and metric. Feature-aware templates (genres or plot summaries) generally helped despite more invalid outputs. "Maximize diversity" templates did not reliably maximize diversity.
13. [Research] Cost: traditional re-rankers took 0.05–0.18 s per user, while LLMs took 12.6–31.6 s (Llama) or up to 25 s (OpenAI, an upper bound from rate-limit sleeps), about three orders of magnitude slower. OpenAI calls for 500 users cost about $161 (about 78.4M input tokens).
14. [Research] Trade-offs (Table 10): LLM re-rankers need no explicit item features and can use implicit cross-domain knowledge. They are limited by context size (about 4k tokens for the models tested), do not handle item cold start (new releases are unknown after the training cutoff, and RAG is suggested), do not always produce valid rankings, and depend on items having distinctive, known titles. Non-unique titles are a problem.
15. [Research] Future work suggested: regenerate on invalid output, better fuzzy matching, few-shot demonstrations, RAG for item features, step-by-step instructions (for example, "at each step, you select an item that is different from the ones already included"), and evaluating novelty and serendipity.
16. [Inferred] For a personal anime/show recommender, a two-stage design fits this evidence. First generate or retrieve a larger relevant candidate set, then have the LLM pick a diverse final 10 with each candidate's genres or tags and a short synopsis in the prompt. Keep the candidate ranks visible and validate every output title against the candidate list or a database, regenerating rather than accepting near-miss titles.
17. [Inferred] Do not expect an instruction like "maximize diversity" to work reliably. State concretely what diversity means (for example, no more than two titles per genre, or a mix of eras and formats), and check the result in code (genre coverage, pairwise distance).

## Limitations or bias
- Only 500 test users, and the authors note wide confidence intervals on most metrics. Two datasets, with diversity defined by genres only.
- Older models (GPT-3.5 era, Llama 2, about 4k context). Zero-shot only, with no few-shot examples, chain of thought or RAG retrieval. Results may not hold for current models.
- Invalid items were filled with random candidates, which mixes hallucination cost with ranking skill. The OpenAI latency figure is an upper bound, not a measurement.
- The anime dataset was filtered to Roman-alphabet titles released before 2021 to favor LLM familiarity, which flatters LLM performance on anime.

## Summary-only items
- Per-template, per-model numbers in Appendix B (Tables 11–12) were not individually checked. Only the main-text tables were used.

## Best used for
- Evidence on LLM-based diversity re-ranking: prompt template design, the relevance/diversity trade-off in numbers, invalid-title handling, and costs. The anime-dataset results apply directly to an anime recommender.
