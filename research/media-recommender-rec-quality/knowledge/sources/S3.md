## Learned points
1. [Research] Definition: a recommendation list is calibrated when it reflects a user's various past interests in their proportions. A user who watched 70 romance and 30 action movies should get about 70% romance and 30% action. The concept is borrowed from classifier calibration and fairness in machine learning.
2. [Research] Core problem: training for accuracy crowds out lesser interests. In the extreme case (like always predicting the majority class), the most accurate top-10 for a 70/30 user is 100% romance. With MovieLens-20M numbers, even the 10th-best romance title is more likely to be played than the best action title (ratio about 2.33/2.1 > 1). So pure ranking by p(i|u) gives zero action titles.
3. [Research] The cause is ranking, not the model. Sampling genres in proportion keeps balance but costs accuracy. Deterministic top-score ranking maximizes accuracy but loses balance. The effect also appears with latent topics or embeddings and with multi-genre items, and it is described as independent of any particular model trained for accuracy.
4. [Research] This also hurts shared accounts: the interests of less active household members get crowded out. Over time, unbalanced lists may narrow a user's interests, similar to echo chambers or filter bubbles.
5. [Research] Metric: C_KL = KL(p||q̃) between p(g|u) and q(g|u). p(g|u) is the genre distribution of the play history, optionally weighted by recency. q(g|u) is the genre distribution of the recommended list, optionally weighted by rank. q is smoothed with α = 0.01 to avoid infinite divergence. KL is zero when perfectly calibrated and penalizes small genres heavily: going 2% → 1% counts as worse than 50% → 49%. It slightly favors more uniform distributions. The Hellinger distance is a gentler alternative.
6. [Research] Algorithm: greedy post-processing re-rank (maximal marginal relevance). It maximizes (1−λ)·Σ scores − λ·C_KL over a list of N items, appending one item at a time. A submodular surrogate gives a (1 − 1/e) optimality guarantee, and every prefix of the list is near-optimal, which matters because users see only the top rows or scan top-down. Calibration terms can be stacked for several attributes (genres, subgenres, language, movie vs TV show), each with its own weight.
7. [Research] Calibration is different from diversity. Diversity (low redundancy) would push a 70/30 user toward 50/50 or toward unwatched genres. It does not guarantee that action rises from 0% to about 30%. The right diversity trade-off would differ for each user.
8. [Research] Calibration alone keeps the user inside their past interests (a filter bubble). The fix is to blend a prior: p̄(g|u) = β·p0(g) + (1−β)·p(g|u), where p0 is uniform or the population average, so that genres outside past interests also appear. The paper's experiments did not use this prior. Steck calls it important for practical use.
9. [Research] Data: MovieLens-20M converted to implicit "plays" (ratings ≥ 4 kept). About 10M plays, about 21k movies, about 140k users. Multi-genre movies split their weight equally across genres. 99/1 train/test split (about 100k test plays). Baseline: a 50-dimensional weighted matrix factorization tuned for recall@50. Default λ = 0.99.
10. [Research] Results (averages over test users), from λ = 0 to 0.999: C_KL@10 fell from 0.677 to 0.054 and C_KL@50 from 0.185 to 0.005. Recall@50 stayed near 0.46 up to λ = 0.9 (0.464 → 0.460), was 0.453 at λ = 0.95, and dropped to 0.339 at 0.999. Recall@10 fell faster, from 0.209 to 0.146 at λ = 0.9 and 0.082 at 0.999. Large calibration gains are available for small accuracy losses, until λ gets large.
11. [Research] Practical tip from the results: raise λ gradually during the greedy re-rank. The top slots then stay close to the accuracy ranking, and later slots increasingly cover lesser interests.
12. [Research] Baseline calibration quality varies widely across users. For the 10% least-calibrated users, Drama and Adventure were over-represented. Lesser interests (Musical, Mystery, Thriller, Western) were essentially missing. Documentaries made up about 3% of plays on average but were almost absent from the baseline top-50. With λ = 0.99 the genres were well calibrated, and Drama became slightly under-represented to make room.
13. [Research] Calibration is presented as a user-centric fairness notion: fairness toward a user's interests, not toward groups of people. Equalized odds and statistical parity do not apply because genre base rates differ. An item-centric view (an item recommended twice as often should be consumed twice as often) is left for future work.
14. [Research] Compared with prior proportionality metrics, DP (ref. [9], from search-result diversification) gives 5:5 and 7:3 the same score against a 6:4 target. BinomDiv does not reach a fixed value at perfect calibration, cannot be averaged across users, and underflows for lists of hundreds of items (as on the Netflix homepage).
15. [Inferred] For a prompt-based show/movie/anime recommender: compute or ask the model to estimate the genre/format mix of the user's history (e.g., 50% anime, 30% prestige drama, 20% comedy), and instruct it to match that mix roughly in the list rather than filling it with the dominant taste. Reserve a small, explicitly labeled slice (the β prior) for outside-history exploration.
16. [Inferred] LLM rankers are also biased toward dominant patterns (S4 shows popularity bias). A two-step design is closer to Steck's post-processing: generate a scored candidate pool, then pick the final list greedily to satisfy proportion targets, with the top slots most relevance-driven. That should behave better than asking for a balanced list in one shot. Multiple attributes can be calibrated at once (genre, medium, length or episode count, language).

## Limitations or bias
- Offline-only evaluation on a single dataset (MovieLens-20M simulated as implicit feedback), with one baseline model. There is no user study or online test, so the user-perceived value of calibration is asserted, not measured.
- Uses MovieLens's coarse genre labels with equal weight per genre. The diversity-promoting prior is proposed but not tested.
- Read from a third-party mirror. The content matches the ACM-formatted paper, but the host is not the author's.
