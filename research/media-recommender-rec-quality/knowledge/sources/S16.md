## Learned points
1. [Research] Defines cold-start recommendation (CSR) as modeling "new or interaction-limited users or items"; cold start arises from new items, new users, or whole new platforms with sparse data. The survey frames solving it as a fairness/diversity issue too (new items get overlooked) and a retention issue (new users leave if early recommendations are irrelevant).
2. [Research] Taxonomy of nine cold-start tasks in four groups (Table 2, Fig. 4): Long-tail (users/items with fewer than k interactions); Normal cold-start (user, item, user-item — new entities with fewer than k interactions); Strict cold-start (user, item, user-item — zero interactions); System cold-start (zero-shot: no collaborative-filtering information at all, only short-term histories; few-shot: a few interactions plus CF patterns learned from other few-shot users).
3. [Research] How cold users/items are split in experiments is inconsistent: by interaction count k (default), by time of first appearance, or by random sampling. The authors call for unified definitions and benchmarks (Sec. 7.5), noting few papers use real cold users from industrial platforms.
4. [Research] The roadmap organizes methods by "knowledge scope," growing from (a) content features (genres, titles, descriptions, profiles, reviews), to (b) graph relations (interaction, social, knowledge graphs), to (c) domain information (cross-domain transfer from data-rich domains), to (d) world knowledge from LLMs. Fig. 1 illustrates content features with movie genres (Action, Horror, Sci-Fi, Animation, Romance).
5. [Research] Content-feature methods split into Data-Incomplete Learning (strict cold-start; content only, e.g., DropoutNet zeroes preference inputs in training so the model learns to rely on content) and Data-Efficient Learning (normal cold-start; meta-learning on few interactions).
6. [Research] LLM roles are split into "LLM as the Recommender System" (prompting or tuning) and "LLM as the Knowledge Enhancer" (representation enhancement or relation augmentation).
7. [Research] Prompting strategies: (i) Direct prompting — e.g., GPT-3.5 tested zero/few-shot on rating prediction, sequential and direct recommendation, explanation generation and review summarization; PromptRec handles extreme system cold-start by reframing recommendation as sentiment analysis over natural-language user and item profiles. (ii) Multi-step prompting — e.g., a 3-step GPT-3 pipeline: capture user preferences, select representative movies, then recommend. (iii) Retrieval-augmented recommendation — retrieve item characteristics, candidate items, or similar users' histories, then prompt.
8. [Research] Key caveat: direct prompting "demonstrate[s] less competitive results than traditional methods fully trained on user-item interactions," which motivated multi-step and retrieval-augmented designs.
9. [Research] Tuning-based LLM recommenders (TALLRec yes/no instruction tuning with LoRA; P5 unified text-to-text; CoLLM, A-LLMRec, BinLLM inject collaborative signals). Direct-prompting LLMs "struggle to handle and transfer rich collaborative filtering information," and some reach cold-start competitiveness only by sacrificing warm-start accuracy.
10. [Research] Hallucination/valid-item problem: one research line explores item identifiers "to accurately generate potential items and avoid hallucination, especially in zero-shot settings." TransRec uses multi-facet identifiers (ID, title, attributes) plus FM-index constrained generation so only valid identifiers are produced; RecSysLLM uses an entity pool in a tree; IDGenRec learns textual IDs.
11. [Research] LLM as knowledge enhancer: LLMs encode text/multimodal item descriptions to replace weak ID embeddings for cold items (EasyRec, SAID); KAR generates "inferential and factual knowledge" about items; ColdLLM simulates user interactions with cold items to "warm" them; CSRec builds a common-sense knowledge graph from LLM knowledge.
12. [Research] Future directions: multi-modal cold start (missing modalities; users differ in sensitivity to modalities), recommendation foundation models, efficiency (LLM latency; suggests LLMs for high-level planning such as cold-start cluster selection while traditional models do low-level real-time ranking), privacy (side information can be sensitive; federated learning, differential privacy), and unified benchmarks.
13. [Research] Scale: survey covers 220 papers through Dec 2024 vs 8–91 in earlier cold-start surveys (Table 1); publication counts on LLM-based CSR rise sharply in 2023–2024 (Fig. 2a).
14. [Inferred] A personal show/movie/anime recommender starting with no ratings is a "user cold-start" case in this taxonomy, but the LLM's world knowledge acts as the content/knowledge source. The survey's direction suggests a short multi-step flow (elicit preferences, pick representative anchor titles, then recommend) instead of one bare request.
15. [Inferred] To limit hallucinated titles, the survey's grounding ideas map to prompt-level rules: require each recommendation to carry multi-facet identifiers (title, year, type, and for anime the studio or season) and, when a catalog or watch list is available, constrain picks to it (retrieval-augmented recommendation).
16. [Inferred] New or obscure releases are an "item cold-start" problem for the LLM itself, because its knowledge has a training cutoff. The recommender should flag uncertainty for very recent titles and not claim facts it cannot verify.

## Limitations or bias
- Survey of methods, not an empirical comparison: it reports no head-to-head numbers for prompting vs trained models, so claims like "less competitive" are not quantified here.
- Focused on trained systems and academic benchmarks; offers little direct guidance for a pure prompt-based single-user recommender.
- Self-described "first" and "pioneering" claims; authors maintain a related GitHub list (possible promotional framing).
- Several mid-survey method sections (graph, cross-domain) were skimmed only through the taxonomy figure.

## Summary-only items
- Graph-relation (Sec. 4) and domain-information (Sec. 5) method details: known only from the taxonomy figure (Fig. 3) and contents list, not read in full.

## Best used for
- Vocabulary and definitions for cold-start variants (user/item/strict/system, zero-shot/few-shot, long-tail).
- Mapping LLM roles (recommender vs knowledge enhancer) and prompting strategies (direct, multi-step, retrieval-augmented).
- Citing the hallucination-grounding idea (multi-facet identifiers, constrained generation) for LLM recommenders.
