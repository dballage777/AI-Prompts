## Learned points
1. [Research] Three modeling paradigms (Fig. 2): (1) LLM Embeddings + RS (LLM as feature extractor feeding a traditional recommender); (2) LLM Tokens + RS (LLM generates tokens/summaries of preferences that feed a recommender); (3) LLM as RS (the prompt holds profile, behavior and task instruction, and the LLM outputs the recommendation directly). A prompt-based personal recommender is paradigm 3.
2. [Research] Taxonomy (Fig. 3–4): Discriminative LLMs (BERT-style; fine-tuning, prompt tuning) vs Generative LLMs (GPT-style), the latter split into non-tuning (prompting, in-context learning) and tuning (fine-tuning, prompt tuning, instruction tuning).
3. [Research] A general recommendation-prompt framework from an evaluation of ChatGPT on five tasks has three parts: task description, behavior injection (user-item interactions), and format indicator (constrain output). Others add role instructions at the start (e.g., "You are a news recommendation system now.").
4. [Research] A study comparing prompts with Items only, Language only (natural-language preference description) and Language+Items found zero- and few-shot prompting with language-only preferences "remarkably competitive" with item-based collaborative filtering, especially in near cold-start scenarios.
5. [Research] Prompting tricks for ranking: recency-focused sequential prompting (emphasize recent interactions); bootstrapping (shuffle candidate order several times and average, to reduce position bias); sliding-window ranking for long candidate lists; multi-perspective criteria ensembles for consistent pointwise ranking.
6. [Research] Multi-step pipelines: an LLM summarizes user preferences (e.g., from music and TV history into "pop music" and "fantasy movies"), a retrieval module shrinks the candidate pool, then a prompt with history, profile and candidates produces the recommendation. Agent-style systems (Chat-REC, InteRecAgent, RAH) use the LLM as controller calling retrievers, databases and memory over multi-turn dialogue.
7. [Research] In-context learning (demonstrations in the prompt) improves LLM recommendation on most tasks and can control output format and content. But the choice and number of demonstrations remain open questions, with few studies.
8. [Research] Non-tuned LLMs beat random guessing with good prompts but generally "fail to surpass" recommenders trained on task-specific data. Simple ID-based collaborative filtering stays highly competitive for warm items. In one case, a heuristic (repeat the last product title) beat a fine-tuned mT5.
9. [Research] Hallucination/out-of-corpus items: when LLMs generate items directly, "they may produce items that are out-of-corpus." Grounding fixes include multi-facet indexing (numeric ID, title, attributes) with FM-index constrained generation, and a bi-step approach that maps generated tokens to real items by embedding distance reweighted by popularity.
10. [Research] Model biases (Sec. 5.1): position bias (candidate order affects ranking; earlier items preferred; behavior order poorly captured); popularity bias (items discussed widely in pre-training corpora rank higher, which reduces diversity and is hard to fix because it is tied to the corpus); fairness bias (sensitive attributes such as gender or race); personalization bias (hard to turn text understanding into collaborative-filtering-quality personalization).
11. [Research] Prompt-design challenges: most work represents items only by name and users only by a list of item names, which is "insufficient." Context-length limits also cap history and candidate lists; workarounds include selecting representative history items and sliding windows.
12. [Research] Strengths: zero/few-shot ability mitigates cold start. On explanation generation, ChatGPT without fine-tuning beat some supervised baselines, and human raters judged its explanations clearer and more reasonable than the ground truth.
13. [Research] Evaluation problems: outputs deviate from the requested format or the model refuses; autoregressive models struggle with list-wise ranking; generative recommendations of never-seen items lack evaluation methods. Benchmarks (MovieLens, Amazon Books) are small and likely leaked into pre-training data, biasing zero-shot results.
14. [Inferred] For a prompt-based show/movie/anime recommender: write natural-language taste descriptions (what the user loves and why), not just title lists. Put the most recent and most-loved titles first. Use a strict output format with title, year and type. Ask for verification of existence to counter out-of-corpus hallucination.
15. [Inferred] Popularity bias comes from the pre-training corpus, so a prompt cannot fully remove it. The prompt should explicitly request a mix of popularity tiers, and a candidate list (if used) should be shuffled to avoid position bias.

## Key quotes

- "Popular items, which are often extensively discussed and mentioned in the pre-training corpora of LLMs, tend to be ranked higher." — https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19860

## Limitations or bias
- Literature survey with few quantified comparisons; most findings are summaries of cited works, not verified here.
- Coverage ends around mid-2024 (v5) and references early ChatGPT/GPT-3.5-era studies; newer models may behave differently.
- Self-described "first" comprehensive survey of generative LLM4Rec; one author affiliation is an industry recruiting firm, which explains job-recommendation emphasis.
- No venue stated in the PDF.

## Summary-only items
- Findings attributed to cited papers (e.g., the language-only prompt competitiveness, ChatGPT explanation human evaluation) are known only through this survey's summaries.

## Best used for
- Taxonomy of LLM-for-recommendation paradigms and prompting techniques.
- Documented LLM recommender failure modes: position bias, popularity bias, out-of-corpus hallucination, format drift.
- Prompt-design guidance (task description + behavior injection + format indicator; recency emphasis; candidate shuffling).
