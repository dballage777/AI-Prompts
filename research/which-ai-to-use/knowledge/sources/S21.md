## Learned points
1. The hub lists 20+ benchmarks and 100+ evaluated models, grouped as Agentic, Safety, and Frontier; there is no single overall ranking. Leaders differ by benchmark (for example GPT-6 Astra leads HLE-Diamond, Opus 5.5 leads Remote Labor Index and SWE Atlas Overall, Gemini 4 Argon leads MCP Atlas, GPT 6.1 Sol leads MultiChallenge, Muse Wonder 1.0 leads HiL-Bench), as of the page read on 2026-11-07. [Documented]
2. Scale ranks by "Rank (Upper Bound)": 1 + the number of models whose lower 95% CI bound exceeds this model's upper bound, so models with overlapping intervals share ranks. Raw score gaps of a few points are often not significant. [Documented]
3. Integrity rule: a model can only be featured "the FIRST TIME" an organization encounters the prompts; leaderboards combine private datasets (to prevent overfitting) and open-source sets (for comparability). [Documented]
4. HLE-Diamond (with Center for AI Safety): 1,000 frozen questions (500 reasoning, 500 knowledge) cut down from 2,500. Top score 60.6% (GPT-6 Astra), then Claude Opus 5.5 55.0%, Gemini 4 Argon 53.2%. When HLE launched in January 2025, frontier models scored in the single digits. Scale expects it to stay useful for 6–12 months. [Documented]
5. HLE-Diamond also reports calibration error (how well a model's stated confidence matches its accuracy). Calibration varies widely even among top models: Claude Opus 5.5 7.1, GPT-6 Astra 28.1, Gemini 4 Argon 53.3, DeepSeek V4 Pro 76.3. Scale stresses calibration "as an important metric alongside accuracy," which matters for how far to trust a confident answer. [Documented]
6. MultiChallenge tests realistic multi-turn chat in four categories: instruction retention, inference memory of user information, reliable versioned editing, and self-coherence (including avoiding sycophancy, "unconditionally agreeing to human users"). These map closely to classroom-style iterative use. [Documented]
7. MultiChallenge method: a plain LLM-as-judge agreed with human raters only 36% of the time, while LLM judges with instance-level yes/no rubrics reached 93% agreement. Test items were kept only if at least 3 of 6 2024 frontier models failed them, so the benchmark is biased against those six models. [Documented]
8. MultiChallenge current top scores: GPT 6.1 Sol 81.95 ± 4.16, Gemini 4 Argon 78.70, Muse Spark 75.52; Claude Haiku 5.5 67.67. Older models (e.g., GPT-4.1 39.43) score far lower, which shows how fast this changes. [Documented]
9. Remote Labor Index (RLI): 240 real freelance projects from 358 Upwork freelancers (230 private for scoring), mean human time 28.9 hours, total value $143,991. Expert humans grade whether a "reasonable client" would accept the AI deliverable. Inter-annotator agreement is 94.4%. [Documented]
10. RLI automation rates remain low: the current top is Opus 5.5 at 21.25%, GPT 6 Astra 20.83%, Fable 5.1 17.92%, Gemini Argon 4 17.08%. At launch the best agent (Manus) managed 2.5%. Even the best agents fail most real end-to-end work projects. [Documented]
11. RLI failure modes in failed projects: poor quality 45.6%, incomplete deliverables 35.7%, technical/file issues 17.6%, inconsistencies 14.8%. Successes cluster in audio, image generation, report writing, and data retrieval. [Documented]
12. HiL-Bench measures whether agents recognize missing, ambiguous, or contradictory information and ask a clarifying question instead of silently guessing ("selective escalation"). Its narrative text says the best model at writing reached only 24% Pass@3 when it had to decide to ask, versus 75–91% with full information upfront. The "bottleneck is not capability but judgment." The current table (Muse Wonder 1.0 at 79.67) uses a different, newer scale, so the narrative appears to describe an earlier version. [Documented; the mismatch is Inferred]
13. MCP Atlas tests real tool use: 1,000 human-written tasks over 36 real MCP servers and 220 tools, 3–6 tool calls per task, with distractor tools. Methodology changed in April 2026 (new judge, retries, 100-tool-call budget) and all models were re-scored. So scores from before and after a methodology change can't be compared. [Documented]
14. DistressBench: 718 clinician-written crisis conversations (suicide/self-harm), scored on weighted rubrics. The best model meets 88.4% of criteria, the median 71.4%, and clinician references 98.7%. In 35.3% of conversations where a model fully recognized the crisis, it still failed to route the user to human help. Relevant to students using chatbots for emotional support. [Documented]
15. DistressBench notes that older safety tests (single-turn refuse/comply) miss real failures: implicit ideation, "sycophantic validation to preserve rapport," missing crisis resources, and safety behavior that slips after the first turn. [Documented]
16. FORTRESS measures both harmful compliance (Average Risk Score) and over-refusal of benign paired prompts (Over-Refusal Score). This is an explicit safety-versus-helpfulness tradeoff: some models are safer but "overly cautious." Current top (lowest risk) is claude-opus-5-5-max at 5.78. [Documented]
17. Takeaway for choosing an AI: no single "best" model exists across tasks; the leader depends on the task (coding agent, tool use, multi-turn chat, expert knowledge, crisis safety). Check the task-specific board and its date, and treat CI-overlapping models as ties. [Inferred]

## Limitations
- Scale is a commercial data/evaluation vendor to the labs it ranks. Benchmark design choices are its own.
- Some narrative text on subpages (RLI, HiL-Bench, FORTRESS ranking table) appears older than the live score tables and cites earlier models, so narrative and table numbers may not match.
- Many benchmarks use LLM judges. Agent results depend on the scaffold (e.g., Claude Code vs. mini-SWE-agent).
- These measure API or agent setups, not the consumer chat apps a teacher uses. No education-specific benchmark was among the pages read.
- Charts/figures were not readable. The SWE Atlas subpage returned a near-empty shell.
- Model names are recorded as shown on the page on 2026-11-07. They are not verified against other sources.
