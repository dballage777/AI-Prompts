## Learned points
1. DRB measures how well LLM agents do open-web research. The live site says there are 169 real-world tasks, and each one comes with 10–100k webpages stored offline plus "carefully curated answers." The May 2025 paper described 89 task instances across 8 task categories, so the benchmark has grown since the paper. [Documented]
2. Agents search a frozen copy of the web called "RetroSearch." It runs a live Serper (Google) search, keeps only pages that exist in the frozen database, writes new snippets with a small LLM, and returns results in Google format. This makes runs reproducible as the web changes. The paper reports that offline agents rank about the same as live-web agents. [Documented; Research]
3. DRB leaderboard as of 2026-06-10 (score, cost per task, runtime in seconds): Opus 4.6 (high) 0.553, $0.53, 183 s; Sonnet 4.6 (high) 0.549; Opus 4.5 (high) 0.548; GPT-5.5 (high) 0.540, $0.36; Opus 4.6 (medium) 0.532; Gemini 3 Flash (low) 0.498 at only $0.10; Gemini 3.1 Pro (high) 0.478; Grok 4 0.473; o3 0.452; GPT-5.4 (low) last at 0.351. [Documented]
4. Even the top score is about 0.55, so the best agents get roughly half of the possible credit on these hard research tasks. Human-level web research is not solved. [Inferred from point 3]
5. A newer model or a higher reasoning-effort setting does not always score higher. Opus 4.8 (high) scored 0.502, below Opus 4.6 (high) at 0.553 and Opus 4.5 (high) at 0.548. GPT-5 (high) scored 0.481, below GPT-5 (low) at 0.496. Gemini 3 Flash (high) scored 0.479, below Flash (low) at 0.498. [Documented data; Inferred interpretation]
6. Cheap models can come close to the leaders. Gemini 3 Flash (low) scored 0.498 at $0.10 per task. Haiku 4.5 (low) scored 0.455 at $0.10. The site has dedicated "accuracy per dollar" and "accuracy per second" charts, but those charts did not render as text. [Documented; charts PARTIAL]
7. Scoring method: scores are "averaged first per task category … then across all tasks." Runtime is estimated from ReAct steps, not measured as wall-clock time. [Documented]
8. Paper (May 2025): of the commercial research products tested, "ChatGPT-o3" (regular ChatGPT with o3 and search) was the clear best and beat OpenAI's own o3-based Deep Research. One explanation the authors offer is that ChatGPT-o3 checked its answers before finishing. [Research]
9. Paper: the standard Perplexity Pro workflow beat Perplexity Deep Research. Gemini went the other way: Gemini Deep Research did noticeably better than Gemini 2.5 Pro with web search. Claude Research with extended thinking did slightly better than Claude 3.7 with web search. [Research]
10. Paper's reading of the results: for research with clear success criteria, "deep research" post-training adds little. People who prefer Deep Research products may be responding to longer background coverage rather than higher accuracy. [Research]
11. Common failure modes (paper): obvious, non-humanlike errors, such as Gemini 2.5 Pro taking the wrong statistic from a document and o3 repeatedly searching for a document ID that doesn't exist. Searching was weak too: queries copy the wording of the prompt, verbose queries get wrapped in exact-match quotes and return nothing, and OR operators go unused. [Research]
12. Paper's summary of capability: frontier agents fall short of a capable human given ample time. They can solve tasks that take a non-expert human about 20 minutes, as long as the task needs little planning and offers few chances to wrongly conclude the answer is found, and even then they are not fully reliable. [Research]
13. Paper limitations: only 89 instances and two runs per agent, so the statistics are weak. Some tasks are graded with LLM help, which adds noise. Agents could only read static pages, with no clicking or scrolling. The commercial-product sample was small. [Research]
14. Practical testing tip from the paper's methods: when the task and the formatting instructions were given in a single prompt, some web research tools began hallucinating an answer without doing any research. The authors therefore sent the task first and the formatting instructions second. [Research]
15. On the same site, the BTF-3 forecasting benchmark (2,386 questions, evaluated June–August 2026; Brier score, lower is better) ranks FutureSearch SOTA 0.116, Claude Opus 5 (xhigh) 0.120, Claude Opus 4.8 (xhigh) 0.132, GPT-6 Astra (low) 0.135, GPT-5.6 Sol (medium) 0.137, Muse Spark 1.3 0.141, Claude Sonnet 5 0.142, GLM-5.3 0.149. The site reports 95% bootstrap confidence intervals and paired significance tests. Some neighbouring pairs are not significantly different, for example Opus 4.8 vs GPT-6 Astra (p = 0.279). [Documented]
16. The site points out that model training cutoffs (June 2025 to May 2026) can affect forecasting results. It ran leakage checks on Claude Opus 5 (recall probes, timing gradients, a confidence audit, a trace audit) and found no leakage. It also notes that a model with a more recent cutoff may still have an advantage. [Documented]
17. For choosing a research tool, DRB shows three things: rankings depend on the effort setting and the agent harness, cost and speed differ by more than 10x at similar accuracy, and a vendor's branded "Deep Research" mode is not reliably better than its standard chat-with-search mode. [Inferred]

## Limitations
- The DRB leaderboard was last updated 2026-06-10, so it is about 5 months old. Models that appear elsewhere on the same page (Claude Opus 5, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol) are not on DRB. It lists no Grok 4.x versions after Grok 4 and no Gemini 3.x Deep Research product.
- The commercial-product comparison (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude research modes) comes from the May 2025 paper, and its sample was small. Those products have changed a lot since then.
- Per-category (radar) results and the cost and speed charts did not come through as text.
- The page reports no confidence intervals for DRB. Differences of about 0.01 between models may not be meaningful.
- FutureSearch sells forecasting and research agents, which is a possible bias, mainly on the BTF boards.
- The 169 tasks on the site and the 89 in the paper differ. The site does not explain what changed.
