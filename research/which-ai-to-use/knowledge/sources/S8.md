## Learned points
1. Frontier models are converging. As of March 2026 the top Arena Elo ratings are Anthropic 1,503, xAI 1,495, Google 1,494, OpenAI 1,481, Alibaba 1,449 and DeepSeek 1,424. Four companies sit within 25 Elo points, "shifting competitive pressure toward cost, reliability, and domain-specific performance." [Research]
2. Benchmarks saturate fast: frontier models gained 30 points on Humanity's Last Exam in one year, and SWE-bench Verified rose from 60% to near 100% in a year. Evaluations meant to last years now saturate in months. [Research]
3. Benchmark reliability is doubtful. Invalid-question rates run from 2% (MMLU Math) to 42% (GSM8K), and Arena standing "may partly reflect adaptation to the platform rather than general capability." Leaderboards should not be the only basis for choosing a chatbot. [Research; the advice is Inferred]
4. Open versus closed models: the top closed model leads the top open model by 3.3% (March 2026), up from 0.5% in August 2024. Six of the Arena top 10 are closed. [Research]
5. The US–China gap has effectively closed: the top US model leads by 2.7% as of March 2026, and the main page names Anthropic's top model as the leader. [Research]
6. The frontier is jagged. Gemini Deep Think earned an IMO gold (35 points), yet the top model reads analog clocks correctly only about half the time. The chapter page says 50.6% versus 90.1% for humans; the main page says 50.1%, an inconsistency within the source. [Research]
7. In professional domains (tax, mortgage, corporate finance, legal), models score 60–90%, and the top 15 are separated by as little as 3 points per benchmark. Model choice matters less than verification in high-stakes fields. [Research; the implication is Inferred]
8. Agents: OSWorld success rose from about 12% to 66.3%, but agents still fail roughly 1 in 3 attempts on structured benchmarks. [Research]
9. Hallucination and sycophancy: on a new knowledge-versus-belief benchmark, hallucination rates across 26 top models range from 22% to 94%. When a false statement is framed as the user's own belief, performance "collapses." For example, GPT-4o dropped from 98.2% to 64.4% and DeepSeek R1 from over 90% to 14.4%. [Research]
10. Language gap: AI works best in English. On HELM Arabic, a regional model beat GPT-5.1 and Gemini 2.5 Flash, and models lost close to half their accuracy in a Slovenian dialect. This is relevant for English-learner and multilingual families. [Research]
11. Safety and transparency: models rate "Good" or "Very Good" on AILuminate under normal use, but safety drops under jailbreaks. The average Foundation Model Transparency Index score fell from 58 to 40 in 2025. Documented AI incidents rose to 362 in 2025 from 233 in 2024. [Research]
12. Education: over 80% (four in five) of US high school and college students use AI for schoolwork. Only half of middle and high schools have AI policies, and just 6% of teachers say those policies are clear. Students mostly use AI for research, essay editing and brainstorming. [Research]
13. Adoption: generative AI reached 53% population adoption within 3 years, faster than the PC or the internet. The US ranks 24th at 28.3%. Organizational adoption is 88%, generative AI is used in at least one business function at 70% of organizations, and agent deployment is in the single digits. [Research]
14. Consumers derive value mostly from free tools: estimated US consumer surplus was $172B a year by early 2026, up from $112B, and median value per user tripled. [Research]
15. Productivity gains are largest in structured, measurable work (customer support 14–15%, software 26%, marketing output 50%) and smaller for deeper reasoning. Evidence also raises concern that heavy AI reliance may bring long-term learning penalties. [Research]
16. Labor: employment for software developers aged 22–25 has fallen nearly 20% since 2024, and one third of organizations expect AI-driven workforce reductions in the coming year. [Research]
17. Public opinion (2025 surveys): 59% globally say AI's benefits outweigh its drawbacks, while 52% say AI makes them nervous. 58% of employees use AI at work at least semi-regularly. On jobs, 73% of experts expect a positive impact compared with 23% of the US public. [Research]
