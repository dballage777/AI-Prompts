## Learned points
1. Picks (Sept 2026 version, 4 tools): Claude, "for professionals"; Google Gemini, "for Google integrations"; Microsoft Copilot, "for an integrated experience"; Perplexity, "for searching the web." [Reported]
2. The framing holds that ChatGPT "is still the best general-purpose chatbot," so an alternative must be best at something specific. [Reported]
3. Selection criteria: do something better than ChatGPT, be easy to use (no running your own models), integrate with the apps you use, work reliably, and stay up to date ("Six months is a long time in AI right now"). [Reported]
4. Chatbots have converged on features. All the top picks search the web, work with documents and images, and offer a reasoning model, so differentiation comes from integrations and specialties. [Reported]
5. Claude: most people find its default writing style more natural than ChatGPT's, and it responds more empathetically. The article credits it with a safety focus, Projects, Artifacts, MCP (which Anthropic created), Claude Code, and built-in agentic features ("previously called Claude Cowork"). The con is a focus on code and automation over image generation. Price: free, or Pro at $17 a month billed annually. [Reported]
6. Gemini: deep integration with Google apps (Docs, Gmail, Drive, Maps, YouTube, Flights, Hotels), a "Personal Intelligence" feature that proactively pulls context from them, and an embedded presence in Chrome and Android. Strong image (Nano Banana Pro) and video generation. Cons are less polish and features, plus "the odd bug." ChatGPT still wins on data analysis, reasoning and customization. Price: free, or Google AI Plus at $4.99 a month. [Reported]
7. Copilot shares models with ChatGPT and Claude (Microsoft invests in both OpenAI and Anthropic). It is built into Edge and the Office apps and is best in the Edge sidebar, where it can answer questions about the current page. The con is that it lags ChatGPT in features. Price: free, or from $9.99 a month within Microsoft 365. [Reported]
8. Perplexity: rated the best search and research features. It gives more sources, lets the user restrict sources to the web, academic papers or Reddit, and offers a choice of model (GPT, Gemini, Claude, Sonar). Its Comet browser and Computer agent features are "still finicky," and it is more awkward than ChatGPT for general tasks. Price: free, or Pro at $20 a month ($17 billed annually). [Reported]
9. Meta AI and Grok were cut in the latest update ("I just can't get behind them as strong alternatives"). They are suggested only for social integration. This contrasts with ZDNET (S9), which ranked Grok third. [Reported; the contrast is Inferred]
10. The article cites Zapier's AI Workflow Index (375 companies): 38% send chatbot output to Slack or Teams and 21% to Google Sheets. It also says 58% of people spend 3 or more hours a week revising or redoing AI "workslop." The second statistic's source and sample were not given in the text. [Reported]
11. Decision rule offered: for safety and professional features choose Claude; if you live in Google Workspace, Chrome and Android choose Gemini; for Microsoft and Edge users choose Copilot; for research with sources choose Perplexity. In short, choose by ecosystem and use case rather than a single "best." [Reported]
12. For schools on Google Workspace, Gemini's integration argument is likely decisive. Claude is the pick when writing quality and safety matter more than images. [Inferred]

## Limitations
- There is no scoring, no test protocol and no published prompts. The evaluation is qualitative and one author's opinion.
- Vendor content marketing: every pick is framed around Zapier integrations, which may favor tools that have them.
- The dates are inconsistent (byline Sept 14, 2026; content update May 2026; review Sept 2026), so feature and price details may be months old.
- The "workslop" statistic is uncited in the text.
