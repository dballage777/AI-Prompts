## Learned points
1. The comparison table covers Copilot, Perplexity, ChatGPT and Claude. Gemini has its own description box but no column in the table. "Best For" labels: Copilot, "General-purpose AI with web search"; Perplexity, "Research and information retrieval" (also news and financial updates); ChatGPT, "Versatile conversations and content generation"; Claude, "Thoughtful humanistic conversations." [Documented]
2. Accuracy row: Copilot, "Real-time but can be inaccurate"; Perplexity, "Excels in accuracy, real-time"; ChatGPT, "Depends on pre-trained knowledge, limited web access"; Claude, "Depends on pre-trained knowledge." No evidence or test is cited for these ratings. [Documented] (librarian judgment, not tested)
3. The table mixes eras. ChatGPT is listed as GPT-4o / GPT-4o mini and Copilot as "GPT-4 with Bing integration," with limits of "5 responses per conversation" and "4000 characters per prompt." Meanwhile Claude is listed as "Claude Sonnet 4.6." Rows were evidently updated unevenly. [Documented] / staleness judgment [Inferred]
4. Claude entry: Sonnet 4.6 (free) is described as a reasoning model with extended thinking, Haiku 4.5 is "for quick responses," and "Opus 4.8 is $17/month for complex tasks." Pricing: free plan, $20 Pro, tiered Max plans. The $17 and $20 figures conflict (possibly annual vs monthly billing; not explained). [Documented]
5. Prices listed: Copilot is free to UC Merced users, $30/month for Microsoft 365 Copilot, and $20/month for Copilot Pro. Perplexity Pro is $20/month or $200/year. ChatGPT Plus is $20/month. [Documented]
6. Institutional licensing drives the choice. The university gives Copilot free to its users, which illustrates that "which AI to use" in schools is often decided by what the institution licenses. [Documented] / generalization [Inferred]
7. Listed limitations: Perplexity "Lacks image generation capability." The free ChatGPT and free Claude versions have knowledge cut-offs. Claude "Cannot generate images based on text" and cannot run code outside sandboxes. [Documented]
8. Research subpage: specialist scholarly AI tools are compared (Scite, Elicit, Litmaps, Inciteful, Research Rabbit, Scholarcy, Semantic Scholar, Consensus) along with their underlying databases. Examples: Elicit is built on the Semantic Scholar corpus (138M papers plus 545,000 clinical trial registrations, per Elicit's FAQ); Consensus claims "over 400M+ million" documents. [Documented] (figures are vendor claims relayed by the library)
9. Key caveat for research tools: they use freely available papers or public abstracts and "DO NOT have access to pay-walled content." [Documented]
10. Teaching and Learning subpage lists educator tools with prices and "best for": MagicSchool (free; Plus $100/year; "Schools looking to integrate AI into teaching"), NotebookLM (free with Google account; note-taking and summarization; "Limited to Google Workspace users"), Eduaide.ai, QuestionWell, Quizlet, ClassPoint, Gradescope, and Twee. [Documented]
11. The "AI in research" subpage lists limitations: bias inherited from training data, plagiarism risk, and "Data Misinformation." It advises cross-checking AI output against reliable sources. [Documented]
12. The K-12 subpage points to AI-literacy curricula (aiEDU, AI4K12, MIT DAILy and Day of AI, and ISTE hands-on AI project guides for elementary and secondary teachers). These are relevant to a high school educator. [Documented]
13. Takeaway: library guides are useful for orientation and institutional context but go stale quickly and do not test tools. Verify model and price rows against vendor pages before relying on them. [Inferred]

## Limitations
- No testing or methodology. The ratings are librarian characterizations.
- Content dates are inconsistent within the page (GPT-4o-era Copilot and ChatGPT rows next to Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.8). The guide-wide "last updated" date does not mean every row was revised.
- The table has no Gemini column even though Gemini is described below it.
- The Opus 4.8 price ($17/month) conflicts with the $20 Pro figure in the table.
