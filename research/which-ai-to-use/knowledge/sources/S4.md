- Source type & possible bias: A commercial, independent benchmarking company (founders Micah Hill-Smith and George Cameron). It is backed by investors including Nat Friedman, Daniel Gross, and Andrew Ng, and sells data and "Optima" custom benchmarks. It says its evaluations are run independently. It measures API models, not consumer chat apps. Its own benchmarks (AA-*) are mixed in with third-party ones. Model labs promote its results, as the testimonials on the About page show.

## Learned points
1. The site measures API models and agents on intelligence, cost per task, time per task, and latency. Its stated aim is to help users "choose the best model and provider for your use case." Scale: 500+ models benchmarked, 100+ inference providers, 1T+ evaluation tokens (About page). [Documented]
2. The Intelligence Index v5.0.1 (methodology updated Nov 3, 2026) is a weighted average of four categories at 25% each. Knowledge Work: AA-Briefcase 15%, AutomationBench-AA 5%, GDP.pdf 5%. Coding: FrontierCode 2.0 15%, Terminal-Bench 4.0 10%. Science: Terminal-Bench-Science 15%, CritPt v2 hard subset 10%. General: AA-Omniscience v1.1 15% (10% accuracy plus 5% non-hallucination) and HLE-Diamond 10%. [Documented]
3. In v5 (Oct 27, 2026), 60% of the Index uses private test sets or held-out answers, a move against benchmark contamination. The suite is mainly English-language and text-based; multilingual, visual, and speech abilities are benchmarked separately. [Documented]
4. Testing rules: identical conditions for all models, zero-shot instructions, temperature 0 for non-reasoning models and 0.6 for reasoning models (unless the lab recommends another), pass@1 scoring aggregated across repeats, and up to 30 retries on API failure. [Documented]
5. Leaderboard snapshot (read Nov 7, 2026), Intelligence Index score and cost per task: Gemini 4 Carbon (high) 60, $7.76; Claude Opus 5.5 (max) 59, $10.04; Gemini 4 Carbon (medium) 58; Claude Opus 5.5 (xhigh) 57; GPT-6 Astra (max) 57, $6.24; GPT-6.1 Sol (max) 55, $1.27; Claude Sonnet 5.5 (max) 55; Gemini 4 Argon (high) 50; Kimi K3.1 Pro 46; Claude Haiku 5.5 (max) 36; Grok 4.7 (xhigh) 35. [Documented]
6. Timeline of the lead. At the v5 launch (Oct 27, 2026), Claude Opus 5.5 (max) led at 58, ahead of GPT-6 Astra (57) and GPT-6.1 Sol (55). One day later (Oct 28), Gemini 4 Carbon (high) scored 59 and took #1. The live leaderboard now shows 60 for Carbon and 59 for Opus, presumably after the v5.0.1 update. The lead changes within days. [Documented; reason for score shift Inferred]
7. Availability: Gemini 4 Carbon was "rolled out to selected users and is not yet publicly available" when it took #1 (Oct 28, 2026). The top benchmark model may not be one a consumer can use. [Documented]
8. Effort/thinking settings matter a lot. Claude Opus 5.5 scores 35 at low effort and 58 at max, at 10x the cost per task ($0.98 vs. $10.04). GPT-6.1 Sol's low-to-max spread is 20 points. This supports S1's advice to choose a thinking level as well as a model. [Documented]
9. Cost-efficiency: GPT-6.1 Sol (max) scores within 4 points of Opus 5.5 (max) "at about an eighth of the cost per task." Among the top-10 models, cost per task varies more than 13x (Oct 27, 2026). [Documented]
10. No single model leads everything. In v5, Opus 5.5 led 3 of 9 evaluations (FrontierCode, HLE-Diamond, Omniscience) and five other models led the remaining six. GPT-6 Astra led science and physics; Claude Sonnet 5.5 led AA-Briefcase agentic knowledge work; Kimi K3.1 Pro led Terminal-Bench 4.0; Gemini 4 Argon led AutomationBench. [Documented]
11. Domain capability ranks (homepage table, Nov 2026): Claude Opus 5.5 (max) ranks #1 in all six domains shown (Finance, Strategy & Ops, Legal, Healthcare/Medical, Engineering, Economics), followed by GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol. The table compares 15 of 24 models, and Gemini 4 Carbon is not among them. [Documented]
12. Hallucination (AA-Omniscience v1.1, 3,000 questions across 6 domains). The metric rewards correct answers, penalizes wrong ones, and doesn't penalize abstaining (range −100 to 100). Gemini 4 Carbon (high) leads at 54, with Opus 5.5 (max) at 48. Hallucination rates: Gemini 4 Carbon 11%, Gemini 4 Argon 4%, versus "50% for both Claude Opus 5.5 (max) and GPT-6 Astra (max)." Highest raw accuracy: Claude Fable 5.1 (max) at 68%. High accuracy and low hallucination are different properties. [Documented]
13. The original AA-Omniscience paper (arXiv 2511.13029; 6,000 questions, 42 topics) found Claude 4.1 Opus top at only 4.8, "one of only three models to score above zero." Its conclusion: models "should be chosen according to the demands of the use case rather than general performance" when knowledge matters. [Research: authored by AA staff, posted to arXiv]
14. Sycophancy and transparency: AA-PersonalityBench has a Transparency Index (truthful, admits uncertainty, disclaims assumptions, flags nuances, with sycophancy counted against). Claude Sonnet 5.5 (max) ranks highest of 42 models and GPT-3.5 Turbo lowest. A typical 95% confidence interval is about ±5 points on a 1–100 scale. [Documented]
15. Open-weights models fell further behind in v5. The best (Kimi K3.1 Pro, weights not yet released) is 12 points behind the leader, versus 3 in v4.3. Among models with released weights, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM-5.3 score 32, below Claude Haiku 5.5 (max) at 36. [Documented]
16. Agentic tasks drive cost: they make up 55% of Index weight but use a median of about 90% of output tokens. Median output tokens per task rose from 35k to 59k between v4.3 and v5. [Documented]
17. The site also runs blind-vote human-preference arenas (Elo) for text-to-image, image editing, video, and text-to-speech, plus a Model Recommender. Those charts didn't render, so no rankings were captured. [Documented; content not captured]
18. Inferred for educators: benchmark leadership changes within days, the top model may be invite-only, and API scores at max effort don't equal the consumer chat app at default settings. Use AA to see trends (hallucination rate, effort sensitivity, cost) rather than to pick one "best" chatbot. [Inferred]

## Limitations
- Most charts are JS-rendered and returned no data, including the image, video, and speech arenas, the per-trait PersonalityBench leaderboards, and the Progress Index chart. Only text tables and article prose were captured.
- The site measures API endpoints at set effort levels, not the consumer apps (ChatGPT, Claude.ai, Gemini app) that teachers use, whose defaults may differ.
- Many benchmarks are AA's own (AA-*). AA is a commercial firm whose investors and testimonials come from the industry it measures.
- Scores differ slightly between the article (Carbon 59) and the live leaderboard (Carbon 60). The page doesn't explain why, though it is likely the v5.0.1 revision.
- Results are a Nov 7, 2026 snapshot and change within days.
