- Source type & possible bias: Large self-selected survey, n = 49,009 kept responses from 177 countries (about 15K excluded). Respondents were recruited mainly through Stack Overflow's own channels, so highly engaged Stack Overflow users are overrepresented (methodology page). Developers only, not general consumers or teachers. Stack Overflow also sells data licensing to AI companies (site header).

## Learned points
1. Adoption: 84% are using or plan to use AI tools in development, up from 76% the year before. 47.1% of all respondents and 50.6% of professional developers use AI tools daily (n = 33,662 answering). [Documented]
2. Sentiment fell even as use rose. Favorable views dropped from over 70% in 2023 and 2024 to 60% in 2025. Learners are less favorable (53%) than professionals (61%). [Documented]
3. Trust is low. 46% distrust the accuracy of AI output and 33% trust it. Only 3.1% "highly trust" it. Experienced developers are the most cautious: 2.5% highly trust and 20.7% highly distrust (n = 33,244). [Documented]
4. Learners trust more than professionals: 6.1% of those learning to code "highly trust," versus 2.7% of professionals. Less experienced users trust the output more. [Documented]
5. Complex tasks: only 4.4% say AI handles complex tasks "very well." 39.6% rate it bad or very poor. The share of professionals who think AI struggles with complex tasks was 35% in 2024 and 29% in 2025. [Documented]
6. Top frustration: "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite" (66%), then "Debugging AI-generated code is more time-consuming" (45.2%). 20% say they have "become less confident in my own problem-solving" (n = 31,476). [Documented]
7. When they would still ask a human: "When I don’t trust AI’s answers" (75.3%), ethical/security concerns (61.7%), "When I want to fully understand something" (61.3%). [Documented]
8. Tasks done "mostly" with AI now: search for answers 54.1%, content/synthetic data 35.8%, learning new concepts 33.1%. Strongest resistance: deployment/monitoring (75.8% don't plan to use AI) and project planning (69.2%). [Documented]
9. Vibe coding is not mainstream: 72.2% say no and 5.3% say "No, emphatically" (n = 26,564). [Documented]
10. AI agents: 52% either don't use agents or use only simpler tools. 37.9% have no plans to adopt them. 14.1% use agents at work daily (n = 31,877). [Documented]
11. Agent impact is personal, not team-wide. About 69–70% agree agents increased productivity or saved time on specific tasks, but only 17.3% agree they improved team collaboration. 52% say AI tools/agents had a positive effect on productivity. [Documented]
12. Agent concerns: 87% are concerned about the accuracy of information from AI agents, and 81% have data security/privacy concerns. [Documented]
13. Out-of-the-box assistants used by agent users: ChatGPT 81.7%, GitHub Copilot 67.9%, Google Gemini 47.4%, Claude Code 40.8%, Microsoft Copilot 31.3%, Perplexity 16.2% (n = 8,323). [Documented]
14. LLMs used for development in the past year: OpenAI GPT 81.4%, Claude Sonnet 42.8%, Gemini Flash 35.3%, OpenAI Reasoning 34.6%, Gemini Reasoning 25.6%, DeepSeek Reasoning 23.3%, Meta Llama 17.8%, X Grok 11.1% (n = 16,474). [Documented]
15. Claude Sonnet use differs by group: 44.9% of professional developers versus 29.8% of learners. Learners lean more on Gemini Flash (42.6%) and DeepSeek Reasoning (37.6%), possibly because of free access (inferred reason). [Documented; reason Inferred]
16. Admired/desired LLMs (2025): Claude Sonnet has the highest "admired" share in the table at 67.5% and is second most desired at 33.3%. OpenAI GPT is most desired at 51.2% and 61.2% admired. Gemini Reasoning is 65.2% admired. The page's caption ("most admired LLM this year (behind Gemini Reasoning)") does not match its own table. [Documented; mismatch noted]
17. For prompt and tool advice: even daily heavy users verify AI output, and "almost right" errors are the main cost. Advice should build in verification steps, especially for novices, who trust more. [Inferred]

## Limitations
- Data were collected in mid-2025, so they are about 17 months old at reading. Model families named (e.g., "Claude Sonnet," "Gemini Flash") are 2025-era groupings.
- Developers only, recruited through Stack Overflow channels. This doesn't generalize to teachers, students, or general consumers.
- Model questions are "used for development work," not overall quality ratings. Admired and desired figures measure sentiment, not tested performance.
- Several questions had much smaller subsamples (e.g., agent tools n ≈ 2,700–8,300).
- Some charts (sankey, scatter of sentiment vs. task) did not render as readable text.
