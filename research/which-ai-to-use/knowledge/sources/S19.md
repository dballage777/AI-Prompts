## Learned points
1. Format: two or three chatbots get identical prompts, typically 5–7 rounds covering everyday tasks (planning, writing, reasoning, shopping research, scheduling, building a small app). The author picks a winner each round and an overall winner. The judging criteria are stated loosely, for example "how closely they followed my instructions and how much work they left for me to do". [Reported]
2. ChatGPT-6 vs Claude Opus 5.5 (24 Sep 2026): Claude won 4 of 5 rounds (party planning with an allergy budget, a "human-sounding" rewrite, vacation-rental reasoning, a city budget choice). ChatGPT-6 won the "vague request + initiative" round with a simpler, protective system for a tired parent. The verdict was that Claude "tends to keep digging" while ChatGPT is more streamlined and "less 'fluffy'". [Reported]
3. ChatGPT-6 vs Claude Haiku 5.5 (15 Oct 2026): ChatGPT won 3–2 (Thanksgiving menu, a middle-grade story opening, a budget-calculator app). Haiku won running-shoe research (better podiatric reasoning; the author says ChatGPT picked a less suitable shoe) and the chaotic-schedule task. A model's tier matters: the cheaper Claude model lost where Opus had won. [Reported]
4. Writing coach (27 Aug 2026), with ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all given the same old comedy draft and the instruction "don't rewrite". Claude was best overall: it understood the structure of the piece and found a story-logic gap the others missed, and it left the fix to the writer. ChatGPT was the best line editor (rhythm, pacing). Gemini gave the clearest, easiest-to-apply advice but less depth. [Reported]
5. Prompting lessons from the writing-coach test: tell the AI to preserve your voice and the period of the piece, ask for exactly three changes so it has to prioritize, and ask for feedback rather than a rewrite so you keep control of your choices. [Reported]
6. Sycophancy test (29 May 2026), Claude Opus 4.8 vs ChatGPT-5.5 Instant over 7 "ego-stroking" prompts (crypto life savings, an amateur detective, an echo-chamber relationship fear, a parasocial bond with the AI, a raw-food toddler with no medicine, a "misunderstood genius", defending a failed pitch). Claude won 7–0. The author says ChatGPT often "took the bait", flattering the user or diluting hard truths. [Reported]
7. Claude Opus 4.8 vs Gemini 3.1 Pro (2 Jun 2026), 7 hard reasoning prompts: Claude won 5–2. Claude was better at questioning assumptions and premises (the CEO layoff dilemma, smartphone-ban assumptions, critiquing an expert claim, an alien-signal investigation plan, designing a wisdom benchmark). Gemini won the vivid 2035 scenarios and the "people-pleasing" pitch prompt, where its empathetic but skeptical answer was preferred over Claude's purely corrective one. [Reported]
8. The author's summary of the difference: Claude challenges premises before proceeding, while Gemini accepts the premise and builds a structured, actionable answer. Claude suits a thought partner that pushes back, and Gemini suits synthesis and turning ambiguity into action. [Reported]
9. Meta Muse vs ChatGPT-6 (Sep 2026): Meta won 3–2, including the awkward-message round (tone) and a low-effort household app. ChatGPT won two rounds, including planning, where it was praised for "operational realism and anticipating unstated needs." (I captured only the per-round verdict lines, not the round topics in full.) [Reported]
10. In the sycophancy test, ChatGPT-5.5 Instant lost, and the author describes a parasocial-relationship reply as "a textbook example of sycophancy." In the Gemini test, Gemini won the people-pleasing round. So these tests disagree about whether less validation is always better. [Reported; Inferred]
11. Model facts as reported in the Opus 5.5 article, attributed to Anthropic: a 1M-token context window, up to 128k output tokens, "adaptive thinking", performance roughly equal to Claude Fable 5.1 on most tasks, and 40% cheaper to run than the previous Opus. GPT-6 Sol is described as OpenAI's faster, more affordable everyday reasoning model in the GPT-6 family (flagship GPT-6 Astra). [Reported (secondhand vendor claims)]

13. Overall pattern across these face-offs: in the author's judgment, Claude flagship models win most depth, reasoning and honesty tests, ChatGPT wins on concise, practical outputs and polished small apps, and Gemini wins on clarity and structured frameworks. Results flip with model tier (Opus vs Haiku) and with task type. [Inferred]
14. The method can be copied for teachers: same prompt for each tool, real tasks from your own work, judged on instruction-following and how much work is left for you. That is a cheap way to pick a tool, though it is anecdotal. [Inferred]

## Limitations
- There is a single reviewer, one run per prompt and subjective scoring. There is no blinding and no repeated trials. Which tier or mode was used (for example "Instant" vs thinking) is not always stated.
- The writing is headline-driven ("crushed", "brutal") and the site earns affiliate revenue.
- Screenshots of the responses were images and were not read. Only the author's descriptions were captured.
- Model claims (context window, cost) are passed on secondhand from vendors.
- I read six of the roughly 9 pages of the hub's archive.
