# S25 — Beyond Benchmarks: How Users Evaluate AI Chat Assistants (arXiv 2603.25220)
- URL (given): https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25220
- Final URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25220 and full text at https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25220 (v1)
- Access: OPENED (abstract plus HTML full text read through Section 7; figures not viewable, but tables were readable)
- Date read: 2026-11-07
- Publication / last updated: Submitted March 26, 2026 (v1, cs.HC). Data collected in late 2025 (instrument changed about October 21, 2025).
- Pages read: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25220 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25220
- Source type & possible bias: Preprint, not peer-reviewed, by three independent researchers (Awan, Noor, Munaf). Convenience-sample online survey distributed mainly through Reddit AI/ML/productivity communities and professional networks. 388 total responses. The quantitative analytic sample is N = 237 (second wave), with 180 full completions overall. The sample skews toward power users and technology workers (79.5% daily users; developers 28.1%, students 22.2%; North America 38.2%, South Asia 36.4%).

## Learned points
1. Satisfaction among the top platforms converges. On a 5-point scale: Claude M = 3.80 (n = 45), ChatGPT 3.78 (n = 139), DeepSeek 3.78 (n = 40), Gemini 3.51 (n = 88), Grok 3.50 (n = 26), Mistral 3.32 (n = 19), and Llama 2.95 (n = 21). The top three show no significant pairwise differences. The overall effect is small (ε² = 0.043) and mostly driven by Llama's low score. [Research]
2. Net Satisfaction Score (% extremely satisfied minus % dissatisfied): DeepSeek +12.5, Claude +11.1, ChatGPT +8.6, Gemini −4.5 (the only major platform below zero), Llama −19.0. [Research]
3. Multi-homing is the norm. Respondents use a mean of 2.83 platforms (median 3). 82.4% use two or more, 52.9% use three or more, and only 17.6% use a single platform (n = 170). The authors read this as low switching costs and choosing a model per task. [Research]
4. Usage share in the sample: ChatGPT 66.7%, Gemini 47.7%, Claude 24.9%, DeepSeek 24.5%, Grok 14.8%, Mistral 12.7%, Llama 11.8% (N = 237). [Research]
5. Each platform draws users for a different reason. ChatGPT's top adoption driver is UI/UX (4.06). Claude's are work-task fit (3.84) and answer quality (3.80), with lower UI/UX (3.47). DeepSeek's is word-of-mouth (4.02). Grok's is censorship/content-policy alignment (3.69). [Research]
6. Use-case ratings show specialization. Claude leads Technical/Analytical tasks (4.00) and is significantly above Gemini (3.19, p = 0.001), but not significantly above ChatGPT or DeepSeek after correction. ChatGPT leads Learning & Research (3.89) and Communication (3.78). Gemini narrowly leads Content Creation (3.48 vs. ChatGPT 3.46). [Research]
7. Role differences have small cells. Among developers, Claude scored 4.07 (n = 14) vs. ChatGPT 3.73 (n = 33). Among students, Claude scored 4.22 (n = 9) vs. ChatGPT 4.09 (n = 32). Neither difference is inferentially reliable. [Research]
8. Paying doesn't raise satisfaction. Free and paid users did not differ significantly for ChatGPT (3.72 vs. 3.87, p = 0.358), Claude, or Gemini. The authors conclude satisfaction tracks the base model rather than premium features. [Research]
9. Anchoring and first-mover effect. 71.9% of ChatGPT users said it was their first AI tool. Those users rated it 4.16, while those who came to ChatGPT after another platform rated it 2.82 (p < 0.001). This is exploratory and uncorrected, and it is cross-sectional. [Research]
10. ChatGPT is the main origin for switchers: 78.6% of switchers to Claude, 85.7% to Gemini, and 83.3% to Grok came from ChatGPT. Among Claude users, using more platforms correlates with lower Claude satisfaction (ρ = −0.39). [Research]
11. Price sensitivity is high and similar across platforms. About 55–60% of paid users would change behavior after a hypothetical 25% price increase (ChatGPT paid n = 69: 43.5% keep, 21.7% downgrade, 20.3% switch, 14.5% stop). No cross-platform difference was found (p = 0.883). [Research]
12. Top frustrations (n = 170 open-ended answers): hallucination 22.4% (including fabricated citations), censorship/over-refusal 15.3%, context loss 10.0%, usage limits 8.8%, slow responses 8.2%, instruction-following failures 7.6%, verbosity 7.6%, sycophancy 2.9%. [Research]
13. Frustrations differ by platform. Censorship complaints go disproportionately to ChatGPT and Claude, hallucination complaints most to ChatGPT and Gemini, and sycophancy complaints "targeted Claude specifically." These are counts from a small sample coded by the authors without inter-rater checks. [Research]
14. The authors frame hallucination and censorship as opposing failure modes: tightening filters may raise perceived censorship, and loosening them raises hallucination risk. [Research]
15. Most requested features (n = 159): image/video generation 15.7%, long-term memory 11.9%, better factual accuracy 8.8%, web access with citations 8.2%. [Research]
16. Implication for evaluation: once models cross a capability threshold, "interface quality, response style, content policy, pricing, and ecosystem integration may matter more" than benchmark gains. The authors call for standardized user-centric instruments. [Research]
17. Limitations the authors acknowledge: power-user skew, self-selection, a mid-survey instrument change (DeepSeek and Mistral added in week 2), small Mistral and Llama samples, within-subjects anchoring, a cross-sectional design, possible ceiling compression of the 5-point scale, and possible survivorship (ChatGPT) and novelty (DeepSeek) effects. [Research]
18. For advice to educators: the evidence suggests the top general assistants are roughly interchangeable for satisfaction. Pick by task fit, ecosystem, and content policy, and expect people to use several. But this sample is not teachers or typical students. [Inferred]

## Exact quotes
- "the top three platforms (Claude, ChatGPT, and DeepSeek) receive statistically indistinguishable satisfaction ratings despite vast differences in funding, team size, and benchmark performance." — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25220
- "users treat these tools as interchangeable utilities rather than sticky ecosystems: over 80% use two or more platforms, and switching costs are negligible." — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25220
- "Hallucination and content filtering remain the most common frustrations across all platforms." — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25220
- "Sycophancy complaints targeted Claude specifically, suggesting that its conversational style may sometimes prioritize agreeableness over correctness." — https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25220

## Limitations
- This is a non-peer-reviewed preprint using a convenience sample recruited largely on Reddit. It skews toward tech workers and daily users, so it is not representative of teachers, K-12 students, or casual users.
- Small per-platform samples (Claude n = 45; Mistral and Llama about 20). Subgroup findings are exploratory.
- Self-reported satisfaction on a 5-point scale may compress real differences.
- Data are from late 2025 and the models have since changed. The paper doesn't record which model versions respondents used.
- Qualitative coding was done without inter-rater reliability checks. Theme counts are rough indicators.
- The paper's references section and figures were not checked.
