All figures are self-reported usage, not measured usage or quality.

## Learned points
1. Which chatbots U.S. adults say they ever use (Feb 2026, n=5,119): ChatGPT 44%, Gemini 24%, Copilot 17%, Meta AI 14%, Grok 8%, Claude 6%, Character.ai 3%. [Documented]
2. By age: ChatGPT is used by 61% of 18–29s, 55% of 30–49s, 37% of 50–64s, and 19% of those 65+. Gemini: 30/34/20/11. Claude: 10/9/4/1. Character.ai: 7% of 18–29s. Pew notes that not all of these differences are statistically significant. [Documented]
3. Any chatbot use: 49% of U.S. adults in 2026, up from 33% in 2024 (question wording changed; the 2024 version asked only those who had heard of chatbots). By age in 2026: 66% (18–29), 61% (30–49), 42% (50–64), 23% (65+). [Documented]
4. ChatGPT trend: 18% (2023), 23% (2024), 34% (2025), 44% (2026), with a wording change in 2026. Pew: "ChatGPT still dominates and its reach is growing." [Documented]
5. Frequency: 24% use chatbots daily (12% several times a day, 4% almost constantly); 25% use them several times a week or less; 51% don't use them. Daily use is about a third among adults under 50 and 7% among those 65+. [Documented]
6. Uses (share of all adults): searching for information 42%; tasks at work 38% (of employed adults); fun 25%; creating or editing images and videos 24%; medical advice 20%; diet and fitness 20%; news 13%; emotional support 10%; companionship 4%. Among 18–29s, 20% use chatbots for emotional support. [Documented]
7. Perceived impact among users: more say chatbots help than hurt their productivity (30% vs. 5%), how informed they are (28% vs. 5%), and creativity (21% vs. 11%). Most say chatbots neither help nor hurt their happiness or relationships. [Documented]
8. Why non-users abstain (major + minor reason): not interested 83%; concerned about how personal information is used 79%; "don't trust them to give accurate information" 76% (45% major); don't know how to use them 55%; fear of judgment 14%. [Documented]
9. 67% of non-users say they are not too or not at all likely to use chatbots in the next 12 months; only 5% say extremely or very likely. [Documented]
10. Attitudes are skeptical even among users. 40% expect AI's impact on society over the next 20 years to be negative and 16% positive. 63% say AI is advancing too quickly. 71% think AI will make their personal information less secure. Adults under 30 are the most negative: 48% expect a negative impact on society. [Documented]
11. 60% say they read AI summaries at the top of search results; another 10% aren't sure. [Documented]
12. Method: ATP Wave 187, Feb. 17–23, 2026; 5,119 respondents out of 5,854 sampled (87% survey response rate, 3% cumulative). Conducted online (n=4,930) and by phone (n=189) in English and Spanish, with an oversample of Asian adults weighted back. Seven respondents were removed for satisficing. [Documented]
13. Pew notes elsewhere that searching for information is also a top chatbot use among U.S. teens (Pew teen report, Feb 24, 2026, linked but not read here). [Documented, summary-only]
14. Inferred: usage share doesn't track the quality rankings in S2–S4. Claude is used by only 6% of U.S. adults yet ranks at or near the top on S4 benchmarks, and Gemini's 24% reflects reach through Google products. When advising teachers, expect most students and parents to know ChatGPT first. [Inferred]

## Limitations
- The data is self-reported "ever use." It doesn't measure intensity, paid vs. free plans, or which chatbot is the person's primary one.
- The survey covers adults only (18+). The teen report and the gender and race/ethnicity chapters were not read.
- The survey was fielded Feb 2026, nine months before this reading; S5's own trend line shows shares changing fast.
- Wording changes in 2026 make comparisons with 2023–2025 approximate, and Pew flags this itself.
- The topline and questionnaire PDFs were not read.
