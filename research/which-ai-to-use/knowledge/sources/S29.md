## Learned points
1. Test design: three research prompts were run on both ChatGPT ("ChatGPT's newest model," not named) and Perplexity, with a per-round winner. The prompts were (1) brainstorm 10 tech-journalism research topics with why each matters in 2026, a challenge, and a research question; (2) a college-student subtopic outline for a paper on AI chatbots, prioritizing 30 years of quantitative data; (3) turn "most underrated Disney animated movies" into 5 research questions with testable hypotheses and data sources. [Reported]
2. Result: Perplexity won all 3 rounds and the overall verdict. [Reported]
3. Deciding factor was sourcing. Perplexity attached links to real sources (e.g., Reuters Institute research) and, in round 2, "brought 10 links to my attention (which is something ChatGPT didn't do)." Round 3 also had 10 sources. [Reported]
4. Perplexity also supplied more follow-up questions and suggested next steps (e.g., "What metrics best evaluate chatbot affordability for students?"), and gave more structure (working titles, core chapters). [Reported]
5. ChatGPT followed instructions fully ("did just what I asked") and offered extra deliverables (a full proposal outline, 10 academic sources to cite, a thesis and hypothesis). In the reviewer's account its responses were less extensive and did not include links. [Reported]
6. The winner was judged on "more detailed and vast" output and more sources. The reviewer did not report checking whether the cited links supported the claims, and accuracy was not measured. [Reported] / gap [Inferred]
7. The author's general view of Perplexity's strengths: it pulls up-to-date information, cites credible sources, and analyzes uploaded documents. [Reported]
8. Recommendation dated March 2026: for "deep research" and learning a new topic, use Perplexity over ChatGPT. [Reported]
9. The verdict rewards length and number of links. For educators, a tool that gives fewer but verified sources may be better than one that gives more unverified ones; checking that citations exist and say what is claimed remains essential. [Inferred]
10. Context on site cadence: related-article links on the same page (Nov 2026) reference "ChatGPT-6," "Claude Opus 5.5," and "Claude Haiku 5.5." The March 2026 test predates those models. [Reported] (headline links only, not read)

## Limitations
- Only 3 prompts, one tester, and subjective judging. There was no accuracy or citation-validity check.
- The ChatGPT model and plan (free vs paid, search on or off, deep research on or off) are not stated, and neither is the Perplexity mode. This makes the comparison hard to reproduce.
- The tester disclosed a prior preference for Perplexity.
- Affiliate-revenue publication.
