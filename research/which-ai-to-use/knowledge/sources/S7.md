## Learned points
1. By July 2025, ChatGPT's consumer product had been adopted by around 10% of the world's adult population, from its launch in November 2022. [Research, summary-only]
2. Early adopters were disproportionately male, but the gender gap "has narrowed dramatically." Growth rates were higher in lower-income countries. [Research, summary-only]
3. Method: a privacy-preserving automated pipeline classified usage in a representative sample of ChatGPT conversations. Sample sizes are not visible in the abstract. [Research, summary-only]
4. Work-related messages grew steadily, but non-work messages grew faster, from 53% to more than 70% of all usage. Most ChatGPT use is personal, not professional. [Research, summary-only]
5. Work usage is more common for educated users in highly paid professional occupations. [Research, summary-only]
6. The three most common topics, "Practical Guidance," "Seeking Information" and "Writing," together make up nearly 80% of all conversations. [Research, summary-only]
7. Writing dominates work-related tasks. The authors attribute this to chatbots' ability to generate digital outputs, which traditional search engines lack. [Research, summary-only]
8. Computer programming and self-expression are both relatively small shares of ChatGPT use. This contrasts with Claude, where coding is the largest category (S6). [Research, summary-only; the comparison is Inferred]
9. The authors argue ChatGPT's economic value comes mainly through decision support, especially in knowledge-intensive jobs. [Research, summary-only]
10. Cross-cutting point: S6 (Claude) and S7 (ChatGPT) both show a large and growing personal and non-work share. Platform user bases differ, though: ChatGPT is general-consumer and guidance- and writing-heavy, while Claude skews toward coding and technical work. Advice on "which AI" should account for this difference rather than treating either platform's usage data as universal. [Inferred]
11. Media pickup: the NBER page lists later citations, including the World Economic Forum (July 28, 2026) and GOV.UK "AI Scenarios 2030" (June 15, 2026). The paper remains a widely used reference. [Reported]

- The paper is OpenAI-authored and about ChatGPT only, and it is a working paper rather than a peer-reviewed article.
