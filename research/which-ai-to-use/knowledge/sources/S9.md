## Learned points
1. Scope: only the free tiers of eight chatbots were tested (112 tests in all), and model names were deliberately avoided because free-tier models "may change at any time" (the "gumbo" analogy). Results describe the free products as they were at test time, not the paid models. [Reported]
2. Rubric: 10 text tests at 10 points each (web summarization, explaining a concept to a 5-year-old, Fibonacci math, a social-media opinion, literary themes, a Boston travel itinerary, interview-nerves support, Latin translation with cultural context, JavaScript regex code, and a story of at least 1,500 words) plus 4 image prompts at 5 points each, for 120 points total. The exact prompts are published so readers can rerun them. [Reported]
3. Ranking and "best for" labels (Feb 2026): ChatGPT 109 (best overall; text 91, image 18); Microsoft Copilot 97 (best for Microsoft users); Grok 96 (best travel itinerary); Gemini 95 (best for image generation; text 77, image 18); Perplexity 93 (best for verified search; text 81, image 12); Claude 89; DeepSeek 78; Meta AI 77. [Reported]
4. Claude's free tier "immediately lost 20 points" because it does not generate images, and it requires sign-in. It did well on factual questions and the long-form story but was weak on web search and, surprisingly, on coding. The rubric's image weight penalizes text-only tools. [Reported; the rubric critique is Inferred]
5. About half the text prompts (explaining to a child, math, cultural discussion, literary analysis, translation) were "handled nearly perfectly by almost all" chatbots. Differences show up mainly in web retrieval, itineraries, coding, long-form writing and images. [Reported]
6. A common failure was summarizing one specific web article. Many bots pulled in tangential articles. Copilot, Gemini and Perplexity all strayed from the article, and ChatGPT logged-out returned a Taiwanese portal page in traditional Chinese. [Reported]
7. Gemini's free tier ranked lower than expected: it could not produce a Latin translation Google Translate could read back, gave an unhelpful itinerary, and required a personal account to make images. It was strong on factual questions and fast, good images (Nano Banana). [Reported]
8. Perplexity shows sources up front, which the reviewer valued, but he found its web search no better than a typical Google search and was "underwhelmed." [Reported]
9. Free tiers were generous: almost no throttling across 10 back-to-back prompts, and several worked without login. Heavy daily use will still hit limits. [Reported]
10. Paid tiers noted: ChatGPT Plus $20 and Pro $200; Copilot Pro $20; SuperGrok $30 or $300; Google AI Pro $19.99 and Ultra $249; Perplexity Pro $20 (students $5) and Max $200 a month. [Reported]
11. Anthropic has promised that Claude will remain ad-free, which the article contrasts with news that OpenAI plans to allow ads in ChatGPT. [Reported]
12. Method lesson: run the same fixed prompt set across tools, score each prompt on a rubric, and test the tier your users will actually have, for example free logged-out accounts for students. [Inferred]
13. The lower-ranked bots tended to answer the emotional-support prompt with a generic "You've got this!" rather than practical tips. Reviewing qualitatively catches shallow answers. [Reported]

## Limitations
- One reviewer ran one pass per prompt with subjective scoring and no repeat runs, so the 1–3 point gaps between Copilot, Grok, Gemini and Perplexity are within noise.
- Only free tiers were tested, so the results do not reflect paid or education plans.
- The test date is unclear (likely late 2025), so the results are about 9–12 months old by November 2026.
- Affiliate links and the parent company's litigation with OpenAI are potential biases.
