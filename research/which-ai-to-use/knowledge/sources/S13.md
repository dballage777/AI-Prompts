- Source type & possible bias: UX research firm. This is a retrospective survey of U.S. panel participants, n=420 (ChatGPT 113, Claude 103, Gemini 101, Grok 103). The firm sells its platform, its SUPR-Q license and industry benchmark reports. The data is self-reported perception from people who already use each product, not task-performance testing.

## Learned points
1. Study design: in May 2026, MeasuringU ran a retrospective study of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok with 420 U.S. panel participants, who rated their most recent experience. The sample was 52% female and 47% male, and 65% were under 40. It follows up a 2025 benchmark (Grok was new in 2026). [Documented]
2. Precision: the authors call the samples "modest but adequate" to detect medium-sized differences, about ±10% for binary metrics and ±5 points for 0–100 scales. Small gaps between products are within noise. [Documented]
3. UX-Lite (perceived ease and usefulness): 2026 means ranged only 5.5 points, from 77.8 (Grok) to 83.3 (ChatGPT), with no significant product effect (F(3,416)=1.7, p=.16). [Documented]
4. Year-over-year moves on UX-Lite: Gemini made the largest shift, dropping from the upper-right quadrant (above average on both ease and usefulness) to the lower-left. ChatGPT stayed the most stable. Claude improved meaningfully in usefulness but still lags in ease. Grok sits slightly below average on both. [Documented]
5. SUS (System Usability Scale): ChatGPT led, but the range was only 3.1 points (78.4–81.5), with no significant difference (p=.41). All four products scored above the cross-product average of 68, a grade of at least B+. ChatGPT rose 5.3 points from 2025. [Documented]
6. Net Promoter Score in 2026: Claude 28%, Grok 17%, Gemini 12%, ChatGPT 7%. NPS declined for every product measured in 2025. In 2025 Claude led ChatGPT by only 4 points; in 2026 it led by 21. [Documented]
7. The authors' explanation for NPS: ChatGPT's share fell as competitors grew; Claude's lead reflects its "recent dominant growth"; Gemini's growth comes partly from integration into the Google ecosystem rather than user choice, which may dilute recommendation intensity. The confidence intervals mean an NPS of 0 is plausible for ChatGPT and Gemini but not for Claude or Grok. [Documented]
8. Top complaints from open-ended "one thing you dislike" answers: accuracy and reliability were the most common across products. ChatGPT, Gemini and Grok were criticized for inaccurate or inconsistent responses, and Grok for "hallucination loops." [Documented]
9. Claude was the exception. Its users cited usage limits (on both free and paid tiers), performance on long conversations, and task limitations or prompt misinterpretation rather than accuracy. [Documented]
10. Gemini and Grok users repeatedly complained about slow responses. ChatGPT users mentioned lag in long conversations and limited features in the free version. Gemini users mentioned it misreading what they asked. Grok users mentioned image and video generation errors and privacy concerns. [Documented]
11. Sample ChatGPT user quote on overconfidence: "It's very confident in its errors, so I need to pay careful attention…". A Gemini user said they have to fact-check information Gemini found. Users across all products are still doing their own verification. [Documented]
12. Tech savviness (TAC-10): means ranged 6.3 (ChatGPT) to 7.0 (Claude), a statistically significant but probably not practically meaningful gap, much smaller than the 2.3-point gap in 2025. Claude's user base skews slightly more tech-savvy. [Documented]
13. Context: as AI providers compete, chat interfaces are moving from simple chat to tabbed experiences with more complicated terminology and navigation. The authors frame added features as possibly adding complexity. [Documented]
14. For choosing a chatbot: perceived usability is roughly equal across the four big products, so ease of use is not a strong differentiator. Differences show up in recommendation intent, usage limits (Claude), speed (Gemini, Grok) and perceived accuracy. [Inferred]
15. For prompting advice to students and teachers, the persistent accuracy complaints support building verification steps into any workflow, whichever product is used. [Inferred]

## Limitations
- Self-selected current users rated their own product, with no head-to-head task testing and no objective accuracy measurement.
- U.S.-only panel skewed young; about 100 people per product. Most between-product differences on SUS and UX-Lite are not statistically significant.
- Figures were not readable, so the exact 2025 values and the per-product ease/usefulness subscale numbers are not captured. The full report was not read.
- Microsoft Copilot, Meta AI and Perplexity were not included.
