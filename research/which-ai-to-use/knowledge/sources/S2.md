## Learned points
1. Picks as of Aug 26, 2026: Best Overall ChatGPT; Best Value Google Gemini; Best for Windows Users Microsoft Copilot; Best for AI Search Perplexity; Best ChatGPT Alternative Claude; Best for Searching X Grok. [Reported]
2. ChatGPT is picked for its "exceptional underlying tech" (the GPT-5.6 family: Luna lightweight, Terra balanced, Sol flagship, with adjustable intelligence levels), comprehensive responses, a friendly tone, customization, and memory. Paid plans run $8–$200/month. Excellent deep research. PCMag reports OpenAI discontinued Sora 2, so ChatGPT no longer generates video. [Reported]
3. Gemini is "the most generous" free plan: top models including 3.1 Pro and Nano Banana Pro, plus 15 GB of Drive storage. Current models are 3.7 Flash (everyday use) and 3.1 Pro (complex reasoning). Its image (Nano Banana Pro) and video (Veo 3.1) generation are rated best-in-class. Its models are "not best-in-class in terms of intelligence" but "more than smart enough for most tasks." [Reported]
4. Gemini is rated best if you work in Google Workspace (Docs, Drive, Gmail, Sheets). Its agent, Spark, is called "the most effective one I've tested to date." Gemini Live voice is available only on mobile. [Reported]
5. Copilot runs on OpenAI models, but "Microsoft isn't always up front about which ones." It performs "competently, though not exceptionally." Its main appeal is integration across Windows and Microsoft 365, and full 365-app integration costs extra. [Reported]
6. Perplexity is rated top for research and web search because of "generally accurate responses." It is less conversational. Paid plans let users pick models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, which PCMag calls "a great place to compare AI models." Pro is $17/month and Max $167/month (billed annually). [Reported]
7. Claude's defining strength is coding, especially Claude Code, which PCMag says needs no programming experience. Models named: Opus 5 and Sonnet 5, plus Fable 5 and Mythos 5 as the most expensive flagships. PCMag says its intelligence outperforms Copilot, Gemini, Grok, and Perplexity. Downsides: usage limits "can be aggressive," paid plans start at $20, and it has no native image or video generation (it renders visuals in HTML through Claude Design). [Reported]
8. PCMag recommends Claude for the "privacy-conscious," calling Anthropic's policies straightforward with meaningful control over personal data. [Reported]
9. Grok searches X automatically and has loose moderation and NSFW features (companions, explicit images and video). PCMag says it is neither the fastest nor the smartest, and recommends ChatGPT or Claude for serious research or app-building. [Reported]
10. Costs: every chatbot PCMag tests has a free version. Paid plans usually cost $10–$20/month. Gemini is named the best free chatbot. [Reported]
11. Test method: PCMag gives each chatbot a series of prompts and compares the responses for accuracy, consistency, complexity, and depth, tailoring tests to each product's features. Areas covered: complex reasoning, creative writing, deep research, file processing, image editing/generation, video generation, voice chat, and web search. It excludes locally run and single-purpose bots, such as companion apps. [Reported]
12. PCMag admits its testing can't cover everything: "it's impossible to fully vet the scope of an AI chatbot," results can change after updates, and reviews are accurate only as of their publication date. [Reported]
13. Reliability cautions: chatbots "aren't great at explaining how they work," so verify what they say. AI-text detectors are imperfect, and you "often simply cannot" tell whether something is AI-generated. Courts can compel AI companies to hand over chat logs. [Reported]
14. Censorship varies by product. PCMag cites DeepSeek refusing to discuss Tiananmen Square, and Grok as extremely permissive, including images of real people. [Reported]
15. PCMag says chatbots are "absolutely" not conscious and cannot truly be a friend or therapist. [Reported]
16. Inferred for a school audience: PCMag's recommendations hinge on ecosystem fit (Google school → Gemini; Microsoft district → Copilot) and on the task (research → Perplexity; coding → Claude). That framing suits teachers. Grok's NSFW features make it unsuitable for students. [Inferred]

## Limitations
- The site earns affiliate revenue and runs sponsored placements on the page.
- PCMag publishes no scores, test prompts, or quantitative results; the testing is qualitative.
- Some embedded spec data is stale. The Copilot spec table still lists "GPT-4" (spec last updated 2024–2025), while the body text cites GPT-5.
- The page has a typo ("Vero 3.1" for Veo 3.1).
- It is dated Aug 2026 and says the lineup is "unchanged." Model versions it names, such as Opus 5 and GPT-5.6, lag those S4 lists in Nov 2026 (Opus 5.5, GPT-6.x).
- PCMag's separate reviews and comparison articles (ChatGPT vs. Claude/Gemini) were not read.
