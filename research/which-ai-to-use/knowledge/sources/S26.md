## Learned points
1. As of March 2026, the list of nine "best AI personal assistant apps" with "best for" labels is: Zapier (building safely with AI), ChatGPT (best overall), Claude (coding and long-context writing), Google Gemini (Google Workspace integration), Reclaim (calendar/time management), Perplexity (real-time research), Motion (project management), Superhuman (inbox), Granola (meeting transcription). [Reported]
2. Selection criteria: (a) core capability and intelligence ("only considered apps powered by top-tier models"); (b) integration and ecosystem fit with calendar, email and project tools; (c) usability. The scope is knowledge-work assistants, not voice assistants (Siri, Alexa). All-in-one agent apps like OpenClaw were left out for lack of integrations, security, and maturity. [Reported]
3. Method claim: reviewers spend "dozens of hours researching and testing" and test each app "as it's intended to be used." The page gives no scoring rubric, sample tasks, or numbers. [Reported]
4. ChatGPT, best overall (March 2026): GPT-5.2 is the newest model and "automatically assesses the complexity of your question" to choose between an instant answer and deeper reasoning. Features: Advanced Voice Mode with camera, thinking mode plus web search, deep research, Agent mode ("hit-or-miss"), Canvas, Projects, custom GPTs. Listed cons: "Web search citations can be hit or miss"; vague questions get vague answers. [Reported]
5. Claude, coding and long-context writing: its default writing style "feels more natural than ChatGPT." The author found it willing to give blunt critique, which she contrasts with tools that are "too polite." Strengths: Claude Code, Artifacts, an agentic mode that works on granted folders and in Chrome, and connectors/MCP. Cons: feature updates lag ChatGPT, and token limits are easy to hit on complex tasks. [Reported]
6. Safety caution about agentic assistants: because agentic AI "can read, edit, and delete files," the author says to be deliberate about what you grant access to. [Reported]
7. Gemini, Google Workspace: it searches Gmail and Calendar and works with Docs, Drive, Keep, Maps and YouTube. It is better at search and retrieval than at long multi-step actions, which the author frames as a strength for small everyday requests. Also covered: Canvas app-building, a Deep Research mode that produces an editable research plan first, and Gems (custom versions). Cons: "Response quality varies"; less polished than ChatGPT. [Reported]
8. Perplexity, research: "Web search with citations by default." It has three modes: Basic Search, Research (more sources and longer reports), and Labs (documents, slides, dashboards). Users can pick the underlying model (GPT, Claude, Gemini, Sonar) and limit sources to academic papers or social sites like Reddit. Cons: weaker for creative tasks and less versatile than general chatbots. [Reported]
9. Prices as of March 2026: ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro and Perplexity Pro each start at $20/month. Gemini paid access is via Google One Premium ($9.99/mo) or Workspace Starter ($16.80/user/mo). All three big chatbots have free plans. [Reported]
10. Specialist tools beat general chatbots for some narrow jobs: Reclaim for habit and calendar defense, Motion for project timeline prediction, Superhuman for email that mixes AI drafting with fixed snippets, and Granola for meeting notes that combine your own notes with the transcript. [Reported]
11. Superhuman rationale: AI-only emails "often sound generic," so mixing AI generation with pre-written snippets keeps your own voice. This is a design principle that transfers to prompt use (combine fixed text with generated text). [Reported] / transfer [Inferred]
12. The article's framing is that the real power comes from connecting assistants to existing apps (integration over raw model quality). This conveniently favors the publisher's product. [Reported] / bias note [Inferred]
13. For an educator, the practical rule the article implies is: pick the assistant that already lives in your ecosystem (Gemini for Google Workspace schools; Copilot is not covered), Perplexity for cited lookups, and Claude or ChatGPT for drafting. [Inferred]
