## Learned points
1. Framing: choosing a chatbot is "like hiring a new employee." Pick by the jobs you need done (code, write, search, images, privacy) rather than a general ranking. [Reported]
2. "Best for" verdicts as of 2026-04-30: ChatGPT for complex tasks; Google Gemini as best value overall; Microsoft Copilot for productivity inside Microsoft's ecosystem; Perplexity for research, fact-checking, and source-backed answers; Claude for long-form content, coding, and document analysis; Grok for social media research; DeepSeek for developers, math/logic, and budget users; Meta AI for WhatsApp/Instagram users. [Reported]
3. ChatGPT: described as the most widely used assistant with "over 900 million weekly active users as of 2026," running GPT-5-series models, with Projects and Canvas workspaces, memory, image generation, deep research, and agents. Listed cons: generic insights in niche areas, costly premium tiers, and occasional inaccuracies. [Reported]
4. Gemini: positioned as an extension of Gmail, Docs, Sheets, and Drive with real-time Google Search access "with citations" and strong image/media generation. Cons: less flexible outside Google, occasional inaccuracies, and privacy concerns from data integration. Ideal for people and teams already on Google tools. [Reported]
5. Copilot: built into Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Teams, and Windows. It can work with email, calendars, and files. Pros include "enterprise-grade security." Cons: limited outside Microsoft and weak media generation. [Reported]
6. Perplexity: an "answer engine" that runs live web search and gives clickable citations. Spaces act as research hubs, and users can switch between GPT, Claude, and Gemini models. Recommended for students and researchers who need verifiable information. Cons: less conversational, limited creativity, and weaker deep analysis. [Reported]
7. Claude: credited with the most natural prose, "Constitutional AI" guardrails, Artifacts side-window, and a context window the article states holds "up to 200,000 words." Cons: "No image generation" and "Usage limits hit fast." (The 200,000-word figure is the article's claim. It is not checked here, and the article may be confusing words with tokens.) [Reported]
8. Grok: plugged into X for real-time trends, with a looser moderation style. Cons: weak sourcing and limited professional use. [Reported]
9. DeepSeek: the R1 reasoning model is open-source and can be self-hosted. Strong in math and logic. Caveats: "Chinese-hosted platform" with data privacy and political censorship concerns, and no image generation. The article claims its step-by-step reasoning is "a better tutor than any other bot" for math students. This is unsupported opinion. [Reported]
10. Meta AI: social-first, inside WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook, focused on image and short video generation ("vibes") and voice. It is not designed for complex analytical work. [Reported]
11. Quick hits: Duck.ai for privacy (a wrapper that strips IP and metadata), Zapier Agents for automation across 8,000+ apps, Poe as a multi-model aggregator, and Pi for emotional support/EQ. [Reported]
12. Pattern: the verdicts mostly follow the ecosystem the user already lives in (Google → Gemini, Microsoft → Copilot, Meta apps → Meta AI). Users already inside an ecosystem are the article's main deciding factor. [Inferred]
13. Every major chatbot reviewed lists "inaccurate information" or weak sourcing as a con. Only Perplexity and Gemini are credited with citations. [Reported]
