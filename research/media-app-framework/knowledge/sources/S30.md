## Learned points
1. [Reported] (author's estimate, explicitly a guess) Matt Carroll estimated the Flutter team at "about 50 people" serving "on the order of 1,000,000 developers", a ratio of 1:20,000. Google does not publish team size.
2. [Reported] (author's claims) Flutter's headcount was "frozen circa 2023", followed by "a small number of layoffs" in early 2024, with possible expansion "through outsourcing". The author says Google's "corporate re-focus on AI caused the Flutter team to de-prioritize all desktop platforms", leaving Flutter "in maintenance mode for 3 of its 6 supported platforms" (Mac, Windows, Linux) as of Oct 2024.
3. [Reported] (author's claims) Backlog: issues "can easily linger for years". When triage finally happens, reporters are asked for details they no longer remember. The author says a company blocked by a Flutter bug for around 2 years may have to abandon Flutter.
4. [Reported] (author's claims) External contribution friction: limited reviewer time, "contentious review conversations", demands for design docs and calls, and a "communication monoculture". Fewer than 1,500 people had ever contributed to the framework in nearly a decade. The author argues Flutter team members "don't actually use Flutter" to ship apps.
5. [Reported] (project plan, 2024) Flock was framed as "Flutter+": a fork that stays "constantly up to date with Flutter", adds community bug fixes and features, and recruits "dozens" of reviewers. Planned phases: (1) automatically mirror the master, beta, and stable branches and tags; (2) mirror the engine and build and upload engine binaries daily; (3) fixes and extensions, with "moderation" to avoid merge conflicts. Use was through FVM with `.fvmrc` `{"flutter":"master","flutterUrl":"https://github.com/join-the-flock/flock.git"}`.
6. [Documented] (site, 2024 snapshot) The original get-started text (snapshot January 2025) says "For now, Flock is just a direct copy of Flutter". Its sample output shows Flutter 3.27.0-1.0.pre.200 on master, dated 2024-10-22. That was Phase 1 at the time.
7. [Documented] (site, 2026) Status on flutterfoundation.dev as of Nov 2026: every journal article is dated Oct–Nov 2024, and the journal says these "retain their 2024 dates; they are not current release announcements". No 2025 or 2026 release, status, or progress post was found. New 2026 content is generic "practical guides" (SDK trials, reproduction, Nest artifact maps) authored by "Flock", which "are not presented as new personal work" by Carroll or Jesse Ezell.
8. [Documented] (site, 2026) The current guidance is cautious: "A working Flutter app does not need a fork simply because one exists". It recommends evaluating Flock only for "a specific change you can test", in a reversible trial. `master` is described as "a moving reference, not a frozen release". The repository is now `join-the-flock/flock`, and the docs note that the 2024 documentation used a different GitHub organisation.
9. [Documented] (about page) The site says it is "back at our original address" and calls itself the official home of Flock and Nest. Nest is the tooling for maintaining Flutter forks.
10. [Inferred] The pages read show no evidence that Flock has shipped fixes, stable releases, or Phase 3 work in 2025–2026. The GitHub repo was not checked because github.com is blocked, so activity there is unverified. For this project Flock is not a practical dependency. Its value is as a 2024 signal of community worry about Flutter staffing, desktop priority, and review throughput.
11. [Inferred] The concerns that matter for a mobile-only app (iOS and Android are not among the de-prioritized platforms in the author's account) are slower bug triage and review. Those concerns would need checking against current Flutter release cadence and issue data from other sources, which this source does not provide.
