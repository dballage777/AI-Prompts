## Learned points
1. [Documented] As of Feb 24, 2026 the React Foundation has officially launched, hosted by the Linux Foundation. "React, React Native, and supporting projects like JSX are no longer owned by Meta" — they are owned by the React Foundation.
2. [Documented] Eight Platinum founding members: Amazon, Callstack, Expo, Huawei, Meta, Microsoft, Software Mansion, Vercel. Huawei joined after the October 2025 announcement (which listed seven).
3. [Documented] The foundation is governed by a board of directors with a representative from each member; Seth Webster is executive director.
4. [Documented] Technical governance is to be independent of the foundation board: "React's technical direction will continue to be set by the people who contribute to and maintain React." A provisional leadership council was formed to determine the structure; an update was promised "in the coming months."
5. [Documented] Still in progress as of the launch post: finalizing technical governance; transferring repositories, websites, and other infrastructure to the foundation; exploring ecosystem support programs; planning the next React Conf.
6. [Documented] Planned foundation role (Oct 2025 post): maintain infrastructure (GitHub, CI, trademarks), organize React Conf, and fund the ecosystem (financial support of projects, grants, programs); aim is to be "vendor-neutral" with no single company "overrepresented."
7. [Documented] Rationale given: React "has outgrown the confines of any one company," with substantial contributions from outside Meta.
8. [Documented] The react.dev blog index (checked 2026-11-25) shows no later post announcing the finalized technical governance; the next post after the launch is React 19.3 (Sep 9, 2026). The site footer reads "Copyright © React a Series of LF Projects, LLC and its contributors."
9. [Inferred] For a React Native + Expo choice: Expo (the build/tooling vendor this project would depend on) and Software Mansion and Callstack (major RN library maintainers) are founding members, and RN no longer depends on a single corporate owner. Meta remains a member. This reduces single-vendor governance risk compared with Flutter's Google-led roadmap (S18), but the posts say nothing about funding amounts, Meta's continued engineering headcount on RN, or RN release cadence.
10. [Inferred] Because technical governance was still "provisional" at launch and no finalized structure appears on the react.dev blog by late 2026, decision-makers should treat the governance change as real for ownership but incomplete for technical process.
