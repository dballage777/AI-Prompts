## Learned points
1. [Documented] On 2025-06-17 Zulip made its Flutter app the official Android and iOS app, replacing the old React Native app. It ships as an ordinary upgrade to the existing store listing (Play package id `com.zulipmobile`).
2. [Documented] Timeline: the beta post (2024-12-12) says "For over a year, we've been rebuilding mobile Zulip from scratch atop a new technical foundation powered by Flutter". So the rewrite started around late 2023. The public beta ran from December 2024 and the general release came in June 2025, roughly 18 months in total.
3. [Documented] More than 2,000 users installed beta versions between December 2024 and June 2025. The beta was a separate app that could sit next to the legacy app on a device.
4. [Documented] Main stated gain: the new app is "much faster and more responsive". It scrolls through thousands of messages smoothly and loads older history before the user reaches the top. The posts also give a caveat: in organizations with 10K+ users, the first connection can still take several seconds.
5. [Documented] Reasons given for leaving React Native: Flutter is "a far better platform for building excellent mobile UIs for a complex product like Zulip". They cite its architecture ("built for speedy and pixel-perfect apps"), "high code quality, excellent documentation", and an open-source community that handles bug reports and PRs well. They also say developer experience lets them build features "at a pace and quality that would never have been possible with the old codebase".
6. [Documented] The beta post promised a more detailed "future blog post next year" on the RN-to-Flutter story. No such post appears in the blog's RSS feed as of 2026-11-25. The detailed technical rationale is therefore not published on the blog.
7. [Documented] Zulip launched before reaching full feature parity: at launch, channel management and full LaTeX rendering were missing. They deliberately shipped once "the great majority" of users would see it as an improvement. Android users could sideload the legacy app if they needed it.
8. [Documented] New features: full multi-account support (stays connected to all servers at once). It supports Zulip Server 7.0+ (May 2023) and keeps users logged in, except accounts unused since 2021.
9. [Documented] The app is small in UI-string terms: fewer than 250 translatable strings, under 7% of Zulip's total. Translation runs on Weblate.
10. [Documented] Team size is not stated in either post. The posts mention only "the Zulip core team" and a "mobile team" (the 2025 ten-year post refers to "the mobile team responding to users' feedback about the new app").
11. [Inferred] For this project, this is a credible first-party case of a small open-source team choosing Flutter over React Native for a list-heavy, performance-sensitive UI. It gives no evidence about video playback, in-app purchases, AI-assisted development, or building without a Mac.
12. [Inferred] The timeline (about 1 year of rewrite before a public beta, about 18 months to replace the old app) is a useful reality check on a full rewrite by an experienced team without heavy AI agents. It does not measure greenfield development speed.

## Limitations or bias
- These are self-reported product announcements. They give no benchmarks, no React Native version, and no account of what exactly went wrong in RN (the old app's RN version and architecture are not stated).
- Survivorship and enthusiasm bias: the posts are user-facing marketing for the new app.
- Team size and cost are not disclosed. The promised detailed write-up was not found.
