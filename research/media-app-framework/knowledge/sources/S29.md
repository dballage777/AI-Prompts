## Learned points
1. [Reported] (anecdotal; Shopify article as quoted in-thread) Shopify's stated reason: "LLMs changed one of the core assumptions behind our 2020 decision". "Building the same feature in Swift and Kotlin no longer carries the cost it used to." Commenters quote it saying "React Native apps can be fast. Ours are." They also quote it as using greenfield rebuilds, an internal loop system called "Helix", and keeping parity through "shared specifications, tests, and review checkpoints" rather than a shared code layer. A commenter posted Shopify's own timeline: the RN adoption post in Jan 2020, "five years of RN" in Jan 2025, the New Architecture migration in Sept 2025, and back-to-native in Sept 2026.
2. [Reported] (anecdotal) The largest subthread (158 replies) starts with a developer saying Codex converted a 15–20-screen RN app to native Swift plus Kotlin/Jetpack Compose overnight (about 6 hours), using Maestro for UI testing. They say they know neither Swift nor Kotlin, and the result "launches instantly". The top reply (86 sub-replies) asks how they can judge an Android build without using Android or knowing Kotlin. Replies split between "just install it and QA; maintainability concerns are overblown" and "two vibe-coded codebases you don't understand", with fear of the first production incident. Another poster tried the same approach on "a few complicated apps", found keeping separate codebases stable "still was a headache", and "switched web, ios, android all back to expo/rn".
3. [Reported] (anecdotal) Another developer reports pointing an agent at an Electron app with 750 unit tests and about 50 integration tests, plus MCP access to click through it. They got a "nearly feature complete" native iOS port in about 3 hours. Manual testing found 3 bugs and 1 performance issue, which the agent fixed after profiling builds with Xcode. A recurring theme is that good test suites and fast feedback loops are what make agent-driven native work succeed.
4. [Reported] (anecdotal) The most common counter-argument is about team size: "The vastly undersold topic ... is how many developers do you have?" Small-team and solo commenters say Shopify (said in-thread to have about 3,000 engineers) is irrelevant to them. One wrote: "We have the option of using flutter, or not launching an app at all. The concept of going full native is a non-starter." Another: "I don't have 6 engineers to throw at Native, let alone 6000." Another: "I'm building an app that targets Android, iOS, Windows, Mac, Linux. There's no way I'm going to be maintaining 5 different codebases, even with LLMs."
5. [Reported] (anecdotal) Parity skepticism: "Of course agents can produce two implementations that look the same today. That's not the hard part. The hard part is keeping them the same." Others note "2 codebases means twice the tokens" and that complexity is still a cost with AI.
6. [Reported] (anecdotal) OTA updates came up repeatedly as RN's killer feature, which Shopify did not address. Examples: "The most valuable feature which RN have is OTA updates". Another: "Being able to ship hotfixes instantly ... has saved our asses multiple times". One commenter asked how OTA passes Apple review, with no definitive answer in the excerpts read. Native-side OTA through WebAssembly products was mentioned as emerging.
7. [Reported] (anecdotal) App Store review times are disputed. One side says they are "typically < 24 hours". Others report recent multi-day to 2-week waits ("Just this week I've had an app stay for 9 days in review and another one for 8 hours. Same dev account"; someone overseeing about 20 apps sees "2 days+ as the average"). Some cite runway.team/appreviewtimes. This unpredictability is used as an argument for OTA-capable stacks (RN, Capacitor).
8. [Reported] (anecdotal) Expo-specific comments: a side-project RN developer who "took breaks often" had to do "big updates with expo to make it work again". Another says Expo's libraries are "unstable". One says RN now releases breaking changes "way faster" and nudges developers to Expo, which "lead to my burnout". On the other side, one notes most AI vibe-coding app builders use Expo/RN because of hot refresh and iteration speed, and that RN became "boring technology" in 2026. Another points to Expo's universal native UI components (`docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/ui/universal/`).
9. [Reported] (anecdotal) Flutter comments are mixed. Several small teams are happy: one runs three free apps (one with 17,000+ users) and "rarely need[s] to write native code", others say "Still loving flutter" or "a great experience so far". There are also unsupported claims that Flutter is "pretty abandoned" or a "dead-end". One developer said that in H2 2026 an LLM-built Flutter app was "a horrible buggy mess" they could not debug "because I don't speak dart", so they switched to RN because they could read JS. One commenter cited a RevenueCat report (2026-06-03) saying RN apps make more revenue than Flutter or native apps (not verified here).
10. [Reported] (anecdotal) Kotlin Multiplatform (KMP) and Compose Multiplatform got many positive mentions as a middle path for AI-assisted work. Examples: "I've been using LLMs to build with KMP for about 10 months and it's a delight". "KMP can be seen as a token optimization at this point. Business logic is shared". Compose Multiplatform is "quite good", with native SwiftUI where needed. Criticisms: the Gradle/JVM toolchain is a turn-off, and on iOS KMP "was definitely an extra layer that didn't feel fully native". Others prefer shared Rust through UniFFI with native UIs. Someone shipped a Sudoku app that way, saying "with AI doing two apps makes more sense".
11. [Reported] (anecdotal) Mac/Xcode comments: no one in the excerpts read discussed building iOS without a Mac. One solo developer with a "moderately successful app" says they never read the code and only open Xcode "if I have to modify signing certificates". Agents profiling with Xcode came up as a Mac-dependent step. Opinions on Xcode range from "head, shoulders ... above every other toolset" to JetBrains being "miles ahead".
12. [Reported] (anecdotal) Store-account friction for solo developers: one says their agent-built side project cannot justify the Apple developer fee. Another gave up on Android distribution "because of their 20 testers requirement" (Google Play closed testing for new personal accounts; the poster was unsure whether it still applies).
13. [Reported] (anecdotal) Other recurring arguments: npm supply-chain attacks as a security reason to leave RN; a claim that RN's New Architecture refactor itself prompted Shopify to reconsider; an RN contributor's view that performance gains over the New Architecture are "minimal for normal UIs"; and claims that separate native teams drift out of sync. One commenter who migrated a mid-size RN app to native mostly without LLMs called the "React Native tax of unnecessarily difficult upgrades, impedance mismatch ..., and incredibly uneven library quality" reason enough on its own, and stressed investing early in integration tests as guardrails for later LLM use.
14. [Reported] (anecdotal) Framework-vendor voices with an interest took part, for example the NativePHP builder calling the move "backwards" and the "SuperNative" builder calling all current cross-platform tools "pre-AI abstractions". Several commenters said the decision only makes sense for large organizations: "when you are large enough to allocate sufficient resource, you should go native, if not, go flutter/react native".
15. [Inferred] For a solo, no-Mac, Claude Code developer, the thread's weight of anecdote suggests three things. (a) Agents can now produce two native apps quickly, but long-term parity, debugging in unfamiliar languages, and token cost fall on one person. (b) OTA updates are a real advantage for solo maintainers facing unpredictable review times. (c) A shared-code option (RN/Expo, Flutter, or KMP) remains the common small-team choice. The thread gives no evidence about building iOS without a Mac.
