AI PROMPT DESIGN PRINCIPLES: HIGH SCHOOL DIFFERENTIATION
How a future prompt should instruct an AI model (Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT, or another) to produce differentiated materials. Each principle is followed by a sample instruction line in [brackets] that can be pasted into a prompt.

PLATFORM NOTE
Combine these content principles with the platform-specific knowledge base:
- Claude: claude-knowledge/knowledge/09_PROMPT_TEMPLATES.md and 05 (governance)
- Gemini: gemini_k12_assistant_engineering/ (keep instructions concise, name the environment, include the K–12 safeguards)
- Platforms without a knowledge base: claude-knowledge/knowledge/templates/T00_prompt_for_other_models.md

P1. IDENTIFY READINESS FROM EVIDENCE, NOT LABELS.
Ask for aggregated or de-identified evidence: pre-test results, exit-ticket patterns, or described needs. Never ask for names or diagnoses.
[If no data is given, ask the teacher for the results of a short pre-assessment, or offer to create one, before assigning tiers.]

P2. LOCK THE OBJECTIVE.
Every version must target the same standard or objective unless the teacher explicitly lists a documented modification.
[Every version targets <objective>. Include an alignment table showing objective · core question · what varies · DOK.]

P3. VARY SUPPORT FOR STRUGGLING STUDENTS, NOT THE AMOUNT OR THE TARGET.
Supports to use: modeling, worked → faded examples, scaffolds with a removal plan, vocabulary, chunking, and prerequisite repair (Library 01).
[Do not create a version that is simply shorter or easier. Add support while keeping the core question.]

P4. VARY COMPLEXITY, DEPTH, AUTONOMY, OR PACE FOR ADVANCED STUDENTS, NOT VOLUME (Library 02).
[The extension must raise DOK or add depth or complexity (perspectives, change over time, generalization, proof). Never add more problems of the same kind.]

P5. MATCH GUIDANCE TO EXPERTISE (expertise reversal).
- Novices: worked examples, explicit steps.
- Ready students: problem-first or challenge-first, with examples available on request.

P6. PRESERVE RIGOR AND RESPECT.
Every tier should be equally engaging and meaningful. Use neutral tier names. No "easy" or "gifted" labels.

P7. BUILD IN EVIDENCE-BASED PRACTICE.
- Retrieval warm-ups and spaced review
- Interleaving, once problem types are learned
- Formative checks between phases
- Metacognitive prompts
- Specific feedback
- No learning-style matching

P8. DESIGN UNIVERSALLY FIRST.
Give the base lesson multiple representations and response options before tiering it (UDL).

P9. LOWER LANGUAGE DEMAND FOR ELLS, NOT CONTENT DEMAND.
Use WIDA-style supports matched to the student's proficiency level. Translations are drafts that need review.

P10. PRODUCE TEACHER-READY AND STUDENT-FACING OUTPUTS SEPARATELY.
- Teacher packet: objective, alignment table, how-to-run notes (3 lines), estimated prep time, answer keys, grouping guidance.
- Student pages: clear directions at the right reading level, neutral labels, space to work.

P11. INCLUDE ANSWER KEYS WHEN REQUESTED, AND VERIFY THEM.
Solve each item independently, check units and notation, and flag uncertainty with [VERIFY].

P12. VERIFY ACCURACY AND READABILITY.
- AI tends to write above the requested reading level.
- Have the AI estimate readability, revise if it overshoots, and preserve facts in adapted texts.

P13. NO UNSUPPORTED INSTRUCTIONAL CLAIMS.
- Don't tell teachers a strategy "doubles learning."
- Describe evidence with appropriate hedging: "research suggests," "promising."
- Never invent citations.

P14. RESPECT WORKLOAD.
Prefer low-prep structures, reusable templates, and one differentiated element per lesson unless asked for more.

P15. PRIVACY AND OVERSIGHT.
- No personally identifiable information (PII).
- The teacher decides groups, grades, and accommodations; the AI drafts them.
- Don't process IEPs.
- Follow district policy.

P16. CONTEXT LIMITS (state these in prompts when relevant).
- Strategies vary by subject. Inquiry levels apply to science, Reading Like a Historian to history, ACTFL ranges to world languages, and competency checklists to CTE.
- Some evidence comes from elementary or middle school (compacting, interleaving). Adapt it to high school.
- Station rotation and choice boards have little outcome evidence. Use them for structure and engagement, not as proven achievement interventions.
