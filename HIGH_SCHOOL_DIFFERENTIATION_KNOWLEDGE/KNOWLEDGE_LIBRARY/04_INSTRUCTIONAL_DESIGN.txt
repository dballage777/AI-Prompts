KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY 04: INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN & DELIVERY

I1. DESIGN UNIVERSALLY FIRST, THEN DIFFERENTIATE   (EXPERT · R12; EMERGING outcome evidence)
- Build options into the base lesson for every student:
  - multiple representations (text, visual, worked example)
  - multiple ways to respond
  - clear goals
  - choice where it serves the goal
- UDL 3.0 goal: learner agency.
- AI prompt: "Include at least two representations of the key idea and two response options in the base lesson before creating tiers."

I2. FLEXIBLE GROUPING   (MODERATE, CONTESTED · R16, R08, R02; EEF 2026)
- Groups are temporary and decided by data. The basis changes with the purpose:
  - readiness (reteach or extension)
  - interest
  - random (Building Thinking Classrooms)
  - mixed (peer support)
- Size 2–4. Groups of 5 or more show little benefit.
- Evidence notes:
  - Low attainers may gain more in mixed groups.
  - High attainers may gain more in similar-ability groups.
  - Between-class tracking: near-zero benefit for average and low students.
- AI prompt: produce a grouping plan from aggregated data, with the purpose and duration of each group. Never use names; use "Group A: 6 students with error X."

I3. SMALL-GROUP MINI-LESSON WHILE OTHERS WORK   (MODERATE · R05, R27)
- Lesson structure:
  1. Whole-class launch (10 min)
  2. Formative check, either a hinge question or a pre-check
  3. Split:
     - teacher-led reteach for "not yet"
     - on-level practice
     - extension for "got it"
  4. Exit ticket
- AI generates all three strands from one objective, plus the routines that keep independent groups working (checklists, answer-check stations, "ask 3 before me").

I4. STATION ROTATION (blended)   (PRACTITIONER; thin HS evidence · R28)
- Typical stations:
  - teacher-led (differentiated small group)
  - online adaptive or practice
  - collaborative task
  - independent or offline
- Rotate every 15–20 minutes, or over 2 days.
- AI generates the station materials, the rotation schedule, and directions at each station.

I5. FLIPPED INSTRUCTION   (MODERATE · R25 d = 0.42)
- Short instruction videos or readings at home. Class time is used for practice and small groups.
- Effects are larger in shorter interventions.
- Equity: students need home access, or an in-class alternative.
- AI generates the video script outline, the guided notes, and an entry check.

I6. SELF-PACED MASTERY UNITS (Modern Classrooms model)   (PRACTITIONER/EMERGING · R24)
- Teacher-made short videos, then self-paced lessons, then a mastery check before moving on.
- Three tiers of content in each unit:
  - must-do
  - should-do
  - aspire-to-do
- Pacing is bounded within the unit (deadlines at the unit end).
- Watch for: students who need many mastery attempts may fall behind (physics study). Add teacher check-ins.
- AI generates the unit "tracker", the lesson checklists, mastery checks with parallel forms, and the extension "aspire-to-do" tasks.

I7. THINKING CLASSROOMS (math and beyond)   (PRACTITIONER · R23)
- Random groups of 3, vertical whiteboards, rich tasks.
- The teacher differentiates in the moment with hints and extensions to keep each group in "flow".
- Consolidation from the groups' work at the end.
- AI generates thin-sliced task sequences, a hint ladder and an extension ladder for each task, and the consolidation notes.

I8. EXPLICIT INSTRUCTION SPINE + DIFFERENTIATED PRACTICE   (MODERATE–STRONG · R26)
- Shared modeling, then differentiated guided and independent practice (Library 01 S1/S2, Library 03 D1).
- Most efficient default for high school: one lesson and three practice paths.

I9. PEER-ASSISTED LEARNING / PEER TUTORING   (MODERATE · near-miss evidence)
- Structured roles and scripts. Reciprocal (students switch roles). Run in school, not as an add-on outside school.
- Avoid: permanently making advanced students the tutors.

I10. SUBJECT-SPECIFIC DELIVERY NOTES
- Math:
  - worked examples paired with problems
  - interleaved review
  - low-floor/high-ceiling tasks
  - Building Thinking Classrooms
  - hint ladders
- ELA:
  - text sets, from leveled to original
  - literature circles with format choice
  - explicit comprehension strategies
  - discussion protocols
  - writing scaffolds that are faded over time
- Science:
  - inquiry levels: structured → guided → open. Vary how much autonomy students have in the practice, not the concept itself.
  - POGIL-style roles
  - units and data analysis scaffolds
- Social studies:
  - Reading Like a Historian: sourcing, contextualization, close reading, corroboration
  - adapted documents for different reading levels
  - DBQ scaffolds
  - multiple perspectives
- World languages:
  - ACTFL ranges (Novice / Intermediate / Advanced) × modes (interpersonal / interpretive / presentational)
  - tasks at the student's current range with a stretch toward the next
  - proficiency rubrics
- CTE:
  - competency checklists
  - tiered skill demonstrations
  - student-captain pods
  - align IEP transition goals with pathways and credentials
  - co-plan with special education staff
  - little research exists
- Electives (arts, PE, etc.): product and choice differentiation, tiered skill progressions, portfolio assessment. Research was not specifically retrieved.
