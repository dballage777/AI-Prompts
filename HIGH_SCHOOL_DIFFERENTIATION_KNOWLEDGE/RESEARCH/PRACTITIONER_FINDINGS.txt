PRACTITIONER & COMMUNITY FINDINGS (Pass 4)
Compiled 2026-11-05. These findings are anecdotal or reported by programs. Don't treat popularity as proof of effectiveness.
Access limits: Reddit was reached only through mirror snapshots. Discord, Facebook, and X were not accessible. YouTube and podcasts were visible only through search listings.

REPEATED TEACHER COMPLAINTS
- "Differentiation looks great in theory, collapses under real class sizes, real time limits, and real burnout." (Reddit, r/teaching and r/Teachers, via mirror)
- Administrators push small groups without providing the staffing to run them. Managing several groups at once is hard in high school classes. (Reddit)
- High school teachers carry 100–180+ students across 5–6 periods, on schedules driven by credits. (MTSS coordinator sources)
- Research on high school teachers in Quebec identified the main obstacles as the number of students and groups, and a lack of time and resources.
- In a 2008 Fordham survey, 83% of teachers said differentiation was somewhat or very difficult to implement.

WHAT TEACHERS REPORT WORKS (and stays manageable)
1. Default to low-prep moves:
   - hinge questions with on-the-spot regrouping
   - "must do / may do" lists
   - tiered exit tickets
   - extension questions placed at the bottom of the standard sheet
2. Build a bank of tiered tasks gradually (one unit at a time) instead of rebuilding them every unit.
3. Use learning teams of 4–5 instead of many separate groups (Edutopia, McCarthy). Research (Lou 1996) suggests 2–4 per group is better for learning.
4. Tier only the major projects. Combine them with product choice: RAFT, choice boards.
5. Use "Mild / Medium / Spicy" problem sets: one handout, three entry points, and students self-select with teacher guidance. (Georgia CTM 2024; low-floor/high-ceiling practice)
6. Building Thinking Classrooms: random groups of 3 at vertical whiteboards. The teacher differentiates in the moment with hints and extensions. Very popular with high school math teachers.
7. Self-paced mastery units (Modern Classrooms): teacher-made instructional videos free the teacher for small-group and 1:1 help. Students move on after a mastery check.
8. Station rotation (Catlin Tucker): a teacher-led station acts as the small-group reteach. Other stations handle practice, online work, and collaboration.
9. Literature circles with format choice (print, e-book, audio) and grouping by text level, with a common culminating project. (SREB, Georgia high school example)
10. Tier 2 intervention staff pull students out for missing foundational skills, so class time can stay focused on grade-level work. (This depends on the school's staffing.)

IMPLEMENTATION PITFALLS THAT PRACTITIONERS NOTE
- Turning "easier" into "less work," or "advanced" into "more work."
- Fixed groups that become de facto tracks.
- Differentiating by learning style (unsupported; see R17).
- Giving leveled texts forever, without a path back to grade-level text.
- Choice boards whose options don't line up with the objective.
- Retake policies that cause students to under-prepare the first time (an opinion that appears in critiques).

TEACHER-RECOMMENDED RESOURCES (seen in community and search listings)
Cult of Pedagogy; Catlin Tucker (The Balance; station rotation); Building Thinking Classrooms; Modern Classrooms Project; Edutopia; Digital Inquiry Group (Reading Like a Historian); IRIS Center modules; Byrdseed (gifted); Prep Period Podcast (high school); The Unteachables Podcast (a realistic view of DI).

LESSER-KNOWN BUT VALUABLE
- Hess Cognitive Rigor Matrix: raises the complexity of a task without adding to its length.
- Kaplan Depth & Complexity: thinking tools for enrichment in any subject.
- ACTFL performance ranges: world-language differentiation by proficiency.
- NCII data-based individualization: for students who don't respond to Tier 2 support.
- Productive failure tasks: offer advanced students a challenge-first option.
