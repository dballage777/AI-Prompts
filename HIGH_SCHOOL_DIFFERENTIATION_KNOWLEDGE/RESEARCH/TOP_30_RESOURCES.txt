1 · R03 · 8.45 · 9/9/8/8/8/10/5 · Official practice guide
IES/WWC Practice Guide: Improving Adolescent Literacy: Effective Classroom and Intervention Practices
(NCEE 2008-4027; panel chair Michael Kamil; grades 4–12)
URL: ERIC record ED502398 (URL verified via search). The IES page itself was blocked.
- Key findings [SEARCH-OFFICIAL]: five recommendations, each with an evidence rating.
    1. Explicit vocabulary instruction.
    2. Direct, explicit comprehension-strategy instruction.
    3. Extended discussion of text meaning (wording not fully confirmed).
    4. Motivation and engagement.
    5. Intensive, individualized intervention for struggling readers.
- Struggling students: explicit vocabulary and strategy instruction in every content area,
  plus intensive intervention given by trained specialists.
- Advanced students: extended discussion and interpretation tasks.
- Assignments and assessment: build vocabulary and strategy practice into content assignments.
- Limitations: published in 2008; several recommendations rest on moderate evidence.
- For AI prompts: content-area materials should pre-teach Tier 2 and Tier 3 vocabulary,
  model a comprehension strategy, and include discussion questions.

2 · R05 · 8.40 · 9/8/9/8/7/9/8 · Expert / research synthesis
Dylan Wiliam, Embedded Formative Assessment (2011; 2nd ed. 2018), and
Black & Wiliam (1998), "Inside the Black Box" (a synthesis of 250+ studies).
URL not verified; these are books and articles.
- Key findings [SEARCH-SECONDARY about primary works]: five strategies.
    1. Clarify learning intentions and success criteria.
    2. Elicit evidence of learning (hinge questions, exit tickets, all-student response).
    3. Give feedback that moves learners forward.
    4. Activate students as resources for one another.
    5. Activate students as owners of their learning.
- Struggling and advanced students: this is the engine for deciding WHO gets WHAT next.
- Assessment: every lesson needs one or more evidence points tied to a decision rule.
- Limitations: specific effect-size claims circulating online were not verified.
- For AI prompts: generate hinge questions whose distractors each reveal a misconception,
  plus a sort rule (got it / partial / not yet → next activity).

3 · R02 · 8.30 · 10/5/9/9/9/8/9 · Expert framework (book)
Carol Ann Tomlinson / ASCD: the differentiated instruction model.
(How to Differentiate Instruction in Academically Diverse Classrooms; The Differentiated Classroom)
URL not verified (ASCD blocked).
- Key findings [SEARCH-SECONDARY about the primary framework]:
    - Differentiate content, process, product, and learning environment.
    - Base those choices on readiness, interest, and learning profile.
    - Groups are flexible and data-driven. They are not permanent "high" or "low" groups.
    - Every student gets "respectful tasks": equally engaging and important.
- Limitations:
    - The framework's outcome evidence is modest (see R01, R29).
    - "Learning profile" is often misread as learning styles, which are unsupported (R17).
- For AI prompts: tiers must share one objective and be respectful.
  Never label students. Separate readiness tiers from interest-based choice.

4 · R22 · 8.20 · 9/7/9/8/7/9/8 · Curriculum and research organization
Digital Inquiry Group (formerly the Stanford History Education Group; independent since January 2024):
Reading Like a Historian.
URL verified: https://www.inquirygroup.org
- Key findings:
    - Four historical-thinking skills: sourcing, contextualization, close reading, corroboration.
    - Document-based lessons.
    - Adapted and scaffolded documents for different reading levels [SEARCH-SECONDARY].
- Struggling students: modified documents, vocabulary glosses, guiding questions.
- Advanced students: unmodified primary sources, more documents, corroboration across perspectives.
- For AI prompts: produce a "same documents, adapted text" set, keeping the historical claim
  and the evidence intact.

5 · R06 · 8.15 · 7/10/8/8/6/10/7 · Peer-reviewed review
Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013), Improving Students' Learning With Effective
Learning Techniques. Psychological Science in the Public Interest. DOI not verified.
- Key findings [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]:
    - High utility: practice testing (retrieval) and distributed practice.
    - Moderate utility: elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, interleaving.
    - Low utility: highlighting, rereading, summarization, the keyword mnemonic, imagery.
- Applies to every student.
- For AI prompts: build retrieval warm-ups and spaced review into every differentiated set.
  Don't generate "highlight and reread" study tasks.

6 · R04 · 8.00 · 7/9/9/7/7/9/7 · Evidence synthesis (official charity)
Education Endowment Foundation (UK): Teaching & Learning Toolkit.
URL verified (domain): educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk
- Key findings [SEARCH-OFFICIAL]: average months of additional progress.
    - Metacognition and self-regulation: +8 (May 2025 update; earlier listed as +7).
    - Feedback: +6.
    - Mastery learning: +5, with weaker effects in programmes longer than about 12 weeks.
    - These are averages over wide variation.
- 2026 EEF study (via TES): low attainers progressed similarly in sets and in mixed groups;
  high attainers progressed about 2 months slower in mixed groups.
- Limitations: UK context; averages hide big differences in how well programmes were implemented.
- For AI prompts: include metacognitive prompts (plan, monitor, evaluate) and feedback steps.

7 · R07 · 8.00 · 8/9/7/7/10/9/5 · Peer-reviewed theory and research
Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller (2003), "The Expertise Reversal Effect," Educational Psychologist.
Also: Renkl & Atkinson on fading; Salden et al. (2010). URL not verified.
- Key findings [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]:
    - Worked examples help novices.
    - The same guidance becomes redundant or harmful for more expert learners.
    - Fading the steps beats an abrupt switch to problem solving.
    - Best established for well-structured domains (math, physics).
- This resource is the clearest research basis for giving struggling and advanced students
  DIFFERENT levels of guidance:
    - Novices: full worked examples, then faded examples.
    - Advanced: problems first, with examples available on request.

8 · R13 · 8.00 · 9/7/8/8/8/9/6 · Official training module (OSEP-funded)
IRIS Center, Vanderbilt: "Differentiated Instruction: Maximizing the Learning of All Students."
URL verified: https://iris.peabody.vanderbilt.edu/module/di/
- Key findings: free module, about 3 hours, with a certificate.
  Covers content, process, and product (assessment), with video examples [SEARCH-OFFICIAL].
- Use: background for teachers who are new to DI.

9 · R26 · 8.00 · 8/7/9/8/8/8/8 · Expert syntheses
Archer & Hughes (2011), Explicit Instruction. Rosenshine (2012), Principles of Instruction.
Gradual release (Pearson & Gallagher 1983; Fisher & Frey). URL not verified.
- Key findings [SEARCH-SECONDARY]:
    - Lesson sequence: explanation → modeling → guided practice → independent practice.
    - Small steps, checks for understanding, and a high success rate.
    - Fisher & Frey's gradual release has four phases (I do / we do / you do together / you do alone).
      It is recursive, not strictly one-way.
    - Best for well-structured content.
- Struggling students: this is the core Tier 1 and Tier 2 approach.
- For AI prompts: lessons should follow I do / We do / You do, with checks in between.

10 · R08 · 7.95 · 8/10/6/7/9/10/4 · Second-order meta-analysis
Steenbergen-Hu, Makel & Olszewski-Kubilius (2016), "What one hundred years of research says about
the effects of ability grouping and acceleration on K–12 students' academic achievement,"
Review of Educational Research.
URL verified (full study PDF via a squarespace host in search results).
- Key findings [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]:
    - Between-class tracking yields little benefit.
    - Within-class grouping, cross-grade subject grouping, and special grouping for gifted
      students are positive.
    - Acceleration: g = 0.70 versus non-accelerated same-age peers.
- For AI prompts: favor flexible within-class groups and acceleration paths over fixed tracks.

11 · R01 · 7.85 · 10/8/6/8/7/9/5 · Peer-reviewed systematic review
Smale-Jacobse, Meijer, Helms-Lorenz & Maulana (2019), "Differentiated Instruction in Secondary
Education: A Systematic Review of Research Evidence," Frontiers in Psychology 10:2366.
URL verified: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02366/full
- Key findings [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]:
    - Small-to-moderate positive effects on achievement: d ≈ +0.741, or +0.509 excluding one outlier.
    - The evidence base is scarce and heterogeneous; the authors call it an indication of
      possible benefit.
    - The comparison study, Deunk et al. (2018), covers PRIMARY school.
- Limitations: few high-quality secondary studies.
- For AI prompts: present DI as promising but dependent on implementation. Don't overclaim.

12 · R10 · 7.85 · 8/7/9/7/8/9/6 · Research center plus studies
Reis, Renzulli et al.: Curriculum Compacting (National Research Center on the Gifted & Talented, UConn).
URL verified: gifted.uconn.edu (Curriculum Compacting and Achievement Test Scores, PDF)
- Key findings [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]:
    - Teachers cut 40–50% of regular curriculum for talented students with no loss on
      achievement tests (Reis et al. 1998, Gifted Child Quarterly 42).
- Limitations: mostly elementary samples. The authors flag secondary use as an open question.
- For AI prompts: pre-assess, document what is already mastered, and replace practice of mastered
  content with enrichment or acceleration.

13 · R20 · 7.85 · 8/6/9/8/8/9/7 · ELL frameworks
WIDA "Focus on Differentiation" (2012) and the SIOP Model (Center for Applied Linguistics;
Echevarría, Vogt & Short; secondary edition: Making Content Comprehensible for Secondary English Learners).
URLs verified: https://cal.org/siop/research/ ; WIDA Focus PDF (Wyoming DOE host)
- Key findings:
    - Write language expectations for each English Language Proficiency (ELP) level with the
      WIDA Can Do Descriptors.
    - Pair those expectations with scaffolds.
    - Check each student's domain profile (reading, writing, listening, speaking); levels differ by domain.
    - SIOP: 8 components, 30 features. Its evidence is supportive but largely developer-linked
      [SEARCH-OFFICIAL / SECONDARY].
- Core rule: lower the LANGUAGE demand, not the CONTENT's cognitive demand.

14 · R24 · 7.85 · 10/5/9/8/8/6/9 · Practitioner program (evaluations commissioned by the program)
Modern Classrooms Project: blended, self-paced, mastery-based model.
URL verified: https://modernclassrooms.org (2018–21 independent program evaluations)
- Key findings [SEARCH-SECONDARY]:
    - Video instruction, self-pacing within a unit, and mastery checks.
    - 89% of MCP teachers report meeting students at different levels, versus 44% of peers.
    - 2019–20 evaluation: 55 teachers, 1,929 students.
    - A University of Kentucky dissertation reports gains in math achievement and self-efficacy.
- Limitations:
    - Opt-in samples; commissioned evaluations; no randomized trial.
    - One physics study: students who needed many mastery-check attempts had lower test averages.

15 · R11 · 7.80 · 9/10/6/6/7/9/6 · Randomized controlled trials
UChicago Education Lab: Saga high-dosage math tutoring RCTs (Guryan, Ludwig et al.).
URLs verified: saga.org/impact/research ; overdeck.org research summary ; urbanlabs.uchicago.edu PDF
- Key findings [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]:
    - About 2,600–2,700 students in grades 9–10 per study.
    - Intent-to-treat effects: math test +0.12 SD, math GPA +0.22.
    - Effects for students actually treated: +0.28 and +0.52.
    - Math course failures fell 49% (trial 1). Little fade-out.
- Limitations: needs trained tutors and scheduled time. Chicago, 2013–15.
- For AI prompts: the AI should support the tutor (problem sets, diagnostics), not replace them.

16 · R18 · 7.70 · 8/6/9/7/9/8/7 · Rigor framework
Webb's Depth of Knowledge (2002) and Hess Cognitive Rigor Matrix.
URLs verified: ASCD blog "What exactly is Depth of Knowledge"; UConn Project Bump Up NAGC 2023 slides
- Key findings:
    - DOK levels: recall; skills and concepts; strategic thinking; extended thinking.
    - The popular "DOK wheel" misrepresents Webb's model.
    - Hess matrices can raise a task's complexity for advanced learners.
- For AI prompts: label items by DOK. Raise DOK for enrichment rather than adding more items.

17 · R27 · 7.65 · 8/7/8/7/8/9/6 · Classic research plus reviews
Bloom (1984), "The 2 Sigma Problem"; mastery learning; Brookhart & Guskey et al. (2016),
"A Century of Grading Research" (Review of Educational Research).
URLs verified: Bloom 1984 PDF (gwern mirror) ; uknow.uky.edu (Guskey)
- Key findings:
    - Mastery learning cycle: formative check → correctives for students who haven't mastered;
      enrichment for students who have.
    - The 2-sigma magnitude is contested; replications are smaller.
    - Standards-based grading and retakes: definitions vary; secondary effect evidence is thin.
- For AI prompts: generate parallel correctives and enrichment for each formative check,
  plus a parallel reassessment form.

18 · R15 · 7.55 · 7/9/7/7/8/9/4 · Meta-analysis
Sinha & Kapur (2021), "When Problem Solving Followed by Instruction Works: Evidence for Productive
Failure," Review of Educational Research. DOI 10.3102/00346543211019105 (seen in search results).
- Key findings [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]:
    - 53 studies, 166 comparisons.
    - Problem solving before instruction beats instruction first: g = 0.36
      (0.37–0.58 when the productive-failure design is followed closely).
    - Younger learners and domain-general skills favored instruction first.
    - Students need some prior knowledge.
- For AI prompts: offer a challenge problem BEFORE instruction for ready students.
  Students missing prerequisites get explicit instruction first.

19 · R23 · 7.55 · 9/5/9/7/7/7/10 · Practitioner-research framework
Peter Liljedahl, Building Thinking Classrooms in Mathematics (2020).
URL verified: https://www.buildingthinkingclassrooms.com
- Key findings [SEARCH-SECONDARY]:
    - Vertical non-permanent surfaces (whiteboards on walls).
    - Visibly random groups of 3.
    - Thinking tasks.
    - Hints and extensions given to keep each group in "flow" (in-the-moment differentiation).
- Limitations: peer-reviewed effect sizes not located. Very popular with high school math teachers.

20 · R12 · 7.50 · 8/5/8/8/8/9/8 · Official framework
CAST: UDL Guidelines 3.0 (July 2024).
URL verified: https://udlguidelines.cast.org/
- Key findings:
    - Three principles: engagement (why), representation (what), action and expression (how).
    - Version 3.0 adds attention to identity, bias, and systemic barriers.
    - The stated goal is now "learner agency."
- Evidence [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]:
    - Capp 2017: positive effects on the learning process; outcomes not demonstrated.
    - King-Sears et al. 2023: 20 studies, of which a critique notes only 2 were K-12.
- For AI prompts: build options into the base design (multiple representations and response modes)
  before tiering.

21 · R17 · 7.50 · 7/10/7/6/5/10/6 · Peer-reviewed review (what NOT to do)
Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer & Bjork (2008), "Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence," PSPI.
URL verified: digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1765
- Key finding [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]: virtually no evidence for "meshing" (matching instruction
  to a learning style).
- For AI prompts: forbid differentiating by visual, auditory, or kinesthetic learning styles.
  Use readiness, interest, and accessibility instead.

22 · R09 · 7.40 · 8/8/6/7/8/9/5 · Research compendium
A Nation Empowered (2015; Belin-Blank Center; Assouline, Colangelo, VanTassel-Baska,
Lupkowski-Shoplik) and Steenbergen-Hu & Moon (2011), Gifted Child Quarterly 55:39–53.
URL verified: journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0016986210383155
- Key findings:
    - Acceleration is "the most effective curriculum intervention for gifted children."
    - Positive effects on academic achievement; social-emotional effects slightly positive.
- High school forms: subject acceleration, AP, dual enrollment, credit by exam,
  compacted course sequences.

23 · R14 · 7.40 · 7/9/8/6/6/9/4 · RCT (grade 7)
Rohrer et al. (2020): interleaved mathematics practice, cluster RCT. IES-funded; reviewed by WWC.
URL verified: https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Study/88770
- Key finding [SEARCH-OFFICIAL]: 54 classes, 787 students. Unannounced test 61% vs 38%, d = 0.83.
- For AI prompts: practice sets should mix problem types once students have learned them.

24 · R21 · 7.40 · 8/6/8/7/8/9/5 · Official professional standards
ACTFL Performance Descriptors and NYSED world-language performance indicators.
URL verified: https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/actfl-performance-descriptors
- Key findings:
    - Ranges: Novice, Intermediate, Advanced.
    - Modes: interpersonal, interpretive, presentational.
    - Design tasks at the learner's current range that push toward the next range.
    - Use proficiency-oriented analytic rubrics.
- For AI prompts: world-language tasks are differentiated by proficiency range, not by grade level.

25 · R16 · 7.35 · 8/8/7/6/8/9/3 · Meta-analysis
Lou et al. (1996), "Within-Class Grouping: A Meta-Analysis," Review of Educational Research 66(4).
URL not verified.
- Key findings [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]:
    - Small-group work vs no grouping: +0.17.
    - Same-ability vs mixed-ability groups: +0.12 overall.
    - Groups of 5 or more benefit little.
    - Low-ability students reportedly did better in mixed groups.
    - Methodological critiques exist.
- For AI prompts: plan groups of 2–4. Vary the grouping basis by purpose.

26 · R28 · 7.25 · 9/4/9/7/7/6/10 · Practitioner media
Cult of Pedagogy (Jennifer Gonzalez) and Catlin Tucker, "The Balance" (blended learning,
station rotation). Also relevant: Prep Period Podcast (high school); The Unteachables.
URL verified: cultofpedagogy.com. Catlin Tucker episode via Pocket Casts.
- Key findings [SEARCH-SECONDARY]: practical, low-prep differentiation; station rotation;
  examples by grade band. Practitioner opinion, not research.

27 · R19 · 7.20 · 8/5/8/7/8/8/7 · Enrichment framework
Sandra Kaplan: Depth & Complexity (USC).
URL verified: sites.usc.edu/gifteded
- Key findings:
    - Depth: mastery of the discipline (patterns, rules, ethics, big ideas).
    - Complexity: connections across time, perspectives, and disciplines.
    - The icons are only part of the framework. Usable K–12, all subjects.
- For AI prompts: enrichment means a deeper or more connected task, not a longer one.

28 · R30 · 7.05 · 9/6/8/5/7/7/6 · Emerging research
Evidence on the quality of AI-generated differentiation materials.
URLs: arxiv.org/pdf/2405.00864 verified; the LDRP study was seen via EdWeek coverage.
- Key findings:
    - Learning Disabilities Research & Practice (Nov 2025): six AI tools, including Diffit,
      generated 410 passages. Passages requested at grade 3 averaged grade 5.5–6.3 readability.
    - A vendor (MagicSchool) reports grade-level alignment about two-thirds of the time.
    - GPT-4 math multiple-choice sets: 14 of 20 judged valid; one answer key miscalculated.
    - Proportional-reasoning study: ChatGPT-4 got 10 of 14 correct and reproduced common
      student errors.
- For AI prompts: require readability self-checks, answer-key verification, and teacher review.

29 · R25 · 6.85 · 8/8/7/5/5/8/4 · Meta-analysis
Wagner, Gegenfurtner & Urhahne (2020): flipped classroom in secondary education.
URL verified: econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1024/1010-0652/a000274
- Key finding [SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED]: 25 articles, N = 2,323, d = 0.42 versus traditional
  instruction. Effects were larger in shorter interventions.
- Use: frees class time for small groups. Watch for students without home access.

30 · R29 · 6.70 · 9/4/7/6/7/7/8 · Critical debate
Delisle (2015), "Differentiation Doesn't Work" (Education Week); rebuttals by Tomlinson,
Wiggins, and McTighe; Fordham Institute (2008) teacher survey.
URL verified: edweek.org/teaching-learning/opinion-differentiation-doesnt-work/2015/01
- Key findings:
    - 83% of teachers said DI is somewhat or very difficult to implement.
    - Hertberg-Davis: "no one was actually differentiating," and regular-classroom DI is weak
      for challenging the most able students.
    - Rebuttals argue DI works when implemented in a manageable way.
- Why it is included: it keeps prompts realistic about teacher workload and stops them overclaiming.
