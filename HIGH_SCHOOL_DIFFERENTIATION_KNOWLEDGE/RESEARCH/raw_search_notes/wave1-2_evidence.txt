[Tomlinson DI model] (secondary summaries; primary sources are Tomlinson 1999/2014 and ASCD)
- Adjust content (what students learn), process (how they make sense of it), product (how they show learning), and learning environment (the affective climate).
- Base adjustments on readiness (proximity to the learning goals), interest, and learning profile.
- Grouping is flexible: students are not permanently "high" or "low"; groups shift by task, concept, and the most recent assessment data.
- Tasks at every level should be "respectful tasks" (equally engaging and important).

[Smale-Jacobse, Meijer, Helms-Lorenz & Maulana 2019, Frontiers in Psychology, doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02366; PMC6883934] (SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED)
- Systematic review of DI in SECONDARY education.
- Most studies found small-to-moderate positive effects on achievement: d ≈ +0.741, or +0.509 with one outlier removed.
- The authors call this an indication of possible benefit and say more research is needed.
- The secondary-level evidence base is scarce.
- Deunk et al. 2018 is a review and meta-analysis at the PRIMARY level ("some potential when implemented well").
Related figures, of lower reliability:
- Visible Learning MetaX lists differentiation at 0.58 across 6 meta-analyses.
- A 2026 Frontiers meta-analysis on ELLs reports g = 0.78 (21 of its 56 effect sizes come from middle/high school).

[CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0, July 2024] (SEARCH-OFFICIAL)
- Same three principles: engagement (why), representation (what), action & expression (how).
- Version 3.0 adds attention to systemic barriers, identity, bias, and joy.
- The stated goal changed from "expert learners" to "learner agency".

[Grouping] (SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED)
- Steenbergen-Hu, Makel & Olszewski-Kubilius 2016, Review of Educational Research: a second-order meta-analysis of about 100 years of research (13 meta-analyses).
  - Between-class tracking did not help learning much.
  - Within-class grouping and acceleration were reported as positive (g = 0.42 overall figure seen; context unclear).
- Kulik & Kulik 1982 (secondary schools, 52 studies): clear gains only for high-ability students given enriched honors instruction; near-zero effects for average and below-average students.
- Lou et al. 1996 (meta-analysis of within-class grouping) is cited but was not retrieved.

[Curriculum compacting: Reis, Renzulli et al., NRC/GT UConn] (SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED / OFFICIAL)
- Teachers eliminated 40–50% of the regular curriculum for academically talented students with no drop in achievement-test results (Reis et al. 1998, Gifted Child Quarterly 42:123–129).
- Mostly elementary samples; the authors themselves list secondary-level use as an open question.

[EEF Teaching & Learning Toolkit] (SEARCH-OFFICIAL)
- Metacognition & self-regulation: +7 → +8 months (May 2025 update).
- Feedback: +6 months (older versions said +8).
- Mastery learning: about +5 months; impact decreases in programmes longer than about 12 weeks.
- These are averages that hide wide variation.
- Figures for individualised instruction and within-class attainment grouping were not retrieved.

[Tiering practice] (district and practitioner documents: Cobb County tiered assignments PDF, CBSD packet, Berks IU)
- All tiers share the same objective.
- Tier by resources, outcome, process, or product.
- Start from the demanding standard (plan the advanced tier first), then adjust downward to keep tasks "respectful".
- HS example (Pythagorean theorem): Tier 1 applies the formula to simple triangles; Tier 2 devises and solves a real-world application.

[IES/WWC practice guides] (SEARCH-OFFICIAL)
- Assisting Students Struggling with Mathematics: Intervention in the Elementary Grades (WWC 2021-006). Grades K–6, so apply to HS with caution. Six recommendations:
  1. systematic instruction
  2. clear, concise mathematical language
  3. representations, including semi-concrete
  4. the number line
  5. word problems
  6. timed fluency activities
- Improving Adolescent Literacy (NCEE 2008-4027, Kamil, chair; grades 4–12). Recommendations:
  1. explicit vocabulary instruction
  2. direct, explicit comprehension-strategy instruction
  3. extended discussion of text meaning (wording not fully confirmed)
  4. motivation and engagement
  5. intensive, individualized intervention for struggling readers
- Also exists: Providing Reading Interventions for Students in Grades 4–9 (2022).

[Worked examples / expertise reversal] (SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED; primary sources: Kalyuga et al. 2003, Educational Psychologist; Kalyuga 2007; Salden et al. 2010, Instructional Science)
- Worked examples help novices.
- The same guidance becomes redundant or harmful as expertise grows (expertise reversal).
- Fading steps from examples beats an abrupt switch to problem solving. Adaptive fading did better than fixed fading in the lab; classroom results were mixed.
- Advanced learners can start with problems, with worked examples available for reference.
- These effects come mostly from well-structured domains (math, mechanics).

[Dunlosky et al. 2013, Psychological Science in the Public Interest] (SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED)
- High utility: practice testing and distributed practice.
- Moderate: elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, interleaving.
- Low: summarization, highlighting, keyword mnemonic, imagery for text, rereading.
- Agarwal classroom retrieval-practice work not retrieved.

[Saga high-dosage tutoring RCTs, Chicago, UChicago Education Lab, Ludwig & Guryan] (SEARCH-PEER-REVIEWED)
- About 2,600–2,700 9th/10th graders per study (2013–15).
- Intent-to-treat effects: math test 0.12 SD, math GPA 0.22.
- Effects on students actually treated: 0.28 and 0.52.
- Math course failures fell 49% in the first trial. Effects showed little fade.
- Meets ESSA Tier 1. Context: mostly low-income male students of color.

[Wiliam formative assessment] (secondary summaries; primary sources: Embedded Formative Assessment 2011; Black & Wiliam 1998 "Inside the Black Box", a synthesis of 250+ studies)
Five strategies:
1. Clarify learning intentions and success criteria.
2. Elicit evidence of learning: hinge questions, exit tickets, all-student response.
3. Feedback that moves learners forward.
4. Activate students as resources for one another.
5. Activate students as owners of their learning: traffic lights, learning logs.

[Secondary MTSS] (practitioner, state, and vendor sources)
- Scheduling time for targeted support is the main high school challenge (federal "Implementing MTSS in Secondary Schools" guide, Appendix F, has sample schedules).
- Tier 2 must align with Tier 1.
- Intervention enhances core instruction but cannot replace it.
- At the secondary level the focus shifts from prevention to remediation.
- NCII data-based individualization not retrieved.

[Mastery learning: Bloom 1984 "2 Sigma"] (primary paper surfaced, gwern mirror)
- Tutored students averaged above 98% of the control group; group mastery learning above 84% (about 1 SD).
- The 2-sigma magnitude is contested; replications are smaller.
- The mechanism is formative tests → correctives → enrichment for students who already show mastery.
- Guskey and Modern Classrooms not yet retrieved.

[Explicit instruction] (secondary)
- Archer & Hughes 2011: systematic, direct, engaging, success-oriented.
- Rosenshine: explanation → modeling → guided practice → independent practice. Best suited to well-structured content.
- Gradual release (Pearson & Gallagher 1983; Fisher & Frey): I do → we do → you do together → you do alone. Recursive, not strictly linear.
