# Differentiation, Accommodations, ELL, and IEP-Adjacent Support

## Principles
- **Differentiate the supports, not the expectations, unless the teacher says otherwise.** Keep the objective and adjust the access points: language, representation, scaffolds, and practice amount.
- **Describe needs, not students.** Use "a group that needs fewer items and a word bank", not "Maria (IEP, ADHD)".
- **The assistant suggests and the teacher decides.** This matters most for anything tied to legal documents.

## Differentiation menu (assistants can offer this as numbered options)
1. Tiered versions: approaching, on-level, and extending. Keep the same objective at each tier.
2. Language supports: word bank, sentence frames, glossary, simplified directions, bilingual glossary (draft only).
3. Representation: visual or graphic organizer, worked example, manipulatives described in text, audio-friendly text.
4. Response options: oral, drawing, typed, multiple choice with explanation.
5. Chunking: fewer items per page, steps numbered, checkpoints.
6. Extension: open-ended problems, transfer tasks, student-created questions.

## Accommodations vs modifications
- **Accommodations** change *how* students access or show learning (extended time, read-aloud, fewer items per page).
- **Modifications** change *what* is learned (a reduced standard).
- The assistant can draft materials that **implement** accommodations the teacher lists.
- It must **not decide** which accommodations a student receives. That belongs to the IEP or 504 team.

## IEP-adjacent assistants (strict boundaries) [D+ X56–X58]
- Some districts **prohibit** using AI to write any part of an IEP or 504. Others allow de-identified support. **Check district policy first.** Templates must include that check.
- Allowed by default:
  - generic goal *examples* for a skill;
  - progress-monitoring tools such as probes and data sheets;
  - materials that implement listed accommodations;
  - plain-language explanations for families, without student details.
- Not by default:
  - uploading IEPs or evaluations;
  - entering diagnoses tied to a student;
  - drafting legally binding sections unless district policy explicitly allows it.
  - Certified special-education staff own substantive content.

## English language learners
- State the proficiency level (e.g., WIDA level or district scale) and the home language only if the teacher chooses to.
- Use comprehensible input, visuals, sentence frames, cognates, and translanguaging scaffolds. Engagement benefits are reported; achievement evidence is limited [C X44–X46].
- Translations are **drafts**, and a fluent speaker should review them. Watch for AI pushing dominant-standard language norms [D X44 note].

## Intervention (reading / math)
- Follow the district's program and the teacher's diagnostic information (aggregated or de-identified).
- The AI generates practice items, decodable-style word lists (when the teacher supplies the phonics scope), and progress-monitoring forms.
- AI-delivered intervention lacks a strong evidence base [knowledge gap]. Keep it in a materials role, with teacher delivery.
