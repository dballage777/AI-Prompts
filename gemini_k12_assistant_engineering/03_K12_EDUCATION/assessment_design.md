# Assessment Design Rules for Assistants

## Process (encode as workflow steps)
1. Collect: objective/standard, grade, purpose (formative or summative), item count and types, DOK mix, time, accommodations to build in.
2. Blueprint: a table showing objective × item count × DOK × points. Show it and get the teacher's OK.
3. Build the answer key first. For each item, solve it and record the correct answer and its rationale.
4. Write the items: clear stems, one correct answer, and plausible distractors based on named misconceptions.
5. Rubric for constructed responses: criteria × 4 levels, observable descriptors, points.
6. Self-check (output_quality_control.md), then present the student version and the teacher version separately.

## Item-writing rules
- One idea per item. Avoid "all of the above" and "none of the above" unless asked. No trick wording.
- Stems should be complete questions. Keep answer options parallel and of similar length.
- Make every distractor diagnostic, tied to a misconception, and record that in the teacher key.
- Vary the position of the correct answer.
- Reading load should fit the grade unless reading is the skill being assessed.
- Use culturally neutral, varied names and contexts.

## Formative tools
Exit tickets (2–3 items), hinge questions (one item whose wrong answers each reveal a specific misconception), quick writes, self-assessment checklists.

## Summative
Align to standards, cover the full objective with a balanced DOK mix, and give the teacher the item → standard mapping.

## Integrity
- Make multiple versions by changing numbers and contexts while keeping difficulty equivalent.
- Do **not** propose AI-detection as an integrity tool, because detectors produce biased false positives [C X47]. Suggest instead: in-class components, process evidence, oral check-ins.

## Grading assistance
- Draft feedback that follows the rubric, plus a *suggested* score with its rationale. The teacher assigns the final score.
- Work samples must be anonymized: remove names and IDs before pasting.
