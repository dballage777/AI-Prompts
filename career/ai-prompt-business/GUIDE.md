# AI Prompt Writing Career Guide: Contract AI Work and Selling School Prompts

Prepared 2026-11-26 for an Indiana high school teacher.
- **Goal:** side income while teaching.
- **Time:** about 15 hours a week during the school year, full time in summer.
- **Interests:** freelance prompt writing, AI training and consulting for schools, AI model training and rating work, and selling school prompts.

**Evidence labels:**
- **Documented:** official platform pages, laws, fee schedules and job posts.
- **Research:** surveys and studies (sample noted).
- **Reported:** news and industry articles.
- **Anecdotal:** forums and personal stories.
- **Inferred:** my own reasoning.
- **Summary-only:** seen only in a search snippet; the page could not be opened.

All pages were accessed 2026-11-26 unless another date is shown. Numbers in brackets point to the Sources list in section 7.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Education expertise is paid directly in AI model training work.** Mercor's standing "Education & Training Expert" pool lists **$30–$85/hr**, part-time and remote, for people with teaching experience. A closed K-12 listing paid $40–$50/hr and required 5+ years of K-12 experience [B1, B2]. *Documented.* Work is project-based and can end suddenly. Several platforms face worker lawsuits, and a 2025 article reported a project shutdown that affected thousands of workers [B6, B9].
- **Indiana PD is sold at real day rates.** Central Indiana ESC (Keep Indiana Learning) charges districts **$1,540–$2,200 per day** for custom professional learning [C1], and Indiana University's INSTRC charges **$2,700 per full day** and **$550–$850 per webinar** [C2]. *Documented.* These are what organizations charge, not what a presenter takes home. An independent consultant's published rates are **$600 per half day and $1,200 per full day** [C3]. *Documented, one provider.*
- **The State of Indiana has paid current educators to write test items.** In 2022, IDOE paid **$125 per completed item** (15–40 items per writer), plus a **$300** training honorarium [C4]. *Documented, 2022 data, so check current terms.*
- **General freelance rates for "prompt engineers" vary widely.** ZipRecruiter shows an average of **$47/hr**, with most earning **$36–$61/hr** [A6]. *Summary-only.* Prompt-writing gigs on Fiverr start at about **$10–$100** [A8]. *Summary-only.* Marketplace fees: **Fiverr takes 20%** [A2], **Upwork takes 0–15%** per contract [A1] (*Reported*), and **Contra takes 0%** [A3] (*Documented*).
- **Selling school prompts is possible, but the rules changed this month:**
  - **TPT:** banned "entirely or primarily AI-generated" resources on **2026-11-19**, and AI disclosure becomes required in 2027 [D1].
  - **Etsy:** bans standalone AI prompt products [D8, D9].
  - **Gumroad:** prohibits selling "AI services" [D7].
- **Most sellers earn little; a few earn most of the money.** In a study of 152,000+ TPT stores, the median store had **$131 in lifetime sales**, 34% of stores had sold nothing, and the top 1% took **81%** of sales [D4]. *Research, 2020.* TPT teacher prompt packs sell for **$5–$32**, mostly $8–$20 [D3]. *Documented, sampled 2026-11-26.*
- **Your district may own what you made for your job.** Under federal copyright law, a work "prepared by an employee within the scope of his or her employment" is a work made for hire [E1]. Indiana NEOLA-model policy 3231 says materials made on paid time or as part of assigned duties belong to the school corporation. It also requires notifying the Superintendent *before* starting own-time work [E3]. *Documented.*
- **Taxes in 2026:**
  - **Self-employment tax** is **15.3%** on net self-employment earnings of $400 or more [A13].
  - **Indiana** adds **2.95%** state tax plus county tax (0.5%–3.0%) [A15, A16].
  - **Quarterly estimated payments** apply if you'll owe $1,000 or more beyond withholding [A14].
  - **1099-NEC threshold** is now **$2,000** [A11]. All income is taxable whether or not you receive a form [A12]. *Documented.*

**What drives pay** (*Inferred from the sources above*):
- Recognized expertise. Education expert pools pay more than general rating pools.
- Proof of results: samples, a portfolio and testimonials.
- Who the buyer is. Districts pay day rates; marketplaces push prices down.
- Whether you reach clients directly. Platform fees cut your take.

AI freelance work has been mixed. Upwork's 2026 index reports that AI freelancers earn **34% more per hour**, but per-contract pay for generative AI and creative work **fell 13%** [A7]. *Research, summary-only.*

**Do certifications help?** Proven skill pays more than certificates.
- In a survey of 4,722 companies, uncertified AI skills earned pay premiums averaging 15%, while AI certifications averaged 8.1% [A17]. *Reported.*
- 91% of 3,128 US hiring-involved workers rated certificates as valuable, but interviews and on-the-job tests ranked first [A18]. *Research.*
- A low-cost certificate such as Google AI Essentials ($49/month on Coursera, under 5 hours [A19]) can help a profile. It does not replace samples.
- Neither study covers freelance buyers specifically.

### 2.4 How contracts work

1. **Proposal.** Restate the client's problem, describe your approach, show one relevant sample, and give a price and timeline.
2. **Scope.** List the deliverables (for example, "5 tested prompts plus a 2-page user guide"). Set the number of revision rounds, what's out of scope, and who owns the result.
3. **Rate.** Hourly, per project, or per session or day. For PD, list prep time separately (the independent consultant in [C3] bills prep hours).
4. **Payment.** On marketplaces, work only on funded milestones [A4]. Off platform, invoice with net-15 or net-30 terms, and ask for a 50% deposit on larger projects (*Inferred*).
5. **Simple contract.** The Freelancers Union has a free Contract Creator tool [A20]. Indiana school corporations cannot accept auto-renewal, choice-of-law or indemnity clauses [C10]. *Documented, from one district's purchasing procedure.*
6. **Taxes (US and Indiana, 2026):**
   - Clients send a 1099-NEC once they pay you **$2,000 or more** [A11].
   - Payment apps and marketplaces send a 1099-K above **$20,000 and 200 transactions** [A12].
   - All income is taxable either way.
   - Self-employment tax is **15.3%** on net self-employment earnings of $400 or more [A13].
   - Federal estimated tax is due **Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15 and Jan 15** if you'll owe $1,000 or more beyond withholding. Raising the withholding on your school paycheck also counts [A14].
   - Indiana state tax is **2.95%** (2.90% in 2027), plus the county rate where you lived on Jan 1 [A15, A16].
   - Indiana estimated payments go through INTIME or Form ES-40 [A16].
   - Talk to a tax professional about business deductions.
7. **Working for your own district.** Indiana's conflict-of-interest law (IC 35-44.1-1-4) makes it a **Level 6 felony** for a public servant to knowingly profit from a contract with the entity they serve without the required disclosure [C9, E4].
   - The law has exceptions. Salary and employment-contract pay are exempt, and so are amounts of $250 or less in 12 months [C11].
   - Disclosure uses SBOA Form 236. The board must accept it at a public meeting before acting on the contract [C9].
   - **Questions to check:** Should extra PD pay from your own district go through payroll rather than a vendor contract? Ask your business office or the State Board of Accounts. This is not a legal conclusion.

### 2.5 Red flags and scams

- **Paying to get paid.** The FTC says paying up front for a job, "so-called training," starter kits or certifications is a sign of a scam [B11, B12].
- **Task scams.** Reports rose from about 5,000 in all of 2023 to about 20,000 in the first half of 2024. Job-scam losses topped $220M in that half-year. They often start with an unexpected text or WhatsApp message, pay small amounts at first, then ask you to "deposit money to unlock earnings" [B13]. *Documented.*
- **Fake AI-training sites.** A fake site posing as "Outlier" took $95,000 in crypto in one week [B14]. *Reported.* Only apply from a platform's official site.
- **Account black markets.** "Verified" Outlier and Prolific accounts are sold on WhatsApp and Facebook [B15]. *Reported.* Never buy or rent an account.
- **Payment off the platform.** Leaving Upwork before a contract starts breaks its Terms of Service and removes payment protection [A21]. *Summary-only.*
- **Unpaid "test projects"** that look like real client work, or unverified client payment methods [A21]. *Summary-only.*
- **Legitimate platforms can still be risky:**
  - Contractors report sudden removal from projects with no notice and no appeal [B6].
  - Some Handshake contractors were suspended and not paid [B9].
  - There are worker-misclassification lawsuits against Scale AI (a $12.5M settlement covering California workers only), Surge Labs/DataAnnotation, Mercor and Handshake [B6]. *Reported.*
  - Treat this income as irregular.

### 3.2 What sells

- **Formats found on TPT** (sampled 2026-11-26) [D3]:
  - large prompt collections (100–500 prompts) at $8–$20;
  - "AI for Teachers" bundles with prompts, posters, policy templates and slides, at about $32;
  - Gemini Gem setup guides and custom AI assistant starter kits at $10–$15;
  - PD guides on building custom assistants at about $15.
- **Very few reviews.** Only 1 of the 20 top-ranked "ChatGPT prompts for teachers" results had any reviews. *Inferred:* demand is still forming, and buyers can't tell good products from bad yet.
- **Products likely to stand out (Inferred):**
  - A tested, task-specific workflow: one problem solved end to end, with the prompt, a teacher guide, sample outputs and troubleshooting tips.
  - Clearly human-written material, which TPT's new policy rewards [D1].
- **TPT's own advice** [D18–D21]:
  - Write titles of up to 80 characters, using the phrases buyers search for.
  - Put your best keywords in the first 1–3 sentences of the description.
  - Make the cover at least 750×750 px, plus 3 more preview images. 62% of surveyed buyers said thorough previews matter most.
  - Use bundles to raise what each buyer spends, and don't underprice to start.

### 3.3 Realistic earnings

- **TPT, study of 152,393 stores with paid items** [D4], *Research, 2020*:
  - Median lifetime sales were **$131**; the mean was $24,615, pulled up by the top sellers.
  - 34% of stores had **$0** in sales.
  - The top 1% of stores took **81%** of sales.
  - 92% of transactions were $5 or less.
- **TPT's CEO (2022):** the "average seller is bringing in low single-digit thousands annually." More than 300 sellers have earned $1M+ in total [D22]. *Reported, from the company.*
- **Two sellers interviewed (2022):** one averaged about $50/month; the other earned $100–$200/day [D22]. *Anecdotal.*
- **Gumroad (2020):** the top 10% of creators earned about 92% of the money [D23]. *Reported.*
- **PromptBase:** no official earnings data. Blog claims of "$500–$2,000/month" are *Anecdotal or summary-only* and unverified.
- **Honest summary:** the data is thin and dated. The typical result is small. A few sellers earn a lot, usually after years of building a catalog.

### 3.4 Teacher checks (questions to check, not legal conclusions)

**Who owns it?**
- Federal law treats work made "within the scope of employment" as owned by the employer [E1, E2].
- NEOLA-model Indiana policy 3231:
  - Materials made on paid work time or as part of prescribed duties "shall remain the property of the Corporation."
  - The Board may give up its rights to own-time work made without district data, facilities or equipment.
  - Staff must notify the Superintendent before starting such work [E3].
- Some Indiana districts use a policy that automatically assigns works made "in whole or in part" with school resources, with the creator receiving 35% of net revenue [E5].
- The NEA argues teachers should own their materials, but that is union policy, not law [E6].

  Check:
  - Does your contract or master agreement say who owns teacher-created materials?
  - Which version of policy 3231 (or a similar policy) has your board adopted?
  - Were these prompts made on paid time, for assigned duties, or with district devices, accounts or AI licenses?

**District devices and accounts.**
- Typical Indiana policy says district technology "shall not be used for private gain or commercial purposes," including offering personal products for sale [E7].
- Users have no expectation of privacy on district systems [E7, E8].
- Check: Keep all business work on personal devices and accounts.

**Student privacy.**
- FERPA protects personally identifiable information in education records [E9].
- Check: Make sure no sample, screenshot or example output includes student names, work, grades, IEP or 504 details, or rosters.
- Check: Use made-up sample data in every preview.

**Your AI use, and copyright.**
- The U.S. Copyright Office says "prompts do not alone provide sufficient control" over the output, and purely AI-generated material isn't protected. Human selection, arrangement and modification can be [E10, E11].
- Check:
  - Which parts did you write yourself?
  - Does the TPT listing meet the "not primarily AI-generated" rule?
  - Will you be ready to disclose AI assistance in 2027 [D1]?

**Brand names.**
- OpenAI doesn't allow "GPT" in product names and requires accurate, non-endorsing references [E12].
- Google allows phrases like "for" or "compatible with," with a ™ attribution [E13].
- Check: Use titles like "Lesson Differentiation Prompt Kit (works with Gemini™ or ChatGPT)" rather than "ChatGPT Differentiation Pack."

**Keeping prompts current.**
- AI models get retired on a schedule. For example, OpenAI shuts down some models on 2026-12-11 [E14].
- Check:
  - Avoid naming specific model versions.
  - Use platform-neutral wording.
  - Plan a refresh twice a year (TPT also recommends reviewing prices twice a year [D21]).

## 6. Uncertainties and gaps

- **TPT's AI policy is brand new (2026-11-19)** and doesn't say whether prompt packs are allowed. The official FAQ was blocked (403). Ask TPT seller support before listing.
- **Upwork and Fiverr pages were blocked.** Their fees come from third-party articles (*Reported*). Check them on the platforms.
- **Pay aggregator pages** (ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor, Indeed) were blocked, so those figures are summary-only.
- **TPT earnings data is from 2020–2022.** No current median was found.
- **No Indiana board minutes** were found showing a teacher-led AI PD rate. Indiana figures are what organizations charge, not what presenters are paid.
- **The 2022 IDOE item-writing rate** may have changed.
- **AI training platforms change often:** rates, project availability and lawsuits. Listed rates are ceilings, not guarantees.
- **Gumroad's "AI services" ban** may or may not cover prompt guides. Ask Gumroad.
- **Indiana's student-data-privacy statute** wasn't confirmed. FERPA applies either way.
- **Conflict-of-interest exceptions** (IC 35-44.1-1-4(c)) were read on FindLaw, not the official Indiana code site. Confirm with SBOA or the district's lawyer.
