## 1. What a district actually buys (Inferred)

Districts rarely buy "a prompt." They buy something that solves a staff problem, comes with training, and doesn't create privacy or legal risk. Four packages fit your work:

| Package | What's in it | Who signs off | Ease of sale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Staff assistant kit + training** (best place to start) | Your differentiation and gradebook assistants as portable instruction files, installed in the **district's own** AI accounts (Gemini for Education, ChatGPT Edu or Claude). Includes a teacher guide and one PD session. | Curriculum director or assistant superintendent | Easiest. Teachers use it and no student data goes in. |
| **2. District license for the kit** | The same kit, licensed for all staff, plus yearly updates as AI tools change | Curriculum or technology director | Medium. Needs a written license and update promise. |
| **3. Custom assistant build** | An assistant built for one department's task, such as special education paperwork help, co-teaching planning or parent communication drafts | Department head plus technology director | Medium. Like a business build (file 06), but with school privacy rules. |
| **4. Student-facing assistant** | Anything students use directly, or that processes student data | Technology director, privacy review, possibly the board | **Hardest.** Needs a student data privacy agreement and age rules. Leave this until later. |

**Keep it staff-facing and student-data-free at first.** That avoids most privacy hurdles. *Inferred.*

## 2. How Indiana districts buy

**Purchasing thresholds** (Indiana public purchasing law, IC 5-22-8)
- **Under $50,000:** bought under the district's own small-purchase policy, usually a quote, a purchase order and the business office.
- **$50,000–$150,000:** the district must invite quotes from at least 3 sellers.
- **Over $150,000:** competitive bidding.
- Splitting a purchase to stay under a threshold is not allowed.
- These are *Summary-only*, from State Board of Accounts training slides; confirm against the current law. Your packages will almost always fall in the **under $50,000** tier.

**Paperwork to expect**
- A W-9, a written quote, and a purchase order before you start. Board approval of the payment claim comes before you're paid. *Documented*, GUIDE.md section 2.4.
- **Contract terms Indiana school corporations can't accept:** auto-renewal, out-of-state choice of law, and indemnity clauses. *Documented*, GUIDE.md.
- **Questions to ask each district:**
  - Do you need a certificate of liability insurance?
  - Do you need a business name or registration?
  - Do you need a signed data privacy agreement, even for staff-only tools?
  - Note: requirements vary by district. I didn't find a single statewide checklist.

**Student data privacy**
- Indiana's education service centers (ESCs) provide all Indiana districts access to a statewide **Student Data Privacy Alliance**. It uses the national Student Data Privacy Consortium (SDPC) registry and its standard agreement, the National Data Privacy Agreement (NDPA). *Summary-only.*
- If your product ever touches student data, expect to sign that agreement.
- Staff-only tools with no student data may not need it, but ask.

**AI-specific review**
- IDOE publishes AI guidance for schools. No statewide vetting or approval rule for AI vendors was found. A 2026 Stateline report says districts are largely on their own when buying AI. *Summary-only.*
- Expect each district's technology director to review your product. Have a one-page "safety and privacy summary" ready (section 5).

**Accessibility (federal ADA Title II rule)**
- Public entities' web content, including vendor-supplied digital materials, must meet **WCAG 2.1 AA**.
- Compliance dates were pushed back a year: **April 26, 2027** for entities serving 50,000+ people, and **April 26, 2028** for smaller ones. *Summary-only*, from the Federal Register interim rule (2026-04-20) and law-firm summaries. It wasn't confirmed which tier school districts fall into.
- What this means for you (Inferred):
  - Make your guides accessible: use real headings, alt text, good color contrast and accessible PDFs or Docs.
  - Say so in your quote.

**Platform timing**
- Google retires Gems for education accounts **no sooner than June 1, 2027**, including in Classroom. It's replacing them with "skills" (file 06). *Documented.*
- Don't sell a Gem-only product to a district. Deliver portable instruction files plus setup steps for whichever platform the district uses.

## 3. The ownership and conflict checks (questions to check, not legal conclusions)

These apply to every district sale.

1. **Who owns the assistants?**
   - You built the differentiation and gradebook assistants for your job.
   - Under policy 3231, your district may own them. NEOLA-model wording says the district can still use materials **royalty-free** even when it waives ownership.
   - Before selling to **other** districts:
     - get written clearance or a waiver from your district;
     - or build new versions on your own time and devices.
   - Some Indiana policies give the creator a revenue share (for example, 35% at one district) instead of full ownership.
2. **Your own district.**
   - Selling to the district that employs you triggers Indiana's conflict-of-interest law (IC 35-44.1-1-4). That law requires a Form 236 disclosure accepted at a public board meeting before the contract is approved.
   - It may also be blocked by the policy above, since the district may already own the work.
   - **Safer routes:** paid extra-duty work through payroll, or offering it free as part of your job.
3. **Outside-activity notice.** Tell your Superintendent before starting, as policy 3231 requires.
4. **Use your own devices and personal accounts** for all business work.

## 4. Pricing (all Inferred, based on sourced benchmarks)

**Benchmarks:**
- AI training courses: $49 per person for one course, $149 for a 4-course suite (AI for Education).
- Indiana ESC custom PD: $1,540–$2,200 a day.
- Indiana University's training center: $2,700 a day.
- AI half-day workshop: $6,000+ (Global Online Academy).
- Independent presenter: $600 per half day, $1,200 per full day.

| Package | Suggested starting price | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Pilot:** kit for 1 building or department + one 90-minute training + 30 days of email support | **$750–$1,500** | Below the cost of an ESC PD day; easy to approve on a purchase order. |
| **District license** (kit for all staff + 2 trainings + 1 year of updates) | **$2,000–$5,000**, scaled by district size | Comparable to one or two ESC PD days, plus updates. |
| **Custom assistant build** for one department | **$1,500–$4,000** | Based on business setup ranges ($300–$1,000+), adjusted for school requirements: review, accessibility and documentation. |
| **Yearly update renewal** (optional, renewed by a new purchase order each year) | **$300–$1,000/yr** | Covers re-testing after AI tool changes. It can't auto-renew (Indiana rule). |

Price your first 1–2 districts at the low end in exchange for a written reference and permission to share results.

## 5. Sales kit to prepare

- [ ] **One-page product sheet:** the problem, what's included, the training, and price tiers.
- [ ] **Safety and privacy summary (one page):**
  - staff-facing only;
  - no student data required, and instructions tell staff never to enter it;
  - works inside the district's own AI accounts, so you never see their data;
  - follows the IDOE AI guidance;
  - accessible documents.
- [ ] **Demo:** a 5-minute screen recording using made-up data.
- [ ] **Quote template:** scope, price, timeline and Indiana-safe terms (no auto-renewal, indemnity or out-of-state law). Include space for a W-9.
- [ ] **Simple license:** the district may use it for its staff; no resale; updates for X months; you keep ownership of the general method.
- [ ] **Clearance letter** or waiver from your own district.

## 6. How to reach buyers (Inferred)
1. **Start with an ESC.**
   - Offer the kit + training as an ESC session (file 03).
   - The ESC reaches many districts at once and handles some of the purchasing.
   - Ask the ESC how it adds outside presenters' products to its catalog.
2. **Pilot with one nearby district** (not your own). Email the curriculum director with the product sheet and safety summary, and offer the pilot price.
3. **Districts with AI or digital-learning grants.** IDOE posts its 2026 Digital Learning Grant recipients on its Digital Learning page. Those districts already have a budget for this.
4. **Conferences:** present at the Indiana Connected Educators (ICE) conference or an ESC summer institute, then follow up with attendees.
5. **Results sell.** After each pilot, gather a short staff survey (time saved, how useful it was) to use in your next pitch. Don't overstate results.
